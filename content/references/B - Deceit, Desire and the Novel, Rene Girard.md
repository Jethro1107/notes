# Mimetic nature of desire is expressed in its geometry <span class="tag" tag-name="Desire"><span class="smallcaps">Desire</span></span>

Desire towards an object is often viewed as linear - I, a conscious, logical human being expresses interest towards an object. There are many reasons we give towards 'the why' for this desire - it is attractive, it is useful, it is needed - the common point of departure being that it originated from myself. The 'nature' of the object invoked a flame within me, the passion is sufficient to account for the desire. The desire is thus spontaneous, and I am original.

The straight line portrayed is present in human desire, but it is not the whole picture. **Girard believes desire is triangular** - the mediator exists above this line, connecting between subject and object, which often <u>reveals the true nature of desire</u>.

We often choose the object of desire based on the desires of others, for the passion of others towards this object gives it normative value.

**The subject imitates the desire of the mediator for the object** - his desire is not spontaneous, and he is not an original, but a copy.

## Chapter 1: 'Triangular' Desire

Initially difficult to read as it draws from 19th century french literature as sources of the 'novelistic' truth, but principally the aim is to show that certain literature, either classified into romatic or novelistic reveal the true nature of desire.

- **Mimetic desire as 'triangular' desire** - Desire for an object does not arise autonomously from the individual, but is mediated by another person (mediator), who influences what the other desires
    - **Triangular structure** - systematic view of the nature of desire as a gestalt comprised w/ 3 components:
        - <u>Subject</u> - the hero who desires
        - <u>Object</u> - the thing to be desired
        - <u>Mediator</u> - the individual who influences the subject's perception of the object as desriable
- **Internal vs external mediation** - whether mimetic desire descends into mimetic rivalry is dependent on <u>'spiritual' distance</u>:
    - **External mediation** - mediator mediates desire of the hero through a safe distance (unable to close the gap), influencing, yet not threatened by the hero's imitation (the mediator will never be a rival):
        - <u>Hero</u> - proclaims aloud the true nature of his desire (i.e. openly worship hismodel)
            - <u>Don Quixote</u> (Cervantes) - guided by the desires of the fictional character Amandis, while he himself mediates desires of Sancho (geographically close, socially distant)
            - <u>The Red and the Black</u> (Stendhal) - Julien mimicks desire of Napoleon
        - <u>Object</u> - can be fictionary, other-worldly
        - <u>Mediator</u> - role of guidance towards the object,
    - **Internal mediation** - mediator and the hero exist in the same social sphere, where both are capable of competing for the same object (esp. if the hero gains the attention of the mediator)
        - <u>Hero</u> - does not boast his efforts to imitate but carefully hides, torn between submissive reverence and irretrievable malice (i.e. hatred)
- **The romantic lie** - Spontaneity of desire as the pervasive central dogma in the 19th century, where the Hero himself is thoroughly original (emphasised in romantic literature)
    - Girard argues that human desire is inherently deceitful because subjects often believe Their desires are independent and original when, in fact, they are imitative. This self-deception obscures the triangular nature of desire.
    - The subject often refuses to acknowledge the role of the mediator, falsely believing that their desire for the object is spontaneous and authentic.
- **Self and other in literary structure** - novelists have intuitive understanding on the epistimeological structures of their times and express them through the medium of their art (p3):
    - <u>Systematic criticism as knowledge</u> - systematic criticism to abstract away the essance of the metaphysical 'systems' in place
    - <u>Definition of value</u> - based on ability to enhance comprehension, rather than dilute or dissolve complexities of the literature it engages with

## Chapter 4: Master and Slave

- **Romantic analysis depicts Manichean concept of the novel** - implies presence of 'right versus wrong', the hero depicts a value system that is admirable, because the author despises the villains
- **Novelists describe a novelistic truth** - description of an ontological phenomenon, i.e. the spreading of this ontological sickness where people start to imitate desires (<u>Desire is contangious</u>):
    - <u>Cervante's Don Quixote</u> - friends of Don Quixote rush to drive Don Quixote away from his desire to become Amandis, but at the end the doctor beccomes just as rabid as the patient (cf relationship between Harley Quinn and Joker)
- **Desire is contagious, and becomes more contagious as distance between mediator and subject closes in** - i.e. <u>Contagion and proximity are one and the same phenomenon</u> (p99):
    - **World where internal mediation is prevalent** - no one is capable of spontaneous desire, and resorts to imitation, i.e. the mediator mediates his neighbours desire w/o having the capacity for spontaneous desire, and he will be tempted to <u>copy the copy of his own desire</u> (start of rivalry and violence)
    - **Double (or reciprocal) mediation** - causes the metamorphosis of the object – intrinsic value of the desired object is rendered insignificant as they draw closer
    - **Altisidora vision of the underworld** (p102) - devilish tennis players batting books across the court (symobolises reciprocal character in which imitation assumes):
        - <u>When you compete you have already lost</u> - although it would be natural for those who won to be happy and those who lost to be sorry, everybody was grumbling and grousing and cursing
            - Because this rival stems from each one holdiing the Other responsible for the misfortune which falls upon him (equal cause of suffering for all)
            - One cannot simply withdraw from the game, as it implies you are not the Original, but the Imitator
        - <u>Setting in the underground</u> - All imitated desire, no matter how noble and inoffensive, will drag its victims (i.e. model-disciple, disciple-model) through the depths of hell
        - <u>Books filled with air and stuffing, ang growing astonishingly in number</u>
    - **Indifference plays a role in genesis of reciprocal desires** - the subject may not have genuine interest of the object of desire, but is acquired through imitation of a mediator:
        - **Principles of coquetry** (收兵) - to imitate one's lover desire is essentially to desire oneself, thanks to the lovers desire: (i want a better analysis of this)
            - The coquette flirts not to surrender to the person who approaches, but as a means to feel more precious
            - The value she sees in herself is based exclusively on the favour which is regarded by Others
            - Vicous cycle of double mediation:
                - Indifference expressed by the coquette drives desire of the lover
                - The desire further feeds into coquetry
    - **Dissimulation as a means of acquisition of the object** - hide the desire one feels and pretend a desire one does not feel (i.e. lying which is the norm, or vanieux, the passionate being is extremely rare in the post-revolutionary world)
    - **Dialectic of 'master and slave'** (p109, ?contrasting Hegel?) - Hegelian dialect resides on violence, novelistic dialectic resides in hypocrisy (triumph over force depicted in Stendhal's Black ove Red)
