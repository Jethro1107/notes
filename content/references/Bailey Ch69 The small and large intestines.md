# Tumours of the large intestine

## Benign tumours of the large intestine

- **Polyp** - clinical description of any protrusion from the mucosa, encompassing a variety of histologically different tumours:
    - Inflammatory polyp - pseudopolyps in UC (skin lesions)
    - Metaplastic polyps
    - Harmartomatous polyps - Peutz-Jeghers polyp or juvenile polyp
    - Adenomatous polyp - classified histologically into tubular, tubulovillous and villous
- **Adenomatous polyp:**
    - **Pathology:**
        - Tubular adenoma - resembles berry on stalk (pedunculated)
        - Villous adenoma - flat spreading lesion
    - **Clinical features** - typically on incidental findings on colonoscopy for colonic Sx and rectal bleeding:
        - Villous tumours - altered bowel habits (diarrhoea, mucous secretions), ocassionally hypoK, hypoalbuminaemia (?malabsorption)
    - **Risk of malignant transformation** - dependent on size and type:
        - Increasing risk w/ size - \>5 cm requires resection by <u>endoscopic therapies</u> due to maligant potential
        - Type of adenoma - villous \> tubular
    - **Mx:**
        - Endoscopic Tx for colonic lesions - Colonic snare polypeptomy, Endoscopic mucosal resection (EMR), transanal endoscopic microsurgery (EMR)
        - Surgical Tx for rectal lesions - transanal resection, proctectomy, argon beam ablation

### Familial adenomatous polyposiis

- **Definition** - familial cancer predisposition syndrome <u>defined clinically by presence of \> 100 colorectal adenomas</u>, but also characterised by duodenal adenomas and multiple extra-intestinal manifestations
- **Genetics** - defective adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) gene on Ch5
- **Pathogenesis:**
    - **Inheritence of defective copy of adenomatous polyposis coli (APC) gene** - AD inheritence pattern (M=F), most (80%) have +ve family Hx (FHx of early onset, multiple CC and FAP-associated malignancies), although can occur in new mutation of the gene
    - **Invariably leads to colorectal cancer** - 100% in patients w/ FAP, manifests as early CC
    - **Malignant potentials:**
        - Endodermal derivatives - adenoma of duodenum, stomach, SB, LB, thyroid, biliary tree, liver (hepatoblastoma)
        - Ectodermal derivatives - epidermoid cysts, congenital hypertrophy of retinal pigment epithelium (CHRPE), pilomatrixoma, brain tumours
        - Mesoderna derivatives - desmoid tumours, osteomas, dental problems
- **Clinical features:**
    - **Multiple adenomas** - identifiable since 15y and is invariable in 30y (\> 100 adenomas carpeting colon)
    - **Early onset of CA colon** - typically 10-20y after polyposis, 2/3 will have \>1 CA
    - **Colonic Sx** - typically in new mutations or family members not screened:
        - Altered bowel habits - loose stools, blood and mucous
        - Lower abdominal pain
        - Weight loss
- **Screening policy** - at risk family members screened by genetic testing + yearly endoscopy since 10-12y

## Malignant tumours of the large intestine

- **Colorectal carcinoma** - common neoplasm of the large intestine, including the rectum
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Incidence - 3rd in incidence, 2nd in mortality (HK cancer registry)
    - Demographic - M=F
- **Etio-pathogenesis of colorectal cancer:**
    - Adenoma-carcinoma sequence - cancer development arises from adenomatous polyps after a sequence of genetic mutations influenced by environmental factors
    - **Environmental risk factors:**
        - Diet - high intake of red meat (haem and N-nitroso compounds), animal fats, low dietary fibre intake
        - SHx - Smoking, alcohol
    - **PMH** - Inflammatory bowel disease, FHx of CC and FAP related cancers
- **Pathology of colorectal cancer:**
    - Macroscopic pathology - 1) Annular (likely cause LBO), 2) Tubular, 3) Ulcer, 4) Cauliflower
    - Histopathology - Adenocarcinoma
- **Mode of spread of colorecal cancer:**
    - **Direct invasion** - longitudinal, transverse, radial:
        - Longitudinal - along the colon
        - Transverse - through the colonic wall
        - Radial - to adjacent organ (e.g. SB, stomach, pelvic organs, anterior abdominal wall, ureters, duodenum, posterior abdominal wall)
    - **Lymphatic spread** - towards central LN
    - **Haematogenous spread** - liver (via portal vein), lungs (via pulmonary cerculation), ovaries, brain, kidney and bone
    - **Transcoelomic spread** - direct invasion through serosa or subperitoneal lymphatics
- **Clinical features of colorectal cancer:**
    - **Left-sided CA colon** (common) - typically presents w/ <u>altered bowel habits</u> or <u>PR bleeding</u>
    - **Right-sided CA colon** - typically presents w/ iron-deficiency anaemia or as an abdominal mass
    - **Hepatic or splenic flexure** - non-specific upper abdominal discomforts immediately
    - **Presentation as surgical emergency** (20%) - typically presents as IO or peritonitis
        - Prognosis - a/w poorer prognosis irrespective of staging
- **Ix of colorectal cancer:**
    - **Endoscopy** - visualise up tot the sigmoid or the entire LB on rectal bleeding and biopsy for histological diagnosis:
        - Flexible sigmoidoscope (sedation not required) - 60-cm fibreoptic sigmoidoscope that assesses the LB up to splenic flexure (detect 70% of CA colon and almost all that presents w/ PR bleeding)
        - Colonoscopy (sedation required) - visualise entire LB aiming at the caecum to detec the primary tumour, synchronous polyps, or multiple carcinomas
    - **Imaging:**
        - CT thorax and abdomen (gold standard) - T-staging and identification of distant metastasis (liver and lungs)
        - Double-contrast barium enema - apple-core lesion (a constant irregular filling defect)
        - CT virtual colonoscopy - additional modaility that replaces barium enema (able to pick up polyps as small as 6mm)
- **Staging of colorectal cancer** - TNM staging (international standard):
    - **T-staging:**
        - T1 - into submucosa
        - T2 - into muscularis propria
        - T3 - into pericolic fat or sub-serosa not breaching serosa
        - T4 - breaches serosa or invades adjacent oragns
    - **N-staging:**
        - N0 - no nodal involvement
        - N1 - 1-3 LN involvement
        - N2 - 4+ LN involvement
    - **M-staging:**
        - M0 - no metastasis
        - M1 - distant metastasis
- **Prognosis** - overall 5y survival approx. 50%:
    - **Prognostic factors** - tumour staging (esp. LN status):
        - T1-3 - typically cured by surgical resection w/ 90% 5y disease-free survival
        - T4 - 5y survival approx. 60-70%
        - N+ - 5y survival approx. 30%
        - M1 - 5y survival \< 10%

### Management of colorectal cancer

- **Principles of management:**
    - Local disease - surgical Tx +/- adjuvant chemotherapy
    - Metastatic disease - hepatic metastasis for resectable diseases, palliative chemotherapy
- **Surgical Tx:**
    - **Pre-operative preparation:**
        - **Mechanical bowel preparation** (oral preparation to clear faecal material) - not routinely performed other than rectal cancer, due to <u>increased risk of wound infection</u>
        - **Prophylactic anticoagulants** - increased risk of VTE in general surgical patients and those w/ malignancy, give compression stockings and prophylactic SC LMWH
        - **Prophylactic ABx** - against bowel organisms to prevent intra-abdominal sepsis and wound infections
        - +/- Counselling and marking appropriate site for <u>stoma</u>
    - **Operation** - removal of primary tumour and locoregional LN:
        - **Right hemicolectomy** - indicated for CA caecum or ascedning colon:
            - Procedure - resection of the caecum, desccending colon, along with terminal 20cm of the ileum and proximal 1/3 of the transverse colon:
                1.  Incision of peritoneum lateral to ascending colon
                2.  Elevation of the right colon along with leaf of peritoneum containing vasculature and lymphatics, from the posterior abdominal wall (preserve posterior abdominal wall structures)
                3.  Ligation of arteries - iliocolic artery at SMA +/- right colic artery (10% of individuals).
                4.  Division of the greater omentum up to the point of resection of the transverse colon
                5.  Resection and anastamosis of the ileum and transverse colon (sucture or stapler technique)
        - **Extended right hemicolectomy** - indicated for CA transverse colon and splenic flexure:
            - Procedure - resection of the entire transverse colon, requiring ligation of the whole transverse mesocolon
        - **Left hemicolectomy** - indicated for CA descending colon and sigmoid colon:
            - Procedure - resection of the entire descending colon and sigmoid:
                1.  Mobilise left colon and sigmoid colon (w/ care to left ureter and gonadals) completely along white line that marks the lateral attachment of mesocolon
                2.  Mobilise splenic flexure by contining disection from below and from the transverse colon side by entering lesser sec
                3.  Ligation of IMA (near origin) and IMV (at lower border of pancreas)
                4.  Resection and anastamosis
        - **Emergency surgery** - for 20% of patients presenting w/ surgical emergency (obstruction, haemorrhage, perforation)
    - **Post-operative care:**
        - Monitor for complications - e.g. sepsis or peritonitis due to anastomotic leakages
        - Supportive care - NG drainage and cautious introduction of oral fluids and then diet
        - Continue prophylactic ABx
        - Enhanced recovery programme (ERP) - shorten LOS from 10-14d to 2-3d by modulating surgical response
- **Adjuvant chemotherapy** - currently no role for pre-operative chemotherapy, but 5-fluorouracil-based Tx shown to improve outcomes for node-positive diseases
- **Hepatic resection** - can be resected for isolated liver metastasis (5y survival approx. 30%)

### Follow-up of colorectal cancer

- **Aims of FU:**
    - Identify synchronous tumour (3%) - tumour present at time of Dx of 1st tumour, but not picked up due to emergency presentation or incomplete assessment
    - Identify metachronous tumour (3%) - tumour developed after the initial malignancy
- **Ix of FU:**
    - Surveillance colonoscopy - identify syncrhonous or metachronous tumours
    - Routine liver imaging (US/ CT) - because 1/2 CC patients will have liver metastasis at any point of life
    - Serial measurement of carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA)

# Intestinal diverticula

- **Intestinal diverticula** - structural abnormalities reflecting hollow out-pouchings that can occur from the esophagus to the recto-sigmoid junction
    - <u>Congenital diverticula</u> - All 3 coats of bowel are present in the wall of the diverticulum (e.g. Meckel's diverticulum)
    - <u>Acquired diverticula</u> - Absence of muscularis layer in the wall of the diverticulum, caused by herniation of the mucosa and submucosa through the muscularis layer

## Jejunal diverticula

- **Definition** - diverticula arising from the mesenteric side of jejunum through hermination of through penetrating sites of blood vessels
- **Clinical features of jejunal diverticula:**
    - Asymptomatic - typically an incidental finding at surgery or radiology
    - Malabsorption - as a result of bacterial stasis competing for nutrients
    - Acute abdomen - due to inflammation +/- perforation of the diverticula
    - Haemorrhage (rare) - rarely seen in jejunal diverticula
- **Mx:**
    - Resection +/- anastamosis or stoma formation:
        - Resection - dependent on how much of the proximal SB affected (precludes resection)
        - Anastamosis or stoma formation - dependent on degree of contamination, physiological stability, and local resources for Mx of high-output jejuostomy

## Meckel's diverticulum

- **Pathology of Meckel's diverticulum**
    - Rule of 2 - 2% of population, 2 ft (60cm) from ileocaecal junction, classically 2" (5cm) long.
    - Remnant of the embryonic vitellointestinal duct - structurally contains all 3 layers of the GI tract and has its own blood supply (may contain ectopic gastric, colonic, or pancreatic mucosa), w/ fibrous cord connecting the apex to the umbilicus
- **Clinical presentation of Meckel's diverticulum** - vast majority asymptomatic (can be left alone in incidental finding during abdominal surgery)
    - Haemorrhage - only if gastric mucosa is present causing peptic ulcer (typically presents as maroon rectal bleeding or melaena despite cleared upper GI and lower GI workup, diagnosed by Meckel's scan)
    - Meckel's diverticulitis - presents like appendicitis (looked for in surgery when anormal appendix is found), or PPU if perforated
    - Intussusception - as lead point of ileoileal or ileocolic intususception
    - Chronic ulceration - manifests as chronic poorly localised periumbilical pain (midgut origin)
    - IO - connection to the anterior abdominal wall (via umbilicus) can cause direct IO or formation of volvolus
    - Perforation

## Colonic diverticular disease

- **Definition** - Out-pouchings of the colon where the mucosa and submucosa layers herniate through the muscular wall at sites where blood vessels penetrate the bowel wall
- **Epidemiology** - exceedingly common in the elderly, overwhelmingly in the sigmoid region:
    - **Prevalence** - \> 60% in elderly over 80y (75% in \> 70y in Western world)
    - **Common sites** - sigmoid (except south-east Asia, where right-sided diverticula is more common)
- **Etiology** - not fully understood:
    - **Deficient dietary fibre** in refined Western diet (supported by epidemiological data) - increased intraluminal pressure causing herniation of mucosa (esp. in normally narrow sigmoid)
    - ?Role of **aging** and altered collagen structure in colon
- **Clinical features of colonic diverticula:**
    - **Diverticulosis** - completely asymptomatic, with incidental findings
    - Mildly symptomatic (****painful diverticulosis****) - abdominal distension, flatulence, and sensation of heaviness in the lower abdomen (indistinguishable from those of IBS)
    - **Presentation w/ complications** (10-30%):
        - ****Diverticulitis**** - inflammation of the diverticula causing lower abdominal pain, w/ lower abdominal tenderness and guarding
        - **Perforation** (complicated diverticulitis) - most commonly contained leading to pericolic abscess formation, but occasionally leading to generalised peritonitis
        - **Intestinal obstruction** - Progressive fibrosis leads to stenosis (large bowel obstruction), or loops of SB becomes adherant to inflammed colon resulting in SBO
        - **Haemorrhage** - erosion into vasculature presenting as LGIB
        - **Fistula formation** (5%) - colovesical (most common), colovaginal, enteroolic, colocutaneous fistula formation
- **Mx of diverticular diseases:**
    - Lifestyle modifications - take high-fibre diet and bulk forming laxatives
    - Pain relief - Antispasmodics

### Diverticulitis:

- **Definition** - inflammation of one or several adjacent diverticula, which may be uncomplicated or complicated (contaminated)
- **Pathogenesis** - not fully known, proposed to be obstruction of diverticula by faecolit
- **Clinical features of diverticulitis:**
    - Persistent lower abdominal pain
    - Altered bowel habits - loose stools or constipation
    - Fever, malaise and leukocytosis - differentiate diverticulitis from painful diverticulosis
- **Signs of colonic diverticulitis:**
    - Tenderness and guarding over lower abdomen (LLQ or RLQ ocassionally)
    - Ocassionally palpable sigmoid colon (LLQ) - if extremely thickened from inflammation
    - PR examination - tender mass if pericolic abscess formation (requires radiological confirmation)
- **Classification of contamination** (Hinchey classification of complicated diverticulitis):
    - Grade I - Mesenteric or pericolic abscess
    - Grade II - Pelvic abscess
    - Grade III -Purulent peritonitis
    - Grade IV - Faecal peritonitis
- **Ix for suspected diverticulitis:**
    - **CBC w/ differentials** - leukocytosis (can have normal WBC in up to 45% patients)
    - **CRP** - non-specifically elevated
    - **Serum amylase and lipase** - elevated especially if complicated with peritonitis
    - **Urinalysis** - sterile pyuria due to adjacent inflammation (presence of colonic flora suggests colovesical fistula)
    - **Imaging:**
        - Plain CXR and AXR - look for pneumoperitoneum (perforation)
        - CT - presence of localised bowel wall thickening, increased soft tissue density, abscess formation and fistula formation
        - +/- Soluble contrast enema (barium enema avoided) - if CT access limited to look for intraluminal inflammation and contrast extravastatation
    - **Endoscopy** - <u>delayed after 6 weeks from the acute attack</u>:
        - Colonospic findings - necks of diverticula within bowe lumen +/- strictures
    - **Mx of acute diverticulitis:**
        - Conservative Mx (Best Medical Tx):
            - NPO - rest the bowel
            - Catheterisation - prevent risk of colovesical fistulation
            - General meausres - IV fluid resuscitation and analgesics
            - IV ABx - cover G- bacilli and anaerobes
            - +/- surgical drainage of pericolic abscesses (\> 5cm diameter)
        - Surgical Mx for diverticular disease:
            - Emergency surgery - to control peritoneal sepsis:
                - Indications - generalised peritonitis, failure to respond to best medical Tx
                - Procedures - Laparotomy and thorough washout of contamination + Hartmann's procedure (safest) / resection w/ colonic washout and anastamosis (+/- defunctioning loop ileostomy)
            - Elective surgery - controversial, but offered to selected patients w/ recurrent attacks

# Stomas

- **Definition of stoma** - an artificial opening made in the LB or SB to divert faeces or flatus outside the abdomen where they can be collected in an external appliance (temporary or permanent)
- **Features of stoma:**
    - Time course - may be temporary or permanent
    - Bowel involvement - character. content, position:
        - Ileostomy - sprouted, w/ liquid output, typically in right iliac fossa
        - Colostomy - flush, w/ semi-solid output, typically in left iliac fossa
- **Loop colostomy:**
    - Indications of loop colostomy - used to defunction a distal anstamosis after an anterior resection (less commonly employed and loop ileostomy now commonly performed)
    - Procedure - Mobilise loop of colon to surface, colostomy performed and edge of excision sutured to adjacent skin margin
    - Complications - prone to prolapse, and potential poor healing of anastamosis
- **End colostomy:**
    - Indications - after abdominoperineal excision of rectum (APR) or as part of Hartmann's procedure
    - Procedure - Bring divided colon through left iliac fossa trephine in rectus abdominis and skin, typicall at lateral edge of rectus sheath, above and medial to ASIS (prevents colostomy bag from impinging the ASIS)
- **Loop ileostomy:**
    - Indications - defunctioning of a low rectal anastamosis or an ileal pouch
    - Procedure - Mobilise a knuckle of ileum through skin trephine in right iliac fossa, w/ ileostomy incision, pulled over the top to more proximal part to create a spout
- **End ileostomy:**
    - Indications - temporarily in subtotal colectomy w/out anastamosis, or permanently in panproctocolectomy
    - Procedure - Mobilisation of ileum through rectus abdominis muscle, and formation of a spout
- **Complications of stoma:**
    - Skin irritation
    - Prolapse
    - Retraction
    - Ischaemia
    - Stenosis
    - Parastomal hernia
    - Bleeding
    - Fistulation
