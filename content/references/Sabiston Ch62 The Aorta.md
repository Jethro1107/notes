## Acute Aortic Syndromes

### Aortic aneurysms

- **Definition** - defined by an increase in size of \> 50% above the normal arterial diameter, occuring anywhere from the aortic root to the aortic bifurcation
- **Epidemiology of AAA** - difficult to estimate as most are asymptomatic:
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 3-10% based on **large-screening studies in US population**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - recent decline over past 2 decades in 1st-world countries attributable to decreased tobacco burden
- **Classification of aortic aneurysms based on pathoanatomy:**
    - **Classification by morphology:**
        - <u>Fusiform aneurysms</u> - exhibit smooth, circumferential dilatation across the entire vessel
        - <u>Saccular eneurysm</u> - focal outpouching of the arterial wall
    - **Classification by structure:**
        - <u>True aneurysm</u> - aneurysmal wall containing all 3 layers of the vessel wall
        - <u>False aneurysms</u> (pseudoaneurysm) - focal deficits in artery wall with an associated collection of blood surrounded by connective tissue (either degenerative, infective or traumatic in origin)
- **Classification of aortic aneurysms based on site** - <u>different absolute size criteria</u> due to difference in mean aortic diameter between ascending, descending and infrarenal aorta:
- **Etiology or risk factors of AAA development** - associated w/ factors resulting in 1) weakening of aortic wall, and 2) increased local haemodynamic forces:
    - **Non-modifiable risk factors:**
        - <u>Demographic</u> - increasing age, male gender
        - <u>Hereditary Connective Tissue Disorders</u> - Marfan's syndrome, Vascular Type Ehlers-Danlos, Familial thoracic aortic aneurysm and dissection
        - <u>+FHx</u> - less well defined entities that contribute to significant increase in incidence of aneurysms in patients w/ +ve FHx
        - <u>Concurrent aneurysms</u> - <u>esp. in M</u> presenting w/ concurrent iliac aneurysm, femoropopliteal aneurysm
    - **Modifiable risk factors** - similar to atherosclerotic risk factors (N.B. DM is a protective factor):
        - Smoking (key factor in development, expansion and rupture)
        - Hypercholesterolaemia
        - HTN
    - **Protective factors for AAA development** - female gender, black race, <u>diabetes mellitus</u>

- **Clinical presentation of AAA** - most patient are asymptomatic, whereas symptomatic patients are suggestive of rupture or impending rupture:
    - **Asymptomatic AAA** - discovered as a result of 1) screening (mainly in USA), 2) incidental finding on P/E, 3) Incidental finding on radiography
    - **Symptomatic non-ruptured AAA** (5-22% of patient) - increased risk of aneurysm ruptures:
        - **Abdominal pain** - indolent onset of abdominal pain due to compression of adjacent structures:
            - <u>Site</u> - periumbilical, but back, flank, pelvic or groin pain is also described
            - <u>Onset</u> - indolent onset (cf sudden onset in ruptured AAA)
            - <u>Quality</u> - vague, poorly localised non-specific pain
            - <u>Exacerbating and relieving factors</u> - unaffected by posture and movement
        - **Limb ischaemia** - acute limb ischaemia from distal thromboembolism of atherothrombotic debris
        - **Constitutional symptoms** - chronic abdominal pain, weight loss, low-grade fevr due to aortitis
    - **Ruptured AAA** - classical triad of abdominal pain, hypotension and pulsatile mass (25-50%)
- **Ix and Dx evaluation of AAA:**
    - **USG** - diagnostic and important for surveillance of AAA not large enough for repair:
        - <u>Advantages</u> - good test properties, non-invasive and cost-effective
        - <u>Clinical use</u>:
            - 1\. Dx of AAA
            - 2\. Surveillance of AAA in those not warranting AAA repair
            - 3\. Detection, localisation, and quantification of endoleaks after EVAR
    - **Contrast CT aortogram/ angiography** (CTA) - gold-standard for pre-operative planning:
        - <u>Clinical use of CTA in AAA</u> - mainly for pre-operative planning:
            - 1\. Diameter measurement - greater reproducibility of diameter measurements than by USG
            - 2\. Assessment of aneurysmal anatomy - assess the viability of EVAR
            - 3\. Detection of vessel calcification and thrombus (important information for EVAR)
            - 4\. Detection of concurrent arterial occlusive disease
            - 5\. Multiplanar and 3D reconstruction for operative planning
        - <u>Limitations of CTA in AAA</u>:
            - 1\. Substantial radiation exposure - limited utility in the setting as a serial examination
            - 2\. Use of iodinated contrast - risk of contrast nephropathy in a population w/ high incidence of comorbid CKD
    - **IV gandolinum contrast MRA** - alternative to CT with benefits being the elmination of ionizing radiation and iodinated contrast
- **Principles of Mx of aortic aneurysm** - size and Sx plays a large role in Mx:
    - **Mx of asymptomatic AAA** - RF control, regular surveillance, and surgical intervention once indicated
        - <u>Medical therapy</u> - BP control and lipid control (lack of conclusive evidence regarding prevention of AAA enlargement, but often indicated as patients are comorbid)
        - <u>Surveillance</u> - routine surveillance for non-surgical aneurysms by USG where interval is based on size and risk of rupture
        - <u>Surgical Mx</u>:
            - **Approaches** - open vs EVAR
    - **Mx of symptomatic non-ruptured AAA** - immediate surgical Mx for symptomatic AAA to prevent rupture
    - **Mx of ruptured AAA** - resuscitation, diagnosis and urgent surgery

1.  Management of asymptomatic abdominal aortic aneurysm

    - **Principles of Mx:**
      - <u>Non-surgical aneurysms</u> - Mx by risk factor control and regular surveillance to prevent eneurysm enlargement
      - <u>Surgical aneurysm</u> - surgical intervention once size-criteria is met
    - **Size criteria for surgical intervention in asymptomatic aortic aneurysm** - more lenient threshold of \> 5mm in Asians with AAA due to smaller mean aortic diameter: 
    ![](img/size_criteria_for_intervention_of_asymptomatic_aortic_aneurysm.png)
    - **Lifestyle modification:**
      - **Smoking cessation** - current smoking a/w increased risk of aneurysmal enlargement (~0.4mm/y) and rupture
      - **Physical activity** - avoid vigorous activity and contact sport (risk of rupture or dissection to sudden spikes in BP)
    - **Optimal medical therapy** - aims at prevention of aneurysmal dilatation or even regression:
      - **Blood pressure management** - aims at reduction of systolic pressure and pulse pressure:
        - <u>Selection</u> - beta-blockers (selective or non-selective)
        - <u>Evidence</u> - prevention of aneurysmal dilatation using BBs, ARB and ACEi in selected patients w/ <u>Marfan's syndrome</u> (no current evidence for those w/o connective tissue disorders)
      - **Lipid-lowering agents** - reduced progression of atherosclerotic diseaseL
        - <u>Selection</u> - statins (HMG-CoA inhibitors)
        - <u>Evidence</u>:
          - 1\. Reduced rate of AAA enlargement
          - 2\. Improves survival after open or endovascular AAA repair
          - 3\. Reduced risk of adverse cardiovascular events (stroke, MI, SCD)
      - **Anti-platelet therapy** (e.g. asprin) - secondary preventive benefits in this population
    - **Regular surveillance** - regular follow-up and surveillance of aneurysms to identify cases that require surgical intervention (e.g. symptomatic or meet size criteria):
      - **Modality of surveillance** - <u>abdominal USG</u> as low-cost, acceptible sensitivity, and minimal harm to patient
      - **Surveillance intervals** - guided by understanding of differential growth rates dependent on size (Society for Vascular Surgery Guidelines):
        - \< 2.6cm - no further screening required (does not meet Dx of AAA)
        - 2.6-2.9cm - not AAA, re-examination at 5y
        - 3-3.4cm - re-examination at 3y
        - 3.5-4.4cm - re-examination at 12mo
        - 4.5-5.4cm - re-examination at 6mo
    - **Surgical intervention for AAA** - open AAA repair vs endovascular aneurysm repair (EVAR):
      - **Evidence comparing open vs EVAR** - UK EVAR1 and EVAR 2 trials randomize \> 5.5mm aneurysms for elective open repair vs EVAR:
        - <u>Evidence</u>:
          - 30-day mortality rate favourable for EVAR (1.8% vs 4.3%, P=0.02)
          - Long-term survival is comparable between open and EVAR (early benefits eventually lost)
          - EVAR is more costly (increased graft related complications and need for intervention)
        - <u>Implications</u> - EVAR offers early survival benefits (particularly over 30-day post-operation extending to 6y), but survival benefits are eventually lost (comparable long-term survival)
        - <u>Current paradigm</u> - roughly 80% of non-emergent AAA is repaired with endovascular approach
      - **Open AAA repair:**
        - **Considerations for open repair:**
          - <u>Age</u> - younger patients can be considered for open repair
          - <u>Comorbidities</u> - patients w/ multiple comorbidities are unsuitable for massive aortic surgery esp. as it involves cross-clamping the aorta
          - <u>Etiology of AAA</u> - patients w/ connective tissue disorders are better managed by open repair
          - <u>Patients willingness to life-long surveillance</u> - patient unwilling for surveillance may be managed w/ open repair
      - **Endovascular aneurysm repair** (EVAR):
        - **Procedure** - placement of <u>modular graft components</u> delivered via the iliac or femoral arteries to exclude AAA from circulation
        - **Anatomical considerations for EVAR:**
          - <u>Aneurysmal neck</u> - requires sufficient length and minimal angulation
          - <u>Aneurysmal contents</u> - w/o extensive mural thrombus or calcification
          - <u>Proximal seal zone</u> - diameter not too large to avoid proximal seal
          - <u>Access vessel anatomy</u> - sufficient diameter and minimal tortuosity and calcification present in distal seal zones
        - **Post-operative evaluation of success and surveillance imaging** - life-long surveillance necessary for assessment of <u>integreity of EVAR</u>:
          - **Imaging modalities** - serial CT scans (ionising radiation) +/- duplex USG
          - **Intervals for surveillance imaging** - 1mo, 6mo, 12mo post-operatively and q1y thereafter
        - **Complications of EVAR:**
          - <u>Systemic complications</u>:
            - **Vascular injury** - e.g. iliac artery injury or arterial dissection
            - **Thromboembolic complications** - mesenteric ischaemia, acute renal failure
            - **Cardiac?**
          - <u>Graft-related complications</u>:
            - **Graft infection**
            - **Graft thrombosis**
            - **Graft migration**
            - **Endoleak** (see below)
        - **Types of endoleaks** - leakage in blood into aneurysm causing aneurysmal dilatation after EVAR repair:
          - <u>Type 1 endoleak</u> - leak at endograft landing site:
            - Type 1A - leakage at proximal landing zone
            - Type 1B - leakage at distal landing zone
          - <u>Type 2 endoleak</u> - retrograde flow from aortic collaterals into aneurysmal sac (from lumbar arteries or IMA)
          - <u>Type 3 endoleak</u> - junctional leaks due to endograft component separation
          - <u>Type 4 endoleak</u> - leakage as a result of fabric porosity
          - <u>Type 5 endoleak</u> - expanding aneurysm without demonstrable blood flow
        - **Principles of Mx of endoleaks** - dependent on type:
          - <u>Repair necessary</u> - Type 1 and 3 endoleak, as there is coninuous flow into AAA resulting in aneurysmal expansion
          - <u>Repair controversial</u> - Type 2 endoleak can be closely observed of there os absence of sac growth (sac growth with otherwise stable type 2 endoleak may be caused by delayed type 1 and 3 endoleak)

## Aortic Dissection, Intramural Haematoma, Penetrating Aortic Ulcers

- **Terminology** - aortic dissection (AD), intramural haematoma (IMH) and penetrating aortic ulcers (PAH) may prepresent the same disease within a spectrum, differing in part by the level of involvement of the aortic wall:
    - <u>Aortic dissection</u> - disease of the media
    - <u>Intramural haematoma</u> - disease of the media
    - <u>Penetrating aortic ulcer</u> - disease of the intima or media
