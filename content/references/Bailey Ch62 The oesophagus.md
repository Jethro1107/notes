# Ix for esophageal pathologies

## Radiography

- ****Barium Swallow**** - swallowing of barium contrast to demonstrate strictures, SOL, anatomical distortions or abnormal motility
    - Role - overshadowed by upper endoscopy but able to characterise strictures, CA, and achalasia (inaccurate for GERD)
    - C/I - suspected perforation
- ****CT**** - cross sectional imaging for suspected CA esophagus, and to demonstrate perforation

## Endoscopy

- ****Upper endoscopy**** (OGD) - diagnostic and therapeutic for many oesophageal pathologies:
    - Instrument - flexible video OGD vs rigid oesophagoscope (rendered obselete)
    - Role:
        - Diagnostic - visualise oesophagus, OGJ, stomach, and duodenum, for Bx and obtaining cytological specimen
        - Therapeutic - removal of FB, stricture dilatation, haemostasis
    - Novel techniques - better distinguish mucosal abnormalities not readily seen in white light by narrow band imaging and fluorescence

## Endosonography (Endoscopic USG)

- Principles - high-frequency (5-30 MHz) transducer at tip of endoscope to provide details of layers of esophageal wall and mediastinal structures
- Radial echo-endoscopes - rotating transducer to create transverse sectorial images at right angle to long axis of esophagus (as alternating hyper- and hypo-echoic bands)
- Linear echo-endoscopes - linear sectorial images for FNA and Bx

## Manometry

- ****Manometry**** - pressure sensors that are diagnostic of motility disorders
- ****Instrument**** - multilumen catheter with ring of pressure recording orifaces at different levels from oesophagus to stomach
    - ****Conventional manometry**** - pressure recording orifaces 5cm apart
    - ****High resolution manometry**** (HRM) - pressure recording orifaces 1cm apart, generating a spatio-temporal plot

# Perforation

- **Etiology of perforation of the esophagus:**
    - Iatrogenic - therapeutic upper endoscopy
    - Barotrauma - Bourheave syndrome (spontaneous performation)
    - Pathological perforation (rare) - ulcers or tumours of the esophagus
    - Penetrating injury
    - Foreign body
- **Mx of esophageal perforation:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - Operative Mx - direct repair of perforation (a/w surgical risks)
        - Non-operative Mx - limit effects of mediastinitis and promote healing
        - Dependent on:
            - Site of esophagus - cervical vs thoracolumbar
            - Etiology of perforation - instrumental vs spontaneous
            - Underlying pathology - benign vs malignant
            - Septic load - fasted and empty vs obstructed with stagnant residue
- **Boerhaeve syndrome:**
    - **Pathophysiology:**
        - Retching/ vomiting against a closed glottis causes rapid rise esophageal pressure, causing it to burst at it's weakest point (lower third)
        - Leakage of material into mediastinum and pleura - results in mediastinitis and eventually sepsis
    - **Clinical features:**
        - **Chest pain and epigastric pain** - typically following a large meal, bout of drinkng
        - **SOB** - often common if there is pleural involvement
    - **Diagnosis:**
        - CXR - reveals pneumomediastinum, pneumothorax, or pneumoperitoneum +/- pleural effusion

# Gastro-esophageal reflux disease

- ****Competence of OGJ**** - prevents reflux of gastric contents into esophagus, dependent on:
    - Physiological function - tonic contraction of LES
    - Anatomical location - in relation to diaphragm and esophageal hiatus (angle of His)
- ****Physiological reflux**** - reflux occuring in relation to post-prandial transient lower oesophageal sphincter relaxations (TLOSR)
- ****Epidemiology:****
    - Most common upper GI condition in Western World - declining prevalence of H. pylori infections, increasing risk factors of GERD
    - Explains parallel rise in incidence of AD of esophagus in Western World
- ****Pathophysiology of GERD**** - interactions between 1) LES functioning, 2) Intra-abdominal pressure, 3) Esophageal clearance
    - Loss of LES function (Hypotensive LES) - in early GERD, symptoms reflect increased TLOSR, but in advanced GERD, reflects a generally low LES pressure
    - Increased intra-abdominal pressure - central obesity, delayed gastric emptying, pregnancy
    - Defective esophageal clearance - e.g. achalasia
- ****Clinical features of GERD:****
    - ****Triad of Sx**** - Reflux (heartburn), epigastric pain, regurgitation
        - Reflux Sx - retrosternal burning pain ascending from thorax to throat, a/w extra-GI manifestations
        - Epigastric pain - ocassionally radiates to back
        - Regurgitation - regugitation of acidic or bitter contents, and results in bitter belching
    - ****Provacation**** - by food (\<1h), posture, and increased intra-abdominal pressure:
        - Food - Sx of exacerbated by food, especially those that delay gastric emptying (e.g. fats, spicy food)
        - Posture - stooping or supine position (noctural reflux a/w severe GERD almost always)
        - Factors increasing intra-abdominal pressure - e.g. exercise
    - Other manifestations:
        - ****Odynophagia**** - occurs in some patients after hot beverages, citrus drinks or alcohol
        - Atypical manifestations (extra-GI presentations):
            - Angina-like chest pain - likely due to reflux induced esophageal spasms
            - Respiratory Sx - GERD-associated asthma, chronic cough, hoarseness (laryngeal Sx)
        - ****Dysphagia**** - sinister sign and indication of upper endoscopy, suggestive of complications (e.g. peptic strictures, AD esophagus) or etiology (e.g. motility disorder)
- ****Diagnostic evaluation**** - empirical clinical diagnosis based on symptomatology and responsiveness to PPI Tx:
    - ****Subjective diagnosis**** - for uncomplicated patients:
        - Diagnostic questionaire - not routinely performed
        - PPI trial - treatment responsiveness to 8w trial of PPI (90% responsive within even a few days)
    - ****Objective diagnosis**** - indicated for 1) non-responsive to PPI trial, 2) presence of alarm features:
        - Upper endoscopy + biopsy - histological confirmation of reflux esophagitis, and complications (e.g. peptic strictures, Barette's esophagus)
        - HRM
        - 24h pH and combined impedence-pH recording - gold standard for diagnosis of GERD
    - ****Upper endoscopy:****
        - ****Indications:****
            - Unresponsive to PPI trials
            - Alarm features - 1) Weight loss, 2) Dysphagia/ Odynophagia, 3) Upper GI bleeding
        - ****Role:****
            - Diagnosis of erosive GERD (30% of all GERD) - LA classification of erosive GERD
            - Identification of etiology - hiatal hernia, EoE, achalasia etc.
            - Identification of complications - Barrett's esophagus, strictures, erosive GERd etc.

## Management of GERD

- ****Principles of management of uncomplicated GERD:****
    - Medical Mx - PPI trial
    - Surgical Tx - fundoplication
- ****Medical Tx of GERD:****
    - ****Initial regimen**** - <u>8w standard dose PPIs</u>
        - Duration - 8 weeks minimum
        - Efficacy - \> 90% can expect complete mucosal healing (also important in peptic strictures, significantly prolonging intervals between dilatation)
    - ****Step-down approach**** - dose reduction to lowest dose that renders patient symptom free (can result in cessation of Tx)
    - ****Refractory to initial course**** - dose increase of PPIs +/- addition of <u>H2 antagonists</u> and <u>formally workup patient</u>
- ****Surgical Tx of GERD**** - many operations, but all based on fundoplication:
    - Principles - 1) Create abdominal segment of esophagus, 2) crural repair, 3) wrap created w/ fundus around intra-abdominal esophagus (fundoplication)
    - Techniques:
        - Total fundoplication (Nissen) - passage of gastric fundus behind the esophagus and completely encircle the distal esophagus
            - Advantages and disadvantages - a/w more short-term dysphagia but more durable repair
            - Complications - 'gas bloat' syndrome, unable to belch and excessive flatus post-meals (require convertion to partial fundoplication)
        - Partial fundoplication (Posterior/ Toupet or Anterior/ Dor) - Partial wrap created by gastric fundus
            - Advantages and disadvantages - a/w less short term S/E but higher long-term failure rates
        - Hill technique
        - Belsey mark IV operation

- ****Barett's esophagus**** - a pre-malignant condition characterised by intestinal metaplastic change in esophageal mucosa into columnar-lined esophagus (CLO) in response to chronic gastric reflux

# Neoplasms of the esophagus

- ****Benign tumours**** (relatively rare) - papillomas, adenomas, gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GIST), lipoma, granular cell tumour

## CA esophagus

- ****Epidemiology:****
    - Incidence - not top 10 in incidence, but no. 10 in mortality (5-10% 5y survival)
    - Demographic:
        - Male predisposition - 2-3x
        - Increasing incidence w/ age
        - Extreme geographical variation - Most commonly see in cancer belt (SE Asia, South Africa) and in China (85% SCC), AD more common in the West than SCC
- ****Risk factors:****
    - SCC - geographic predisposition (endemic regions), tobacco, alcohol
    - AD - Obesity (?through links w/ GERD and Barrett's)
- ****Pathology:****
    - Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) - affects upper 2/3 of esophagus
    - Adenocarcinoma (AD) - affects lower 1/3 of esophagus
    - ****Mode of metastasis**** - direct invasion, lymphatics, haematogenous spread:
        - ****Direct spread**** - laterally (through layers of esophagus) or longitudinally (along mucosa), transperitonally if intra-abdominal CA
        - ****Lymphatics**** - via submucosal lymphatic channels (non-segmental contiguous spread, where length of esophagus \> epithelial area affected), usually caudally +/- LN involvement cranially (mediastinal) or caudally (coeliac axis)
        - ****Haematogenous spread**** - liver, lung, brain, bones
- ****Clinical presentation**** - typically presents until primary tumour becomes advanced:
    - ****early non-specific Sx**** - dyspepsia, abnormal swallowing, or identified on endoscopic surveillance of pts w/ Barrett's esophagus
    - ****Classical features of mechanical obstruction**** - dysphagia, regurgitation, odynophagia, vomiting weight loss (presence of these features suggests <u>advanced disease</u>):
    - ****Clinical features of advanced malignancy:****
        - Recurrent laryngeal N. palsy (hoarseness indicates incurable disease)
        - Horner's syndrome
        - Chronic spinal pain
        - Diaphragmatic paralysis
        - Palpable LN +/- cutaneous mets
- ****Initial evaluation:****
    - Endoscopic Bx and Cytology - diagnostic accuracy \> 95% by combining histology and cytology

### General assessment and staging

- ****Assessment of general health**** - assessment for fitness of potential curative therapies
- ****Staging Ix:****
    - ****Indications**** - reserved for patients suitable for potentially curative therapies, and not performed for medically unfit/ patients who do not contemplate curative procedures (divert considerations for paliative Tx)
    - ****General approach to staging Ix:****
        - Aims - to classify CA esophagus into early (T1/2, NO) and advanced (T3/4, N1)
        - Routine bloods - LFT (raise suspicion of hepatic metastasis, but not all elevated)
        - r/o haematogenous spread (PET-CT, CT or MRI)
        - Assessment of locoregional stage - EUS, laparoscopy, bronchoscopy
        - Determine Tx modality:
            - Early disease - endoscopic therapies, surgical approach
            - Locally advanced disease - multi-modal approach
            - Distant metastasis - palliative +/- chemoradiation
    - ****Cross sectional imaging**** (CT or MRI) - typically CT to identify haematogenous metastasis (94-100%)
    - ****Endoscopic ultrasound**** (EUS):
        - ****Role**** - assessment of 1) depth of tumour penetration (T stage), 2) regional LN soread (N stage)
        - ****Interpretation**** - breach of ultrasound interface (T1-4)
    - ****Laparoscopy:****
        - ****Indications**** - for intra-abdominal CA of oesophagus and cardia which have potential for intra-abdominal and hepatic metastasis
        - ****Procedures**** - technique to visualise intra-abdominal cavity for metastasis, and tissue/ cytology sampling for <u>peritoneal carcinomatosis</u>
    - ****Bronchoscopy:****
        - ****Indications**** - for CA of upper 2/3 esophagus, which is typically at time of diagnosis for local invasion into trachea and bronchi
        - ****Interpretation**** - impingement or invasion of main airways (30% of new upper 1/3 CA esophagus)

### Treatment of CA esophagus

- ****Principles of Mx:****
    - Patient selection - 2/3 patients w/ CA esophagus will have incurable disease at presentation
    - ****Principles of surgery:****
        - Radical esophagectomy - important aspect of treatment of CA esophagus either alone (early disease) or in multimodal approach (advanced disease)
        - Adequate lymphadenectomy - important in minimising risk of lower reccurence (as esophageal neoplasms that have access to submucosa can access lymphatic channels)
            - Extent of lymphadenectomy - 2-field lymphadenectomy generally for AD, and 3-field lymphadenectomy for SCC
    - Neoadjuvant Tx w/ surgery - evidence (meta-analysis) showing some role of neo-adjuvant chemotherapy in adenocarcinoma
- ****Two-phase esophagotomy**** (Lewis-Tanner):
    - ****Mobilisation of stomach**** - through abdominal incision
    - ****Reccession and mobilisation of esophagus**** - through right thoracotomy

# Motility disorders

- ****Esophageal motility disorders**** - readily understood as presence of dysphagia in the absence of mechanical causes (e.g. stricture), diagnosed by specific abnormality on esophageal manometry
- ****Clinical features of esophageal motility disorders:****
    - Dysphagia - logical clinical feature, but in reality might not be the predominant complaint
    - Pain - functional pain thought to arise from spasms arising from abnormal motor activity, perception (visceral hypersensitivity) or combination of both
        - Note: Often extensively worked up for other causes of chest pain before esophagus is considered
    - Associated GI motility problems
    - Associations with GERD
- ****Classification of esophageal motility disorders:****
    - Disorders of pharyngo-oesophageal junction - Neurological (stroke, PD, motor neuron disease, MS), myogenic (MG, muscular dystrophies), Zenker's diverticulum
    - Disorders of the esophageal body - Diffuse oesophageal spasm, CREST syndrome (SSc), nutcracker esophagus, esophagitis (reflux, allergic, eosinophilic), idiopathic
    - Disorders of the LES - achalasia, incompetent lower sphincter

## Achalasia

- ****Definition**** - uncommon esophageal motility disorder characterised pathophysiologically by non-relaxing LES and aperistalsis of the esophageal body
- ****Epidemiology:****
    - Incidence - rare (1.6/100,000/y)
    - Demographic - no sex predisposition (M=F), occurs at any age (typically 25-60y)
- ****Pathophysiology of achalasia:****
    - ****Loss of ganglion cells in the myenteric plexus**** - loss of distal inhibitory neurons results in loss of intrinsic inhibitory innervation of LES
    - ****Early-stage achalasia**** - vigorous ****spastic**** achalasia, where esophagus exhibits contractile but non-peristaltic activity a/w pain
    - ****Late-stage achalasia**** - ****aperistaltic**** esophagus, causing dilatation proximal to obstruction (empties by hydrostatic pressure of its contents but remains incomplete after equilibrating), can progress into <u>tortuous 'megaoesophagus'</u>
- ****Clinical features of achalasia:****
    - ****Pain**** - functional pain from esophageal spasms as early clinical features (often mistakened for reflux Sx)
    - ****Dysphagia**** - common mild and remain untreated for many years but eventually present, suggestive features include:
        - Type of bolus - liquid alone, or liquid + solid
        - Reflux Sx unresponsive to PPI trial
        - Suggestive endoscopic features
    - ****Regurgitation**** - frequent, causing aspiration into trachea, especially at night
- ****Natural Hx and Prognosis:****
    - Disease course - progressive dilatation of esophagus, with tortuous megaesophagus etc.
    - Malignant potential - achalasia is a risk factor of SCC of esophagus
- ****Ix of achalasia:****
    - ****Upper endoscopy**** (raise clinical suspicion and <u>exclusion of pseudoachalasia</u>) - 1) dilatation of esophagus, 2) tight cardia, 3) food residue in esophagus
    - ****Barium swallow/ Timed barium esophogram:****
        - Dilated oesophagus
        - Hold up of barium in esophagus (poor emptying into stomach)
        - 'Birds-beak appearance' - tapering stricture in distal esophagus
    - ****High resolution manometry**** (<u>diagnostic</u>) - Chicago Classification Version 4.0:
        1.  Elevated median Integrated relaxation pressure (IRP)
        2.  Absence of normal peristalsis (spastic contractions or esophageal aperistalsis)
    - ****Endoscopic functional lumen imaging probe**** (EndoFLIP) - support diagnosis if HRM equivocal:
        - Low distensibility index of LES - increased pressure resulting in inflation of FLIP balloon
        - Abnormal (spasms) or absent (aperistalsis) esophageal contractions - unable to stimulate secondary peristalsis
- ****Diagnosis**** - typical achalasia Sx, diagnostic HRM findings, and supportive ancillary Ix (e.g. barium swallow, upper endoscopy)
- ****DDx of achalasia:****
    - GERD - common regurgitation Sx as chief complaints, but response to PPI and distinguished by HRM
    - Pseudoachalasia (r/o on upper endoscopy and EUS) - achalasia-like disorder due to malignant invasion or paraneoplastic effect on the myenteric plexus (e.g. AD of cardia), presenting with similar clinical, radiological and manometric findings
    - Other esophageal motility disorders (distinguished by HRM findings) - distal esophageal esophagus, jackhammer esophagus

### Management of achalsia

- Responsiveness to Tx - one of the motility disorders responsive to palliative Tx
- ****Principles of Mx**** - reduce resting pressure of LES to enable passage of food:
    - ****Lifestyle modification**** - important but not sufficient
    - ****Pharmacological Tx**** - ineffective for long term use (reserved for failed botox injections and bridge to definitive Tx)
        - Nitrates - e.g. sublingual isosorbide dinitrate
        - CCBs - e.g. sublingual nifedipine
    - ****Surgical Tx:****
        - Pneumatic dilatation
        - Botulinin toxin injection
        - Hellen's myotomy
        - Peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM)
        - Esophagectomy
    - ****CA surveillance**** - controversial, risk increased but absolute risk remains low
- ****Pneumatic dilatation****
    - Procedure - forceful dilatation w/ pneumatic balloon dilation of LES to disrupt the muscles and render it less competent
        - Graded pneumatic dilatation - multiple endoscopies are frequently required
- ****Botulinin toxin injection****
    - Procedure - endoscopic injection of botox into LES to interfere w/ excitatory cholinergic activity
    - Efficacy - short-lasting effect (requires repeated injections)
    - Indications - short efficacy, reserved for elderly w/ commorbidities who cannot tolerate more invasive procedures
- ****Heller's myotomy****
    - Procedure - minimal access laproscopic cutting of muscle fibres of the LES and cardia +/- partial fundoplication (Heller-Dor's operation)
    - Efficacy - 90% effective for symptomatic relief (preserved for failed dilatation)
    - Complications:
        - GERD - prevented by concomittant anti-reflux surgery (9% vs 32%)
        - Perforation
        - Pneumothorax
        - Bleeding
        - Vagal injury
        - Infecions
    - ****Peroral endoscopic myotomy:****
        - Procedure - a form of normal oriface transluminal endoscopic surgery (NOTEs), with myotomy performed through endoscopic means through <u>endoscopic submucosal dissection</u>
        - Complications - severe GERD

# Diverticula

## Zenker's diverticulum (pharyngeal pouch)

- ****Definition**** - posterior outpouching of the mucosa and submucosa through the dehiscence of Killian (between oblique and transverse fibres of inferior pharyngeal constrictor)
- ****Pathophysiology of Zenker's diverticulum**** - mechanism of formation not exactly unknown:
    - Small diverticulum - typically impedes oropharyngeal phase of swallowing, resulting in <u>oropharyngeal dysphagia</u>
    - Large diverticulum - collection of food debris and fundus descends into mediastinum to compress the esophagus extraluminally, resulting in <u>hallitosis</u>, <u>repeated pneumonia</u> and <u>esophageal dysphagia</u>
