# Pathophysiology of cardiogenic pulmonary oedema

- **Definition** - common, and potentially fatal cause of acute respiratory failure due to rapid accumlation of fluid within lung interstitial and/or alveolar spaces secondary to increased <u>pulmonary capillary wedge pressure</u> (PCWP) or <u>LA filling pressures</u>
- **Etiology of cardiogenic pulmonary oedema:**
    - <u>Acute decompensated heart failure</u> - due to all heart diseases, principally MI, PE, Cardiac tamponade, hypertensive emergency, arrhythmias, acute valvular failure
    - <u>Volume overload status</u> - primary fluid overload (e.g. massive blood transfusion), IV fluid administration, severe renal failure
- **Pathophysiology** - symptoms arise from increased transudation of fluids into pulmonary interstitium and alveolar spaces:
    - **Increased LA filling pressure** - most often secondary to increased LV filling pressure (i.e. LVEDP), resulting in a rise in pulmonary capillary pressure
    - **Fluid transudation due to Starling's Forces** - increased capillary hydrostatic pressure results in net transudation into lung interstitium and alveolar spaces, with some evidence of **pulmonary capillary stress failure** resulting in failure of blood-gas barrier and <u>diffuse alveolar haemorrhage</u>
- **Predisposing conditions of cardiogenic pulmonary oedema** - pre-existing heart conditions that may develop into ADHF and cardiogenic pulmonary oedema:
    - <u>Systolic dysfunction</u> - e.g. CAD, HTN, valvular heart disease, cardiomyopathies, hypothyroidism
    - <u>Diastolic dysfunction</u> - e.g. hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, arrhythmias (e.g. AF)
    - <u>LVOT</u> - e.g. severe AS, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, severe HTN
    - <u>Mitral stenosis</u> - rapid rise in left atrial filling pressure
    - <u>Renovascular HTN</u> - precipitating factors and predisposing factors via HTN
