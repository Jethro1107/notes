# Key Management Concepts: WIfI, GLASS, TAP and PLAN

- **Nomenclature regarding chronic arterial insufficiency:**
    - <u>Intermittent claudication</u> - chronic atherosclerotic disease of the lower limb resulting in arterial claudication in the absence of rest pain
    - <u>Crital limb ischaemia</u> - out-dated terminology emcompassing chronic atherosclerotic disease of the limb with sufficient stenosis and haemodynamic consequence resulting in rest pain or tissue loss
    - <u>Chronic limb-threatening ischaemia</u> - much broader spectrum of patients with varying degree of ischaemia sufficient to contribute to foot and leg ulcers, delayed wound healing, and increased amputation risk
- **Evolving concepts around chronic limb-threatening ischaemia** (CLTI):
    - **Increasing prevalence of DFU** - due to neuropathy and PAD, 25% of patients will develop one diabetic food ulcers (DFU)
    - **Diabetic foot ulcers and PAD:**
        - <u>Clinical association of DFU and PAD</u> - significant people of DFU have PAD (49-66%), which is not routinely evaluated, such that opportunities in revascularisation is missed
        - <u>PAD a/w more complications</u> - delayed woud healing and higher risk of amputation
    - **Limitations of old classification systems** (e.g. Fontne and Rutherford):
        - <u>Exclusion of diabetics</u> - DFU were considered distinct entities from the out-dated concept of pure critical limb ischaemia, such that classification systems were not applied, and diabetic foot were only managed by wound care or amputation (w/o revascularisation)
        - <u>Rigid classification</u> - haemodynamic parameters were too rigid, w/o considering details of wound characteristics and severity of infection (both of which affect care of DFU)
- **Patient, limb, anatomy** (PLAN) - assessment of patient's estimated risk for intervention, and estimates of short- and long-term mortality:
    - **Patient factors:**
        - **Intermittent claudicants** - generally good prognosis:
            - <u>Survival</u> - 75-80% 5y survival
            - <u>Risk of amputation</u> - 5% 5y risk
            - <u>Implications on Mx</u> - managed by lifestyle modification and best medical Tx, with surgical Tx considered if refractory to BMT and favourable anatomy
        - **Chronic limb-threatening ischaemia** (CLTI) - poorer prognosis:
            - <u>Survival</u> - 50% 5y survival (further stratified into average and high-risk group based on comorbid status, with implications on Tx, N.B. BASIL trial)
            - <u>Risk of amputation</u> - much higher risk, dependent on WIfI limb stage
            - <u>Factors affecting prognosis</u> - advanced age (\> 80y), bedbound status, O2-dependent COPD, G5CKD
            - <u>Implications on Mx</u> (BASIL trial):
                - **High risk group** (\< 50% 2y survival) - EVT or bypass surgery have similar outcomes
                - **Low risk groups** (\> 50% 2y survival) - better outcomes w/ bypass surgery
    - **Limb factors** - based on WIfI classification
    - **Anatomy factors** - based on GLASS
- **Society for Vascular Surgery WIfI classification** - new classification system applicable to **chronic limb-threatening ischaemia** irrespective of DM status:
    - **Application of WIfI classification** - limb staging system applied at clinical presentation showing <u>considerable prognostic value in prediction of amputation risk at 1y</u>
    - **Staging in WIfI system** - applied at presentation:
        - **Wound** - assess presence and severity of ulcers and gangrene
        - **Ischaemia** - assess haemodynamic status by ABI, or by toe pressure or oximetry if ABI incompressible
        - **Foot infection** - based on presence, extent of infection and systemic inflammatory response (SIRS)

    
    ![](img/wifi_classification.png)
    - **Pronostic clinical stages by combination of of WIfI stages** (Delphi Consensus) - predicts <u>amputation risk at 1y</u>:
        - **Risk of amputation:**
            - Clinical Stage I - very low risk (0%)
            - Clinical Stage II - low risk (8%)
            - Clinical Stage III - moderate risk (11%)
            - Clinical Stage IV - high risk (38%)

    
    ![](img/wifi_classification_risk_of_amputation.png)
- **Glabal Limb Anaatomical Staging System** (GLASS) - characterisation of limb vascular anatomy in patients based on imaging for assessment of patency rates, revascularisation approachs and outcomes of therapy (not yet validated):
    - **Key assumptions and principles of GLASS:**
        - <u>Goal of therapy</u> - in-line flow to the ankle and foot is the primary goal of therapy (a target arterial path \[TAP\] from groin to foot is to be selected)
        - <u>Assessment of vascular anatomy is limb based, not lesion or segment based</u> - assessment of femoropopliteal and infrapopliteal segents by grading in severity on scale of 0-4, which is combined into an **overall arterial anatomical stage**, taking into account that <u>most CLTI are multilevel disease</u>
    - **Approach to anatomical assessment** - must include paedal vessels:
        - <u>Duplex USG</u> - convinent, non-invasive and no-contrast administration
        - <u>Contrast CTA or MRA</u> - esp. if inflow disease (aortoiliac disease) is suspected
        - <u>Digital subtraction angiography</u> - most accurate and best view of the foot

# Endovascular Versus Open Surgical Therapy

- **Surgical Mx of CLTI:**
    - **Evidence in comparing EVT and open bypass surgery** (lack of evidence) - only evidence provided from BASIL trial comparing angioplasty and bypass surgery:
        - <u>Short-term outcomes</u> - no significant differences between open bypass and balloon angioplasty
        - <u>Amputation-free survival</u> - trend towards improved amputation-free survival at 2y in open bypass surgery
    - **Choice of EVT and bypass surgery** - dependent on patient, limb and anatomy (PLAN): 
    ![](img/plan_framework_for_optimal_revascularisation_strategy_in_clti.png)
        - **Patient factors** - based on risk of patients (high vs standard risk based on PLAN)
        - **Limb and anatomical factors** - balance between anatomical complexity (GLASS) and limb status (WIfI stage): 
        ![](img/preferred_revascularisation_strategy_for_standard_risk_patient_where_both_strategies_optimal.png)
    - **Endovascular therapy** - now become thrst operation for intervention in many patients:
        - **Procedures** - balloon angioplasty +/- stenting
        - **Evolution of application** - initially limited use in focal short lesions in larger-diameter vessels, but now <u>can be applied to long segment lesions in tibial and paedal vessels</u>
        - **General considerations** - see textbook
    - **Open bypass surgery:**
        - **Nature of bypass:**
            - <u>Anatomical reconstruction</u> - aorto-bifemoral bypass (for aortoiliac disease), femoro-popliteal bypass
            - <u>Extra-anatomical reconstruction</u> - femoro-femoral bypass, axillo-bifemoral bypass, axillo-popliteal bypass
        - **Technical details** - success of surgery dependent on 1) inflow, 2) conduit and 3) outflow:
            - <u>Inflow assessment</u> - assessment of adequate inflow based on palpation of pulse, duplex USG waveforms, with endovascular or open surgical interventions to provide optimal inflow
            - <u>Outflow assessment</u> - identify of the most proximal disease free artery as outflow
            - <u>Conduits</u> - most critical factor underlying infrainguinal bypass:
                - **Autologous vein grafts** - great saphenous vein (GSV), short saphenous vein (SSV), femoral vein, basilic and cephalic vein
                - **Endarterectomised artery grafts** - superficial femoral artery, radial artery
                - **Prosthetic grafts** - Dacron graft, heparin-bonded Dacron, human umbilical vein, polytetrafluoroetylene (PTFE) grafts
