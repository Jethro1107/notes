# Clinical Assessment

- **Presenting problems of neck disorders** - commonly are pain and stiffness:
    - **Pain** - felt over neck, +/- referred to suprascapular areas, shoulder or upper arm (acute vs chronic)
    - **Stiffness** - intermittent or continuous (ROM)
    - **Deformity** - wry neck due to muscle spasm
    - **Sensori-motor deficits** - over UL or LL
    - Headaches - typically accompanied by other presenting problems in neck disorders
- **Clinical Examination:**
    - <u>Exposure</u> - upper trunk and entire upper limbs
    - <u>Position</u> - start examination in <u>standing</u> position to better visualise deformity and ROM, followed to <u>sitting</u> to examine viscera, and subsequently <u>supine</u> to assess spasm, tenderness and neurological functions
    - **Look:**
        - Scars, swelling, skin changes, deformities and asymetry
    - **Feel:**
        - Palpate over the bony prominence of the scapulo-humeral complex
        - Note for 1) tenderness, 2) spasms, 3) lump
    - **Move** - passive and active ROM:
        - Flexion and extension - by observing occipitomental line (imaginary line from occipitus to chin):
            - Flexion - ability to touch the chest (?30 degrees)
            - Extension - 45 degrees to 60 degrees
        - Lateral flexion - 45 degrees
        - Rotation - 80 degrees
        - Assessment on shoulder movement - read up
    - **Special tests:**
        - <u>Spuring's test</u>:
            - **Technique** - rotation and extension of neck towards ipsilateral sign
            - **+ve result** - worsening of ipsilateral upper limb pain and parasthesia
            - **Interpretation** - radiculopathy caused by disc prolapse
        - <u>Shoulder abduction test</u>:
            - **Technique** - Abduction of shoulder
            - **+ve result** - relief of pain and parasthesia over UL (<u>abduction relief sign</u>)
            - **Interpretation** - relief of pain points towards readiculopathy, worsening of pain points towards shoulder pathology
    - **Neurological Examination** - UL mandatory in all cases, +/- LL
- **Cervical X-rays:**
    - <u>Views</u>:
        - AP and lateral view (mandatory)
        - Oblique views - demonstration of neuroforamina narrowing in suspected radiculopathy
        - Dynamic views - flexion and extension view to assess instability, and C0/1/2 relationship
    - <u>Adequacy</u> - Occiput to C7/T1 junction (esp. common site for fracture-dislocation)
    - <u>Alignment</u> - assessment of malalignment caused by <u>olisthesis/ sublaxation</u> based on 4 anatomical lines in lateral view:
        - Anterior vertebral line - anterior aspect of vertebral body
        - Posterior vertebral line - posterior aspect of vertebral body
        - Spinolaminar line - posterior aspect of spinal canal (anterior to lamina)
        - Spinous process line - along tips of spinal process
    - <u>Bone evaluation</u> - assessment of height, shape (presence of marginal osteophytes) and radio-opacity
    - <u>Disc space evaluation</u> - loss of disc height are suggestive of chronic IV disc degeneration
    - <u>Posterior spinous spaces evaluation</u> - variable distance suggestive of cervical instability
    - <u>Soft tissue evaluation</u> - swelling of paravertebral soft tissue as a result of soft tissue injury:
        - C2 - 6mm or \< 1/2 vertebral depth
        - C6 - 22mm or \< 1 vertebral dept
    - Ensure:
        - Measurement of cervical allignment - normal lordosis of 15-20 degrees:
            - Requires weight-bearing films - e.g. sitting or standing films
            - Inferior end plate of C2 and Inferior endplate of C7
        - Measurement of cervical stenosis and instability:
            - Assumption - normal cord is 10 mm
            - Mid-saggital diameter - relative stenosis if \< 12mm, absolute stenosis if \< 10mm
            - Dynamic stenosis (taken on extension) - distance from from posteroinferior corner of cranial vertebra to anterior superior edge of lamella (dynamic stenosis if \< 12 mm)
            - Olisthesis - slip
        - Measurement of dynamic instability - \> 3.5mm/ 11 degree change between adjacent vertebrae
    - Dynamic views
    - Assess relationship between C0/C1/C2:
        - C1/C2 relationship
            - Antlanto-dental interval - posterior to C1 arch to anterior C2 dens (increase of \< 4mm upon flexion otherwise dynamic instability)
            - Space available for the cord - C2 dens to VB of C1 (should be \>14mm)
            - Causes of C1 and C2 instability - RA, Down's
        - C0/C1 relationship - Basilar invagination (dens moves up and impinges on brainstem and spinal cord)
            - Chamberlains line - from hard palate to opisthion, i.e. posterior extent of foramen magnum (dens should not pass)
            - McGregor line - posterior part of hard palate to caudal point of occipital curve (dens should be \> 4.5mm)
- Advanced imaging techniques - CT and MRI

# Deformities of the neck

## Torticollis (Wry neck)

- **Definition** - deformity causing chin twisting upwards and rotated (primary or seconadary)
- **Congenital torticollis** - fibrous and contracted SCM on one side causing twisting to <u>contralateral side</u>
- **Secondary torticollis** - due to muscle spasm over SCM (typically caused by acute disc prolapse)

# Vertebral anomalies

# Acute intervertebral disc prolapse

- **Pathophysiology** - typically immediately above or below C6:
    - On background of predisposing abnormality - increased tension of the nucleus propulsus in most cases
    - Precipitated by local strain and injury - especially during unguarded <u>flexion and rotation</u>
- **Clinical features:**
    - <u>Clinical course</u> - original attack presents acutely precipitated by severe strain, while subsequent atacks may be sudden or gradual and precipitated by more trivial cause (well between attacks)
    - <u>Neck pain and stiffness</u> - caused by disc protrusion compressing on posterior longitudinal ligament
    - <u>Radiating pain and parasthesia</u> - towards subscupular region (referred), and one of the UL (rarely both), where parasthesia is extremely suggestive of disc prolapse (radiculopathy features)
    - Weakness - extremely rare
- **Evaluation and Dx:**
    - Cervical X-Ray - lateral view reveals narrowing disc spaces
    - Cervical MRI (diagnositic) - disc protrusion pressing on adjacent nerve roots
- **DDx of acute intervertebral disc prolapse:**
    - <u>Acute soft tissue strain</u> - only presents with neck pain and stiffness (last weeks to months), with **absence of neurological Sx**
    - <u>Neuralgic amyotrophy</u> (acute brachial neuritis) - severe and sudden pain w/ prominant motor features (e.g. winging of scapulae suggestive of serratus anterior weakness)
    - <u>C-Spine infections</u> - characteristic unrelenting rest pain, w/ severe local spasms, where X Ray shows erosion of vertebral end plates
    - <u>Cervical tumours</u> - progressive neurological signs
- **Mx:**
    - General measures - heat and analgesia
    - Treatment of prolapse:
        - Neck collar - rest and prevents unguarded movements
        - Traction by head harness (up to 8kg) - apply traction (\< 30 min at a time) to enlarge disc space and permitting prolapse to subside
        - Disc removal - anterior surgery w/ fusion by bone grafts and restoration of normal IV height

# Chronic disc degeneration (cervical spondylosis)

- **Pathophysiology of chronic disc degeneration:**
    - **Age-related degeneration of IV disc** - common from middle age onwards, even in people w/o acute episodes
    - **Disc collapse and changes to VB** - reduced disc height puts excess stress on vertebral bodies, resulting in formation of osteophytic bone spurs along anterior and posterior margins of vertebral bodies (can cause radiculopathy by encrouching on neuroforamina)
- **Clinical features** - many are asymptomatic, and can experience as little Sx as neck stiffness:
    - <u>Clinical course</u> - gradual and progressive, with acute exacerbations and long periods of quiescence
    - <u>Neck pain and stiffness</u> - radiates to scapulae and down one or both arms
    - +/- <u>Neurological Sx</u> (Parasthesia and weakness) - typically radicular pattern manifesting as ocassional Sx during exacerbations
- **Ix:**
    - Cervical X Ray - reduced disc height over several disc spaces, osteophyte formation over VB, osteoarthritic change in uncovertebral joints (AP view), +/- narrowing of neuroforamina in oblique view
    - MRI - assessment of nerve root compression
- **DDx:**
    - <u>Rotator cuff lesions</u> - similar to referred pain from cervical spondylosis
    - <u>Nerve entrapment syndromes</u> - e.g. median or ulnar nerve entrapment
    - <u>Cervical tumours</u> - typically progressive, unremitting Sx
- **Mx:**
    - General measures - heat and massage during painful episodes
    - Physiotherapy - mainstay of conservative Mx
    - Surgical Tx - indicated if severe Sx and presence of neurological Sx:
        - Foraminotomy (posterior approach) - best for relieving radiculopathic features (referred pain and neurological Sx), with demonstrated nerve root compression on MRI
        - Anterior discetomy and fusion - best for unrelievable neck pain and stiffness
        - IV disc replacement

# Pyogenic infection

- **Definition** - Bacterial infection of the cervical spine
- **Epidemiology** - uncommon (often misdiagnosed), can occur at <u>any age</u>
- **Microbiology:**
    - Staphylococcus - access via haematogenous spread
- **Clinical features:**
    - <u>Neck pain and stiffness</u> - typically w/ severe muscle spasm and hence severely restricted movements
    - <u>Systemic Sx</u> - often mild (hence easily missed)
    - ?absence of precipitating injury
- **Ix:**
    - CBC - mild leukocytosis
    - ESR - non-specifically elevated
    - Cervical X Ray:
        - Initial change - non-specific narrowing of disc spaces
        - Late changes - bone destruction of adjacent VB endplates and complete collapse
- Mx:
    - Immobilisation - initially by traction, then subsequently by collar after subsiding
    - ABx - typically required
    - Surgical Tx - typically for drainage of abscess formation

# Tuberculosis

- **Definition** - rare but possible infection of the IV disc and adjacent vertebral bodies by M. tuberculosis
- **Pathophysiology:**
    - **Cold infection of IV disc and anterior parts of adjacent vertebral bodies** - C-spine collapse into <u>kyphosis</u>
    - **Retropharyngeal abscess formation** - swelling on posterior triangle w/ dysphagia
    - **Direct cord damage** - causes permanent neurological deficits
- **Ix** - similar approach as pyogenic Ix of cervical spine
- **Mx:**
    - Anti-TB drugs
    - Immobilisation - w/ brace of plaster cast
    - Surgical Tx:
        - Operative debridment and anterior fusion
        - Emergency surgery - drain retropharyngeal abscess, cord decompression, cervical instability

# Rheumatoid arthritis

- **Cervical involvement in RA** - severely affected in 30% of patients with rheumatoid arthritis
- **Pathoanatomy in RA** - 3 types of lesions:
    - Erosion of atlantoaxial joint (C1/2) and transverse ligament - major cause of cervical instability
    - Erosion of atlanto-ocipital articulation - causing basilar invagination
    - Erosion of facet joints - causing olisthesis
- **Clinical features of cervical RA:**
    - <u>Neck pain and stiffness</u> - ROM severely restricted
    - Neurological complications - uncommon despite startling X-ray appearance:
        - <u>Radiculopathy</u> - pain and parasthesia in the UL
        - <u>Myelopathy</u> - caused by ?olisthesis resulting in cord compression (UMN and LMN signs on UL and UMN LL weakness)
    - <u>Vertebrobasillar insufficency</u> - due to basilar invagination, manifesting as vertigo, tinnitus and visual disturbance
- **Ix:**
    - Cervical X Ray:
        - Erosive arthritis over several layers
        - Features of atlanto-axial instability - increased atlanto-dental interval
        - Features of atlanto-occipital erosion - Chamberlains line, McGregor line:
            - Chamberlains line - from hard palate to opisthion, i.e. posterior extent of foramen magnum (dens should not pass)
            - McGregor line - posterior part of hard palate to caudal point of occipital curve (dens should be \> 4.5mm)
    - CT/ MRI - viewing soft tissue involvement
- **Mx:**
    - Conservative Mx if absent neurological features - pain relieved by <u>collar</u>
    - Operative stabilisation:
        - Indications - 1) severe and unremitting pain, 2) +ve neurological spins
        - Procedure - Arthrodesis (posterior approach) by halo body cast, internal fixation and bone grafting

# Ankylosing spondylitis

- **Clinical features:**
    - <u>Neck pain and stiffness</u> - typically years after onset of backache
    - Kyphotic and rigid neck, with preservation of C0-C1-C2 joints - cause chin on chest deformity, and affects seeing paces ahead
- **Mx:**
    - Cervical spine osteotomy - indicated for chin-on-chest deformity, and inability to lift head high enough to see more than 10 paces ahead
