# Esophageal Cancer

## Epidemiology and Clinical Presentation of Esophageal Cancer

- **Epdemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - 18,000/y in US, 572,000/y worldwide
    - <u>Demographic</u> - male preponderance (M:F = 3-4:1)
    - <u>Mortality</u> - late presentation and highly fatal (5y survival ~19%)
- **Histopathological classification of CA esophagus** - different staging groupings under 8th edition TNM staging system:
    - <u>Squamous cell carcinoma</u> (SCC) - theoretically can affect the entire esophagus but tends to affect the proximal and middle esophagus
    - <u>Adenocarcinoma</u> (AD) - arises from the distal esophagus from pre-malignant intestinal metaplasia (Barrett's esophagus)
- **Pathological classification of CA esophagus by anatomical site:**
    - **Cervical tumours** - arises from cervical esophagus from the hypopharynx and extends to thoracic inlet at level of sternal notch (15-20cm from incisors on endoscopy)
    - **Upper cervical tumours** - arises from thoracic inlet to the azygous veins (20-25cm from incisors)
    - **Mid-thoracic tumours** - arises from azygous veins to the inferior pulmonary veins (25-30cm from incisors)
    - **Lower tumours** - arises distal to inferior pulmonary veins to GEJ (\> 30cm from incisors)
    - **GEJ tumours** - GEJ AD are classified by Sjewert classification and subsequently staged and treated as CA esophagus or CA stomach
- **Risk factors of esophageal cancer:**
    - **Risk factors of SCC** - similar proposed pathogenesis as SCC elsewhere:
        - **Demographic** - Male sex, certain race (East and Central Asia, China), genetic predisposition
        - **Smoking and alcohol** - Strong risk factors and have <u>synergistic effects</u>
        - **HPV** - only responsible for small subset of SCC (cf other H&N cancers), and clinical implications unclear
        - **Other intrinsic esophageal diseases** - e.g. caustic ingestion, achalasia, Plummer-Vinson syndrome
        - **Other medical conditions** - tylosis, Fanconi anaemia
    - **Risk factors of AD** - obesity, frequency and duration of GERD Sx, Barrett esophagus
- **Clinical presentation of CA esophagus** - most present at locally-advanced stages:
    - **Asymptomatic** (rare) - detection of early-stage tumour due to endoscopic surveillance for Barrett's esophagus or performed for other reasons (e.g. pan-endoscopy)
    - **Features of local tumour:**
        - <u>Dysphagia</u> - gradual onset of progressive mechanical dysaphagia (often reflects T3 disease) that is initially worse on solid food, but do not typically present as initially compensated by self-adapting behaviours
        - <u>Weight loss</u> - invariable weight loss due to reduced food intake and appetite as a result of dysphagia, but also as a result of the catabolic effects
        - <u>Upper GI Bleeding</u> (coffee-ground material, melaena, Sx of anaemia) - chronic occult bleeding by tumour often present and explains fatigue and other Sx of anaemia, whereas acute UGIB (haematemesis) reflects a more severe complication (e.g. aorto-esophageal fistula)
        - <u>Prior GERD Sx</u> (e.g. Heartburn, Regurgitation, Epigastric pain) - Hx of frequent, and prolonged GERD Sx is suggestive of potentially adenocarcinoma
    - **Symptoms of local tumour invasion:**
        - <u>Hoarseness</u> - laryngeal nerve involvement results in hoarseness of voice
        - <u>Recurrent cough and pneumonia</u> - often in late stage of disease caused by tracheoesophageal fistula

## Diagnosis and Staging of Esophageal Cancer

- **Ix** - workup for dysphagia or UGIB:
    - **Barium swallow** (Timed Barium Esophagram) - superceded by endoscopy:
        - <u>Findings</u> - typically an asymetric bulge w/ an infiltrative appearrance, classically 'apple-core filling defect' seen if the tumour results in symmetrical circumferential narrowing
    - **Upper endoscopy:**
        - <u>Role of endoscopy</u> - diagnostic:
            - 1\) Visualise and Bx of tumour
            - 2\) Detection of synchronous lesions (e.g. Barrett's Esophagus)
            - 3\) Assess the location and degree of obstruction
            - 4\) endoscopic therapy for small tumours
        - <u>Endoscopic findings of tumour</u> - variable features (all suspicious lesions warrant Bx):
            - Friable, ulcerating mass
            - Small ulcerations
            - Raised nodule
        - <u>Assessment of location</u> - based on **relative distance from incisior and OGJ**
- **Dx of CA esophagus** - histological Dx after Bx
- **Staging system of CA esophagus** - 8th edition AJCC TNM staging:
    - **Clinical vs pathological vs post-neoadjuvant staging:**
        - <u>Clinical staging</u> - clinical staging based on clinical, endoscopic, and histological features while overall clinical stage grouping is dependent on TNM designation only
        - <u>Pathological staging</u> - pathological staging based on complete examination of the resected specimen during esophagectomy, where pathological stage grouping is dependent on TNM designation, histological grade, and tumour location (only applicable to SCC)
        - <u>Post-neoadjuvant staging</u> - ypTNM staging is applied to operable patient undergoing induction neoadjuvant therapy prior to ressection

- **Staging Ix** - assessment of distant metastasis first, prior to assessment of loco-regional stage:
    - **Contrast CT T+A+P** - initial survey of distant metastasis
    - **PET-CT** - single tracer PET-CT identifies occult distant metastasis and potentially suspicious LN (guiding EUS)
        - <u>Rationale for PET-CT before EUS</u>:
            - 1\. demonstrattion of distant metastasis eliminates the need for EUS
            - 2\. demonstration of suspicious LN enables EUS-guided FNAC
    - **Endoscopic ultrasound** - assessment of loco-regional staging (T and N status):
        - **Role:**
            - <u>Assessment of T status</u> - assessment of depth of invasion, but note that EUS is less Sn for superficial (T1a-T2) lesions, where staging is more accurate by endoscopic mucosal resection
            - <u>Assessment of N status</u> - assessment of no. of involved LN, Sn increased by application of EUS-guided FNAC
        - **Limitations:**
            - 1\. Limited Sn for superficial tumours (T1a-T2) - better assessed by EMR
            - 2\. Obstructing lesions preclued EUS assessment - requires weighing risk of dilatation (perforation) and additional staging information, keeping in mind that most obstructing lesions are locally-advanced and require multi-modal therapy
    - **Bronchoscopy** - assessment of tracheal invasion for proximal and middle third CA esophagus
    - **Laparotomy** - assessment ot peritoneal seeding for intra-abdominal CA esophagus

## Management

- **Principles of Mx:**
    - <u>Superficial (T1a) tumours</u> - can be treated by endoscopic mucosal resection w/o LN Mx as risk of LN metastasis is extremely low and hence additional invasive staging studies are not pursued
    - <u>Locally advanced tumours</u> (T3, or T2 with nodal involvement) - requires trimodal therapy
    - <u>Unresectable or metastatic Stage IV disease</u> (T4a with bulky LN involvement, T4b, M1) - systemic chemotherapy and palliative therapy

### High-Grade Dysplasia and Superficial Cnacer

- **Terminology:**
    - <u>High-grade dysplasia</u> - prominent cytological and architectural derrangements in a subset of patient's w/ Barrett's esophagus and associated w/ higher relative risk of malignant transformation (although absolute risk remains low and there is a concern for over-treatment)
    - <u>Superficial cancer</u> - by definition carcinoma-in-situ (Tis) and T1a disease (invasion into submucosa) ameable to endoscopic Tx
- **Radiofrequency ablation:**
    - <u>Indications</u> - for ablation of entire segment of high-grade dysplasia of Barrett esophagus
    - <u>Principles</u> - circumferential balloon or electrical plate using bipolar electrodes that generate RF energy that generates heat and destroys superfical mucosa
- **Cryotherapy:**
    - <u>Indications</u> - alternative for ablation of entire segment of high-grade dysplasia of Barrett esophagus
    - <u>Principles</u> - alternative use of extreme cold (liquid nitrogen) to destroy superficial mucosa, with added advantage of reduced pain and stricture rate compared to RFA
- **Endoscopic mucosal resection:**
    - <u>Advantages of EMR over ablation</u>:
        - Increased depth of penetration
        - Acquired specimen for assessment of T-staging
    - <u>Role of EMR</u> - staging and potentially curative:
        - 1\. **Definitive Tx** - for superficial cancer w/ low risk of LN metastasis
        - 2\. **Locoregional staging** - EMR as a staging technique for suspicious superficial cancer as Sn of EUS for T1a-T2 disease is low
    - <u>Indications</u> - for suspected superficial CA esophagus (Tis-T1) as **staging Ix** and potential cure
    - <u>Lesions where EMR is considered adequate</u> (NCCN guidelines):
        - Carcinoma in situ (Tis)
        - T1a diseases - although variable risk of LN metastasis (2-15%) depending on size, degree of differentiation, and presence of lymphovascular invasion (see nomogram) 
        ![](img/nomogram_for_prediction_of_LN_metastasis_in_early_ca_esophagus.png)
        - Selected T1b disease - SM1 (superficial third of submucosa) disease a/w relatively low nodal metastasis but up to 40% of SM2/SM3 disease have LN metastasis
    - <u>Complications</u>:
        - General surgical risk - Bleeding, pain
        - Procedure-specific risk - strictures (esp. circumferential resection), perforation

### Locally Advanced Eophageal Cancer

- **Most patient present w/ locally-advanced or metastatic disease** - due to ambiguous, non-specific nature of S/S in early-stage CA esophagus
- **Rigorous definition of locally-advanced disease and metastatic disease** - clinical N status continues to influence Mx:
    - **Locally advanced disease** - theoretically treated by trimodal therapy, but N3 disease irrespective to T status (Stage IV disease) may prompt initiation of systemic therapy
    - **Distant disease** - theoretically, distant LN metastasis should be considered metastatic disease and preclude locoregional therapy, but relative position of regional nodal disease to primary tumour is not currently considered in staging:
        - <u>AD in distal esophagus or OGJ</u> - node involvement outside celiac axis up to paratracheal region considered M1 disease
        - <u>SCC in proximal and middle third of esophagus</u> - node involvement outside periesophageal cervical LN is considered M1 disease
- **Principles of multimodal therapy for locally-advanced disease:**
    - **Historical perspective and evidence** - conclusion is that advancements in the extent of surgery has limited effects on oncological outcomes:
        - <u>Surgery alone +/- radiation</u> (Tx of locoregional disease) - even the most radical extended esophagectomy and LN dissection are inadequate to cure for patients, as most patients often have distant recurrence and metastasis, accounting for mortality (i.e. systemic therapy required to kill off distant metastasis)
        - <u>Trimodal therapy</u> - integration of systemic chemotherapy for targetting systemic micrometastasis demonstrates efficacy over

    
    ![](img/evidence_for_trimodal_therapy_for_ca_esophagus.png)
- **Radiation thrapy:**
    - <u>Neoadjuvant radiation</u> (low-dose 20-40Gy) - no significant benefits of improving R0 ressection rates and improve oncological outcomes, with fear that high-dose radiation would affect peri-operative complications
    - <u>Adjuvant radiation</u> (higher-dose 40-60Gy) - conflicting evidence for avoiding local recurrence and improving survival (trials subjected to selection bias)
- **Chemotherapy:**
    - **Rationale** - cause of death of most locally-advanced CA is distant recurrence:
        - 1\. **Neoadjuvant chemotherapy** demonstrated to improve R0 ressection rates (N.B. MAGIC trial)
        - 2\. **Chemotherapy has synergistic effects w/ radiation for loco-regional control**
        - 3\. Biological response to **neoadjuvant chemotherapy** has been established as an indicator of outcome
    - **Regimens** - Platinum compounds (e.g. cisplatin) in combination with 5-fluorouracil
    - **Neoadjuvant vs adjuvant chemotherapy** - JCOG 9907 trial demonstrates that chemotherapy is usually applied in the neoadjvant setting (statistically significant 5y survival and PFS)
- **Bimodal therapy** (Chemoradiation alone):
- **Trimodal therapy** - combines benefits of each Tx modality as demonstrated in CROSS trial:
    - **Context of evidence** (CROSS trial) - 366 patients in analysis (75% AD, 22% SCC)
    - **Evidence:**
        - <u>Higher R0 ressection rate for trimodal therapy</u> - 92 vs 69% (P \< 0.001)
        - <u>Less node-positive disease in pathological staging after trimodal therapy</u> - 31% vs 75% (P \< 0.001)
        - <u>Longer overall survival for trimodal therapy</u> - 49.4 mo vs 24 mo (P = 0.003)
        - <u>Greater 5y survival for trimodal therapy</u> - 47% vs 34% (P = 0.003)
        - <u>SCC a/w better post-neoadjuvant complete pathological response than AD</u> - 49% vs 29% (P \< 0.001)
