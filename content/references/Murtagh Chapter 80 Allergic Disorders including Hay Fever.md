# Ch80 Allergic Disorders including Hay Fever

- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - allergic disorders affect ~ 20% of population
	- _Common allergic disorders encountered_:
		- Atopy -  allergic rhinoconjunctivitis (hay fever), atopic dermatitis, allergic asthma
		- IgE-mediated food allergies - 7 groups of food allergies resulting in urticaria, angioedema, anaphylaxis
		- Latex allergy - increasing important IgE-mediated hypersensitivity due to universal precautions to decrease transmissible disease
## Atopy

- **Definition** - an inherited tendancy for an exagerated IgE antibody response to common environmental antigens
- **Epidemiology** - dependent on definition (up to 40% of population)
- **Clinical manifestations** - does not always result in clinically significant allergic disorders:
	- By definition a positive response to >= 1 allergen skin-prick test or +ve for specific IgEs
	- Usually +ve FHx of allergic disorders
	- Only 1/3 manifest as allergic disorders, most commonly 1) _allergic rhinitis_, 2) _asthma_, 3) _atopic dermatitis_, or 4) _allergic gastroenteropathy_

## Common Allergens causing Immediate Hypersensitivity

- **Common environmental allergens** - exposure via 1) inhalants, 2) foods, or 3) other routes:
	- _Inhalants_ - house dust mites, pollens, animal dander, cockroaches, mould spores
	- _Foods_ - peanuts, tree nuts, fish, shellfish, milk, eggs, wheat, soy, sesame
	- _Others_ - latex, drugs, occupational

### Inhalant Allergy

- **Clinical manifestations of inhalant allergies:**
	- Allergic rhinoconjunctivitis (hay fever)
	- Asthma
- **Salient points of Hx** - _temporal pattern of symptoms_ and _provacative factors_ help delineate the likely causative allergen:
	- _Seasonal symptoms_ - pollen allergy most likely (esp precipitated by mowing lawn)
	- _perenial symptoms_ - dust mites, household pets, or moulds (esp precipitated by dusting and vacuuming)
### Food Allergy and Intolerance

- **Definition and terminology:**
	- _Food allergy_ - adverse reaction to food products w/ an immunological basis (typically IgE-mediated)
	- _Food tolerance_ - adverse reaction to a specific food ingredient which has a non-immunological basis (e.g. lactose intolerance)
- **Immediate vs non-immediate food allergies:**
	- _Immediate reactions_ - likely IgE-mediated, occurring < 2h
	- _Delayed reactions_ - non-IgE-mediated, occurring > 24h after ingestion
- **Clinical manifestations of food allergy:**
	- Urticarial-like reactions (fleeting, pruritic wheals that resolve rapidly)
	- Gastrointestinal symptoms (anorexia, N/V, diarrhoea, failure to thrive in children)
- **IgE-mediated food allergies:**
	- **Definition** - IgE-mediated food allergies that occur immediately after ingestion
	- **Age of onset** - typically in infants and toddlers (usually resolves by 3-5y)
	- **Pathophysiology** - type-1 hypersensitivity usually occurring _within 30-60 min of ingestion_:
		- _Systemic activation of mast-cell primed w/ specific IgE_ - results in mast cell degranulation occurring in the GI tract, skin, other mucosal surfaces (e.g. airway) etc.
		- _Release of mast cell mediators_ - attributes to vasodilation and exudation, smooth muscle contraction etc.
	- **Common food groups a/w IgE-mediated food allergies** - Big three foods:
		- Cow's milk (able to cross-react w/ soy beans or goat's milk)
		- Eggs
		- Peanuts
		- Others are rare but possible (esp. shellfish, wheat, tree nuts)
	- **Clinical features of IgE-mediated food allergies** - defined by demographic, temporal association w/ suspected food culprit:
		- _Gastrointestinal manifestations_ - occuring **immediately after ingestion** w/ abdominal colic, N/V/D
		- _Cutaneous manifestations_ - flushing or blotchiness/ pallor ; pruritic urticaria (**wheals**) which may be facial or generalised
		- _Manifestations of the upper and lower airway_ - angioedema of the respiratory tract and bronchospasm:
			- Itchy oropharynx (+/- swelliing)
			- Allergic rhinoconjunctivitis (runny nose, itchy eyes and nose)
			- Wheezing
		- _Systemic manifestations_ (anaphylaxis) - higher likelihood if underlying Hx of asthma:
			- Angor animi
			- Dizziness and confusion
			- Cardiovascular collapse
			- Death
	- **Mx:**
		- **Principles of Mx** - RAPRIOP:
			- _Reassurance and education_ - patient education sheet on the nature of disorder
			- _Advice_ - avoidance of suspected food
			- _Prescription_ - non-sedating antihistamine +/- oral glucocorticoids; consider Epi-Pen
			- _Referral_ - referral for specialist allergy advice
			- _Investigations_ - consider skin-prick test
			- _Observation and follow-up_ - may require FU
			- _Prevention_ - Epi-Pen

- **Clinical features of FPIES** - delayed onset of Sx:
	- Projected vomiting
	- Protracted diarrhoea w/ blood in stool and eosinophils

#### Peanut Allergy

- **Overview:**
	- Most common cause of food-induced anaphylaxis in adults
	- Diagnosis confirmed by either skin-prick test w/ peanut extract or RAST testing
- **Clinical features of peanut allergy** - Sx usually begins within minutes of ingestion and follows a predictable progression to anaphylaxis:
	- Itching, burning and swelling of the oropharyngeal space
	- Flushing and urticaria
	- Wheeze and stridor follows
	- Generalised angioedema and collapse may follow
- **Comorbid asthma as a poor prognostic factor:**
	- Higher risk of fatal and near-fatal reactions in young children
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _General advice_ - avoidance of peanut-containing foods
		- _Prescription and prevention_ - carrying an anaphylaxis kit at all times
		- _Desenitisation_ - currently not recommended

## Tests for Specific IgE

- **Skin-prick tests:**
	- _Principles_ - inoculation of suspected allergen into the dermis
	- _Advantages_ - preferred method for most IgE-mediated allergies as results are read at the first consultation
	- _Interpretation_:
		- +ve test - correlate clinically; may be of no diagnostic importance if patient remains asymptomatic
		- -ve test - high NPV, useful for excluding IgE-mediated allergy
- **Serum-specific IgE:**
	- _Assays used_:
		- Radioallergosorbent (RAST) testing
		- ELISA testing
	- _Indications_:
		- Unable to correlate clinical manifestations and skin prick tests
		- Comorbid skin conditions confounding test results (e.g. extensive eczema, dermatographism)
		- Immunotherapy work-up
		- Recent anti-histamine use (< 48h)
## Management Principles

- **Allergen avoidance** - correlating to Hx and skin-prick test results:
	- Aimed at reducing exposure dependent on allergen
	- Inhalants may require special attention to reducing house-dust mites, moulds, and pet selection
	- Food alelrgies may require specific food avoidance
	- Some require a change in environment or occupation if feasible
- **Pharmacotherapy** - prn where avoidance method have failed or are impractical:
	- Antihistamines
	- Adrenaline
	- Sodium cromoglycate
	- Corticosteroids
	- Anticholinergics and sympathomimetics
- **Desensitisation** - repeated administration of small, increasing dose of allergen by subcutaneous injection:
	- Treatment of choice for selected allergies e.g. 1) _wasp allergy, 2) _bee venom allergy_, 3) _egg allergy_, 4) _resistant allergic rhinoconjunctivitis_
	- Not recommended for allergies w/ severe consequences, e.g. _peanut allergy_
	- Close observation for at least > 45 min and performed in adequate resuscitation facilities
## Rhinitis

- **Classification of rhinitis:**
	- **Allergic rhinitis:**
		- Seasonal allergic rhinoconjunctivitis (hay fever)
		- Perenial allergic rhinoconjunctivitis
	- **Non-allergic rhinitis:**
		- Vasomotor rhinitis (eosinophilic vs non-eosinophilic)
#### Allergic Rhinitis

- **Definition** - rhinosinusitis that is is caused by a type 1 hypersensitivity reaction towards an allergen
- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - 20% of adult population and up to 40% oin children
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age - usually begins in childhood w/ 50% having the problem at 15y, and 90% have the problem by 30y
		- Known FHx - occurs in 60% of those w/ +ve FHx of atopy or allergic rhinitis
		- Comorbid atopy - 20% suffer from asthma
- **Comorbid conditions of allergic rhinosinusitis:**
	- Atopic conditions (asthma, eczema)
	- Nasal polyps
- **Classification of allergic rhinitis:**
	- Seasonal vs perennial
	- Intermittent (< 4d of the week or < 4 weeks) vs persistent (> 4d of the week or > 4 weeks)
- **Severity of symptoms:**
	- Mild - normal functioning including sleep and only slightly troublesome symptoms
	- Moderate/ severe - troublesome symptoms with impairment of activities
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Reassurance and education_ - appropriate explanation of causes and reassurance
		- _Advice_ - allergen avoidance along w/ other Sx-based advices
		- _Pharmacotherapy_ - consider antihistamines, mast cell stabilisers, decongestants, and corticosteroids; hyposensitisation considered if refractory
		- _Referral_ - when surgical intervention is required, or for immunotherapy

	- **General advices for patients w/ allergic rhinosinusitis:**
		- _General measures_ - live sensibly w/ balanced exercise, rest, recreation, and maintain a well-balanced diet
		- _Allergen avoidance_ - dependent on the likely allergen (e.g. animal dander, house-dust mites in beddings):
			- Animal dander - keep pets outside and do not allow pets on then bed
			- House dust mites - weekly washing of beddings with warm water
		- _Bothersome eye symptoms_ - avoid rubbing eyes, contact lenses, and wear sunglasses to avoid contact w/ allergens
		- _Bothersome nasal symptoms_ - avoid using decongestants due to risk of rebound rhinosinusitis medicantos
	- **Approach to pharmacological therapy** - selected based on patient's preference and S/E profiles:
		- _Antihistamines_ - oral, intranasal spray, ophthalmic drops
		- _Decongestants_ - oral or topical
		- _Sodium cromoglycate_ - intranasal powder insufflation/ spray, ophthalmic drops
		- _Corticosteroids_ - intranasal (not effective for non-eosinophilic vasomotor rhinitis), oral, ophthalmic drops
	- **Antihistamines:**
		- _Indications_ - first line of treatment for seasonal allergic rhinosinusitis:
			- Used prn when symptoms are intermittent
			- Prophylactic use before periods of high pollen exposure
		- _Selection_ - generally prefer second-generation 'non-sedating' antihistamine that do not cross the blood brain barrier:
			- First-generation antihistamines (if overnight sedation is preferred) - chlorphenamine, diphenhydramine (Benadryl)
			- Second-generation antihistamines - loratadine, cetirizine, fexofenadine, levocetirizine

	- **Oral decongestants:**
		- _Selection_ - oral sympathomimetics:
			- Pseudoephedrine HCl 60 mg po tds (max 240 mg/d) or 120 mg controlled release po bd
		- _S/E_:
			- Palpitations
			- Anxiety/ nervousness
			- Insomnia
		- _Caution_ - used cautiously in patients w/:
			- Hypertension
			- Cardiac disease
			- Hyperthyroidism
			- Glaucoma
			- Prostatic hypertrophy
	- **Intranasal therapy:**
		- _Intranasal antihistamines_ - azelastine and levocabastine are extremely effective in relieving sneezing and itching
		- _Intranasal sodium cromoglycate_ - usually useful in perennial allergic rhinitis but not as effective as intranasal corticosteroids for springtime hay fever
		- _Intranasal corticosteroid sprays_ (Budesonide, Beclomethasone, Fluticasone) - most effective agents for treatment of seasonal allergic rhinitis but slow onset of action (10-14d for peak effect)
		- _Intranasal ipratropium bromide_ - most effective when rhinorrhoea is the major problem
