# Ch2 Disorder of Perception

- **Classification of disorders of perception:**
    - <u>Sensory distortions</u> - a constant, real perceptual object which is perceived in a distorted way
    - <u>Sensory deceptions</u> - a new perception that may or may not be in response to an external stimuli

## Sensory distortions

- **Overview** - a result of:
    - Change in intensity (hyper- or hypo-aesthesia)
    - Change in quality
    - Change in spatial form
    - Distortions of the experience of time
- **Change in intensity** (hyper- or hypo-aesthesia):
    - <u>Hyperasthesia</u> - may be result of 1) intense emotions, or 2) lowering of the physiological threshold
    - <u>Hypo-aestehsia</u> - may be result of raised physiological threshold, or additionally due to deficits of attention (e.g. hypoacusis)
- **Changes in quality** - often visual perceptions are affected by it and are brought by toxic substances:
    - <u>Xanthopsia, chloropsia, erythropsia</u> - colouring of yellow, green and red respectively as a result of drugs (e.g. santonin, poisoning like digitalis)
    - <u>Altered gustation</u> - altered gustation after use of lithium results in a metallic taste (not a hallucination)
    - <u>Altered perception of quality</u>:
        - Depersonalisation - everything appears unreal and strage
        - Mania - everything appears perfect and beautiful
- **Change in spatial form** (dysmegalopsia) - change in size and shape through visual perception, may arise in organic retinal disease, defects of accomodation, organic brain disease (temporal and parietal lesions), but in psychiatric disease (e.g. schizophrenia):
    - <u>Dysmegalopsia</u> - objects perceived to be either larger (macropsia) or smaller (microspia)
    - <u>Metamorphopsia</u> - distorted irregular shapes (N.B. Amsler Grid and AMD)
- **Distortions of the experience of time:**
    - **Two varieties of time from psychopathological standpoint:**
        - <u>Physical time</u> - actual passage of time irrespective of the self
        - <u>Personal time</u> - personal judgement of the passage of time, which is affected by psychiatric disorders
    - **Normal distortions of personal time:**
        - <u>Happiness</u> - time 'flies'
        - <u>Sadness</u> - time passes more slowly, and may be perceived as 'time stand still' in severe depression (esp. psychotic depressive episodes)
    - **Distortion of personal time by psychiatric disorders** - affected by mood, and disorders of thought:
        - <u>Severe depressive episodes</u> - time passses extremely slowly and may perceived as 'time stand still' especially in psychotic depressive episodes
        - <u>Mania</u> - time speeds by and that the day is not long enough to do anything
        - <u>Schizophrenia</u> - may be a delusional elaboration on altered time (e.g. clocks being interferred with), and may resuult in age disorientation in chronic severe schizophrenia

## Sensory deceptions

- **Definition and terminology** - new perception that may or may not be in response to an external stimuli:
    - <u>Illusions</u> - misinterpretation of stimuli arising from an external object
    - <u>Hallucinations</u> - perceptions without an adequate external stimulus
- **Illusions** - not indicative of psychopathology:
    - <u>Proposed mechanism</u> - stimuli from a perceived object combined to a mental image to produce false perception
    - <u>Common forms of illusions</u>:
        - Visual hallucinations
        - Auditory hallucinations (difficult to distinguish from hallucination at times when external stimulus not attributable)
    - <u>Three types of illusions</u>:
        - **Completion illusions** - dependent on inattention and subconsciously drawing from past experience to 'complete the image' (e.g. misreading words w/ faded letters in an old magazine by adding to them)
        - **Affect illusions** - dependent on a particular mood state, e.g. bereaved person may momentarily believe they 'see' the deceased person, or irritated person may perceive innocent gestures as threatening
        - **Pareiodolia** - vivid illusions without patient making any effort as a result of 1) excessive fantasy thinking and 2) vivid visual imagery
    - <u>Illusions, hallucinations, and intellectual misunderstandings</u>
- **Hallucinations** - a <u>false perception</u> which is not a sensory distortion or a misinterpretation, but which occurs in the same time as real perceptions:
    - **Definition of hallucinations** - what distinguishes it from true perception:
        - Comes from within but reacts to them if they were true perceptions coming without
    - **Pseudo-hallucinations** (Jasper 1962):
        - Seen in full consciousness
        - Known to be not real perception and are known to be located not in objective space but in subjective space
        - Independent of the will
    - **Causes of hallucination** - result of intense emotions, psychiatric disorders, suggestion, disorder of sense organs, sensory deprivation, and disorders of the CNS:
        - <u>Emotion</u> - especially in severe depression w/ delusions of guilt, but tend to be disjointed or fragmentary (presence of continuous voices should raise suspicion of schzizophrenia or some intercurrent physical disease)
        - <u>Suggestion</u> - subjects persuaded to hallucinate through hypnosis, task-motivating instructions, or conforming to fantasies and cultural background
        - <u>Disorders of perpheral sense organs</u> - an important DDx from psychopathological causes of hallucinations, e.g. Charles Bonnet syndrome a/w phantom visual images in absence of psychopathology and in clear consciousness
        - <u>Sensory deprivation</u> - prolonged minimisation of stimuli in normal subject will cause the individual to begin to hallucinate
        - <u>Disorders of the CNS</u> - lesions of the diencephalon and of the cortex can produce visual or auditory hallucinations
    - **Hallucination and individual senses:**
    - **Hallucinatory syndromes** - hallucinosis; refers to disorders w/ persistent hallucinations in any sensory modality in the absence of other psychotic features:
        - <u>Alcoholic hallucinosis</u> - rarely persists for \> 1 week, and are usually auditory and occurs during periods of relative abstinence despite clear sensorium, often threatening or reproachful
        - <u>Organic hallucinosis</u> - present in 20-30% of patients w/ dementia, and are often auditory or visual
