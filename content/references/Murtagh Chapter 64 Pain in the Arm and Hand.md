- **Anatomical considerations for pain in the arm and hand:**
	- Focal pathologies in the arm and hand
	- Focal pathologies in the neck

- **Overview of causes of a painful arm and hand:**
	
	![[Pasted image 20260421101842.png]]
	- **Myocardia ischaemia** - must be considered especially for pain experiencing down the inner left arm, and a/w other cardiac symptoms
	- **Spine pathologies:**
		- _Cervical spine pathologies_ (cervical spondylosis, radiculopathy, myelopathy) - associated radiculopathy may transmit to distal parts of the forearm
		- _Upper thoracic vertebrae syndrome_ (T1-4) - poorly understood syndrome where dysfunction of the upper four segments of T-spine results in referred pain in elbow and arms in a non-dermatomal fashion, mimicking tennis elbow or tenosynovitis
	- **Shoulder pathologies** - disorders of the shoulder joint usually is confined to the C5 dermatone and does not usually extend below the elbow:
	- **Elbow pathologies** - well localised within the elbow:
		- _Overuse syndromes_:
			- Tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) - usually a/w repeated backhand movements
			- Golfer's elbow (medial epicondylitis) - usually a/w repeated forehand movements
		- _Degenerative pathologies_ - osteoarthritis of the elbow joint
		- _Inflammatory pathologies_:
			- Rheumatoid arthritis - usually bilateral and conincides w/ other problematic small joints
			- Olecranon bursitis - follow recurrent insults such as trauma, gout, rheumatoid arthritis or infections
			- Septic arthritis - r/o for all acute onset monoarthritis
	- **Wrist and hand pathologies:**
		- _de Quervain tenosynovitis_ - regional pain syndrome w/ involvement of the extensor pollicus brevis, abductor pollicis longus, w/ retrograde radiation towards the forearm
		- _Nerve entrapment syndromes_ - carpal tunnel syndrome, ulnar nerve neuropathy, median nerve neuropathy; generally a/w more marked neurological sequelae
- **Classification of shoulder joint pathology based on anatomy:**
	- _Rotator cuff lesions_ - involvement of >= 1 rotator cuff muscles, including various inflammatory tendinitis or shoulder impingement syndrome
	- _Acromioclavicular joint disease_ - pathology of AC joint
	- _Bicipital tendinitis_ - focal pain and tenderness at the insertion of the long head of the biceps

- **Etiology of hand and wrist pain:**
	- **Articular pathologies:**
		- _Nodal osteoarthritis_ - commonly first MCP joint or involvement of the DIP joints (Herberden nodes), but pain is not localised and radiates down the hand
		- _Rheumatoid arthritis_ - involvement of the MCP, PIP, and wrist joints
	- **Non-articular pathologies:**
		- _Tenosynovitis_ - flexor tenosynovitis or extensor tenosinovitis (De Quervain's disease) are caused by overuse (RSI), often a/w pain, swelling +/- warmth and fine crepitations
		- _Raynaud's phenomenon_ - vasoactive changes over the digits producing pain
		- _Radiculopathies_ - cervical spine radiculopathy at C8/T1 levels produces pain and weakness over the hands
		- _Nerve entrapment syndrome_ - various nerve entrapment syndromes such as CTS, and high/ low ulnar nerve palsy have characteristic sensory and motor Sx
		- _Reflex sympathetic dystrophy_  - gradual onset of chronic pain and swelling, a/w localised osteoporosis, and evidence of regional autonomic dysfunction such as sweating, colour and temperature changes
## Diagnostic Approach

- **Murtaugh's diagnostic approach to pain in the arm and hands:**
	
	![[Pasted image 20260421103124.png]]
- **Serious disorders not to be missed:**
	- _Myocardia ischaemia_ - should be considered for left arm pain, especially if a/w physical exertion (not to the arm), or is of a sudden onset
	- _Venous thromboembolism_ (effort thrombosis) - people working constantly above the heads, causing swelling and pain high in the axilla
	- _Severe infections_:
		- Septic arthritis - primarily affecting large joints such as the shoulder joint and elbow joint (olecranon bursitis)
		- Osteomyelitis - affecting the long bones
	- _Neoplastic disorders_:
		- Malignancies in the shoulder and arm - Pancoust tumour, lymphoma involving the axillary nodes, bone tumours
		- Neoplasms in the forearm and hand (usually benign) - giant cell tumour of the tendon sheath, pigmented villonodular synovitis, neurilemmonma, neurofibroma, rhabdomyosarcoma, synovioma

- **Masquerades:**
	- _Spinal dysfunction_ - focal cervical and upper thoracic pathologies
	- _Depression_ - the hands as the highly emotive organ frequently used to give outward expression of inner feeling:
		- Historically psychiatric disorders manifests as hysterical conversion syndrome w/ non-functioning of the hand
		- Many studies demonstrate the links between shoulder pain syndromes such as shoulder impingement syndrome, polymyalgia rheumatica w/ depression
	- _Hypothyroidism_ - myxoedema may result in bilateral CTS
	- _Diabetic neuropathy_ - that affecting the lower limbs may ocassionally involve the upper limbs, ocassionally resulting in erythomelagia (redness and burning related to heat)
- _Psychogenic considerations_ - the patient is trying to tell us something:
	- _Occupational neurosis_ (repetitive strain injury {RSI}) - repetitive movements that are unavoidable results in chronic pain, but may involve a psychogenic component
	- _Malingering_ - various so-called RSI disorders may in fact be masking a hidden agenda of work avoidance or a 'ticket' for compensation
## Clinical Approach

- **Causes of arm pain a/w sleep disturbances:**
	- _Thoracic outlet syndrome_ - initiation insomnia
	- _Carpal tunnel syndrome_ - maintenance insomnia, often waking the patient in the middle of the night due to pain and stifffness requiring movements of hands for temporary relief
	- _Cervical spondylosis_ - wakes the patient up w/ pain and stiffness that persists well into the day (mimics early morning wakening)
- **Salient points of Hx:**
	- _HPI_ - onset, progression, quality, radiation, severity (sleep disturbances, functional impairment, affective response)
	- _Associated Sx_:
		- Neurological Sx
		- Systemic Sx
	- _Etiologies_ - trauma (e.g. FOOSH), overuse etc.
- **P/E:**
	- _Examination of the joints_ - shoulder joint, elbow joint, wrist and hands
	- _Neurological examination_ - testing sensation, motor power, and reflexes when indicated
	- _Examination of the cervical spine_
- **Ix:**
	- **ECG** - indicated when suspecting myocardial ischaemia
	- **Routine bloods** - CBC, ESR +/- TnT
	- **Imaging:**
		- _X-ray_ - of the affected joints, cervical spine
		- _USG_ - of the affected joints or suspected soft tissue disorders
		- _MRI_ - if suspecting spine pathologies
	- **Electrophysiological tests** - if focal neurolological deficits are present:
		- NCS
		- EMG

### Trigger Finger

- **Definition** - a common work-induced condition characterised by stenosing tenosynovitis of the flexor sheath, eventually resulting in a finger locked in a flexed position
- **Epidemiology** - a common work-induced condition:
	- _Prevalence_ - lifetime risk of 2.6% in the population
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age - most common in 5-6th decade of life
		- Comorbidities - a/w T1DM, RA, gout, hypothyroidism, and amyloidosis
- **Pathophysiology of trigger finger:**
	- _Wear and tear of flexor tendons resulting in inflammation_ - repeated flexion and extension results in attrition wear and tear, fibrillation, and fragmentation, resulting in swelling, oedema, and inflammation
	- _Formation of a nodule_ - chronic inflammation and fibrosis results in formation of a nodule (a thickening in the tendon)
	- _Pulley effect of the fibro-osseous tunnel_ - upon flexion of finger, the thickened nodule becomes trapped proximal to the tunnel, locking the fingers in a flexed position

- **Clinical features of trigger finger:**
	- _Locking of the affected finger in flexed position_ - the finger is trapped due to the pulley effect:
		- Active extension - unable to perform full active extension of the finger
		- Passive extension - the finger can be extended passively w/ the other hand
	- _Triggering_ - palpable crepitus and tenderness when pulp of affected finger rubs over the nodule
- **Anatomical features of trigger finger:**
	- Most commonly affects 1st and 4th digit
	- Nodule typically occurs at the level of the metacarpal hand
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Injections_ - injection of corticosteroids near the nodular swelling under the tendon sheath (70% success rate)
		- _Surgery_ - simple and effective; reserved for when injections unsuccessful
