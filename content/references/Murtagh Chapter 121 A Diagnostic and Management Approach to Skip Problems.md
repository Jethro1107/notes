## Terminology of Skin Lesions

- **Primary lesions:**
	- **Flat lesions:**
		- _Macule_ - circumscribed area of altered skin colour w/o platform elevations < 1cm diameter
		- _Patch_ - macule w/ > 1cm diameter
		- 
		![[Pasted image 20260422165306.png]]
	- **Raised lesions:**
		- _Papule_ - palpable mass on skin surface < 0.5cm in diameter
		- _Plaque_ - a flat0topped palpable mass > 1cm in diameter
		- _Nodule_ - circumscribed palpable mass > 0.5cm in diameter
		- 
		![[Pasted image 20260422165323.png]]
	- **Oedematous lesions** (urticaria):
		- _Wheals_ - area of dermal oedema which is pale and compressible
		- _Angioedema_ - a diffuse area of oedema extending into the subcutaneous tissue
	- **Fluid-filled lesions:**
		- _Vesicles_ - a fluid-filled blister < 0.5cm in diameter
		- _Bulla_ - a vesicle > 0.5cm in diameter
		
		![[Pasted image 20260422165349.png]]
		- _Pustule_ - a visible collection of pus in the skin < 1cm in diameter
		- _Abscess_ - a localised collection of pus in a cavity > 1cm in diameter
	- **Superficial skin infections:**
		- _Furuncle_ - a purulent infected hair follicles, where folliculitis generally refers to smaller follicles, and boils referring to larger follicles
		- _Carbuncles_ - cluster of boils coalescing and discharging through several openings
	- **Haemorrhagic lesions:**
		- _Petechiae_ - small spots of bleeding into the dermis < 2mm in diameter
		- _Purpura_ - larger areas of bleeding into the dermis, which may be macular (non-palpable), or papular (palpable)
		- _Echymosis_ - a larger purpuric lesion
		- _Haematoma_ - a swelling from gross bleeding
	- **Other miscellaneous lesions:**
		- _Comedo_ - a plug of keratin and sebum in a dilated pilosebaceous gland, "blackhead" refers to an open comedo, while "whitehead" refers to a closed comedo
		- _Milium_ - tiny white cyst containing keratin, from occlusion of pilosebaceous gland
		- _Erythema_ - redness of the skin due to increased vascularity
		- _Papilloma_ - warty projections above the skin surface

## A Diagnostic Approach

- **Classification of common dermatological conditions:**
	
	![[Pasted image 20260422165810.png]]
- **Salient points of Hx:**
	- **HPI** - site, onset, progression, quality, severity:
		- _Site_ - localised vs wide-spread informs the DDx:
			- Localised - dermatomal vs anatomical (e.g. face, scalp, flexures, mouth, nails, penis)
			- Widespread - central vs peripheral
		- _Onset_ - duration of rash, e.g. acute vs subacute vs chronic
		- _Progression_:
			- Where did the rash start?
			- How did the rash progress?
		- _Quality_ - pruritic nature of rash narrows down the DDx
		- _Provocation and relieving factors_ - medications (e.g. Whitfield's ointment suppresses dermatophytosis, but exacerbates atopic dermatitis)
		- _Severity_ - marked distress +/- secondary skin changes
	- **Associated Sx:**
		- _Fever_ - infectious causes
		- _Sore throat_ - viral eruptions such as infectious mononucelosis-like syndrome
		- _Weight loss_ - suspicion for malignancy
	- **Associated events:**
		- _Contact_ - any contact w/ person w/ a similar eruption suggestive of an infectious cause
		- _Drug rash_ - newly initiated medications raises suspicion of drug rash
		- _Other exposures_ - questioning whether exposure to new clothes, or noticeable general exposures
	- **PMH:**
		- _Atopic tendancy_ - eczema, allergic rhinitis, asthma
	- **FHx** - skin problems

## Selection of Corticosteroid Preparations

- **Indications** - presence of inflammation
- **Determining the potency needed:**
	- Class I and class II preparations are appropriate for most problems
	- Use the weakest strength for chronic dermatoses but treat severe acute dermatoses aggressively
- **Selection of formulation:**
	- _Creams or lotion_ - reserved for 'weeping' lesions, the face, flexures, and hair-bearing areas
	- _Ointments_ - reserved for dry and scaling skin, or chronic skin lesions, but avoided in 'weeping' areas
	- _Gel_ - reserved for the scalp
	
	![[Pasted image 20260422184709.png]]
- **Topical corticosteroids for chronic dermatosis** - weakest strength to control chronic dermatosis:
	- Face - hydrocortisone 1%
	- Flexures - hydrocortisone 1%
	- Trunk - betamethasone valerate 0.2% or triamicinolone acetonide 0.02%
	- Elbows/ knees - betamethasone dipropionate 0.05%
	- Palms/ soles - methylprednisolone aceponate 0.1%
- **S/E:**
	- _Skin S/E_ - skin atrophy, peri-oral dermatitis, 'steroid acne', rosacea
	- _Adrenal insufficiency_ - especially for high potency preparations, chronic use, and use on thinner skin such as face, genitalia, and intertriginous areas
