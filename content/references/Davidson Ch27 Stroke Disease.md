## Stroke

- **Definition** - a rapid onset of focal neurological S/S or global disturbances of cerebral function due to non-traumatic vascular causes, w/ symptoms lasting \>= 24h or leading to death
- **Classification of stroke disease** - 85% Ischaemic stroke, 15% intracerebral haemorrhage: 
![](img/classification_of_stroke_disease.png)
- **Epidemiology** - common medical emergency and most common adult neurological disease:
    - <u>Incidence</u> - most common adult neurological disease
    - <u>Demographic</u> - incidence increases steeply with age, ?Male preponderance
    - <u>Mortality and Morbidity</u> - 2nd leading cause of death, 4th leading cause of physical disability in China and HK:
        - Mortality - 20% 30d mortality
        - Morbidity - 50% who survive are left with physical disability

### Ischaemic stroke

- **Definition** - stroke caused by occlusion of cerebral arteries caused by either thromboembolic events
- **Etiology of ischaemic stroke:**
    - <u>Thromboembolic complications of extracranial vascular disease</u> (most common cause) - e.g. carotid artery stenosis, aortic arch disease
    - <u>Cardioembolism</u> (20%) - thrombus formation in the heart (e.g. EF) with distal embolisation
    - <u>Thrombosis-in-situ in small perforators</u> (20%)
    - <u>Rare causes</u> (5%) - e.g. cerebral vasculitis, endocarditis, moyamoya disease, cerebral venous diseases etc.
- **Risk factors for ischaemic stroke:**
    - **Non-modifiable risk factors:**
        - <u>Demographic</u> - Increasing age, male sex (except at extreme of ages), Race (Afro-Caribbeans \> Asians \> Europeans)
        - <u>Previous vascular event</u> - Myocardial infarction, stroke, peripheral vascular disease
        - <u>Genetics</u> - +ve FHx for stroke, associations w/ high fibrinogen, homocysteinemia
    - **Modifiable risk factors:**
        - <u>Atherosclerotic risk factors</u> - cigarette smoking, BP, DM, hyperlipidaemia, cardiac disease, obesity, lack of exercise, +/- excessive alcohol intake
        - <u>Cardiac conditions with risk of cardioembolism</u> - e.g. atrial fibrillation, infective endocardtitis
        - <u>Virchow's triad</u>:
            - Hyperviscosity - e.g. polycythaemia
            - Hypercoagulability - e.g. oral contraceptive pills, hormone replacement therapy
- **Pathophysiology of ischaemic stroke:**
    - **Occlusion of cerebral arteries** - arises from different pathophysiological mechanisms, such as thromboembolic complications of extracranial arteries (atherosclerosis), cardioembolisms, or small vessel thrombosis etc.
    - **Altered cerebral dynamics** - altered cerebral dynamics in aims of maintaining cerebral blood flow (CBF), failure of which will result in cerebral ischaemia:
        - <u>Compensation by anastomotic channels</u> - compensation of CBF to blocked territory by opening of anastomotic channels from other arterial territories, stalling ischaemia and infarction
        - <u>Compensation by cerebral vasodilation</u> - reduced resistance to maintain CBF despite reduced cerebral perfusion pressure
        - <u>Compensation by increased cerebral oxygen extraction</u> - maintenace of cerebral metabolism despite reduced CBF, but **such compensation is limited to sub-ischaemic ranges of CBF**
    - **Onset of cerebral ischaemia** - occurs when CBF reduced to ischaemic range, eventually resulting in actual cerebral infarction: 
    ![](img/homeostatic_changes_to_reduced_cbf_and_ischaemia.png)
    - **Process of neuronal ischaemia and infarction:**
        - <u>Cellular hypoxia</u> - inadequate supply of ATP leading to failure of membrane pumps, resulting in influx of Na and water causing **cytotoxic oedema**
        - <u>Excitotoxicity</u> - failed membrane pumps results in membrane depolarisation and opening of Ca2+ channel, resulting in release of glutamate, resulting in excitotoxicity
        - <u>Other metabolic events</u> - e.g. Ca2+-mediated activated intracellular enzymes, free-radical release, lactic acidosis

    
    ![](img/neuronal_ischaemia_infarction.png)
    - **Haemorrhagic transformation of cerebral infarction** - large areas of liquefactive necrosis may be suceptible to haemorrhage after reperfusion
- **Clinical features** - clinically suspected on <u>rapid onset of focal neurological S/S</u> depending on arterial territory:
    - **Focal neurological S/S** - dependent on arterial territory affected and is usually -ve in character (i.e. abrupt loss of function w/o +ve features such as abnormal movements):
        - <u>Onset</u> - rapid onset, usually occurs over minutes (onset over hours or days may raise suspicion of other stroke mimics)
        - <u>Progression</u> - rapidly progressive (i.e. rapidly reaches maximum severity)
        - <u>Common presenting problems</u>:
            - **Weakness** - rapid, progressive unilateral weakness following a **hemiplegic pattern**, initially reduced reflexes, but subsequently brisk
            - **Speech disturbance** - dysphasia, or dysarthria:
                - <u>Dysphasia</u> - inability to speak due to damage to the language centres (Broca's area, Wernicke's area), localising to **dominant frontal or parietal lobe**
                - <u>Dysarthria</u> - difficulty in articulation due to weakness or incoordination of the face, pharynx, palate, tongue
            - **Visual deficits** - different patterns of visual disturbances (e.g. mono-ocular vision loss, contralateral hemianopia), due to ischaemia of optic nerve, optic tracts, or occipital lobe
            - **Ataxia** - a/w stroke causing damage to cerebellum, often with other CN defects (e.g. diplopia and vertigo)
            - **Visuospatial dysfunction** (neglect, apraxia) - neglect of one side, or inability to perform complex tasks despite normal motor, sensory and cerebellar functions caused by damage to no-dominant cortex
    - **Headache** - accompanying feature of all strokes, but are predominent in SAH and ICH
    - **Coma** - uncommon feature in ischaemic stroke, but indicative of large volume lesion (SAH and ICH esp. if at onset of focal S/S) or lesion in the brainstem
    - **Seizures** - unusual for acute strokes
- **Stroke mimics** - S/S caused by non-vascular phenomenon that is considered if Hx atypical (\< 5% in classical presentation): 
![](img/stroke_mimics.png)
- **Stroke localisation** - pattern of focal neurological S/S reflects arterial territory affected and its extent, manifestating as <u>'Stroke Syndromes'</u>:
    - **Anterior territory manifestations** - 1) **unilateral motor weakness** (hemiparesis +/- hemifacial weakness, bulbar symptoms), 2) **hemisensory loss**, 3) **higher cortical function deficits** (dysphasia if dominant, visuospatial dysfunction if non-dominant), 4) **visual field defects** (e.g. mono-nuclear vision loss, homonymous hemianopia)
    - **Posterior territory manifestations** - 1) **visual field defect** (homonymous hemianopia or cortical blindness), 2) **Cerebellar syndrome** (ataxia), 3) **Cranial nerve syndromes** (diplopia, nystagmus, vertigo), 4) **Motor deficits** (crossed or bilateral), 5) **Sensory loss** (crossed or bilateral)

  
  ![](img/stroke_syndromes_based_on_territory.png)
- **Ix** - 1) Dx and localisation of stroke, 2) Distinguish infarction and haemorrhage, 3) risk factor analysis, 4) underlying etiology:
    - **Neuroimaging** - non-contrast CT brain or MRI indicated for all patients w/ acute stroke:
        - **Non-contrast CT brain** - most practical and readily available modality:
            - <u>Role of NCCT brain</u>:
                - 1\. r/o stroke mimics - e.g. subdural haematoma, tumours (primary and metastatic), abscess
                - 2\. r/o ICH (changes will be immediately visible within minutes of stroke onset)
                - 3\. detect cerebral infarction - often minimal changes within first few hours
            - <u>Timing of NCCT</u> - usually \< 24h is adequate if suspecting ischaemic stroke, except alter Mx or ICH more likely:
                - 1\. Anticoagulated patients or with abnormal coagulation
                - 2\. Consideration of thrombolysis or immediate anticoagulation
                - 3\. Clinical deterioration of concsious level or focal deficits
                - 4\. Suspected cerebellar haematoma

        
        ![](img/indications_for_urgent_ct_stroke.png)
            - <u>CT findings suggestive of ischaemic stroke</u>:
                - 1\. Cortical hypodensity and sulcal effacement
                - 2\. Loss of gray-white matter differentiation in BG (lacunar stroke)

        
        ![](img/ncct_early_ischaemic_changes_ischaemic_stroke.png)
        - **MRI** - diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) sequence for detection of ischaemia (earlier than CT), or other sequences showing abnormal perfusion
    - **Cardiac evaluation** - CXR, ECG, echocardiogram
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, clotting profile, ESR, A1c/ FBG, lipid profiles, Troponin
    - **Vascular imaging** - after acute episode to detect atherosclerotic thromboembolic disease:
        - <u>Modalities</u> - Duplex USG, MRA, CT angiography, invasive contrast radiography
        - <u>Assessment</u> - extracranial arterial pathologies such as carotid stenosis
        - <u>Significance</u> - identify etiology of stroke in certain patients which may lead onto specific Tx for secondary prevention
    - **Additional Ix** - esp. for young strokes with clinical suspicion: 
    ![](img/additional_ix_for_young_stroke.png)
- **Mx of ischaemic stroke:**
    - **Aims of Mx:**
        - 1\. Timely reperfusion to salvage the penumbra - minimise brain volume that is infarcted
        - 2\. Prevention of complications
        - 3\. Rehabilitation
        - 4\. Secondary prevention of subsequent events
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Supportive care and medical Tx</u> - Mx in specialised stroke units and acute medical Mx to enable rehabilitation
        - <u>Reperfusion therapy</u> - initiation of anti-thrombotic therapy, or intravenous thrombolysis
        - <u>Mx of risk factors</u> - lifestyle modifications, atherosclerotic risk factor control (e.g. Antitplatelet, lipid-lowering, anti-hypertensives), surgical Tx of carotid stenosis
    - **Supportive care** - ABC and initial neurological evaluation, hydration, swallowing assessment, nutrition, medication, blood glucose management, BP management, fever management, perssure areas, incontinence:
        - **Airway, breathing and circulation** - initial assessment of vital signs and stabilization of airway, breathing in circulation (esp. vomiting in raised ICP, or prominent bulbar symptoms):
            - <u>Airway</u> - early bedside test of swallowing and keep patient NPO if swallowing unsafe or aspiration occurs
            - <u>Breathing</u> - assess RR and SpO2 and give O2 SpO2 \< 95%
            - <u>Circulation</u> - manage circulation as appropriate
        - **Swallowing assessment** - early bedside test of swallowing as dysphagia is common and a major risk factor for aspiration pneumonia:
            - <u>Swallowing functions intact</u> - allow oral medications and food
            - <u>Dysphagia</u> - keep NPO, IV access for medications, +/- enteral feeding by NG tube (if dysphagia persistent)
        - **Nutrition** - provide nutrition orally, via enteral feeding, or intravenously based on nutritional status and swallowing status
        - **Medications** - continue other routes of essential medications if dysphagia present (e.g. aspirin given per-rectally)
        - **Hydration** - most patients w/ stroke may are dehydrated, or at least require maintenance fluid therapy:
            - **Fluid of choice** - normal saline w/o dextrose (avoid hypotonic solutions which exacerbate cerebral oedema, or dextrose solutions which exacerbate hyperglycaemia)
        - **Blood glucose** - treat hyperglycaemia when severe (\> 11.1 mmol/L) and avoid hypoglycaemia (causing neuronal injry)
        - **Blood pressure control** - permissive HTN to maintain cerebral perfusion perssure (esp. in setting of lost cerebral autoregulation) unless there is evident hypertensive crisis (e.g. ADHF, AKI, hypertensive encephalopathy), or concomittent conditions requiring agressive BP lowering (e.g. aortic dissection)
        - **Fever** - Ix for cause and Tx with antipyretics (raised brain temperature causes increased infarct volume)
        - **Pressure areas** - avoid pressure ulcers by:
            - <u>Pressure relieving mattresses</u>
            - <u>Turning immobile patients</u>
            - <u>Maintaining nutrition</u>
            - <u>Treating active infections</u>
        - **Sphincteric dysfunction** - Mx constipation and urinary retention accordingly
        - **Mobilisation** - early mobilisation and avoid bed rest

    
    ![](img/acute_mx_of_stroke.png)
    - **Medical Tx and reperfusion therapy** - IV thrombolysis, mechanical thrombectomy, anti-platelet therapy:
        - **Thrombolysis** - recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) indicated in selected patients:
            - <u>MOA</u> - activate plasminogen to promote fibrinolysis
            - <u>Timing</u> - effect is time-dependent, given **within 4.5h of symptom onset** in **selected patients**
            - <u>C/I</u> (exclusion criteria) - read up
            - <u>Clinical effects</u> - improve overall outcome, where benefit is time dependent (earlier treatment, better outcomes)
            - <u>Cx</u> - increased risk of haemorrhagic transformation
        - **Mechanical thrombectomy** - given within 6h of onset of stroke due to <u>large artery occlusion</u>
        - **Antiplatelet therapy** - ASA:
            - <u>Indications and timing</u> - in all patients in absence of C/I, given immediately unless rt-PA given (withhold for 24h)
            - <u>Clinical effects</u> - reduce recurrence rate, with minimal (but worthwhile) effects on long-term outcomes
        - **Anticoagulant therapy** (e.g. LMWH) - no clinical evidence showing improvement of outcomes, and <u>greatly increased risk of haemorrhagic transformation</u>
    - **Mx of risk factors** - reduce risk of recurrence:
        - **General measures of secondary prevention:**
            - Long-term antiplatelet therapy
            - Long-term statin therapy
            - BP lowering - target BP \< 130/80 mmHg
        - **Measures of patients with AF** - warfarin (target INR 2-3) or DOACs
        - **Measures for carotid stenosis:**
            - Carotid endarterectomy - read up indications (surgery a/w 5% risk of stroke)
            - Carotid angioplasty and stenting - not shown to be as effective as carotid endarterectomy

## Subarachnoid Haemorrhage

- **Definition** - haemorrhage into subarachnoid space, which is usually traumatic, but usually spontaneous
- **Epidemiology** - less common than ISS and ICH, but pertains a much greater mortality:
    - <u>Incidence</u> - 6/100000/y
    - <u>Demographic</u> - typically present \< 65y, F \> M
    - <u>Mortality</u> - 30% immediate mortality for aneurysmal SAH
    - <u>Risk of recurrence</u> - 40% in first 4 weeks, and 3% annually thereafter
- **Etiology of SAH** - most are traumatic:
    - **Saccular aneurysms** (85%) - occurs at bifurcation of cerebral arteries mostly in circle of Willis:
        - Anterior communicating artery (ACoA) - 30%
        - Posterior communicating artery (PCoA) - 25%
        - Middle cerebral artery (MCA) - 20%
    - **Other aneurysms** - e.g. mycotic aneurysm
    - **Non-aneurysmal haemorrhage** - e.g. AVMs, vertebral artery dissection, drug abuse
- **Saccular aneurysms:**
    - <u>Clinical association</u>:
        - Polycystic kidney disease
        - Connective tissue defects - e.g. Ehlers-Danlos syndrome
- **Clinical features of SAH:**
    - **Headache** - sudden (onset to maximal intensity), severe, <u>thunderclap headache</u> (often occipital), lasting for hours or even days (1 in 8 presenting with sudden, severe headache has SAH and must be ruled out)
    - **Deteriorating consciousness** - LOC can occur at onset which affects Hx (should be considered in all comatosed patients)
    - **Vomiting** - often marked accompaniment
    - **Neck stiffnes or pain** - due to subarachnoid blood causing meningeal irritation, but may take time to develop
    - **Raised BP**
- **Signs of SAH:**
    - <u>General appearance</u> - distressed, irritable, and marked photophobia
    - <u>Signs of meningism</u> - Kernig's sign and Brudzinski sign +ve, but may take hours to develop
    - <u>Focal neurological deficits</u> - if a/w intracerebral haematoma
    - <u>Surgical 3rd nerve palsy</u> (rare) - caused by PCoA aneurysm compressing on optic nerve:
        - **Ptosis and dilated pupils** - as peripheral parasympathetic fibres affected
        - **Preserved EOM** - as central fibres intact
    - <u>Subyaloid haemorrhage on fundoscopy</u> (rare) - blood tracking along subarachnoid space around optic nerve
- **Ix and Dx evaluation** - Dx based on +ve CT or LP, and cerebral angiography is required for assessment of approach:
    - **Non-contrast CT brain** - urgent CT required if suspecting SAH:
        - <u>Test properties</u> - -ve CT does not r/o SAH (15% of SAH have initial -ve CT)
        - <u>Findings suggestive of SAH</u>:
            - Hyperdense lesion in the subarachnoid space - e.g. basal cistern (star sign), sylvian fissures etc.
    - **Lumbar puncture:**
        - <u>Timing</u> - performed 12h after symptom onset to detect xanthochromia (if CT -ve)
        - <u>Findings</u>:
            - **Blood-stained CSF** - unable to differentiate between SAH and traumatic tap
            - **Xanthochromia** - specific for SAH as traumatic taps do not cause xanthchromia in the specimen
    - **Cerebral angiography** (CTA vs DSA) - Dx evaluation to determine optimal approach

  
  ![](img/ix_for_sah.png)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - unable to reverse initial injury, but targeted at preventing recurrence, secondary injury, or systemic complications:
        - <u>Supportive Tx</u> - ABC, and optimise ICP and CBP
        - <u>Preventing early and late recurrence</u> (e.g. rebleeding) - treatment of underlying cause (e.g. obliteration of cerebral aneurysm by microsurgical or endovascular approaches, Tx of AVM)
        - <u>Treatment of complications of SAH</u>:
            - 1\. Delayed cerebral ischaemia
            - 2\. Post-SAH obstructive hydrocephalus
            - 3\. Seizures
            - 4\. Hyponatraemia (SIADH)
            - 5\. Systemic complications - e.g. chest infections, DVT
    - **Neurosurgical approach to prevention of recurrence** - e.g. obliteration of aneurysm, Mx of AVMs etc:
        - **Obliteration of cerebral aneurysms** - aim at complete obliteration to prevent re-rupture as soon as feasible:
            - <u>Modalities</u> - coiling preferred as fever perioperative Cx and better outcomes:
                - Surgical - Microsurgical clipping
                - Endovascular interventions - e.g. embolisation, coiling, and flow diverters
        - **Mx of AVMs** - e.g. ligation of feeders, injection sclerotherapy into fistula or draining veins
    - **Prevention of delayed cerebral ischaemia** - major cause of mortalit and morbidity:
        - **Pathophysiology of delayed cerebral ischaemia** - post-SAH vasospasm that starts at D4 and peaks at D7-10
        - **Nimodipine** (vascular-selective CCB):
            - <u>MOA</u> - reduces vasospasm secondary to SAH to prevent delayed cerebral ischaemia
            - <u>Dosing</u> - IV 30-60mg nimodipine for 5-14d, followed by PO 360mg for 7d
        - **Other Tx modalities** - e.g. good CBF control, chemical/ mechanical angioplasty
    - **Obstructive hydrocephalus** - required CSF shunting
    - **Mx of other complications** - e.g. seizures, chest infections, pressure sores, DVT
