# Ch413 Pathobiology of Obesity

- **Physiological significance of adipose tissue** - as a solution to the challenge of the intermittent availability of food:
	- When food is plentiful, excess calories are converted to triglycerides and efficiently stored in the unilocular lipid droplets in fat cells
	- When food is scarce, TG is broken down to FFA and gluycerol which provides as an energy source to other sites of the body
- **We are fortunate enough to not face the problem of intermittent food supply:**
	- In the world we live in, food supply is abundant, and lifestyle tends to be sedentary
	- This results in a _chronic excess of energy intake over energy expenditure_
	- This predisposes to development of _obesity_
- **Variability in the development of obesity:**
	- The excess caloric intake only provides the substrate for development of obesity, but the risk of developing obesity under similar circumstances and of developing illnesses a/w obesity vary greatly between individuals
	- i.e. The extent to which adipose tissue expands in response to over-nutrition varies between individuals
	- Such variations have a strong genetic basis
- **Defining obesity:**
	- _Conceptual definition of obesity_ - obesity is defined as a state of **excessive adipose tissue mass** that **adversely affects health**, but direct measurement of fat mass is not readily undertaken in clinical practice
	- _BMI-based definitions_ - BMI is a measure generally used as a proxy of adipose tissue mass, where cutoffs have be defined based on epidemiological studies (the following figures are based on predominantly white, Western populations):
		- 
		![[Pasted image 20260501184419.png]]
	- _Limitations of BMI_:
		- Effects of race - WHO-based definitions are based on studies of predominantly white, Western populations, and there is a growing evidence that the relationship btween BMI and adverse outcomes is different in people from other ethnic groups (e.g. Asians are predisposed to greater risks at a lower BMI)
		- Effects of body build - e.g. in very muscular individuals, BMI may not be the best proxy of total body fat
		- Effects of sex - women will generally have higher percentage of body fat than men, even w/ the same BMI
- **Effects of fat distribution on clinical diseases:**
	- Central obesity, idependent to the degree of obesity (BMI), is a/w adverse health outcomes such as T2DM, and dyslipidaemia
	- This is defined as a waist-to-hip ratio of > 0.9 in F, and > 1 in M
- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - rapidly increasing prevalence based on epidemiological studies:
		- According to the WHO, obesity has nearly tripled worldwide since 1975 (39% and 13% of > 18y were overweight and obese respectively)
		- NHANES, prevalence of obesity in the US increased from 14.5% to 42.2% from the 1970s to the 2010s
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age groups - no significant differences in prevalence by age groups amongst adults (according to NHANES), however a growing epidemic of childhood obesity
		- Sex - general prevalence is slightly higher in F than in M
		- Race - variable risks of obesity, as seen in the NHANES study
## Physiological Regulation of Energy Balance

- **Body-weight by and large is subjected to fundamental homeostatic control:**
	- In the 1940s, it was demonstrated that rodents _defend their level of body fat_; once returned to ad libitum diets after a short period of enforced caloric restriction or excess, animals either overconsumed or underconsumed calories until they returned to their previous status
	- The _hypothalamus_, which partially sits outside the BBB, serves as a key locus that receives neurohormonal inputs regarding 1) _hunger_, 2) _satiety_, and 3) _expenditure of energy_
- **Hormonal control of energy balance:**
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260501192046.png]]
	- **Leptin** - long-term regulation and as a reflection of total body fat store:
		- _Level of leptin reflects total body fat store_ - importantly in caloric restriction, level of changes of leptin occurs more rapidly than changes in body fat:
			- When fat stores increase, leptin levels increase
			- When fat stores decrease leptin levels decrease
		- _Physiological effects of leptin_ - production of a sense of satiety and reduces food intake
		- _Clinical significance_ - leptin disorders (loss of functional leptin or leptin receptors) results in severe childhood obesity largely as a result of hyperphagia (unsuppressed appetite)
	- **Gastrointestinal hormones** - typically fluctuates immediately related to the maount and type of food ingested:
		- _Examples of gastrointestinal hormones_ - released in response to a meal:
			- Stomach - cholecystokinin (CCK)
			- Small intestine - gastric inhibitory polypeptide (GIP), glucagon-like peptide 1 (GLP-1)
			- Large intestine - peptide YY (PYY), oxyntomodulin (OXM)
		- _Physiological effects_ - sense of satiety (partially also via vagal stimulation by ENS)
	- **Pancreatic hormones** - amylin and insulin also act on hypothalamus to control energy balance
	- **Pro-opiomelanocortin** (POMC) - expressed on highly specialised hypothalamic neurons projecting to the rest of the brain, aimed at _integration of neurohormonal signals regarding energy balance_:
		- 
		![[Pasted image 20260501192147.png]]
		- **POMC-MSH axis** - POMC-derived peptides involves regulation of caloric intake and expenditure:
			- Upon stimulation by leptin, POMC synthesis in the arcuate nucleus is increased (while simultaneously inhibiting AGRP)
			- POMC-derived petpides alpha-/ beta-MSH stimulates melanocortin 4 receptor (MC4R) and results in 1) reduced food intake, and 2) increased SNS and metabolic activity (increased energy expenditure)
			- Gamma-MSH stimulates MC3R regulates linear growth and deposition of nutrients into lean vs fat tissues
		- **Negative control by other neuronal signals** - of clinical significance, AGRP directly switches off MC4R, and defective copies are a/w overeating:
			- Agouti-related peptide (AGRP)
			- Neuropeptide Y (NPY)
			- Gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)
## The Physiology of Nutrient Storage in Adipose Tissue

- **Physiological response during over-nutrition:**
	- _Glycogenesis_ - small amounts of excess energy is stored as glycogen in the liver and skeletal muscles
	- _Lipid synthesis_ - when imbalance is greater, body is forced to store excess energy in a more efficient manner as TG in adipose tissues
- **Adipocytes as specialised cells for lipid storage:**
	- Presence of the unilocular fat droplet as a specialised organelle for TG storage
	- Presence of necessary enzymes for synthesis and breakdown of TG in a manner that rapidly responds to metabolic requirements
	- It is thought that the adverse effects of obesity is not caused by increased fat storage in adipose tissues, but by storage in "non-professional" cells
- **Mechanism of increased fat mass during obesity:**
	- Increased storage of lipids per fat cell
	- Increased synthesis of new fat cells, where ~ 10% of fat cell population turns over every year
## The Causes of Obesity: An Interaction of Genes and Environment

- **Principles of pathogenesis of obesity:**
	- _Cumulative pathology_ - i.e. even if energy surplus by a small amount (e.g. 7 kcal/d), sufficient to develop obesity over years or decades
	- _Gene-environment interaction_ - genetic factors play a major role in predisposing individuals to adiposity, but the caloric surplus is largely driven by environmental factors:
		- Genetic factors - twin studies demonstrate that the degree of adiposity in adult life of identical twins brought up in different families is very similar between the twins but is not at all correlated with that of the adoptive siblings with whom they were raised
		- Environmental factors - economics and social factors likely play a role, where in Western countries, there are more normal-weight people in socially advantaged groups
- **Genetic predisposition to obesity** - to what extent do relative roles of excess energy intake and lower energy expenditure confer to biological predisposition:
	- _Increased energy intake_ (more evidence) - more evidence that genetic predisposition to obesity is largely mediated through brain control of energy intake, where those who carry genetic risk tend to eat more and be less readily satiated
	- _Decreased energy expenditure_ (limited evidence) - very subtle evidence point towards low metabolic rate as a main mechanism of obesity (except in disease states such as hypothyroidism)
- **Environmental factors predisposing to obesity** (the "obesogenic" environment) - obesity cannot simply exist in the absence of sufficient food to lay down and maintain excessive fat stores, but should not thus be thought as a disease of limited self-control:
	- **Modernisation** - effect on individuals are variable, such that some remain normal weight attributable to genetic fortune:
		- _Food items in the market_ - energy-dense, highly palatable food aggressively marketed, made cheaper, provided in larger portions, and made ubiquitously and continuously
		- _Sedentary lifestyle_ - changes in nature of employment, and mechanisation of daily living resulting in reduced energy expenditure
		- _Artificial heating and cooling_ - results in reduced energy expenditure on thermoregulation
	- **Dieting** - dieting are theoretically sound, generally have evidence in short-term weight changes but long-term evidence in terms of weight maintenance and adverse outcomes are less compelling
	- **Epigenetic programming** - growing body of evidence that development of obesity and cardiometabolic diseases may be related to exposures in-utero or early post-natal life
	- **Intestinal microbiome** - limited evidence, but is theoretically exciting
- **Leptin does not prevent obesity** - from an evolutionary standpoint, leptin serves its role in defending loss of body weight, rather than preventing gaining weight:
	- The response curve is not linear
	- Fall in circulating leptin below a person's habitual level provides powerful stimulus to food intake
	- Rise in circulating leptin (either through increasing fat stores or injection of leptin) produces discernible effect on hunger and energy expenditure ("_leptin resistance_")

## Obesity Secondary to Other Disorders

- **DDx of obesity secondary to other disorders:**
	- **Endocrine disorders:**
		- _Hypothyroidism_ - may cause weight gain and on the occasion obesity, but is rarely the sole cause of obesity
		- _Cushing's syndrome_ - often a/w other cutaneous features such as bruising, striae, and centripedal distribution of fat
		- _Insulinoma_ - driven largely by the need to eat more frequently than normal to avoid hypoglycaemia
	- **Hypothalamic damage** - e.g. sellar masses (craniopharyngioma), inflammatory masses, severe TBI, often a/w hyperphagia along w/ evidence of hypopituitarism
## Adverse Consequences of Obesity

- **Overview of adverse consequences of obesity:**
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260501213812.png]]
- **Mechanistic considerations:**
	- _Mechanical/ gravitational effects_ - effects of expanded fat basses, e.g. osteoarthritis, obstructive sleep apnoea, GERD
	- _Metabolic effects_ - insulin resistance predisposes to cardiometabolic diseases and potentially development of certain cancers
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260501223353.png]]
- **Metabolic complications:**
	- _Dyslipidaemia_ - attributed to insulin resistance of obesity, characterised by mixed pattern w/ high TG, and LDL-C
	- _Metabolic-associated steatotic liver disease_ - obesity a/w ectopic fat in hepatocytes, which results in NASH, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma
	- _Type 2 diabetes_ - insulin resistance strongly predisposes to T2DM, likely on top of a genetically predisposed individuals who are less capable of maintain hyper-insulinaemic states over decades
- **Endocrine complications:**
	- _Polycystic ovarian syndrome_ - due to underlying insulin-resistance/ hyperinsulinaemia resulting in irregular menses, anovulation, and hirsutism
	- _Hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism_ - more common in men and results in testosterone deficient states and FSH/ LH that do not rise appropriately
- **Dermatological complications:**
	- _Acanthosis nigricans_ - velvety hypepigmentation in skin folds such as the axilla, groin, and back of the neck
	- _Hidradenitis suppurativa_ - recurrent boils w/ chronically draining sinus tracts ffecting skin areas containing apocrine sweat glands
- **Cardiovascular complications:**
	- _Atherosclerosis_ - coronary aratery disease and ischaemic stroke are the main drivers of morbidoity and mortality
	- _Heart failure_ - increased risk of diastolic dysfunction +/- atrial fibrillation, independent of atherosclerosis
	- _Thrombotic thrombocytopenia purpura_ - strong association w/ obesity
- **Respiratory complications:**
	- _Obesity-hypoventilation syndrome_ - exertional dyspnoea +/- blood gas changes due to increased work of breathing to move a greater mass, as well as impacts on chest wall compliance
	- _Obstructive sleep apnoea_ - enlargement of soft tissues of the mouth and throat results in increased prevalence of sleep apnoea
- **Gastrointestinal disorders:**
	- _Gastroesophageal reflux disease_ - reflux esophagitis due to increased intra-abdominal pressure
	- _Gallstones_ - likely due to dyslipidaemia, and increased risks of biliary colic, cholecystitis, cholangitis, pancreatitis, and gallbladder cancer
	- _Pancreatitis_ - primarily biliary gallstones, although certain individuals may have TG high enough to cause acute pancreatitis
- **Rheumatological disorders:**
	- _Obsteoarthritis of the knees_ - degeneration due to excessive loading onto the knee joints
	- _Gout_ - via increased uric acid synthesis and decreased excretion
	- _Inflammatory arthropathies_ - rheumatoid arthritis and seronegative spondyloarthropathies a/w obesity postulated to be due to the pro-inflammatory state
- **Malignancies:**
	- Obesity has overcome smoking as the most important cause of malignancies
	- Increase of BMI by 5 kg/m2 a/w increase in cancer mortality by 10%
	- A/w various malignancies, strongest w/ colorectal, kidney, pancreatic cancer, adenocarcinoa of the esophagus, and endometrial carcinoma
- **Disorders of the central nervous syndrome:**
	- _Dementia_ - increasing evidence of obesity being a risk factor for dementia but mechanism unclear (?vascular)
	- _Idiopathic intracranial hypertension_ - rare disorder but strongly a/w obesity
