# Introduction

- **Definition of Acute Abdomen** - no universal definition, but refer to S/S of abdominal pain and tenderness representing an underlying surgical abdomen that requires prompt Dx and surgical Tx
- **DDx of acute abdomen** - terminology typically reflects a <u>surgical cause</u>, but must keep in mind of <u>non-surgical causes</u> that mimic acute abdomen:
    - **Surgical acute abdomen:**
        - <u>Inflammation of a viscus</u>:
            - **Inflammation of the alimentary canal** - Appendicitis, Diverticulitis, Meckel's Diverticulitis
            - **Inflammation of HBP system** - Biliary colic, cholecystitis, cholangitis, pancreatitis, liver abscess
            - **Inflammation of other intra-abdominal organs** - acute pyelonephritis, psoas abscess, other intra-abdominal abscess, <u>ectopic pregnancy</u>
        - <u>Obstruction of a viscus</u>:
            - **Intestinal obstruction** - SBO (adhesions, Crohn's, intussusception), LBO (Lower GI malignancy, incarcerated hernia, volvolus)
            - **Obstruction of cystic duct or CBD** - biliary colic (symptomatic gallstone disease), symptomatic choledolithiasis
            - **Obstruction of the urinary system** - urolithiasis, clot colic
        - <u>Ischaemia of a viscus</u>:
            - **Bowel ischaemia** - ischaemic colitis, mesenteric thromboembolism, strangulated hernia (including Ritcher's hernia), Buerger disease
            - **Ischaemia of reproductive organs** - ovarian torsion, testicular torsion, undescended testes
        - <u>Perforation of a viscus</u>:
            - **Perforation of the intra-abdominal esophagus** - Borhaave syndrome
            - **Perforation of the upper GI organs** - perforated peptic ulcer (PPU), perforated upper GI malignancy
            - **Perforation of the bowel** - perforated diverticulosis, perforation after IO
        - <u>Haemorrhage of a viscus</u>:
            - **GI bleeding** - UGIB and LGIB (see related notes)
            - **Splenic rupture** - spontaneous or traumatic
            - **Vascular** - ruptured AAA
            - **Haemorrhage of reproductive organs** - ruptured ovarian cyst, ruptured ectopic pregnancy
    - **Non surgical acute abdomen:**
        - <u>Endocrine and metabolic causes</u> - e.g. DKA/ HHS, Uraemia, Addisonian Crisis, Acute intermittent porphyria, Hereditary Mediterranean fever
        - <u>Haematological casues</u> - acute leukaemia, sickle cell crisis
        - <u>Toxins and drugs</u> - lead poisoning, heavy metal poisoning, narcotic withdrawal, black widow spider poisoning
