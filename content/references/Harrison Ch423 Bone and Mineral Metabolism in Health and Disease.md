## Phosphorus Metabolism

- **Distribution of total body phosphorus:**
    - <u>Bone mineral</u> - accounts for 85% (~600 g) of total body phosphorus
    - <u>Intracellular fluid</u> - major intracellular constituents as both **free anion**, or as component of **organophosphate comounds** (e.g. ATP, nucleic acids)
    - <u>Extracellular fluid</u> - typically exists in several forms, predominantly as H2PO4, NaHPO4, or HPO4
- **Extracellular phosphates** - expressed in terms of elemental phosphorus (0.75-1.45 mmol/L):
    - <u>Forms</u> - H2PO4, NaHPO4, HPO4
    - <u>Transport</u> - 12% of phosphates are bound to proteins
- **Processing of diatary phosphate:**
    - <u>Daily dietary phosphorus</u> - widely available in food, and generally ranges from 500-1000 mg/d
    - <u>Absorption</u>:
        - Efficient absorption by small intestine even if absence of vitamin D (65%)
        - Phosphate absorptive efficiency further enhanced (to 85-90%) in the presence of vitamin D due to active transport mechanisms
        - Absorption is inhibited by high doses of calcium salt, Aluminium-based antacids, or sevelamer hydrochloride (N.B. these enable control of phosphate in CKD)
- **Effects of phosphate on vitamin D metabolism:**
    - Low phosphate levels stimulate renal PCT hydroxulation of 1,25(OH)2D through suppression of FGF23 levels
    - This promotes GI phosphate absorption and renal reabsorption of phosphate
- **Renal handling of phosphate** - control of serum PO4 is controlled by renal tubular reabsorption of the filtered load to match that of dietary intake:
    - <u>Renal reabsorption of PO4 in PCT</u> - mediated by activity of Na/PO4 co-transporters
    - <u>Endocrine regulation of renal reabsorption of PO4</u>:
        - FGF23 (major mechanism) - reduced apical expression of Na/PO4 co-transporter in PCT resulting in increased urinary excretion, and suppression of renal vitamin D synthesis (N.B. activating FGF23 mutation results in hypophosphatemic rickets)
        - PTH - reduced apical expression of Na/PO4 co-transporters in PCT resulting in increased urinary excretion
    - <u>Other factors influencing renal phosphate reabsorption</u> - impaired by:
        - Hypocalcaemia
        - Hypomagnesaemia
        - Severe hypophosphataemia
        - Dehydration

### Hypophosphataemia

- **Definition** - serum PO4 levels below LLN (i.e. \< 9.75 mmol/L)
- **DDx of hypophosphataemia** - 1) inadequate intestinal absorption, 2) excessive renal excretion, 3) rapid redistribution into bones or soft tissue, or 4) accelerated bone formation:
    - **Impaired intestinal reabsorption** - aluminium-containing antacids, sevelamer
    - **Increased renal excretion** (renal hypophosphataemia) - classified into 1) PTH/PTHrP-dependent, and 2) PTH/PTHrP-independent:
        - **PTH/PTHrP-dependent** (biochemical effects exacerbated by concomitent vitamin D deficiency):
            - <u>Raised PTH levels</u> - primary or secondary hypercalcaemia, Familial hypercalciuric hypercalcaemia
            - <u>PTHrP</u> - PTHrP-mediated hypercalcaemia of malignancy
            - <u>Activating mutation of PTH receptors</u> - Jansen chondrodysplasia
        - **PTH/PTHrP-independent** - characterised by low 1,25(OH)2D levels, low-normal Ca, and severe hypophosphataemia (w/ accompanying aminoaciduria) due to renal wasting, and associated rickets and osteomalacia:
            - <u>Excess FGF23 or other phosphatonins</u> - Autosomal dominant hypophosphataaemic rickets (ADHR), X-linked hypophosphataemic rickets (XLH), other rare AR hypophosphataemic syndromes (DMP1, ENPP1), **tumour-induced osteomalacia** (TIO)
            - <u>Intrinsic renal disease</u> - Fanconi syndromes (e.g. Dent disease), cystinosis, Na/PO4 co-transporter mutations
            - <u>Other-systemic disorders</u> - DM and alcoholism (predisposes to iatrogenic hypophosphataemia), other drugs and toxins (see below)
    - **Redistribution from ECF to ICF:**
        - IV glucose
        - Insulin therapy for DKA (classical cause)
        - Recovery from starvation or acidosis
        - Catecholamines
        - Acute respiratory alklaosis

- **Clinical features of hypophosphataemia** - reflect generalised defect in cellular energy metabolism due to depleted ATP:
    - <u>Neuromuscular manifestations</u> - muscle weakness, reduced GCS, disorientation, hallucination, bulbar Sx, occulomotor palsies, ataxia, cerebellar tremor, imapired sphincter control, distal sensory deficits, or Guillain-Barre like ascending paralysis
    - <u>Rhabdomyolysis</u> - may develop from rapidly progressive hypophosphataemia and often overlooked as release of PO4 from myocytes could lead to near-normalisation of circulating PO4
    - <u>Respiratory failure and cardiac dysfunction</u> - due to neuromuscular weakness but generally reversible w/ phosphate Tx
    - <u>Renal tubular defects</u>:
        - RTA
        - Glycosuria
        - Sodium and Ca wasting
    - <u>Haematological deficits</u>:
        - RBC - microspherocytosis and haemolysis w/ impaired oxyhaemoglobin dissociation
        - WBC - defective leukocyte chemotaxis, phagocytosis and bacterial killing
        - PLT - platelet dysfunction w/ spontaneous GI bleeding
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Mx of acute hypoPO4</u> - oral phosphate in mild-to-moderate hypoPO4, empirical IV phosphate therapy in severe hypoPO4 esp. w/ underlying phosphorus depletion
        - <u>Mx of chronic hypoPO4</u> - correction of underlying causes
    - **Oral PO4 replacement:**
        - <u>Regimen</u> - 750-2000 mg/d as elemental phosphorus
        - <u>S/E</u> - higher doses lead to GI discomfort
    - **IV phosphate therapy** - empirical as the underlying deficit of total body PO4 cannot be easily be predicted:
        - <u>Considerations for IV phosphate therapy</u>:
            - Severity and duration of hypoPO4 - by laboratory findings, duration of Sx of hypoPO4, or presence of neuromuscular, cardiopulmonary, or haematological complications of hypoPO4
            - Renal function - half dose if serum creatinine \> 220 mmol/L
            - Presence of Ca problems - correct HypoCa, first prior to IV phosphate therapy; half-dose in presence of hyperCa
            - Concurrent parenteral glucose administration
        - <u>Standard regimen</u> - 0.2-0.8 mmol/kg of elemental phosphorus over 6h (i.e. 10-50 mmol/6h) for those w/ normal renal function: 
        ![](img/iv_phosphorus_therapy.png)
    - **Mx of chronic hypoPO4:**
        - <u>Secondary hypoparathyroidism</u> - vitamin D and Ca supplement generally suffice
        - <u>PTH/PTHrP independent PO4 wasting</u> - oral PO4 + vitamin D and Ca to bypass block in renal 1,25(OH)2D synthesis and prevent secondary hyperparathyroidism
        - <u>TPO</u> - extirpation of responsible tumours
