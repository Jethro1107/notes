# Anatomy of the nose and paranasal sinuses

- **Skeletal framework of the nose** - bony and cartilagenous structures forming the external nose:
    - Nasal bone and frontal process of maxilla
    - Cartilages - upper lateral cartilage, lower lateral cartilage, fibroaereolar tissue
- **Bony framework of the nasal septum:**
    - Anterior - quadrilateral septal cartilage
    - Posterior - superiorly formed by perpendicular plate of ethmoid, and inferiorly formed by Vomer
- **Features of lateral wall of the nasal septum** - turbinates, and openings of the paranasal sinuses (and other ducts):
    - **Turbinates** - superior, middle and inferior turbinates defining the superior, middle, and inferior meatus respectively
    - **Meatus** - spaces <u>beneath</u> respective turbinates for which paranasal sinuses drain into:
        - <u>Superior meatus</u> - drainage of **posterior ethmoid cells**
        - <u>Middle meatus</u> - drainage of **frontal sinuses** (nasofrontal duct), **maxillary sinuses** (maxillary ostium), and **anterior ethmoid cells**
        - <u>Inferior meatus</u> - opening of nasolacrimal duct
- **Arterial supply of the nasal cavity** - dual contribution from branches of the ICA and ECA:
    - **Internal carotid artery** - anterior and posterior ethmoid arteries (branches of opthalmic arteries)
    - **External carotid artery** - maxillary and sphenopalatine arteries
    - **Kisselbach's plexus** - anastamosis of all the arteries in the anterior part of the nasal septum (Common site for epistaxis)
- **Venous drainage of the nasal cavity:**
    - Opthalmic veins - drains into <u>carvenous sinus</u>
    - Facial veins
    - Pterygoid and pharyngeal plexuses

# Examination of the nose and paranasal sinuses

- **Examination of the nasal cavity:**
    - Thudichum's speculum - inspection of the nasal fossa
    - Rigid or flexible nasal endoscope - inspection of the nasal cavity and pharynx

# The nasal septum

## Deviated nasal septum

- **Definition** - deviation of the nasal septum readily which may occur naturally or arisse as a result of nasal trauma
- **Features on rhinoscopy:**
    - Devilation of nasal septum
    - Ipsilateral narrowing of airway
    - Contralateral inferior turbinate hypertrophy

# Epistaxis

- **Definition** - nose bleed
- **Most common site of epistaxis:**
    - Little's area of the anterior septum (Kisselbach's plexus) - causes anterior epistaxis
- **Etiology of epistaxis** - most commonly caused by 1) nose picking, 2) HTN, 3) anticoagulants:
    - **Local disease:**
        - <u>Traumatic</u> - nose picking, nasal trauma, nasal foreign bodies
        - <u>Mass lesion</u> - nasal polyps, juvenile angiofibroma, other nasal tumours, NPC
        - <u>Miscellaneous diseases</u> - infections, granulomatous disorders
    - **Systemic disease:**
        - <u>Drug-related</u> - anti-platelet therapy, anti-coagulant therapy
        - <u>Systemic vascular diseases</u> - HTN, Hereditary Haemorrhagic telangiectasia (Osler's disease)
        - <u>Haematological diseases</u> - leukaemia, haemophilia, Von Wilebrand's disease, coagulation disorders
- **Mx of epistaxis:**
    - <u>Self management</u> - compression of the anterior nasal septum to apply pressure on Kisselbalch's plexus (tilt forward to avoid aspiration)
    - <u>Mx of moderate bleeding</u> - anterior +/- posterior nasal packing with absorbable sponge, or balloon-infilated catheter
    - <u>Refractory bleeding</u> - endoscopy technique or arterial ligation:
        - **Endoscopic techniques** - cauterisation or clipping of bleeding arteries
        - **Arterial ligation** - internal maxillary artery in pterygopalatine fossa, anterior, and posterior ethmoid arteries or ECA

# Nasal polyps

- **Definition** - swelling of sinus mucosa of unknown origin, typically found at the ethmoid sinuses
- **Pathology and histopathology:**
    - **Typically found on ethmoid sinuses:**
        - <u>Ethmoid sinus polyps</u> - each individual ethmoid cell gives rise to a single polyp as its swollen mucosa prolapse and hangs down into the nasal cavity
        - <u>Other sites of nasal polyps</u> - e.g. antrochoanal polyp (single large polyp in the maxillary antrum)
    - **Histopathology** - water-logged stroma with inflammatory infiltrates and eosinophils
- **Clinical features of nasal polyps:**
    - <u>Nasal obstruction</u> - progressive worsening (typically bilateral) nasal congestion
    - <u>Watery rhinorhoea</u> - ?due to oedematous mucosa
    - <u>Anosmia</u>
    - <u>Recurrent chronic rhinosinusitis</u>
- **Dx evaluation of nasal polyps:**
    - <u>Anterior rhinoscopy</u> - limited utility in visualising polyps unless protruding polyps
    - <u>Flexible nasal endoscopy</u>:
        - **Endoscopic findings** - typically <u>bilateral</u>, pale semitransparent grey mass, mobile and insensitive to touch on palpation (cf hypertrophied turbinates which are pinkish, immobile and sensitive to touch)
        - **Role of Bx** - Bx performed if unilateral polyp to r/o tumours
    - <u>HRCT of anterior cranial fossa</u> (children) - r/o meningocoele and encephalocele in childrens w/ suspected polyps
- **Mx of nasal polyps:**
    - <u>Medical Tx</u> - systemic glucocorticoids for symptomatic relief
    - <u>Initial surgical Tx</u> - polyp removal by avulsion through nasal snare, or with powered nasal microresector endoscopy
    - <u>Tx of last resort</u> - ethmoidectomy (considered if multiple recurrence of rhinosinusitis)
