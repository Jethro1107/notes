# Ch1 Introduction

- **Reasons for phenomenology as centre-stage of psychiatry and psychopathology:**
    - <u>Phenomenological psychopathology as the common language</u> - allows use of the single disorder to account for forms and contents of the patient's subjective experience, to enable interdisciplinary communication
    - <u>Phenomenological psychopathology allows rigorous diagnosis</u> - where classification system fails to describe syndromes in detail, for the same symptomatology can be present in different disorders, they are posited that there are sub-classifications (i.e. pheno-phenotypes, or "phenomenological razor")
    - <u>Phenomenological psychopathology allows true understanding of the disturbed human experience</u> - enabling "giving the words" to patients, rather than "fishing for diagnostically-relevant answers"; allows better understanding to make sense of mental suffering, focusing on the complexity and diversity of patient's persspective, and the scruitiny of what is relevant from the patient's perspective
    - <u>Phenomenological psychopathology pinpoints specific abnormalities</u> - rather than defining abnormal as "social and occupational dysfunction", phenomenological psychopathology tries to describe a unifying process that attributes to human suffering and the emergence of the pathological phenomenon
    - <u>Phenomenological psychopathology improves care of psychiatric patients</u> - a quest for meaning allows for caring of troubled human existence
    - <u>Phenomenological psychopathology connects empathy with causal explanation</u> - a science of abnormal subjectivity forming explanations based on deductive and inductive methods
