## Categories of Pain

- **Origins of conscious pain** - subdivided into 3 broad types, namely 1) nociceptive, 2) neuropathic, and 3) psychogenic pain:
	- **Nociceptive pain** - pain arising from stimulation of superficial or deep tissue nociceptors from tissue injury or inflammation, requiring an intact nervous system
	- **Neuropathic pain** - pain caused or initiated by a primary lesion or dysfunction in the peripheral or central nervous system, where there is actual nerve cell or axonal damage due to inflammation, trauma, or degenerative disease
	- **Psychogenic pain** - pain arising in the absence of any discernible injury or threat and where the predominant etiology is psychological or psychiatric
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260428154515.png]]
- **Categories of pain:**
	- Acute pain
	- Chronic cancer/ palliative pain
	- Chronic non-cancer pain
- **Acute pain:**
	- _Definition_ - recent onset, and short duration, that usually has an obvious cause, and a predictable duration
	- _Specific problem areas of acute pain_ - although responsive to analgesia (esp. opiates), often suggestive of an underlying condition requiring Mx:
		- Acute back pain
		- Acute neck pain
		- Acute headacehe
		- Acute orofacial pain
		- Acute abdominal pain
		- Acute haemarthrosis
		- Acute chest pain
		- Sports injuries
		- Trauma
		- Burns
		- Neuropathic pain
	- _SIgnificance of early identification of acute pain_ - early and appropriate interventions have major impact on Tx and prevention of development of chronic pain via the chronic pain cycle (particularly musculoskeletal pain)
- **Chronic pain:**
	- _Definition_ - often considered as a separate disease state, can be defined as pain present for a period > 3 mo or pain present for > 4 weeks more than expected time of recovery
	- _Significance of chronic pain_:
		- Less responsive to acute pain relief incuding opioids
		- Often a/w biological, psychological and social contributions to pain behaviour thus requiring multidsciplinary approach

- **Pain thresholds may be influenced by psychosocial factors:**
	- _Decrease of pain thresholds_ - fatigue, anger, depression, loneliness, home or work environments
	- _Increase of pain thresholds_ - happy/ contented disposition, drive to return to work, empathy, companionship, sleep, rest, pleasant diversions, good home support
## Analgesic Prescribing

- **Prescribing of analgesics based on the nature of pain:**
	- _Nociceptive pain_ - traditional analgesics, e.g. paracetamol, aspirin and other NSAIDs, opioids
	- _Neurogenic pain_ - antidepressants, antiepileptics, membrane stabilisers
- **Medications used to treat pain:**
	- Simple analgesics (aspirin, paracetamol)
	- NSAIDs and COX-2 specific inhibitors
	- Opioids
	- Antidepressants (TCAs, SNRIs), and membrane stabilisers (anticonvulsants, antiarrhythmics)
	- NMDA blockers (ketamine)
	- Alpha-2 agonists (clonidine)
- **Aspects in Mx of chronic pain** (O' Reilly 1994):
	- Emphasise that cure is unlikely
	- Encourage the patient to accept responsibility for pain management
	- Provide ongoing support and interest
	- Promote activity rather than rest
	- Educate patients about their conditions (review X-rays and discuss drugs, particularly the long-term disadvantages of opioids and sedatives)
	- Encourage time-contingent versus pain contingent use of medication
	- Withdraw unsuitable drugs (e.g. opioids and sedatives)
	- Consider use of undiagnosed depression or anxiety as amplifiers of pain perception and treat when necessary
	- Promote the use of relaxation, distraction, and heat or ice packs rather than increased medications
	- Discuss strategy for management of flare ups
	- Refer for psychological support, particularly mindfulness, acceptance-commitment therapy (ACT) and/or cognitive behaviour therapy (CBT)
- **Paracetamol:**
	- _Indications_ - first-line analgesic for mild-to-moderate pain (due to more favourable safety profile than NSAIDs, although less effective)
	- _MOA_ - central COX-1-3 inhibition
	- _Clinical effects of paracetamol_:
		- Moderate analgesic effects (equipotent to aspirin)
		- Antipyretic effects
		- Minimal anti-inflammatory effects
	- _Pharmacokinetics_ - hepatic metabolism and renal excretion (T1/2 ~ 4h)
	- _Formulations of paracetamol_:
		- IR tablets and capsules
		- Oral solutions and suspensions
		- Chewable tablts
		- Souble or effervescent tablets
		- Extended-action formulation 665 mg (up to 8h of pain relief)
		- Suppositories (125 mg, 250 mg, 500 mg forms)
		- Injectable formulations
	- _Dosing_ - 1g PO q4h (max 4g/d)
	- _Caution_ - dose reduction for:
		- Chronic kidney disease
		- Chronic liver disease
	- _S/E_:
		- Gastrointestinal discomfort (e.g. Nausea, dyspepsi) but well tolerated for patients w/ peptic ulcer disease
		- Hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity (only if exceeding maximal doses or w/ hepatic or renal dysfunction)
		- No effects on platelet function
- **Aspirin:**
	- _Indications_ - mild-to-moderate acute pain
	- _MOA_ - NSAID w/ central and peripheral COX inhibition
	- _Pharmacokinetics_:
		- Extremely short half life
		- However metabolised into salicylate acid w/ similar pharmacodynamic properties
	- _Clinical effects of aspirin_:
		- Analgesic effect
		- Anti-inflammatory effect
	- _Dosing_ - 600 mg PO q4h (max 4g/d)
	- _S/E_:
		- GI discomfort and ulceration
		- Increased bleeding risk

## Prescribing NSAIDs

- **Problems w/ NSAID prescription:**
	- Population ageing results in increasing propensity to the pain of arthritic conditions and demands for relief
	- High incidence of side effects, ranging from the trivial to the lethal, w/ many deaths from bleeding ulcers esp. in the elderly
	- Increasing use in conditions where inflammation is minimal where as the main cause is mechanical (e.g. mechanical LBP), yet patient appears to achieve good response to NSAIDs
- **Comparisons between different NSAIDs** - controversial often w/ minimal evidence to back up claims:
	- _NSAIDs of choice in gout_ - indomethacin
	- _NSAIDs of choice in spondyloarthropathies_ - indomethacin, phenylbutazone
	- _Selection of NSAIDs based on half-life_ - shorter half-life a/w less S/E and preferred in elderly and acute pain; longer T1/2 preserved in chronic (cancer) pain:
		- Short T1/2 - aspirin, diclofenac (volteran), tiaprofenic acid, ketoprofen, ibuprofen, indomethecin
		- Long T1/2 - diflunisal, naproxen, sulindac, piroxicam, tenoxicam

## Neuropathic Pain

- **Definition** - pain a/w injury, disease, or surgical section of the peripheral or central nervous system
- **Epidemiology** - common (~ 1%) although under-recognised condition
- **Clinical features of neuropathic pain:**
	- _Site_ - abnormal site characterised by:
		- Absence of ongoing tissue damage
		- An area of sensory loss (anasthesia dolorosa)
	- _Onset_ - chronic, +/- delayed in onset of pain after nerve injury
	- _Character_ - variable:
		- Burning pain
		- Shooting pain
		- Electric pain
		- Stabbing pain
	- _Radiation_ - radiating electric shock-like pains in nerve distribution (i.e. Tinel's sign)
	- _Associated symptoms_:
		- Hyperalgesia - increased pain w/ minimal pain stimulation
		- Allodynia - pain on light touch
		- Dysaesthesia - e.g. ants crawling on skin
		- Regional autonomic instability (reflex sympathetic dystrophy/ CRPS) - pallor or cyanosis, excessive warmth or cold, increased or decreased sweating
- **Neuropathic pain states:**
	- Post-herpetic neuralgia
	- Spinal cord injury pain
	- Plexus tumour infiltration (e.g. pancoust tumour)
	- Diabetic/ alcoholic peripheral neuropathy
	- Post-stroke pain
	- Arachnoiditis
	- Trigeminal neuralgia
	- Atypical facial pain
	- Phantom limb pain
	- Complex regional pain syndrome I (refelx sympathetic dystrophy)
	- Complex regional pain syndrome II (causalgia)
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Initial pain relief_ - traditional analgesics such as aspirin, paracetamol or an NSAID
		- _Analgesic adjuvants for neuropathic pain_ - tricyclic antidepressants, anti-epileptics
	- **Approach to analgesic adjuvants:**
		- _Dosing_ - started in low dose and increased as necessary to maximum or maximally tolerated dose
		- _Other clinical considerations_:
			- Nature of pain - TCAs for burning pain, and anti-epileptics for sharp shooting pain
			- Underlying condition - carbamazepine preferred for trigeminal neuralgia, duloxetine considered for diabetic neuropathy, gabapentin has evidence for post-herpatic neuralgia and diabetic neuropathy
	- **Dosing of analgesic adjuvants:**
		- _Amitriptyline_ - 10-25 mg PO nocte increase q7d to max 75-100 mg
		- _Duloxetine_ - 30 mg PO od (max 60 mg/d)
		- _Carbamazepine_ - 50-100 mg PO bd (max 400 mg bd max)
		- _Gabapentin_ - 100-300 mg PO od increasing as tolerated to tds (max 2400 mg/d dose reduction in CKD)
		- _Pregabalin_ - 75 mg PO od (max 300 mg bd)
		- _Valproate_ - 200-600 mg PO bd
