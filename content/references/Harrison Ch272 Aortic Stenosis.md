# Ch272 Aortic Stenosis

## Global Burden of Valvular Heart Disease

## Aortic Stenosis

- **Definition** -
- **Epidemiology** - most common symptomatic chronic valvular heart disease:
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - increases incidence w/ age (\> 30% of those \>65y generally have aortic sclerosis but progression to valvular stenosis is variable)
        - Sex - male preponderance
- **Process of valvular aortic valve deterioration and calfication:**
    - Likely overlapping features with vascular atherosclerosis, such that most patients also have comorbid coronary artery disease
    - Processes include endothelial dysfunction, lipid accumulation, inflammatory cell activation, cytokine release, and upregulation of several signaggling pathways
    - Fibrocalcific response ensues where myofibroblasts differentiate into phenotypically osteoblasts and actively produce bone matrix proteins that allow for deposition of calcium hydroxyapatite crystals
- **Risk factors of aortic stenosis:**
    - **Non-modifiable risk factors:**
        - <u>Congenitally diseased valve</u> - BAV, unicuspid aortic valve, congenital aortic stenosis
        - <u>Genetic risk factors</u> - strong familial clustering w/ trileaflet valvular diseases, w/ linnks to genetic polymorphisms involving vitamin D receptor, estrogen receptor in post-menopausal women, ApoE4, IL10 etc.
        - <u>Acquired diseased valves</u> - prior rheumatic inflammation (invariable a/w MS and mixed AR), pprior radiation
    - **Modifiable risk factors** - akin to <u>traditional atherosclerotic risk factors</u>:
        - HTN
        - HL (high LDL-C)
        - DM
        - Metabolic syndrome
        - Smoking
        - CKD
        - Lp(a)
- **Bicuspid aortic valve disease** - most common congenital heart valve defect:
    - <u>Epidemiology</u>:
        - 0.5-1.4% births (up to 10% in first-degree relatives of affected individuals)
        - Male preponderance (M:F = 2-4:1)
    - <u>Inheritance</u> - autosomal dominant w/ incomplete penetrance:
        - Single gene deficits has not been identified although mutations in NOTCH1, GATA5, GATA4, NKX2.5 have been atributed
- **Forms of LVOT obstruction** - usually differentiated on basis of P/E and doppler echocardiographic findings:
    - Valvular AS
    - Supravalvular AS
    - Descrete fibromuscular/ membranous subaortic stenosis
    - Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy
- **Pathophysiology of valvular AS:**
- **Clinical features of AS** - rarely asymptomatic until valve oriface has narrowed to ~ 1cm2, generally presenting w/ gradual curtailment of activities and reduced effort tolerance:
    - <u>Dyspnoea</u> - primarily results from elevation of pulmonary capillary pressure caused by elevation of LV diastolic pressures secondary to impaired relaxation and reduced LV compliance
- **Indications of valvular replacement:**
    - Symptomatic AS
    - Reduced LVEF
