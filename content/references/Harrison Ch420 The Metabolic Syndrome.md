# Ch 420 The Metabolic Syndrome

- **Definition** - also known as syndrome X, or insulin resistance syndrome, consists of a constellation of metabolic abnormalities that confer increased risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) and diabetes mellitus
- **Major features of metabolic syndrome:**
	- Central obesity
	- Hypertriglyceridaemia
	- Low levels of HDL-C
	- Hyperglycaemia
	- Hypertension

- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - increasing prevalence w/ greater global industrialisation, in part due to population ageing and increasing prevalence of obesity
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age - principally increases prevalence w/ age (NHANES study: 18.5% among 20-39y; 48.6% among > 40y)
		- Geographic variations - prevalence of metabolic syndrome varies around the world in part reflecting age and ethnicity, and the diagnostic criteria applied:

- **Waist circumference as a proxy of visceral adipose tissue:**
	- It is known that visceral adipose tissue is most strongly related to insulin resistance, and thus the risk of T2DM and associated CVD, however measurement is not readily available
	- Waist circumference serves as a proxy of visceral adipose tissue, but is limited because for any given waist circumference, distribution in adipose tissue between SC and visceral deposits varies substantially
	- The variation likely accounts for differences in risk of CVD and T2DM in different ethicity, which is accounted for different cut-offs for waist circumference in the Harmonizing definition for metabolic syndrome
## Risk Factors

- **Obesity and central adiposity** - increased central adipposity is the driving pathomechanism for metabolic syndrome:
	- _Obesity_ - increased risk of metabolic syndrome due to increasing adiposity
	- _Central adiposity_ - patients who are of normal weight may become insulin resistant, where this phenotype is particularly evident for populations in India, SE Asia, and Central America
- **Sedentary lifestyle** - overall physical inactivity and reduced cardiopulmonary resreves are predictors of CVD events and related mortality:
	- Many components of metabolic sydnrome (central obesity, TG, low HDL, BP, FBG) are a/w sedentary lifestyle in a genetically susceptible persons
	- THose who watch TV/ videos or use computers > 4 h/d have 2x increased risk of metabolic syndrome compared to those using < 1h/d
- **Genetics** - estimated heritability of each metabolic traits > 50%:
	- No single gene explains complex phenotype of metabolic syndrome
	- GWAS suggests a polygenic disorder w/ various candidate genes relating to body weight regulation, insulin resistance, and lipid/ lipoprotein metabolism, although functions of many identified loci remain unknown
- **Ageing** - age-dependency of the syndrome's prevalence underlies ageing as a potential risk factor of metabolic syndrome
- **Diabetes mellitus** - complex relationship because metabolic syndrome was initially described as a condition that increases risk of T2DM:
	- DM is now included in NCEP and the Harmonizing Definitions of metabolic syndrome, but the greatest value is predicting T2DM
	- However, most patients w/ T2DM (75%) have met the criteria of metabolic syndrome
	- In patients w/ T2DM, those w/ metabolic syndrome is a/w higher prevalence of CVD compared to those w/o metabolic syndrome
- **Cardiovascular disease** - similar concept as in DM, where metabolic yndrome was initially described as a condition that increases risk of CVD
- **Lipodystrophy** - various genetic or acquired lipodystrophy may give rise to severe insulin resistance and the components of metabolic syndrome
