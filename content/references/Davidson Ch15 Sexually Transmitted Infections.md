### Syphilis

- **Definition** - sexually transmitted infection through skin or mucous membranes, with the spirochaete Treponema pallidum
- **Transmission of syphilis:**
    - Sexual transmission (through abraisions in skin or mucous membranes)
    - Percutaneous injury
    - Blood transmission
    - Kissing
    - Transplacenteal infections
- **Classification of syphilis:** 
![](img/classification_of_syphilis.png)
- **Natural Hx and clinical manifestations of acquired syphilis** - characterised by latent phases in between:
    - **Primary syphilis** (incubation period of 14-28d) - characterised by painless chancre and inguinal lymphadenopathy which spontaneously resolves within 2-6 weeks:
        - <u>Chancre</u> - painless genital ulcers (10% extragenital), presenting as a dull red macule, that becomes papular and erodes to form an indurated ulcer, subsequently resolving to form a thin atrophic scar
        - <u>Inguinal lymphadenopathy</u> - enlarged, mobile discrete and rubbery LN, and are non-tender
    - **Secondary syphilis** (6-8 weeks after primary infection) - multisystemic infaction caused by disseminated treponema pallidum, particularly under the skin, and will relapse if Tx not administered:
        - **Systemic upset** - mild fever, malaise, headache are common
        - **Rash** - initially macular, but evolves into maculopapular form or papular form, w/ associated scaling, and changes into 'gun-metal' grey as they resolve (lasts up to 12 weeks if not treated):
            - <u>Site</u> - general, symmetrical
            - <u>Onset</u> - 6-8 weeks after development of chancre w/ initial presentation w/ rash over trunk and limbs
            - <u>Progression</u>:
                - Initially over trunk and limbs, subsequently spreads to palm and sole
                - Scaling of papules occurs subsequently and characteristically involves palms, soles and face
                - Condyloma lata (papules coalescing to plaques) over warm moist areas (e.g. vulva or perianal region)
                - Other morphological changes as described above
            - <u>Quality</u> - non-irritable and non-pruritic
        - **Generalised lymphadenopathy** (\> 50%) - generalised, non-tender, discrete LNs
        - **Other manifestations:**
            - <u>CNS manifestations</u> (common in HIV +ve patients) - meningitis, cranial nerve palsies
            - <u>Occular manifestations</u> - anterior and posterior uveitis
            - <u>Visceral manifestations</u> - hepatitis, gastritis, and **glomerulonephritis**
    - **Benign tertiary syphilis** (3-10y after primary infection) - characterised by chronic granulomatous lesion (<u>gumma</u>) over affected skin, mucous membranes, bones, muscles or viscera:
        - <u>Skin manifestations</u> - cutaneous nodules, ulcers, or subcutaneous ulcers w/ gummy discharge (healing a/w charateristic tissue paper scars)
        - <u>Mucosal manifestations</u> - occurs in upper aerodigestive tract manifesting as punched-out ulcers; particularly gummatous involvement of tongue predisposes to leukoplakia and thus oral cavity malignancy
        - <u>Other visceral involvement</u> - bones, brain, spinal cord, liver, testis etc.
    - **Cardiovascular syphilis** - causes <u>aortitis w/ predominant involvement of ascending aorta and coronary ostia</u> manifesting as 1) aortic regurgitation, 2) angina, and 3) ascending aortic aneurysm
    - **Neurosyphilis:** 
    ![](img/clinico_pathological_features_of_symptomatic_neurosyphilis.png)
- **Clinical manifestations of congenital syphilis** - variety of pregnancy outcomes after 4 mo of gestation, when fetus becomes immunocompetent:
    - Miscarriage or still birth
    - Death at term birth of syphilitic baby
    - Birth of baby who develop signs of early congenital syphilis in first few weeks of life
    - Birth of baby w/ latent infections who develops stigmata of congenital syphilis later in life

- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>ABx therapy</u> - dosing and regimen dependent on stage of natural Hx, pregnancy status, and penicillin allergy
        - <u>Contact tracing</u> - partner notification is of upmost importance
        - <u>Monitoring</u> - ensure cure by declining titres of non-treponeal tests to u.d. levels
    - **ABx therapy:**
        - <u>Selection</u>:
            - Benzathine penicillin
            - Doxycycline (for those w/ penicillin allergy but C/I during pregnancy)
            - Azithromycin
        - <u>Regimen</u>:
            - Early syphilis (\< 2y) - 2.4 MU IM benzathine penicillin
            - Late syphilis - 3 doses of 2.4 MU IM benzathine penicillin at weakly intervals
            - Pregnancy - benicillin preferred over other drugs due to good placenteal penetrance
        - <u>Tx reactions</u>:
            - Anaphylaxis - penicillin is a common cause and on-site resuscitation should be available for Mx
            - Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction (more common in early syphilis) - acute febrile illness characterised bby headache, malaise and myalgia that typically resolve within 24h
            - Procaine infection - fear of impending death after accidental IV injection of procain penicillin a/w hallucination and fits
