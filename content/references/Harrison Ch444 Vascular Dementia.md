# Ch444 Vascular Dementia

- **Definition and terminology:**
	- _Vascular dementia_ - traditionally used to decribe subset of dementia cases due primarily to >= 1 symptomatic strokes, now expanded to encompass full impact of cerebrovascular diseases on age-related cognitive decline
	- _Vascular contributions to cognitive impairment and dementia_ (VCID) - descriptions of pathological changes of cerebral vasculature contributing to cognitive impairment, in-isolation, or in conjunction to other neurodegenerative processes ('mixed dementia')
- **Correlation between cerebrovascular disease and cognitive impairments** - symptomatic stroke and asymptomatic vascular lesions both contribute to cognitive impairments:
	- _Sympptomatic stroke_ - at least 50% of stroke survivors exhibit some form of cognitive impairment, which progressively increases w/ longer periods of follow-up
	- _Asymptomatic vascular lesions_ - population-based studies demonstrate substantially increased risk of cognitive impairment among individuals w/o symptomatic stroke but MRI evidence of cerebrovascular diseases, highlightinh cumulative impact of silent small vessel brain disease
	- _Post-mortem studies_ - large community-based samples demonstrate independent contributions of both gross neuropathology and pathological markers of cerebrovascular diseases:
		- Grossly visible infarcts post-mortem known to a/e premortem cognitive decline
		- Various pathological markers such as atherosclerosis, arteriosclerosis, and cerebral amyloid angiopathy accounts for premortem cognitive decline
	- _Epidemiological studies:_
		- Framingham study reported declining 5y age- and sex-adjusted HR for dementia suggesting potential public impact of improving vascular health
		- SPRINT-MIND trial targets supports BP control results in reduced rates of mild cognitive impairments, and combined mild cognitive impairments and probalble dementia

## Subtypes of Cerebrovascular Disease associated with VCID

- **Large cerebral strokes:**
	- _Strategically-placed infarcts_ - rare individual infarcts in specific areas such as thalamus, medial temporal cortex, anterior corpus callosum, or dominant side angular gyrus can sufficiently impair memory to meet memory-based criteria for dementia
	- _Other stroke territories_ - may affect other aspects of cognition such as processing speed, executive function, and visuospatial performances
- **Cerebral small-vessel disease** - two pathogenic mechanisms:
	- **Arteriosclerosis:**
		- _Definition_ - thickening of cerebral arterioles due to infiltration of plasma proteins into vessel walls
		- _Risk factors of arteriosclerosis_ - age, hypertension, diabetes mellitus
		- _Sites of arteriosclerosis_ - typically deep penetrating arteries such as the thalamus, basal ganglia or brainstem
		- _Clinical manifestations_:
			- Accounts for symptomatic ischaemic/ haemorrhagic stroke (e.g. lacunar infarcts, microinfarcts, microbleeds)
			- May attribute to cognitive decline
	- **Cerebral amyloid angiopathy:**
		- _Definition_ - deposition of beta-amyloid peptides in the walls of small cerebral arteries, arterioles, and capillaries w/ consequent loss of normal wall structure
		- _Risk factor_ - primary risk factor being increasing age
		- _Site of cerebral amyloid angiopathy_ - superficial lobar structures such as cerebral cortex, subcortical white matter, cerebral convexity subarachnoid space
		- _Clinical mannifestations_ - symptomatic intracerebral haemorrhage (e.g. lobar haemorrhage)
- **MRI markers of cerebral small-vessel diseases:**
	- **Lacunar infarcts:**
		- _Significance_ - common features of arteriosclerosis and less commonly of cerebral amyloid angiopathy:
		- _MRI findings_ - hyperintense rim surrounding hypointense cavitated core w/ diameter typically 3-15mm on **FLAIR sequences**
	- **Microinfarcts:**
		- _Significance_ - characteristic of both small vessel disease and are generally more wide spread
		- _MRI findings_:
			- Acute microinfarcts - punctate hyperintensities in DWI
			- Chronic microinfarcts - hyperintense lesions on T2-weighted MRI sequences
	- **Cerebral microbleeds:**
		- _Significance_ - readily detectable due to paramagnetic effects of iron and suggests both small vessel diseases depending on area
		- _MRI findings_:
			- Round hypointensities on T2-weighted MRI
			- Area reflects underlying pathogenic mechanisms (arteriosclerosis for deep penetrating regions, and amyloid angiopathy for lobar regions)
	- **White matter disease:**
		- _Interpretation_ - non-specific marker of small-vessel disease ubiquitous w/ ageing:
			- White matter gliosis
			- Demyelination
			- Increased water content
		- _MRI findings_:
			- Hyperintensities on T2-weighted imaging
			- Hyperintensities on FLAIR sequences
			- Advanced MRI methods such as diffusion tensor MRI
		- _Significance of diffuse white matter injury_ - Binswanger's disease or subcortical arteriosclerotic encephalopathy:
			- Clinical syndrome w/ gradual cognitive decline and notable white matter changes
			- Correlates with a progressive confluent subcortical and periventricular white matter disease seen w/ hypoperfusion and hypometabolism
- **Role of accompanying brain pathologies** - vascular dementia usually occurs in combination w/ other neurodegenerative brain diseases (most commonly Alzheimer's disease):
	- Interactions between cerebrovascular and neurodegenerative processes may contribute to dementia that is more than expected from effects of either diseases alone
	- Loss of BBB integrity allows brain penetration of neurotoxics and inflammatory agents and impaired clearance of beta-amyloid
## Treatment of Vascular Dementia

- **Principles of Mx:**
	- _Primary stroke prevention_ - e.g. BP control (target < 130/80 mmHg for most w/ estimated 10y cardiovascular risk), lipid lowering, DM control, exercise, diet, smoking cessation etc.
	- _Secondary stroke prevention_ - no evidence for aggressive lipid lowering and aspirin but are reasonable to be considered
	- _Screening for causes of stroke_ - e.g. AF, vascular imaging
	- _Symptomatic medications for cognitive impairment_ - cholinesterase inhibitors +/- NMDA reasonable in VCID
