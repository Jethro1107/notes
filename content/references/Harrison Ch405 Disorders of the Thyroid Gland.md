## Thyroiditis

- **Definition** - focal inflammation of the thyroid gland characterised by (usually painful) swelling of the thyroid gland
- **DDx of thyroiditis** - classification based on onset and duration of disease:
    - <u>Acute thyroiditis</u> - suppurative thyroiditis, radiation thyroiditis
    - <u>Subacute thyrotditis</u> - Viral thyroiditis, silent thyroiditis, TB thyroiditis, amiodarone-related thyroiditis
    - <u>Chronic thyroiditis</u> - Riedel's thyroiditis, autoimmune thyroiditis (e.g. Hashimoto's thyroiditis)

### Acute Thyroiditis

- **Definition** - inflammation of thyroid caused by suppurative infection of the thyroid
- **Epidemiology** - rare disorder in children and young adults caused by anatomical anomalies
- **Etiology of suppurative thyroiditis:**
    - <u>Patent piriform sinus</u> (usu. left sided) - remnant of 4th branchial pouch connecting oral pharynx w/ thyroid
    - <u>Degeneration of long-standing goitre/ malignancy</u> - theoretically possible in the elderly
- **Microbiological agents of suppurative thyroiditis** - fungal (including Pneumocystis), mycobacterial infections can occur in an <u>immunocomprimised host</u>:
    - <u>Bacterial infection</u> - esp. Staphylococcus, Streptococcus, Enterobacter
    - <u>Mycobacterial infections</u> - TB
    - <u>Fungal infection</u> - Aspergillus, Candida, Coccidiodes, Histoplasma, Pneumocystis
- **Clinical features of suppurative thyroiditis** - abrupt onset of Sx:
    - <u>Fever</u> - febrile illness w/ marked systemic complaints (e.g. lymphadenopathy) common
    - <u>Neck pain</u> - pain over the thyroid referring to the throat (may manifest as **odynophagia**) or ears exacerbated by swallowing, with erythema and tenderness over the thyroid gland
    - <u>Small goitre</u> - goitre typically small in size and do not cause compressive Sx
- **DDx of suppurative thyroiditis:**
    - <u>Complication of thyroid nodule</u> - e.g. rupture/ haemorrhage into thyroid cyst
    - <u>Other forms of thyroiditis</u> - sub-acute thyroiditis, amiodarone-related thyroiditis, other drug-induced thyroiditis, and rarely chronic thyroiditis
    - <u>Malignancies</u> - e.g. lymphoma
- **Signs:**
    - <u>General examination</u> - pyrexia, systemically unwell
    - <u>Neck examination</u>:
        - Erythema over thyroid gland on inspection
        - Tenderness on palpation
        - Minimal goitre
        - Lymphadenopathy
    - <u>Assessment of thyroid status</u> - clinically euthyroid
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, TFT, ESR:
        - <u>CBC</u> - marked leukocytosis
        - <u>TFT</u> - euthyroid
        - <u>ESR</u> - markedly elevated
    - **FNA biopsy** (CT or USG-guided) - shows:
        - <u>Neutrophilic inflammation</u> - infiltration by PMN leukocytes
        - <u>+ve Findings on microbiology</u> - etiology identified by G stain and culture
    - **Imaging** - CT/ USG to guide FNA Bx or surgical drainage of abscesses
- **Complications of suppurative thyroiditis:**
    - **Spread of infection:**
        - <u>Direct spread of infection</u> - retropharyngeal abscess, mediastinitis, jugular vein thrombosis
        - <u>Haematogenous spread of infection</u> - septicaemia
    - **Upper airway obstruction** - direct tracheal obstruction
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Empirical IV Abx</u> - initially guided by gram stain, and subsequently by Sn testing
        - <u>Surgical drainage</u> - may be required if abscess evident

### Subacute Thyroiditis

- **Definition** - also termed de Quervain's thyroiditis, granulomatous thyroiditis, or viral thyroiditis, characterised by viral infections and chronic granulomatous inflammation of the thyroid gland
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - often overlooked as Sx can mimic pharyngitis
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - peak incidence at 30-50y
        - Sex - female preponderance (F:M = 3:1)
- **Pathophysiology of subacute thyroiditis** - viral infection w/ patchy inflammatory infiltration leading to <u>disruption of thyroid follicles</u>, progresses to granuloma and subsequent fibrosis:
    - <u>Initial thyrotoxic phase</u> (several weeks) - follicular destruction resulting in release of TG and T3/4, and subsequent suppression of TSH
    - <u>Subsequent hypothyroid phase</u> (several months) - following depletion of thyroid hormone stores resulting in moderately elevated TSH
    - <u>Euthyroid phase</u> - marks residual inflammation as disease subsides (permanent hypothyroidism in 15% cases)

  
  ![](img/clinical_course_of_subacute_thyroiditis.png)
- **Clinical features of subacute thyroiditis:**
    - <u>Prodromal Sx</u> - malaise and URTI may precede thyroid-related features by up to several weeks
    - <u>Painful goitre</u> - exquisitely tender thyroid gland although goitre is small, often referred to the jaw or ear
    - <u>Altered thyroid status</u> - patient may present in thyrotoxic or hypothyroid phase
- **Signs:**
    - <u>General examination</u> - pyrexia, systemically unwell
    - <u>Neck examination</u>:
        - Erythema over thyroid gland on inspection
        - Tenderness on palpation
        - Minimal goitre
        - Lymphadenopathy
    - <u>Assessment of thyroid status</u> - variable dependent on stage of presentation
- **Ix:**
    - <u>TFT</u> - vaiable dependent on stage of presentation (however T4/T3 ratio are much greater, w/ disproportionate increase in T3)
    - <u>ESR</u> - high
    - <u>Radioactive iodine scan</u> - low uptake of radiodine
    - <u>FNA Bx</u> - if doubtful to r/o bleeding into a cyst or neoplasm
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Suppression of inflammation</u> - typically responds to high dose ASA, NSAIDs, and ocassionally glucocorticoids
        - <u>Routine monitoring</u> - routine TFT every 2-4 weeks
        - <u>Mx of Sx of thyrotoxicosis or hypothyroidism</u>:
            - Thyrotoxicosis - Sx ameliorated by use of beta-blockers
            - Levothyroxine - at low doses to abort Sx related to hypothyroidism
    - **Suppression of inflammation** - approach includes:
        - <u>High-dose aspirin</u> - 600 mg q4-6h
        - <u>NSAIDs</u>
        - <u>Glucocorticoids</u> - 40-60 mg tapered over 6-8 weeks
    - **Levothyroxine replacement:**
        - <u>Indication</u> - if prolonged hypothyroidism
        - <u>Dosing</u> - low doses (50-100 mcg/d) to allow for TSH-mediated recovery
