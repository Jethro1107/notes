# Ch445 Dementia with Lewy Bodies

- **Definition and terminology:**
	- _Lewy body disease_ (LBD) - spectrum of neurodegenerative disorder characterised by alpha-synuclein accumulation and Lewy body formation, where PD, PDD, and DLB are clinical manifestations of the LBD pathology differentiated by time interval between parkinsonism, and dementia
	- _Parkinson disease dementia_ (PDD) - clinical presentation of dementia in patients w/ long-standing PD, often w/ VH, fluctuating attention or alertness, and RBD
	- _Dementia with lewy bodies_ (DLB) - clinical presentation of dementia and neuropsychiatric symptoms preceding or co-emerging w/ parkisonism
- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - second most common cause of neurodegenerative dementia after Alzheimer's disease
	- _Incidence_ - DLB occurs at rate of 7/100000/y; where approximately 10% of patients w/ PD develop PDD per year
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age - prevalence increases w/ age
		- Sex - male preponderence for both PDD and DLB

## Clinical Manifestations

- **Prodromal phase** - both DLB and PDD have a prodromal phase where patients have MCI w/ cognitive deficits that do not have substantial impact on daily life:
	- _PD-MCI_ - primarily characterised by new onset of cognitive impairments in therms of executive, attention, and visuospatial disturbances, and rarely memory
	- _MCI-DLB_ - may be a/w onset of cognitive impairments, as well as neuropsychiatric manifestations (VH), sleep disturbances, fluctuating attention, or parkisonism
	- _MCI-LB_ - new terminology where major features are RBD and parkisonism where it is charlenging to differentiate between MCI-DLB, or PD-MCI
- **Cognitive impairments** - various cognitive manifestations that affects performance of ADLs beyond the debilitating parkisonism:
	- _Executive dysfunction_ - e.g. difficulties in planning, problem solving, performing complex tasks
	- _Complex attention_ - DLB characterised by **inattention and poor concentration**, which charateristically fluctuates within the day
	- _Visuospatial deficits_ - difficulties w/ spatial orientation and navigation
	- _Preserved episodic memory_ - episodic memory is typically **preserved**, but ocassionally may manifest atypically w/ amnesic-MCI
- **Neuropsychiatric manifestations:**
	- _Hallucinations_ - often **well-formed**, (?relatively benign) visual hallucinations, but may be preceeded by a sense of presence behind the person
	- _Delusions_ (uncommon) - usually related to **misidentification** (Capgras syndrome), **morbid jealousy** (infidelity), or persecutory delusions (e.g. theft)
	- _REM sleep behavioural disorders_ (often prodromal) - characterised by nigthmares and **dream enactment**, often violent resulting in injuries to themselves or their bed partners
- **Fluctuating cognition** - fluctuating attention and concentration are other characteristic features:
	- _Marked degree of fluctuations_ - often more marked than other forms of dementia, w/ periods of confusion or severe lethargy leading to **delirious states**, but **rapidly resolving**
	- _Precipitating factors_ - thought as a susceptibility to develop delirious states:
		- Infections - e.g. chest infections, UTIs
		- Medications - esp. sensitivity to psychoative medications
		- Metabolic disturbances - e.g. hypoNa
- **Parkisonism** - key differentiating feature between PDD and DLB is the time interval betweeen onset of parkisonism and dementia:
	- _PDD_ - often cognitive impairments along w/ other manifestations on a long-standing background of PD (PD takes a typical clinical course)
	- _DLB_ - cognitive impairments preceeds or co-occurs w/ parkisonism (PD often develops rapidly w/ early postural instability)
- **Autonomic disturbances** - results in orthostatic hypotension, erectile dysfunction, and constipation, and may present early in DLB

## Pathology and Pathogenesis

- **Pathology of LBD** - synucleinopathies:
	- **Lewy pathologies** - presence of lewy bodies, and lewy nitrites throughout the brainstem, substantia nigra, amygldyla, cingylate gyrus, and the neo-cortex:
		- _Lewy bodies_ - intraneuronal cytoplasmic inclusions that stain w/ PAS, ubiquitin, and Ab against alpha-synuclein, composed of straight neurofilaments w/ amorphous material containing epitopes for Ab against proteins such as ubiquitin and alpha-synuclein
		- _Lewy nitrites_ - neurofilaments within axons and dendrites w/ similar constituents as lewy bodies
	- **Stage of progression of LBD:**
		- _Initiation_ - LB pathology is initiated in the olfactory bulb or independently in the enteric nervous system w/ spread via the Vagus nerve
		- _Brainstem predominant_ - involvement of various brainstem nuclei, particularly the substantia niagra underlying the manifestations of parkisonism +/- RBD, fluctuating cognition, autonomic features (e.g. constipation)
		- _Transitional limbic_ - spread to amygdala, cigulate gyrus
		- _Diffuse neocortical_ - underlies major cognitive and neuropsychiatric manifestations
	- **Mechanism of spread of alpha-synuclein** - prion-like mechanism:
		- Abnormally folded alpha-synuclein aggregates progate transeuronally following connection pathways
		- Hence proceeding from periphery, towards the brainstem, limbic system, and subsequently the neo-cortex
- **Neurochemistry in LBD:**
	- _Cholinergic deficits_ (basal forebrain and pedunculopontine nucleus involvement) - underlies characteristic **fluctuations**, **inattention**, and **visual hallucinations**
	- _Adrenergic deficits_ (locus cereus) - further impairs arousal and alertness
- **Pathogenesis of LBD:**
	- _Genetic predisposition_:
		- Predisposition to LBD - alpha-synuclein duplications and triplications predispose to clinical manifestations of PD or DLB
		- Predisposition to PDD or DLB manifestations - mutations of glucocerebrosidiase (GBA) particularly predicts progression to cognitive impairments
	- _Environmental factors_ - initiating pathology occuring in GI or olfactory area suggests environmental toxins acting on a genetic background ("double-hit hypothesis"), but no environmental exposures implicated from epidemiological studies
## Laboratory Features

- **Approach to Ix:**
	- r/o treatable causes of dementia/ delirium
	- r/o other mimics such as vascular parkisonism, multiple system atrophy, or subdural haematoma
	- Biomarkers for diagnosis of LBD
- **Biomarkers for diagnosis of LBD:**
	- Polysomnography - demonstration of RBD w/o atonia
	- LP and CSF analysis - detection of aSyn by seed amplification assay (SAAs)
	- Skin biopsy - demonstration of skin deposition of alpha-synuclein
	- MIBG cardiac scintigraphy - demonstrating cardiac post-ganglionic denervation
	- SPECT/ PET - dopamine transporter imaging
## Treatment of DLB

- **Principles of Mx:**
	- _Disease-modifying therapy_ - currently no disease modifying agents (Neflamapimod showing promising results in initial phase 2a trial)
	- _Symptomatic Mx_:
		- Cognitive impairments - cholinesterase inhibitors (e.g. rivastigmine, donepezil)
		- Psychotic features - pimavanserin, low-dose clozapine
		- RBD - melatonin typically used but other psychoactive medications used in caution due to worsening cognition and fall risk
		- Mood and anxiety Sx - antidepressants w/ strong anxiolytic properties (e.g. escitalopram, paroxetine, duloxetine, venlafaxine)
		- Orthostatic hypotension - non-pharmacological Mx (e.g. high salt and fluid diet) or pharmacological Tx (fludrocortisone, midodrine)
	- _Mx of carer burden_ - evaluation of carer burden and multidsciplinary Mx
