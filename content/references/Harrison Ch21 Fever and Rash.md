# Ch21 Fever and Rash

- **Classification of rashes associated w/ fever** (excluding localised skin and soft tissue infection) - based based on 1) lesion morphology and 2) distribution:
	- Centrally distributed maculopapular rash
	- Peripheral eruptions
	- Confluent desquamative erythemas
	- Vesiculobullous or pustular eruptions
	- Nodular eruptions
	- Purpuric eruptions
	- Eruptions with ulcers and/or eschars
- **Limitations of classification of rashes associated w/ fever:**
	- Based on most typical disease presentation w/o accounting for atypical presentation (at the end of the extreme, no rash may develop at all)
	- Morphology and distribution of rash may evolve w/ disease

- **DDx of centrally distributed maculopapular rash a/w fever:**
	- **Infectious diseases:**
		- **Viral infections:**
			- _Measles_ (Rubeola; first disease) - lesions arising from hairline and spreads downwards lasting >= 3d, becoming confluent, sparing palms and soles, a/w Koplik's spots
			- _Rubella_ (German measles; third disease) - lesions arising from hairline, but clears as spreads, a/w Forchheimer spots
			- _Erythema infectiosum_ (Parvovirus B19; fifth disease) - bright-red slapped cheeks appearance followed by lacy-reticular rash that waxes and wanes over 3 weeks
			- _Exanthem subitum_ (sixth disease) - diffuse maculopapular eruptions over trunk and neck, resolving within 2d
			- _Infectious mononucleosis-like syndrome_ - infection w/ EBV, CMV, HSV etc, a/w fever pharyngitis and adenopathy
			- _Acute HIV infection_ - non-specific diffuse macules and papules in upper thorax, face and collar lesions (+/- oral or genital ulcers), presenting in similar manner as IM
			- _Dengue fever_ - initially diffuse flushing and rash appears midway throughout the disease
			- _Other viral exanthems_ - echoviruses, and coxackieviruses may result in viral syndromes w/ non-specific maculopapular rash; other notable viral infections include West Nile virus infection and Zika virus infection
		- **Bacterial infections:**
			- _Acute meningococcocemia_
			- _Rikketsial fever_
			- _Typhoid fever_
			- _Other non-ricketsial fever_ - leptospirosis, Borreliosis (including Lyme disease)
	- **Non-infectious diseases:**
		- _Drug fevers_ (exanthematous drug-induced eruptions) - drug reactions w/ eosinophilia and systemic symptoms (DRESS), drug-induced hypersensitivity syndrome (DIHS)
		- _Systemic lupus erythematosus_ - often a/w photosnsivtivity, and other more classical lupus erythematosus
		- _Still's disease_ - transient, evanescent, erythematous papules appearing at height of fever on trunk, proximal extremities
		- _Haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis_ - a central maculopapular rash is a common manifestation, but other cutaneous manifestations include pyoderma gangrenosum, purpura, panniculitis, or Steven-Johnson syndrome
