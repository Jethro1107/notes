## Epidemiology

- **Background:**
	- Acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS) first recognised in 1981, although earliest documented case of HIV infection traced back to a blood sampmle from the Democratic Republic of Congo in 1959
	- AIDS is the clinical syndrome caused by the human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) that progressively impairs cellular immunity
	- HIV probably originates/ evolve from simian immunodeficiency viruses (SIV), infecting local hunters but do not cause disease in natural primate hosts
	- Two forms of HIV known: HIV-1 which causes the global HIV pandemic, and HIV-2 which causes a similar, but slowly progressive and less transmissable disease, and is restricted to Africa 
- **Three groups of HIV-1** - representing 3 separate transmission events from chimpanzee:
	- M group - 'major', worldwide distribution
	- O group - 'outlier', restricted to West Africa
	- N group - 'non-major, non-outlier', restricted to West Africa
- **Subtypes of M group HIV-1** - A-D, F-H, J-K (E and I are recombinant forms of other subtypes):
	- Subtype C of HIV-1 accounts for half of the strains globally, and are prevalent in Sub-Saharan Africa and India
	- Subtype B is predominant in Western Europe, America, and Australia
	- Subtype A and D are non-dominant, but associated w/ slower and faster disease progression respectively
- **Global epidemiology and regional patterns:**
	- ![[Pasted image 20260514164240.png]]
	- _Prevalence_ - 34.2 million people living with HIV/ AIDS in 2011
	- _Incidence_ - 2.5 million new infections in 2011 (declined by 20% over last 10y)
	- _Mortality_ - 1.7 million deaths in 2011 (24% decline since peak in 2005 due to ART)

## Virology

- **Basic virology of HIV** - retrovirus from the lentivirus family:
	- _Viral envelope_ - lipid membrane lined by a matrix protein studded w/ gp120 and gp41
	- _Protein core_ - cone-shaped protein core (p24):
		- Two copies of ssRNA
		- Viral enzymes (e.g. RT, integrase, protease)
	- _Viral genome_ - 3 characteristic retroviral genes and 6 regulatory genes:
		- gag - encodes a polyprotein processed into structural proteins
		- pol - encodes for enzymes reverse transcriptase, integrase, and protease
		- env - encodes envelope proteins gp120 and gp41
		- Regulatory genes - vif, vpr, vpu, nef, tat, rev
- **HIV life-cycle** - begins w/ infection where brought to lymphoid organs by dendritic cells, followed by a phase of viraemia and disseminated lymphoid organ involvement where viral replication occurs:
	- ![[Pasted image 20260514170501.png]]
	- _Attachment to CD4 receptors_ - binding of gp120 to CD4 receptors (Th cells, monocyte-macrophages, dendritic cells, microglial cells), resulting in conformational change in gp120, permitting attachment to co-receptors CCR5 or CXCR4
	- _Binding to chemokine co-receptors CCR5 or CXCR4_:
		- CCR5 utilised during initial infection, where virus subsequently adapts to use CXCR4
		- 1% of population are homozygous for CCR5 delta 32 mutations and do not express CCR5, and hence are immune against HIV
	- _Membrane  fusion and cellular entry_ - viral uncoating mediated by gp41, where protein core is released into the cytoplasm
	- _Reverse transcription_ - DNA copy transcribed from RNA genome by existing reverse transcriptase enzymes (N.B. RT error-prone process which results in considerable viral genetic heterogeneity)
	- _Integration into host genome_ - DNA transported into the nucleus and integrates within host genome by **integrase**, forming **proviral DNA** that persists for the life of the cell
	- _Transcription of pro-viral DNA_ - upon cellular stimulation, transciption of pro-viral DNA to form viral mRNA copies
	- _Translation into viral polyprotein_ - precursor polyprotein (gag) translated from mRNA
	- _Formation of strucutral and functional proteins_ - cleavage of precursor polyprotein by **protease**
	- _Viral assembly and release_ - migration of structural viral proteins and RNA to cell surface and incorporated into host cell membrane into viral envelops
- **Effects on infected CD4 cells:**
	- CD4 lymphocytes actively replicating HIV have a _short life-time_, estimated to be around 1 day (estimated that in asymptomatic HIV-infected people, more than 10^10 virions produced and 10^9 lymphocytes destroyed)
	- Small proportion of CD4 lymphocytes enter _post-integration alatent phase_, which prevents complete HIV eradication
	- Predominently impaired cell-mediated immune response (e.g. mycobacteria, HSV) compared to B lymphocyte
- **Immune response to HIV infection:**
	- _Humoral response_ - development of Ab to a wide range of Ag
	- _Cellular response_ - dramatic expansion of HIV-specific CD8 cytotoxic T lymphocytes resulting in CD8 lymphocytosis and **reversed CD4:CD8 ratio**
