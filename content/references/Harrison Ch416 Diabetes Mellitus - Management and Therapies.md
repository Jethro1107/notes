# Ch416 Diabetes Mellitus - Management and Therapies
## Overall Goals

- **Goals of Tx for all forms of DM:**
	- _Symptomatic relief_ - eliminate all symptoms related to hyperglycaemia (usually resolve when RBG < 11.1 mmol/L)
	- _Prevention of complications_ - reduce or eliminate long-term microvascular and macrovascular complications of DM
	- _Quality of life_ - allow patients to achieve as normal a lifestyle as possible
- **Approaches to longitudinal Mx of DM:**
	- Individualised target level of glycaemic control
	- Provide educational resources of DM
	- Provide pharmacological Tx of DM
	- Avoid hypoglycaemia
	- Monitor/ prevent/ treat DM-related complications
- **Multidisciplinary Mx of patients w/ DM:**
	- _Medical_ - primary care physician +/- endocrinologist/ diabetologist, opthalmologists, neurologists, podiatrists, nephrologists, cardiologists
	- _Allied health_ - pharmacists, certified diabetes educator, nutritionist, behavioral health professional, social worker

## Pharmacological Treatment of Diabetes

### Target Level of Glycaemic Control

### Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

- **Goals of insulin therapy** - implementing a regimen that mimics physiological insulin secretion:
	- _Basal insulin_ - edssential for regulating glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, and ketogenesis
	- _Bolus insulin_ - insulin replacement for meals appropriate for the carbohydrate intake and underlying insulin sensitivity
- **Intensive Mx of glycaemia** - intensive insulin therapy aimed at _normal or near-normal glycaemia_:
	- **Pre-requisites of intensive insulin therapy:**
		- _Patient education_ - continuous and thorough for patients to precisely tune insulin therapy
		- _Comprehensive record keeping_ - comprehensive recording of 1) glucose measurements, and 2) nutritional intake
		- _Variable insulin dosing_ - various modes of delivery to allow for matching of insulin dosing to carbohydrate intake and exercise
	- **Mode of delivery of intensive insulin therapy:**
		- Multiple daily injections (MDIs)
		- Continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII)
		- Sensor-augmented system (suspension of insulin delivery when predicted hypoglycaemia)
		- Automated insulin system/ artificial pancreas (AID)
	- **Benefits of intensive insulin therapy:**
		- _Improved prognostication_ - educed acute metabolic complications (i.e. DKA) and chronic microvascular complications
		- _Greater degree of freedom_ - greater control over DM and improved sense of well-being, greater flexibility in timing and content meals, or exercise

	- **Rapid-acting insulin:**
		- _Selection_:
			- Insulin Lispro (lysine-prolone reversal)
			- Insulin aspart
			- Insuline glulisine
		- _MOA_:
			- **Rapid absorption** - due to less tendancy of **self-aggregation** and hence readily absorbed systemically after SC injection
			- **Short duration of action** - decay of insulin action corresponds to decline in plasma glucose level after a meal, and hence **reduced risk of hypoglycaemia**
		- _Role_ - prandial coverage
	- **Long-acting or ultralong-acting insulin:**
		- _Selection_:
			- Insulin Glargine (asparagine replaced by glycine, w/ two arginine residues added to C terminus of B chain)
			- Insulin degludec (further modification and extenision of C terminus of B chain)
		- _MOA_ - formation of **microprecipitates** in physiological subcutaneous pH resulting in:
			- Slower onset of action
			- Longer duration of action (~ 24h  in glargine, > 42h in degludec)
			- Less pronounced peak
		- _Role_ - provision of basal insulin
- **Insulin regimens** - considerable patient-to-patient variation in peak and duration, w/ most T1DM patients being maintained on 0.4-1 U/kg/d w/ 30-50% given as basal insulin:
	- _Basal insulin_ - provided by intermediate acting (NPH), or long-acting (glargine, degludec) insulin
	- _Pre-prandial insulin_ - regular insulin (30-45 min prior to meal), or rapid-acting insulin (< 10 min)
- **Delivery systems and regimen:**
	- **MDI regimens** - basal-bolus insulin w/ flexible bolus insulin giving greatest flexibility and best chance for near-normal glycaemia:
		- Dose of basal insulin adjusted based on fasting BG
		- Timing and dose of rapid-acting preprandal insulin titrated against CGM/ SBGM results, anticipated food intake, and physical activity
		- Various individualised algorithms integrating 1) preprandial glucose and 2) anticipated carbohydrate load to guide preprandial insulin dosing, but must be adjusted to individual's insulin sensitivity

### Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

- **Principles of Mx of T2DM** - care of T2DM also includes greater attention of conditions a/w T2DM and prevention of DM-related complications:
	- _Individualised glycaemic control_ - implementation of 1) medical nutrition therapy, 2) promote physical activity, 3) weight management, and 4) pharmacological Mx
	- _Treatment of associated conditions_:
		- Dyslipidaemia (at least high-risk for patients w/ DM)
		- HTN (lower BP target at < 130/80 mmHg)
		- Obesity
	- _Screening and Mx of diabetes-associated complications_:
		- Cardiorenal risk reduction integrated into considerations for pharmacological Mx as being the main driver of mortality
		- Microvascular complications (e.g. reetinopathy, neuropathy, diabetic foot) cause significant morbidity

- **Approach to selection of agents:**
	- **Oral anti-diabetic medications vs upfront insulin therapy:**
		- _Oral anti-diabetic medications_ - typically first-line of Tx and selection of agent based on presence of compelling indications
		- _Insulin therapy_ - typically if failed multiple lines of Tx; may be initiated immediately if 1) severe hyperglycaemia (FPG <13.9–16.7 mmol/L), or 2) catabolic features
	- **Selection of oral anti-diabetic medications** - metformin as 1st line, while selection of other OADs dependent on presence of compelling indications:

- **Glucose-lowering agents:**
	- **Selection and considerations:**
		- ![[Pasted image 20260518104304.png]]
	- **Metformin** (Biguanides):
		- _MOA_ - incompletely defined:
			- Inhibition of hepatic gluconeogesis
			- Promotes peripheral glucose utilisation
		- _Dosing_ - start low and go slow:
			- Initiation - 500 mg b.d. (due to slow onset of action and GI S/E)
			- Maximum dose - 1g b.d. (no longer super-dose metformin)
			- Renal dosing - half-dose metformin when eGFR < 45 ml/min
		- _Clinical efficacy_:
			- Reduced FPG and insulin levels
			- Improved lipid profiles
			- Modest weight loss
		- _S/E_ - reduced GI S/E with an extended-release form:
			- GI disturbance - nausea, anoresxia, diarrhoea, metallic tast
			- Lactic acidosis
			- B12 deficiency
		- _C/I_:
			- Renal insufficiency - eGFR < 30 ml/min
			- Risk of acidosis - existing acidosis, unstable CHF, liver disease, severe hypoxaemia
			- NPO - e.g. hospitalisation, prior to surgery, due to risk of dehydration, and shock
			- Contrast administration - avoid AKI
	- **Sulphonylurea:**
		- _MOA_ - insulin secretalogues (as evident by **raised circulating insulin levels**):
			- Interaction w/ ATP-sensitive K channels on beta cells on pancreatic islets, stimulating insulin secretion
			- Hence most effective in individuals w/ recent onset T2DM (< 5y) where residual beta cell function is sufficient
			- Insulin secretion increased irrespective of meals, hence **reduction in both fasting and postprandial glucose**
		- _Dosing_ - usually od or bd dosing, w/ initiation at low doses and increased at 1-2 weeks intervals by CGM or BGM
		- _Pharmacokinetics of SU_:
			- **Hepatic metabolism** - increased drug levels and hypoglycaemia risk during hepatic insufficiency or other enzym inhibitors (N.B. some metabolites are _active_)
			- **Renal excretion** - increased drug levels and hypoglycaemia risk w/ CKD
		- _S/E_:
			- Hypoglycaemia - risk increased especially in longer-acting SUs, usually due to delayed meals, increased physical activities, alcohol intake or increased drug levels (hepatic/ renal insufficiency)
			- Weight gain - results from increased insulin levels an dimproved glycaemic control
		- _Drug-drug interactions_:
			- _Antimicrobials_ (discontinue SU) - e.g. fluoroquinolones, clarithromycin, septrin, metronidazole, and anti-fungal agents
			- _Other drugs_ - e.g. warfarin, aspirin, alpha-glucosidase inhibitors
	- **Incretin minetics** (GLP-1 RAs or in combination w/ GIP RAs):
		- _Indications_ - considered for weight management +/- cardio-renal protection in setting of DM
		- _Selection_:
			- GLP-1 RA - semaglutide (available as daily PO form), liraglutide, dulaglutide
			- Dual GLP-1/ GIP RA - tirzepatide
		- _MOA_ - acting on incretin pathway:
			- **Insulin secretalogue** - Increased glucose-stimulated insulin secretion
			- **Delayed gastric emptying** - contributes to early satiety and weight control
		- _Dosing_ - typically once-weekly injection
		- _Clinical efficacy_:
			- Glycaemic control (w/ no theoretical risk of hypoglycaemia)
			- Weight-loss therapy at higher doses of semaglutide and liraglutide
			- Reduced risk of CVD and DMN
		- _S/E_:
			- N/V
			- Medullary thyroid carcinoma
			- ?Pancreatitis
		- _C/I_ - in patients w/ MEN or medullary carcinoma of thyroid
		- _Drug-drug interaction_ - delayed gastric emptying, affecting absorption of other oral drugs
	- **SGLT2 inhibitors:**
		- _MOA_ - selective inhibition of SGLT-2 co-transporter in the PCT:
			- Inhibition of glucose reabsorption
			- Lowered thresthold for glucose excretion and leads to increased urinary glucose loss (independent of insulin sensitivity and secretion)
			- Mild diuretic through inhibition of Na reabsorption (~ 3-6 mmHg reduction in SBP)
		- _Clinical efficacy_:
			- Glycaemic and BP control
			- Cardiorenal protection
		- _S/E_:
			- Genitourinary infections - increased urinary glucose resulting in increased risk of genital mycotic infections in M and F
			- Hypovolaemia - osmotic diuresis may result in reduced intravascular volume
			- Euglycaemic DKA - ketosis risk increased due to stimulation of glucagon, where ongoing glycosuria may mask hyperglycaemia seen in typical DKA
			- Bladder cancer - possible increased risk w/ dapagliflozin
		- _C/I_:
			- T1DM and selected T2DM w/ profound insulin deficiency
			- G3 CKD (avoid initiating at G3a CKD, and withdraw if G3b CKD)
	- **Thiazolidinedones:**
		- _Selection_ - pioglitazone (actos), rosiglitazone (withdrawn from market)
		- _MOA_ - reduces insulin resistance in adipocytes:
			- PPAR-gamma nuclear receptor agonism in adipocytes
			- Increased insulin sensitivity (reflected by a reduced circulating insulin levels)
			- Promote adipocyte differentiation, reduce hepatocyte fat accumulation and fatty acid storage
			- Redistribution of fat from central to peripheral locations
			- Raised LDL, HDL +/- TG levels (although may not be clinically significant)
		- _S/E_:
			- Fluid retention - mild increase in plasma volume and reduced haematocrit +/- mild peripheral oedema and CHF
			- Diabetic macular oedema - rare cases in patientes experiencing worsening of diabetic macular oedema as issued by the FDA
			- Osteoporosis - results in increased risk of fractures in postmenopausal women taking these agents
			- Bladder cancer - known association w/ thiazolidinediones
		- _C/I_:
			- CHF - absolute C/I in patients w/ NYHA class III/ IV CHF
			- Hepatic insufficiency
			- Pregnancy (warning due to inducing ovulation in those w/ PCOS)
