# Chapter 73 Sore Throat

- **Definitions and terminology:**
    - _Pharyngitis_ - inflammation of the pharynx +/- tonsils, typically caused by viral causes, ocassionally by bacterial infections
    - _Tonsilitis_ - inflammation of tonsils only
    - _Quinsy_ - a peritonsillar abscess

    ![[Pasted image 20260510111150.png]]
- **Clinical presentation of sore throat:**
    - **Sore throat** - ocassionally presents as the sole symptoms:
    - _Onset_ - typically acute, ocassionally chronic
    - _Quality_ - often continuous pain
    - _Provocation_ - aggravated by swallowing, ocassionally resulting in swallowing difficulties in children
    - **URTI symptom complex** - features of common cold or influenza including rhinorhoea/ nasal congestion, cough and sputum +/- systemic complaints (fever, myalgia, fatigue)
    - **Atypical presentation** - children < 4y rarely complain of sore throat during acute pharyngitis and may present w/ _N/V_, _abdominal pain_, and _fever_

## Diagnostic Approach

- **DDx of sore throat:**
    - 
    ![[Pasted image 20260510111745.png]]
    - **Infections:**
    - _Viral infections_ (50%):
      - Respiratory viruses - adenovirus, rhinovirus, coronavirus, influenza virus, picornavirus
      - Infectious-mononucleosis-like syndrome - EBV, herpes simplex virus, varicella, HIV, hand-foot-mouth disease (enterovirus)
      - Other viruses - herpangina
    - _Bacterial pharyngitis_:
      - Beta-haemolytic streptococcus (GABHS)
      - Haemophilus influenzae (N.B. acute epiglottitis in children)
      - Staphylococcus aureus
      - Moraxella catarrhalis
      - Gonococcal pharyngitis
      - Syphilis
    - _Atypical infections_:
      - Oral candidiasis
      - Mycoplasma pneumoniae
      - Chlamydia pneumoniae
    - **Non-infective diseases:**
    - _Cardiovascular diseases_ - ischaemic heart disease
    - _Blood dyscrasias_ - agranulocytosis (neutropennic fever), leukaemia
    - _Gastrointestinal diseases_ - GERD
    - _Rheumatological diseases_ - Kawasaki disease, Behcet disease, scleroderma
    - _Neoplasia_ - oropharyngeal neoplasms
    - _Local irritation_ - tobacco smoke, antiseptic lozenges

- **Probability diagnosis:**
    - _Viral pharyngitis_ (50%) - viral infection supported by presence of coryza prodromata, hoarseness, cough, conjunctivitis, and nasal stuffiness
    - _Streptococcal (GABHS) tosillitis_ - should be considered for testing (modified Centor score), especially in the absence of cough, where ABx should be prescribed because of the potential complications
    - _Infectious-mononucleosis-like syndrome_ - considered if the clinical triad of 1) fever, 2) pharyngitis, and 3) cervical lymphadenopathy
- **Serious disorders not to be missed:**
    - _Severe infections_:
    - Acute epiglottitis - caused by H. influenzae in 2-4y, should be considered when toxic appearance (short febrile illness), respiratory difficulties in the absence of cough, and difficulties of swallowing)
    - Quinsy and deep neck space infections - as a complication of bacterial pharyngitis, and should be considered w/ persistent febrile illness, marked swelling, +/- trismus
    - Diptheria - rare due to vaccination, characterised by whitish pseudomembrane formation w/ risk of cardiac and neurological complications
    - Acute HIV infection - presentation as an infectious-mononucleosis like syndrome and should be evaluated if risk factors are present
    - Vincent angina - fusobacterium infection of the pharynx
    - _Cardiovascular diseases_ - referred pain from myocardial ischaemia
    - _Neopasias_ - haematological malignancies (e.g. acute leukaemia, lymphoma, neutropenic fever), carcinoma of the oropharynx and tough
- **Pitfalls:**
    - _Mimicks of acute streptococcal pharyngitis_ - pitfalls as may result in use of ABx:
    - Infectious-mononucleosis like syndrome - prescription of ABx may result in diffuse maculopapular rash
    - Adenovirus pharyngitis - may mimic streptococcal pharyngitis, especially in young adults
    - _Trauma_ - important and should be considered especially in children:
    - Foreign body - e..g fish bone, causing sudden onset of throat pain, drooling, and dysphagia
    - Vocal abuse - suspected if there is Hx of excessive vocal strain w/ hoarseness
    - Burns - hot foods and drinks, acid or alkali
    - Other irritants - cigarette smoke or from fires, especially in children
    - _Mouth breathing_ - causes of nasal congestion (e.g. allergic rhinitis, adenoid hypertrophy) resulting in mouth breathing causes the pharynx to become dry and sore
    - _Tonsilloliths_ - debris trapped within deep tonsillar crypts are a common cause of vague sore throat, possibility of recurrent bouts of tonsillitis, and hallitosis
- **Seven masquerades list:**
    - _Depression_ - significant association from large studies although practically difficult to screen
    - _Diabetes_ - indirectly associated from immune dysfunction, and increased risk of oral candidiasis
    - _Drugs_ - NSAIDs, corticosteroid inhalants (e.g. asthma) are a/w sore throat
    - _Thyroid_ - patients w/ painful thyroiditis may ocassionally complain of sore throat rather than thyroid pain
    - _Anaemia_ - aplastic anaemia and other haematological malignancies indirectly a/w sore throat through candidiasis and agranulocytosis

## Clinical Approach

- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - **HPI** - onset, localisation, provocation:
    - _Onset_ - acute vs chronic
    - _Localisation_ - ask patient to point where the pain is experienced:
      - Deep pain in the throat
      - Neck pain (ddx thyroiditis)
    - _Provocation_ - pain upon swallowing
    - **Associated Sx:**
    - _URTI symptoms_ - nasal congestion/ rhinorrhoea, cough and sputum
    - _Aural symptoms_ - aural fullness, hearing loss
    - _Eye symptoms_ - eye discomfort (FB sensation), red eye, tearing
    - _Metallic taste_ - suggestive of oral candidiasis
    - _Other respiratory Sx_ - SOBOE, chest pain
    - _Systemic Sx_ - fever, malaise, myalgias
    - **PMH:**
    - _Asthma_ - enquire Hx of using corticosteroid inhalers
    - _Chronic obstructive lung disease_ - enquire prior exacerbations
    - _Other relevant medical Hx_ - e.g. immunosuppression, risk of agranulocytosis etc.
    - **Vaccination** - consider diptheria vaccination Hx
    - **Social Hx:**
    - Smoking
    - Exposures to irritants
- **P/E:**
    - _Vital signs_ - BP/P, RR, SpO2, temperature
    - _General examination_ - pallor (leukaemia), toxicity, cervical LN
    - _Examination of throat_ - inspection of pharynx, tonsils, palate, and uvula:
    - General ppearance - erythema, exudates, abnormal masses, or ulcers
    - Tonsils - swollen ipsilaterally/ bilaterally
    - Uvula - any deviation

- **Available Ix:**
    - Complete blood count
    - Rapid antigen test
    - Throat swab
    - Monospot test
    - Bx of suspicious lesion
    - RBG (for DM)
- **Supportive symptomatic Tx:**
    - _General measres_ - rest, adequate soothing fluids, including icy poles
    - _Analgesia_ - paracetamol, aspirin, ibuprofen
    - _OTC medications_ - throat lozenges and topical sprays not recommended as can sensitize the throat
