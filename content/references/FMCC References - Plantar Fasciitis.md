# Murtaugh's Patient Education for Plantar Fasciitis

- **Other names of plantar fasciitis:**
	- Policeman's heel
	- Jogger's heal
- **Clinical features of plantar fasciitis** - painful area usually situated about 5 cm from back of the heel on the sole of the foot:
	- Usually worse on the first steps, typically after getting out of bed, or getting up to walk after prolonged sitting
	- Usually worse when climbing stairs
	- Relieved after the area warms up with activity
	- Returns towards the end of the day after lots of walking and standing, which is eased w/ resting but again returns on the first step
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260428113952.png]]
- **Etiology** - inflammation of the plantar fascia that attaches to the calcaneous:
	- Tear (e.g. when taking off too quickly)
	- Osteophytes
	- Unrelated to faulty footwear
- **Natural Hx** - self-limiting (~ 18 mo, sometimes as early as 6 mo)
- **Treatment:**
	- _Rest_ - avoid long walks and from running to allow for healing (but allow time for exercise)
	- _Physical therapy_ - hot and cold therapy:
		- Alternately place foot in very hot and then cold water for 30 seconds each for 15 minutes twice a day
		- Commercially available foot hydro-massager
	- _Prescription_:
		- Orthotic aid (e.g. Viscosport) - wearing a pad that raises the heel by 1cm, w/ hole cut out on the tender area to minimise contact
		- Exercise prescription - stretching exercises on the plantar fascia:
			- Standing on the edge of a stairs on the balls of the feet
			- Gently drop heels for 20s, then lift heels for 10s while keeping knees straight x 3
			- Perform the exercise 3x a day
			- 
			![[Pasted image 20260428115207.png]]
		- _Massage_ - self-massage w/ tennis ball on a flat surface and rolling it under foot for few minutes
		- _Corticosteroid injections_ - typically avoided unless Sx severe
		- _Extracorporeal USG_ - shockwave USG therapy for 2 mo can be extremely effective
	- _Referral_ - refer for physiotherapy for stretching exercises
