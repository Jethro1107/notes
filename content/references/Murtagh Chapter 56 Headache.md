# Chapter 56 Headache

- **Patient influence on the manner of presentation:**
	- Influencing us w/ preconceived ideas that they will verbalised (e.g. BP issues, eye complaints)
	- Masquerade concerns for sinister pathologies such as brain tumour or stroke
- **Hypertension and headache:**
	- It is often thought that hypertension is a rare cause of headache, but BP should still be taken
	- BP should be taken to provide reassurance to the patient
	- Should BP be high, consider hypertension as a sign of underlying intracranial pathology (e.g. stroke), and do not assume that the headache is due to hypertension

## Diagnostic Approach

- **Murtaugh's diagnostic approach to headaches:**
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260504213107.png]]
- **Probability diagnosis:**
	- _Respiratory infections_ - the most common cause of acute headache in general practice
	- _Primary headache syndromes_ - the most common causes of chronic recurrent headache are so-called  migraine, tension-type headache
	- _Combination headaches_ (tension-vascular headache)- typified by relatively constant pain lasting for many days, having mixed components such as tension, depression, cervical dysfunction, vascular, or medication-overuse
	- _Transformed migraine_ - progressively increasing frequency of migraine attack until the headache recurs daily (often w/ analgesic abuse), where typical migraine features becomes modified so that the pattern resembles that of tension headache but each attack resembles the unilateral situation of migraine
- **Serious disorders not to be missed:**
	- **Acute headaches:**
		- _Subarachnoid haemorrhage_ - classically presents w/ a thunderclap headache, typically occipital but becomes generalised
		- _Intracranial haemorrhage_ - esp if involving cerebellar, intraventricular, and frontal lobe areas which may or may not be a/w focal neurology
		- _CNS infections_ - esp if constant, severe, generalised headache, and a/w systemic features such as fever, malaise, and neck stiffness
		- _Acute close angle glaucoma_ - often a/w BOV, photophobia, and red eye
	- **Chronic headaches:**
		- _Space-occupying lesions_ - e.g. CSDH, cerebral tumours, pituitary tumours
		- _Temporal arteritis_ - should be considered in all elderly
		- _Benign intracranial hypertension_ - considered in otherwise unexplained chronic headache esp occuring in young obese women
- **Pitfalls** - causes that are controversial, but are known to cause headache due to combination of factors (risk of over-investigation of patients if Hx not detailed enough):
	- Post-traumatic headache
	- Post-procedural headache (e.g. LP, spinal anaesthesia)
	- Exertional headache (including post-sexual intercourse)
	- Cervical dysfunction
- **Seven masquerades checklist:**
	- _Cervicogenic headaches_ - cervical spondylosis presenting w/ headaches, often under-recognised and may seek alternative health professionals for help
	- _Drugs_ - important drugs include:
		- Analgesics (medication-overuse headache)
		- Alcohol
		- Caffeine
		- COC pills
		- NSAIDs
		- Vasodilators (e.g. CCBs, nitrates, sildenafil)
	- _Depression_ - tendancy to psychosomatise
	- _Anaemia_ - especially if chronically developing and Hb , 10 mg/dL
	- _DM_ - hypoglycaemia
	- _Thyroid_ - both hyper- and hypo-thyroidism may present w/ headaches although less likely
- **Psychogenic considerations** - hidden agenda:
	- Mood problems presenting as headaches - patients may be overtly or covertly depressed or anxious:
		- Most characteristic feature of a headache w/ psychogenic component is that the headache is _present virtually every minute of every day for weeks or months on end_
		- These patients often deny that they are anxious, depressed or unduly stress, hence it is important to identify lifestyle factors and historical events that can be a/w the headache (aids Mx)
	- Headaches may provoke catastrophisation - some patients are fearful that headache represents a sinister pathology and requires appropriate reassurance
	- Exageration of headaches for secondary gain:
		- Following an accident (e.g. rear-end collision)
		- Sick leaves
		- Narcotic seeking
- **Features suggestive of a sinister cause of headache:**
	- Time course (acute/ subacute relentlessly progressive tempo)
	- Any focal neurology
	- Fever, confusion, altered mental status, neck stiffness
- **Timeline for important causes of headache/ facial pain:**
	- _Acute severe headache_ - subarachnoid haemorrhage, benign exertional headaches, migraine/ cluster headaches
	- _Subacute, progressive headaches_ - intracranial SOL, temporal arteritis
	- _Recurrent headaches_ - migraines/ cluster headaches, exertional headaches, trigeminal neuralgia
	- _Chronic persistent headaches_ - tension-type headaches, transformed migraine, cervicogenic headache, atypical facial pain, intracranial pathology
- **Diurnal patterns of headache** - vital for narrowing down DDx:
	- _Waking up w/ headache_ - migraine, cervical spondylosis, depression, SOL
	- _Headache that occurs in the mid day_ - frontal sinusitis follows a typical pattern if in the absence of respiratory S/S, namely onset around 9 am, building to a maximum by 1pm, then subsiding in following hours
	- _Headache that occurs towards the end of the day_ - more suggestive of tension-type headache, which also may span accross days

## Clinical Approach

- **Salient points of Hx:**
	- **HPI** - detailed Hx particularly on timing, relieving factors, and associations:
		- _Site_:
			- Laterality - unilateral vs bilateral
			- Localisation - localised vs generalised
			- Pinpointing the pain - e.g. temporal, frontal, occipital (note associated neck pain)
		- _Onset_ - acute vs chronic
		- _Progression and pattern_ - temporal pattern in terms of relative intensity of the pain, and time in which pain occurs is important in Dx of hedaches secondary to specific pathology:
			- At what time do you usually get the headache?
		- _Quality_ - description of the headache:
			- Tension/ band-like headache
			- Pounding/ Pulsatile headache
			- Tenderness when combing or palpating the scalp
		- _Exacerbating and relieving factors_ - analgesia
		- _Severity_ - in terms of:
			- Degree of pain
			- Frequency and duration
			- Distress caused due to catastrophic thinking
	- **Associated Sx** - start w/ generalised question on other symptoms a/w headache:
		- _Aura_ - visual (unusual sensation in eyes like flashing lights), sensory (strange sensations, dizziness), motor (weakness) aura pointing towards migraine
		- _Focal neurological deficits_ - screen for intracranial pathology
		- _Nausea and vomiting_ - can occur in severe migraines, but consider evidence of raised intracranial pressure
		- _Photophobia_ (does light hurt your eyes) - classically a/w migraines, but also a feature of meningism
		- _Other eye symptoms_ - BOV, watering, redness in eyes to r/o acute glaucoma
		- _Fever, chills and rigors_ - r/o CNS infections
	- **Associated events:**
		- _Ideas_ - what do you think causes the headache
		- _Stress_ - stress provokes various primary headache syndromes including TTH and migraines
		- _Trauma_ - recent head trauma for TBI, CSDH, ICH
		- _Sinusitis_ - recent heavy cold, associated runny noses, Hx of recurrent sinusitis
- **P/E:**
	- _Vital signs_ - temperature, BP/P, RR
	- _Neurological examination_:
		- Consciousness - GCS, AVPU
		- Meningism - neck stiffness, Brudzinski sign, Kernig sign
		- Targeted limb examination - compare power bilaterally
		- Eyes - examination of pupils, eye movement, and fundi
	- _Examination of temporal arteries_ - indicated if suspecting temporal arteritis
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260505213426.png]]
	- _Additional signs_ - physical signs specific for particular pathologies:
		- **Upper cervical pain sign** - tenderness over C2/3 spinous processes is specific for **cervicogenic headache**
		- **Ewing sign** - pressing fingers gently upwards and inwards against orbital roof medial to the supra-orbital nerve, indicative of **frontal sinusitis**
		- **Invisible pillow sign** (uncommon) - patient lying supine w/ head on pillow, w/ examiner withdrawing the pillow and supporting the head w/ hand; patient instructed to relax neck muscles and the examiner removes the supporting hand (+ve if head does not relax onto the bed)
- **Ix** - tailored to likely diagnosis:
	- _Routine bloods_ - CBC, ESR/ CRP:
		- CBC - anaemia, leukocytosis
		- ESR/ CRP - raised in temporal arteritis
	- _Imaging_:
		- CXR - if septic or suspected intracranial malignancy
		- X Ray of C-spine - for cervical dysfunction
		- Contrast CTB - brain tumours, cerebrovascular accidents, SAH
		- MRI - better definition of intracerebral structures
	- _LP_ - C/I if suspected raised ICP

## Tension Type Headache

- **Epidemiology** - 75% patients are F
- **IHS criteria for tension-type headache:**
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260505215004.png]]
- **Clinical features of TTH** - headaches that tend to last for hours and recur each day:
	- _Site_ - bilateral and symmetrical, e.g. frontal, over forehead, temples:
		- 
		![[Pasted image 20260505215601.png]]
	- _Onset_ - chronic, nearly daily
	- _Character_ - dull ache, non-pulsatile quality:
		- Tight pressure feeling
		- Heavy weight on top of the head
		- Tight band around the head
	- _Radiation_ - to occiput
	- _Associated features_:
		- Lightheadedness
		- Fatigue
		- Neck ache/ muscle stiffness (occiput to shoulder)
		- Mood disturbances
		- Perfectionism
	- _Timing_ - upon waking, and progressively worsen throughout the day
	- _Exacerbating and relieving factors_:
		- Exacerbating factors - stress and tension (most do not realise), overwork w/ skipping meals, cervical dysfunction
		- Relieving factors - alcohol
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260505215618.png]]
- **Signs:**
	- Features of muscle tension
	- Scalp tender to touch
	- Invisible pillow sign
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Reassurance and education_ - individualsed reassurance and explain pathophysiology of the disease
		- _Advice_ -advice on relaxation in unstructured, or structured ways (i.e. CBT is effective as any drugs in all ages)
		- _Prescription_ - mild analgesics, sedatives, antidepressants
		- _Referral_ - physiotherapy for mobilisation of neck if evidence of cervical dysfunction
	- **Patient counselling:**
		- _Reassurance_ - individualised, appropriate reassurance regarding the unlikeliness of sinister pathology and that further Ix is unlikely required
		- _Education_ - note the disorder of muscle tension, linking psychological state to muscle tension, and muscle tension to pain (e.g. overworked muscles after a gym session)
	- **Advice:**
		- General advice on awareness of mood states and stress management (e.g. be less of a perfectionalist)
		- Lying down in hot bath and practice meditation during an attack
		- Relaxation techniques, yoga or meditation classes
	- **Pharmacological Mx:**
		- _Simple analgesics_ - mild analgesics (e.g. panadol, brufen)
		- _Antidepressants_ - e.g. amitriptyline 10 mg PO nocte increasing to 75 mg if required
