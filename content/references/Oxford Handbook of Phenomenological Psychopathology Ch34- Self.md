# Ch34: Self

- **Principles for better conceptualisation of self in phemenological psychopathology:**
    - Many central figures consider most psychopathological phenomena as a disorder of the self (e.g. schizophrenia according to Sass and Parnas)
    - Conflicting notions and definitions of self in most philosophers (e.g. Husserl, Scheler, Heidegger, Stein, Sartre, Merleau-Ponty etc.)
- **What is self** - answering via negativa, what do phenomenologists typically reject as definition of self:
    - Transcendental principle of unity (Kant)
    - Absence of primative or fundamental nature about the Self, for it is a social construct
- **Pre-reflective for me-ness:**
    - Most phenomenologists defined the experiential presence of self
    - Fundamental and pre-reflective feature or character or dimension of experience rather than some high-level social construct or hidden non-experimentla transcedental precondition
    - "I am always somehow acquainted with myself" (Heidegger), where this self acquantenance is only found in intentional life, i.e. an acquaintence w/ a world-immersed self
    - Always pre-reflective, rather than reflective or introspective: “there is no ‘inner man,’ man is in and toward the world, and it is in the world that he knows himself” (Merleau-Ponty 2012: lxxiv)
    - "It is not reflection which reveals the consciousness reflected- on to itself. Quite the contrary, it is the non- reflective consciousness which renders the reflection possible; there is a pre-reflective cogito which is the condition of the Cartesian cogito." (Sartre)
- **Phenomenological proposals sits in the middle of two opposing views regarding selfhood:**
    - the self is some kind of unchanging soul substance that is distinct from and ontologically independent of the worldly objects and conscious episodes it is directed at and the subject of
    - there is nothing to consciousness apart from a manifold or bundle of changing experiences
