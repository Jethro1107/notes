# Overview of upper gastrointestinal endoscopy

## Introduction

- **Definition of OGD** - endoscopic procedure which entails visualization of the oropharynx, esophagus, stomach, and proximal duodenum (D1-2), with real-time diagnostic, and therapeutic procedures

## Patient selection

- **Indications** - generally indicated for diagnosis, screening/ surveillance, and therapeutic procedures:
    - **Diagnostic examination:**
        - 1\. Dyspepsia that is either 1) unrresponsive to emprical therapy (see notes on dyspepsia), 2) occuring in patient \> 55y, or 3) presence of alarm symptoms
        - 2\. Dysphagia and odynophagia
        - 3\. Persistent vomiting of unknown cause
        - 4\. Acute or chronic upper GI bleeding, or iron-deficiency anaemia
        - 5\. Imaging findings on upper GI imaging - e.g. CT abdomen, barium meal
        - 6\. Work up for SB disease suspected on malabsorption (e.g. celiac disease)
    - **Surveillance:**
        - 1\. Screening for esophageal varices in patient with portal HTN (e.g. cirrhosis)
        - 2\. Screening for dysplasia in patients with Barrett's esophagus
        - 3\. Screening for upper GI malignancies with polyposis syndromes or Lynch syndrome
    - **Therapeutic** - e.g. for upper GI bleeding, prophylactiv variceal banding, dilation of lesions etc.
- **C/I:**
    - <u>Haemodynamic instability</u> - e.g. severe shock
    - <u>Unable to tolerate sedation</u> - e.g. recent ACS, cardiac arrhythmias, severe respiratory disease
    - <u>Bleeding tendancies</u> - e.g. abnormal clotting profile, and platelet \< 20 x 10<sup>9</sup> / L
    - <u>Suspected risk of perforation</u> - e.g. intestinal obstruction, PPU

## Pre-procedure considerations

- **Presedation assessment** - assessment of risk of sedation:
    - <u>Comorbidities</u> - cardiopulmonary disease
    - <u>Medication use</u> - benzodiazepine, opioids, cannabis
    - <u>Anatomical consideration</u>
- **Patient preparation:**
    - **Fasting** - generally indicated in elective cases to minimise the risk of aspiration:
        - <u>Duration</u> - minimum 2h after clear fluids or 6h if light meal (consider prolonging if suspected delayed gastric emptying)
        - <u>Emergency OGD</u> - protect airway by endotracheal intubation if OGD cannot be delayed
    - **Anti-thrombotics** - considerations for discontinuation of anti-platelets and anti-coagulants during periprocedual period by balancing bleeding risk and risk of thromboembolic events
    - **Antibiotic prophylaxis** - indicated for <u>selected patients</u> as risk of infection is low:
        - <u>Cirrhosis patients in UGIB</u> - ABx prophylaxis to reduce rate of **spontaneous bacterial peritonitis**, as well as other infections
        - <u>Percutaneous gastrostomy tube placement</u>
        - <u>General increased risk of infection</u> - e.g. severe neutropenia (ANC \< 0.5 x 10<sup>9</sup> / L) or other states of immunocomprimisation (e.g. haematological malignancies)

## Adverse events

- **Complications** - complication rates \< 1% (\< 0.01% for diagnostic endoscopy):
    - <u>Complications of sedation</u> - cardioulmonary depression (e.g. MI, respiratory arrest, pulmonary embolism)
    - <u>Procedure-related complications</u>:
        - **Aspiration penumonia**
        - **Bleeding** - increased risk if patient has bleeding tendancies
        - **Perforation** - extremely rare unless therapeutic maneuvers are performed (e.g. stricture dilatation, pneumatic dilatation for achalasia)
