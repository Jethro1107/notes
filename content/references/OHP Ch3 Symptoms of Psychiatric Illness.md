# Ch3 Symptoms of Psychiatric Illnesses

- **Symptoms vs Signs** - the terms are used synonymously in psychiatry because they are only detected by eliciting them from patients:
    - <u>Symptom</u> - abnormality reported by the patient
    - <u>Sign</u> - abnormality detected by the doctor by observation or clinical examination
- **Psychopathology** - study of abnormalities in mental state:
    - <u>Descriptive psychopathology</u> - abnormalities of mental states identified and defined through close observation of patient's behaviour and empathic exploration of their subjective experience (i.e. phenomenology)
    - <u>Dynamic psychopathology</u> - e.g. Freudian psychopathology, where abnormalities of mental states are described with a central dogma in mind
- **Qualifiers of symptoms/ psychopathology:**
    - <u>Subjective vs objective</u> - objective findings are noted by an external observer; subjective findiings are those reported by the patient that cannot be validated
    - <u>Form vs content</u> - form refers to the abstracted nature of the symptom; content refers to the culture- and experience-related aspects of the symptom
    - <u>Primary vs secondary</u> - primary Sx are considered arising directly from the mental illness (i.e. can be emphathized but not fully understood); secondary Sx are those that are understandable response to some morbid mental state
    - <u>Endogenous vs reactive</u> - endogenous conditions are mental illnesses that arise spontaneously from within; reactive conditions are mental illnesses that arose in response to external events
    - <u>Psychotic vs neurotic</u> - psychosis refers to disorders characterised by abnormal thought and perception and often w/ minimal insight; neurosis refers to disorders characterised by normal thought and perception, although abnormal affect
    - <u>Congruent vs incongruent</u> (appropriateness of affect) - apparent appropriateness of patient's affect towards their symptoms or their symptoms to their mood
    - <u>Structural vs functional</u> - based on the presence of observable structural changes
