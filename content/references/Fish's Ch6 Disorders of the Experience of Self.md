# Ch6 Disorders of the Experience of Self

- **4 aspects of self-experience** (Jasper 1997):
    - The awareness of the existence and activity of the self
    - The awareness of being a unity at any given point in time
    - The awareness of continuity of identity over a period of time
    - The awareness of being separate from the environment (i.e. awareness of ego boundaries)

## Disturbance of awareness of self-activity

- **Principles:**
    - All events brought to consciousness are associated with <u>a sense of personal possession</u>, although may not be in the forefront of consciousness
    - <u>Personalisation</u> is the awareness of personal posession
- **Depersonalisation** - a dissociative Sx when an individual feels they are no longer their normal natural self:
    - **Major differential** - nihlistic delusion:
        - Usually these are mood-congruent delusions in the setting of severe depression
    - **Characteristics** - occurs at clear consciousness and at full insight:
        - Feeling of unreality is the core of the Sx, and it is almost always an <u>unpleasant experience</u>
        - Often frightening when first encountered and often described as the first sign of going mad, but eventually grows more or less accustomed to it
        - Usually accompanied by a subjective experience for blunted affect, although to the outside observer there is no loss of ability to respond emotionally and appropriately
        - Often occurs in conjunction w/ 'derealisation' the environment experienced as flat, dull, and unreal (feeling of unreality)
    - **Causes:**
        - A major emotional crisis or a threat to life leading to complete dissociation of affect (regarded as an adaptive mechanism that allows subject to function reasonably w/o being overwhelmed by emotion)
        - Moderately stressful situations (mildly, reported to occur at least once in 30-70% young adults \[Freeman 1996\])
        - Psychiatric disorders such as schizophrenia and depressive illness
        - Substance misuse (esp. LSD)
        - Organic brain disease
        - Physiological phenomenon when severely tired
- **Loss of emotional resonance:**
    - **Characteristics** - inability to feel:
        - Feeling that they cannot feel, i.e. being numbed to the situations
        - Unable to generate an emotional response when encountering both animate and inanimate objects in the environment
    - **Causes:**
        - <u>Depression</u> - not only affected by low mood but loss of emotional resonance, most prominent when encountering their loved ones, and this apparent loss of feeling makes patients feel more <u>guilty</u> and <u>morally reprehensible</u>
        - <u>Other depersonalisation states</u> - e.g. trauma, major emotional crisis

## Disturbances in the immediate awareness of self-unity

- **Disturbances in awareness of self-unity:**
    - **Causes:**
        - Psychogenic/ depressive depersonalisation - acting and talking in an automatic way which is perceived as being two persons
        - Delusion of demoniac possession
        - Schizophrenia (uncomomon)

## Disturbances of the continuity of self

- **Disturbances of the continuity of self:**
    - <u>Description</u> - principally feeling that they are not the same person as they were before the illness:
        - Often expressed as a sense of change
        - Some claim to have died under their old name and have come to life as a new person
        - From the context of religious conversion some may refer to as "being born again"
        - Very rarely complaining of experiencing of multiple different personalities
        - Some individuals w/ schizophrenia may describe how they seem to pass from one personality ot another, or to be personifying natural events, animals, and historical figures during acute illness
    - <u>Causes and DDx</u>:
        - Psychogenic/ dissociative states
        - Schizophrenia
        - Rapid-cycling bipolar disorder
        - Borderline personality disorder
        - Malingering
        - Complex partial seizures

## Theory of mind, consciousness and schizophrenia

- **Theory of mind** - or mentalisation, ability of an individual to infer or understand the mental states of others in given situations
- **Most with disturbances in experience of self tend to have deficits in TOM:**
    - See Bentall 2006 for overview of TOM in psychosis and schizophria-spectrum disorders
    - Schiffman et al 2004 emonstrates deficits in perspective-taking among children who went on later to develop schizophrenia spectrum disorders, suggesting TOM impairment prior to development of these disorders
    - Sass & Parnas 2003 proposed a unifying concept of seeing schizophrenia as a fundamentally self-disorder as a unifying account of all Sx
- **Disturbances in TOM can be observed in:**
    - Autism-spectrum
    - Psychotic illnesss (although TOM deficits are non-specific for paranoia and are not invariably present in those w/ schizophrenia)
