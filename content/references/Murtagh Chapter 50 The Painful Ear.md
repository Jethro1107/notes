	- _Burden_ - accounts for 1 in 25 patients in general practice
- **DDx of ear pain** - classified into 1) disorders of the ear, 2) periotic disorders:
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260425152115.png]]
	- **Disorders of the ear:**
		- _External ear_ - trauma, perichondritis, otitis externa, furunculosis, viral myringitis, Ramsey-Hunt syndrome, neoplasia
		- _Middle ear_ - eustachian tube insufficiency, otitis media, cholesteatoma, acute mastoiditis, barotrauma (perforated TM)
	- **Periotic causes:**
		- _Musculoskeletal_ - TMJ dysfunction, upper cervical spinal dysfunction
		- _Inflammatory and autoimmune_ - temporal arteritis, lymph node inflammation
		- _Referred pain via facial nerve_ - pharyngitis, tonsillitis, dental disorders, parotitis
## Diagnostic Approach

- **Murtaugh's diagnostic approach to otalgia:**
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260425152222.png]]
- **Probability diagnosis:**
	- Acute otitis media (most common cause of otalgia)
	- Chronic otitis media and cholesteatoma (often a/w Hx of recurrent AOM)
	- Acute otitis externa
	- Temporomandibular joint arthragia (considered when OM and OE excluded)
- **Serious disorders not to be missed:**
	- **Malignancies** - various mechanisms causing referred pain to the ear:
		- _Oral cavity neoplasms_ - neoplasms of the tongue, oral cavity, oropharynx, palate, or tonsils can be referred to the ear
		- _Nasopharyngeal carcinoma_ - locally important neoplasm that can present w/ otitis media w/ effusions
		- _Carcinoma of the external ear_ - rare, but directly causing ear pain
	- **Cholesteatoma** - locally destructive non-neoplastic mass a/w chronic otitis media, requiring differentiation between safe vs non-safe
	- **Ramsey-hunt syndrome** (considered in elderly) - Herpes zoster re-activation via the facial nerve, a/w pain and vesicular rash over the posterior wall of ear cannal, as well as facial nerve palsy
	- **Acute mastoiditis** - considered a local complication of acute otitis media
- **Pitfalls:**
	- _Barotrauma and perforated TM_ - should be considered if following diving or air travel
	- _Disorders of the oral cavity_ (referred pain):
		- Dental disorders - unerupted wisdom teeth, dental abscesses
		- Disorder of the oral cavity - apthous ulcers, malignancies of the oral cavity and oral pharynx
		- Tonsillar disorders - tonsillitis, quinsy, tonsillectomy
		- 
		![[Pasted image 20260425155024.png]]
	- _Musculoskeletal disorders_ - cervical spine dysfunction, TMJ arthralgia
- **Seven masquerades list:**
	- _Depression_ - depressive illness should be considered in any patients complaining of chronic pain
	- _Upper cervical spine dysfunction_ - resulting in referred pain from the C2-3 levels to the posterior regions of the ear
## Clinical Approach

- **General considerations and pitfalls for otalgia:**
	- Faily to visualisation of the TM before diagnosis and Tx
	- Not checking out possible referral sites such as the oropharynx and teeth
	- Overlooking common musculoskeletal causes
	- Failing to recognise the unsafe ear
- **Salient points of Hx:**
	- **HPI** - site, onset, progression, provacation, quality, radiation, severity:
		- _Site_:
			- In the ear - likely disorder of the ear
			- Behind the ear - consider periotic causes (e.g. C-spine dysfunction, acute mastoiditis)
			- Below the ear - consider parotid gland pathology, TMJ dysfunction
		- _Onset and progression_ - acute vs chronic; static vs progressive vs fluctuating
		- _Provacation_:
			- Swimming - acute otitis externa and otitis media
			- Air travel or diving - consider barotrauma
			- Touch - agnosing tenderness on light touch seen in very little conditions (see below)
		- _Quality_:
			- Jabbing pain - consider neuralgia
			- Dull, non-specific pain - consider other causes
		- _Radiation_ - consider the otalgia being referred pain from other areas
		- _Severity_ - agonising pain seen in few conditions
	- **Associated Sx:**
		- Deafness
		- Discharge
		- Tinnitus
		- Vertigo
		- Sore throat
		- Fever
		- Vomiting
	- **Drug Hx** - allergy to penicillin
- **Causes of agonising ear pain** - handle w/ care during P/E:
	- Perichondritis
	- Acute otitis externa (provoked by pulling the pinna or moving of the jaw)
	- Furunculosis of the external ear
	- Ramsey-Hunt syndrome

- **P/E:**
	- _Examination of the ear_:
		- General inspection - erythema of the pinna (perichondritis), and tenderness on palpation
		- Otoscopy - consider foreign bodies, vesicular rash, and the TM
		- Clinical auditory tests - Rhinne and Webber test
	- _Examination of the oral cavity_ - inspection and palpation
	- _Examination of the temporomandibular joints_- palpation at lateral aspect of the joint disc
- **Ix** - rarely required:
	- _Audiometry_ - audogram and tympanometry and physical measurements of volume of the ear can be performed
	- _Microbiology_ - swabs from discharges if TM perforated
	- _Radiology_ - CT/ MRI if suspecting extra-octic conditions (including malignancies)

### Otitis Externa

- **Definition** - also known as "swimmer's ear", "surfer's ear", and "tropical ear", is the infection of the external auditory canal
- **Predisposing factors of otitis externa:**
	- Allergic skin conditions
	- Earl canal trauma
	- Water penetration (swimming, humidity, showering)
	- Water and debris retention (wax, dermatitis, exostosis)
	- Foreign bodies
	- Contamination from swimming water including spas
	- Use of Q tips
	- Use of hearing aids
- **Etiological agents of otitis externa:**
	- _Bacteria_ - S. aureus, E. coli, Preteus, spp., Klebseilla spp, Pseudomonas spp.
	- _Fungi_ - Candida albicans, Aspergillus spp.
- **Clinical features of otitis externa:**
	- _Pruritis_ - typically the initial presenting features prior to onset of otalgia
	- _Otalgia_ - only in  70% patients:
		- Site - typically described as pain within the ear (w/ sparing of the pinna)
		- Provacation - increased pain w/ movement of the pinna or the TMJ
		- Severity - variable from mild to intense
	- _Discharge_ - typically scant
	- _Hearing loss_ - CHL may occur in the affected ear
- **Signs:**
	- **Inspection on otoscopy:**
		- Oedema (mild to extensive)
		- Discharge (offensive if coliform)
		- Black spores of aspergillus nigra
		- Pale cream 'wet blotting paper debris' of C. albicans
	- **Palpation** - mild tenderness elicited when lifting the pinna
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
	- **Aural toilet:**
		- Gentle suction and dry mopping of the ear cannal w/ a wisp of cotton wool on a fine broach
		- Ensures topical medications to applied into the skin
	- **Syringing** - generally not recommended
	- **Dressing** - insertion of a wick may be required in severe otitis media:
		- A wick soaked in an astringent (e.g. aluminium acetate 4% or glycerin and 10% ichthammol) +/- steroids and antibiotic ointment to reduce swelling and pain
		- Daily replacement of the wick until the swelling has subsided
	- **Topical antimicrobials** - the most effective being an antibacterial, antifungal and corticosteroid preparation:
		- _Selection_:
			- Kenacomb drops 2-3 drops tds
			- Sofradex drops 2-3 drops tds
			- Locacorten-Vioform drops tds
	- **Other measures:**
		- Strong analgesics
		- Oral ABx only if spreading ellulitis
	- **General advices:**
		- _Hygiene_ - keep ears dry, especially thos involved in water sports by water-proofing methods:
			- Cotton wool coated w/ petrolium jelly
			- Antiseptic drying agents after swimming and showering
			- Tailor made ear plugs
			- Bathing caps
		- _Avoid contamination_ -  avoid poking objects such as hair-pins and cotton buds for cleaning the canal
	- **Referral** - considered if:
		- Necrotising otitis externa
		- Septic manifestations (e.g. fever)

### Furunculosis

- **Definition** - staphylococcal infection of the hair follicle in the outer cartilaginous part of the ear canal
- **Clinical features of furunculosis:**
	- _Otalgia_ - usually exquisitely painful
	- _Fever_ - only occurs if the infection spreads in front of the ear as cellulitis
- **Signs** - visible furuncle in the EAM:

	- _Warmth_ - e.g. use of hot face washer, and hot water
	- _ABx_ - antistaphylococcal ABx if fever w/ cellulitis

### Otic Barotrauma

- **Definition** - damage of the tympanic membrane caused by undergoing rapid changes in atmospheric pressures in the presence of eustachian tube dysfunction
- **Pathophysiology of otic barotrauma:**
	- _Pre-existing eustachian tube dysfunction_ - pre-existing occlusion of the eustachain tube results in inability for maintaining the middle ear pressure
	- _Sudden increase in atmospheric pressure_ - failure of compensation of middle ear pressure results in progressive damage of the tympanic membrane
- **Clinical features of otic barotrauma** - may be temporary or persisting:
	- Otalgia
	- Deafness
	- Vertigo
	- Tinnitus
	- Discharge
- **Signs:**
	- _Otoscopy_ - TM shows changes of varying severity:
		- Retraction
		- Erythema
		- Haemorrhage
		- Fluid/ blood in middle ear
		- Perforation
	- _Tuning fork test_ - conductive hearing loss
- **Mx:**
	- _Reassurance_ - most cases are mild and resolve spontaneously in a few days
	- _Prescription_ - methanol inhalations are soothing and effective
	- _Referral_ - consider referral for politzer bag inflation or myringotomy if persistent
- **Prevention:**
	- _Air travel_:
		- Performing valsalva manoeuvre during descent
		- Use of decongestant before boarding and 2h before descent
	- _Diving_ - avoid when active nasal problems, otitis media, or chronic eustachian tube dysfunction
