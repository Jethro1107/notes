- **Forms of sacrifice:**
    - Actual immolation of an animal or a human (i.e. the victim)
    - Lynching
    - Symbolic sacrifice (e.g. mutilation, exorcism)
- Some rituals do not end w/ sacrifice (how to account for the disappearance of sacrifices)
- Components of sacrifice:
    - Unity - even if performed by an individual, the act is performed in the name of the community
- Relating to mimetic rivalry - the opposition of everyone against everyone is replaced by all against one
- The question becomes how mimetic crisis almost always culminates into the Sacrifice
- **Resolution of the prohibition-ritual juxtaposition** - it is important to maintain the fact that primative thinking influenced by the cathartic release of the tension built by the mimetic crisis through arbitrary sacriface results in such an interpretation:
    - <u>Acquisitive mimesis</u> - initially the desire of an object, which is then imitated by others
    - <u>Conflictual mimesis</u> - when sufficient people are charged by mimetic desire that mimesis is capable of self-propagation and hence the object loses value, mimesis is directed at becoming the opponent
    - <u>Victim selection</u> - hostility converge on a particular victim, which is seen as **unique and different**, everyone against everyone becomes all against one
    - <u>Sacrifice and diefication</u> - cathatic release, euphoric sensation of serenity influences retrospective analysis of the incident… all efforts directed towards maintaining this new found peace, as the victim ascends to become the sacred… this reconciles prohibitions and rituals
        - Prohibition - prevent mimesis (as above)
        - Ritual - an understanding that paroxysm of disorde is necessary for resolution to occur (a disease that cures a disease)

- A religion requires myths that involve at least a sacred being, and often power prohibitions and rituals
- **Religion arises from the same mimetic processes that gives rise to prohibition and rituals:**
    - A misinterpretation of the surrogate victim being responsible of both the near-disintigration of the community but also new-found peace
    - Variability in religious systems arises primarily because it is built on 'misinterpretation', the way it is misinterpreted is greatly dependent on the context of the mimetic crisis
- **Examples of myths arising from the victimage mechanism:**
    - Collective murder as in the Biblical story
    - Individual murder, but always appears as doubles (e.g. Cain and Abel, Romulus and Remus)
    - Reversal of the mechanism (e.g. myth of Noah)
- **The sacred contains two components and thus implicates our ambivalence towards it:**
    - Malevolence - as the cause of the dissolution of the community, the sacred is at times 'monstrous', 'vengeful' (N.B. divine retribution for lack of respect of the values maintained by the religious system)
    - Beneficence - new found peace through delivering of key knowledge to actively maintain new-found order (prohibitions and rituals)
- **If the same mechanism were still happening today, they are attenuated and thus unable to produce true religious systems:**
    - A spontaneous psychological experience of guilt, i.e. awareness of the scapegoat phenomenon to some degree
    - Favoured malevolent transference and diminuting benevolence transference, results in difficult deification of the victim (but why)
