# Ch3 Normal Child Development, Hearing and Vision

- **Aspects of development during childhood:**
    - <u>Child development</u> - skill acquistion between birth and 5y, during which there are rapid gains in <u>mobility</u>, <u>speech and language</u>, <u>communication</u>, and <u>independence skill</u>
    - <u>Development during school age</u> - evidence of developmental progression mainly in cognitive development and abstract thinking
- **Monitoring of development:**
    - By parents
    - At regular child health surveillance checks
    - Whever a young child is seen by a healthcare worker (opportunistic screening)
- **Aims of assessing development** - early detection of delayed or abnormal development in order to:
    - 1\. Help children achieve their maximum potential
    - 2\. Provide treatment or therapy promptly (esp. form impairment of hearinga and vision)
    - 3\. Point of entry for care and Mx of child with special needs
- **Factors influencing a child's development** - development represents the interaction of heredity and environment on the developing brain:
    - <u>Hereditary</u> - determines the maximum potential of the child
    - <u>Environment</u> - influences extent to which potential is achieved through <u>meeting a childs needs</u>
- **Development requires a child meeting physical and psychological needs:** 
![](img/development_dependent_on_environmental_ability_to_meet_needs.png)
    - <u>Infants</u> - dependent on carer for physical needs and require limited number of carer to meet simple psychological needs
    - <u>Primary school children</u> - semi-dependent for physical needs and cope w/ new social dynamics
    - <u>Adolescents</u> - typically dependent for physical needs, but may be exposed to greater complexities of emotional needs

## Fields of Development and Developmental Milestones

- **Fields of development** - practical compartmentalisation of neccesary skills acquired into four fields:
    - <u>Gross motor</u> - often most obvious initial area of developmental progress and easily accessed
    - <u>Fine motor and vision</u> - fine motor skills require good vision and are thus grouped together (i.e. adequate vision is a prerequisate to perform fine motor tasks)
    - <u>Speech, language, and hearing</u> - communication through speech and language is dependent on resonable hearing and are thus considered together
    - <u>Social, emotional and behavioural skills</u> - considered a spectrum of psychological skills
- **Nature and features of child development:**
    - <u>Evolution w/ chronological age</u> - similar to the concept of **growth chart** where growth parameters are achieved at a certain age, developmental milestones chart age ranges at which new skills are acquired
    - <u>Sequentially constant, but variable rate</u> - acquisition of **developmental abilities** in each field is remarkably constant between children, but may vary in rate
    - <u>Interdependence of fields of development</u> - deficiency in one skill area can impact acquisition of skill in another area (e.g. hearing impairment not only impairs speech and language, but in acquisition of social, emotional and behavioural skills)
- **Additional skills** - acquired by child and subsequently integrated at a later stage of life:
    - Cognitive - attention and concentration
    - Abstract thinking
- **Developmental milestones** (analogous to growth chart) - a list of skills and the age-range by which it is acquired with reference to population data, reflecting serial evolution of a child in the 4 fields of development:
    - **Whether development is normal** - median age and limit age:
        - <u>Median age</u> - age at which 50% of standard population achieve that level of development (i.e. age by which it is likely reached)
        - <u>Limit age</u> - age at which ~97.5% of standard population (defined by 2 SD) achieve that level of development (i.e. age by which it is expected to be achieved), where a proportion may have underlying problems or social deprivation
    - **Variation in the pattern of development** - not all children development in the same way, for example gross motor functions have variable patterns:
        - <u>Crawling</u> - initial stage by which infants achieve mobility, with limited age for progression to walking estimated at 18 mo
        - <u>Bottom-shuffling</u> - unconventional, yet may be normal (exclude CP) by which infants achieve mobility, although they tend to walk independently later
        - <u>Commando crawl</u> - similar concept to bottom-shuffling
