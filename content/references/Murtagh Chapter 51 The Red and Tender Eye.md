# Chapter 51 The Red and Tender Eye

- **Overview** - the red eye accounts for 80% of eye complaints in general practice:
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260503094552.png]]
	- Acute conjunctivitis accounts for most cases of red eye
	- When unilateral, consider more sinister causes, such as corneal ulcers, keratitis, FB, trauma, uveitis or acute glaucoma
	- The painful eye which is sight-threatened requires comprehensive evaluation to rule out glaucoma, corneal ulceration, and acute uveitis

- **Salient points of Hx:**
	- **HPI** - laterality, onset, progression:
		- _Laterality_ - unilateral red eye requires consideration of more sinister DDx (see below), while bilateral red eye is more likely to be viral/ allergic conjunctivitis
		- _Onset and progression_:
			- FB is typically a/w a very rapid hyperaemia
			- Conjunctivitis or uveitis generally has a gradual onset of red eye
	- **Associated Eye Sx:**
		- _Vision loss_ - loss of vision in the red eye raises suspicion of sinister anterior eye pathologies
		- _Eye pain and discomfort_ - ranges from mild itch, irritation to pain, where the painful red eye suggests sinister anterior eye pathology (see below)
		- _Discharge_
		- _Photophobia_
	- **Associated systemic Sx:**
		- _Recent URTI Sx_ - points towards viral conjunctivitis caused by adenovirus
		- _Genitourinary symptoms_ - consider rheumatological disorders such as AS, and reactive arthritis
		- _Bowel symptoms_ - consider associated IBD and AS
		- _Polyarthritis_ - screen for rheumatological conditions that predisposes to episcleritis
	- **POHx:**
	- **PMH:**
		- _Allergic rhinitis_ - any Hx of hay fever
	- **Social Hx:**
		- _Hx of exposure_ - exposure of 'red eye' at school, work, or home (e.g. have you been in close contact w/ others with the same condition)
		- _Associated trauma_ - as an indicator of intra-ocular foreign body (IOFB), including scratching of eye
		- _Associated incidents relating to exposures_ - e.g. welding, FB, chemicals
- **Considerations for the unilateral red eye:**
	- Viral conjunctivitis (most common)
	- Trauma
	- Foreign body (including IOFB)
	- Corneal ulcer
	- Iritis and uveitis
	- Acute glaucoma

- **DDx of red eye w/ loss of vision:**
	- Scleritis
	- Iritis
	- Acute glaucoma
	- Chemical burns
- **DDx of painful red eye:**
	- Scleritis and episcleritis
	- Keratitis
	- Iritis and uveitis
	- Acute glaucoma
	- Hypopyon
	- Endopthalmitis
	- Corneal ulcerations
- **P/E:**
	- Testing and recording vision
	- Meticulous inspection under magnification, w/ eversion of the eyelids
	- Testing the pupils
	- Testing ocular tension
	- +/- local anaesthetic test, fluorescein staining, subtarsal examination, slit lamp examination
- **Key approach to inspection of the eye** - start w/ general inspection of eye for trauma, and discharge:
	- _Cornea_ - evidence of oedema or abraisions (forescein staining), as well as foreign bodies (hidden under the eyelids)
	- _Injections_:
		- Site - localised (e.g. episcleritis or subconjunctival haemorrhage) vs diffuse
		- Level:
			- Conjunctival injections are smaller and branch from the corner of the eye towards the cornea
			- Episcleral injections are concentrated near the cornea
			- Ciliary injections found around the limbus of the cornea suggest deep-seated inflammatory condition (e.g. anterior uveitis)
	- _Iris_ - for irregularities, mid-dilation, and pupillary reflexes
	-

- **Ix:**
	- _Microbiology_ - swab of discharge for microscopy, culture and PCR
	- _Bloods_ - ESR/ CRP, HLA-B27
	- _Imaging_

## Acute Conjunctivitis

- **Definition** - conjunctival inflammation lasting < 3 weeks
- **Classification conjunctivitis:**
	- _Infectious - bacterial conjunctivitis, viral conjunctivitis
	- _Allergic/ toxic_- allergic conjunctivitis, contact hypersensitivity (dermato-conjunctivitis)
- **Clinical features of conjunctivitis** - generally characterised by 1) red eye, and 2) normal vision and absence of eye pain, but slightly differs based on the underlying cause:
	- _Red eye_ - diffuse hyperaemia of tarsal or bulbar conjunctivae
	- _Foreign body sensation_ - often a gritty, sandy discomfort, in the absence of eye pain
	- _Normal vision_ - vision is preserved w/ normal cornea
	- _Discharge_ - key difference based on the likely etiology
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260503173850.png]]
### Acute Bacterial Conjunctivitis

- **Etiological agents** - usually from contaminated fingers, towels, or face clothes:
	- Streptococcus pneumoniae
	- Haemophilus influenzae
	- Staphylococcus aureus
	- Streptococcus pyogenes
	- Neisseria gonorrhoea (hyperacute onset)
	- Pseudomonas aeruginosa
- **Clinical features:**
	- Gritty red eye w/ clear cornea
	- Purulent discharge
- **Associated features in Hx supportive of Dx:**
	- Purulent discharge w/ sticking together of eyelashes in the morning
	- Typically involves one eye and spread to the other
	- Often a/w Hx of contact w/ person w/ similar symptoms
- **Signs:**
	- Diffuse conjunctival injections and a non-specific papillary response
	- -ve Fluorescein staining
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260503174605.png]]
- **Ix** - consider swab for S/CT if:
	- Neonates
	- Hyperacute or severely purulent conjunctivitis
	- Prolonged infection
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _General advices_ - limit spread by 1) maintaining good occular hygiene, 2) use of separate towels, and 3) avoiding close contact w/ others
		- _Pharmacological Tx_ - saline irrigation, topical/ systemic ABx
	- **Topical ABx** - theoretically mild cases can resolve on its own but may take up to 14d:
		- Chloramphenicol 0.5% eye drops q1-2h for 2d, followed by qid for 7d
		- Chloramphenicol 1% eye ointment od nocte until discharge resolves for up to 7d
		- Framycetin 0.5% eye drops 1-2 drops q1-2h for 1d, followed by q8h until discharge resolves for up to 7d
		- Topical gentamicin or tobramycin (Pseudomonas infections)
	- **Systemic ABx** - required for N. gonorrhoea or chlamydia conjunctivitis
### Viral Conjunctivitis

- **Etiological agent** - adenovirus
- **Clinical features** - almost always a 2-3 week course initially unilateral but w/ cross-infection ocurring in the other eye:
	- Gritty red eye, extremely irritable
	- Copious amounts of watery discharge
- **Associated features in Hx supportive of Dx:**
	- a/w epidemics and a Hx of contact w/ person w/ similar symptoms
	- Often accompanied by a prodrome of URTI
- **Signs:**
	- Diffuse conjunctival injections
	- Follicular response in the tarsal conjunctivae (tiny pale lymphoid follicles)
	- Pre-auricular LN
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _General advices_ - limit spread by 1) maintaining good occular hygiene, 2) use of separate towels, and 3) avoiding close contact w/ others
		- _Pharmacological Tx_ - supportive Tx
	- **Pharmacological Tx:**
		- Cool compress
		- Topical lubricants (artificial tear preparations)
		- Topical vasoconstrictors (e.g. phenylephrine)
### Primary Herpes Simplex Keratitis

- **Definition** - a folicular conjunctivitis a/w lid and corneal involvement
- **Clinical features:**
	- Gritty, watery, red eye
- **Signs:**
	- Dendritic ulcers on fluorescein stain
	- +/- associated lid ulcers
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260503184850.png]]
- **Mx:**
	- Attend to eye hygiene
	- Debridment
	- Acyclovir 3% ointments 5 tiimes a day for 14d or for at least 3 days after healint
	- Atropine 1% 1 drop q12h for the duration of treatment to prevent reflex spasm of pupil

## Episcleritis and Scleritis

- **Anatomy:**
	- _Episclera_ - a vascular layer just beneath the conjunctiva and above the sclera
	- _Sclera_ - tough fibrous layer covering the eyeball
- **Episcleritis:**
	- _Clinical features_ - localised (sectorial) episcleral injections:
		- No discharge or watering
		- Vision normal
		- Self-limiting
		- 
		![[Pasted image 20260503185320.png]]
	- _Clinical associations_ - no particular clinical associations
	- _Mx_ - topical or oral steroids
- **Scleritis:**
	- _Clinical features_:
		- Violaceous injections (intensely red)
		- Eye pain
		- Loss of vision
	- _Clinical associations_:
		- Rheumatological disorders (esp. RA)
		- Herpes zoster infection
		- Granulomatous disorders (sarcoidosis and tuberculoisis)
	- _Mx_:
		- Urgent referral to specialists
		- Identification of underlying autoimmune conditions
		- Corticosteroids/ NSAIDs
