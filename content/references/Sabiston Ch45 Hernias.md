# Introduction

- **Definition** - An abnormal protrusion of an organ or tissue through a defect in its surrounding wall
- **Principles of abdominal wall hernia** - there is a reason for propensity of hernias to develop in particular sites:
    - <u>Lack of striated muscle support over aponeurosis and fascia</u> - e.g. inguinal, emoral, umbilical, linea alba, lower portion of the semilunar line, sites of prior incisions

  
  ![](img/visual_of_abdominal_wall_hernia.png)
- **Terminology:**
    - **Reducibility** - description whether its contents can be replaced within the surrounding musculature
        - <u>Reducible hernia</u> - its contents can be replaced within the surrounding hernia
        - <u>Irreducible (incarcerated) hernia</u> - its contents is not irreducible
    - **Strangulation** - comprimised blood supply to hernia contents (e.g. large hernias that have small neck)
    - **External vs internal hernia:**
        - <u>External hernia</u> - protrusion through all layers of the abdominal wall
        - <u>Internal hernia</u> - protrusion of intestine through a defect <u>within</u> the peritoneal cavity

# Inguinal Hernias

- **Definition** - a type of groin hernia above the inguinal ligament (medial and above the pubic tubercle), classified into direct, or indirect hernia
- **Epidemiology:**
- **Clinical features:**
    - <u>Groin swelling</u> - bulge in the inguinal region is the main diagnostic finding
    - <u>Associated pain</u> - mild pain or discomfort in the region (although 1/3 are completely asymptomatic except for the visible swelling), but extremely painful if incarcerated or strangulated
    - <u>Parasthesia</u> - ocassionally parasthesia over the inguinal region due to compression of the inguinal nerve by the hernia
- **P/E** - diagnostic:
    - **Position** - examined in standing (to maximally visualise the mass) and supine (to reduce the mass) position
    - **Inspection** - for any visible bulges, masses, asymmetry, scars, skin changes followed by cough impulse
    - **Palpation:**
        - **Confirm cough impulse** (after manual reduction of the hernia) - palpate over area w/ visible cough impulse:
            - <u>Direct inguinal or femoral hernia</u> - impulse progresses from deep to superficial
            - <u>Indrect inguinal hernia</u> - impulse progresses from lateral to medial
        - **Comparison between inguinal and femoral hernia** - palpate for pubic tubercle and assess anatomical relationship w/ pubic tubercle:
            - <u>Inguinal hernia</u> - medial and above pubic tubercle
            - <u>Femoral hernia</u> - lateral and below pubic tubercle
        - **Occlusion test** - differentiation between direct and indirect inguinal hernia (50% accuracy):
            - **Technique** - place hand over midpoint of inguinal ligament (midpoint between ASIS and PT) to occlude the deep ring, and visualise anatomical relationship w/ cough impulse
            - **Interpretation:**
                - <u>Direct inguinal hernia</u> - cough impulse visible
                - <u>Indirect inguinal hernia</u> - cough impulse controlled
            - **Clinical relevance** - not clinically relevant as repair is the same regardless of type
- **Dx** - clinical if +ve P/E findings, otherwise Dx by USG
- **DDx of inguinal hernia:**
    - <u>Other hernias</u> - e.g. femoral hernia
    - <u>Masses other than hernia</u>:
        - **Cutaneous and subcutaneous lesions** - e.g. lipoma, sebaceous cyst, haematoma, hidradenitis of inguinal apocrine glands
        - **Vascular lesions** - e.g. femoral artery aneurysm or pseudoaneurysm
        - **Lymphadenopathy** - reactive inguinal or femoral lymphadenopathy, lymphoma, metastatic neoplasm
        - **Testicular masses** - hydrocele, varicocele, ectopic testis, orcho-epididymitis, testicular torsion

    
    ![](img/ddx_of_sgroin_and_scrotal_swelling.png)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - **Approach to Mx dependent on Sx:**
            - <u>Asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic hernia</u> - watchful waiting w/ non-operative Mx
            - <u>Symptomatic inguinal hernia</u> - operative repair (open vs laparoscopic, tension vs tension-free, anterior vs posterior)
    - **Non-operative Mx** - watchful waiting:
        - <u>Indications</u> - for asymptomatic or minimally asymptomatic hernias
        - <u>Evidence</u> - Fitzgibbons et al. demonstrated no added risk (N.B. evidence is not generalised to F or femoral hernia as they are not included oin the study)
        - <u>Symptomatic Tx</u> - Truss, i.e. hernia belt (30% Sx relief)
    - **Operative Mx** - varying approaches:
        - **(Open) Anterior repair** - most common operative approach for inguinal hernia:
            - **Classification:**
                - Tension-free (mesh or Shouldice) repair - standard of care
                - Tension (tissue) repair - when mesh placement is contraindicated (e.g. simultaneous contamination or concomitant bowel resection)
            - **Technical details:**
                - Transverse/ curvilinear incision 1 fingerbreadth below the deep ring and above inguinal ligament
                - Dissection of the subcutaneous tissue and Scarpa fascia
                - Incision of the external oblique fascia to expose inguinal canal
            - **Tissue repair** - Bassini, McVay, Shouldice (considered tension-free)
            - **Tension-free anterior inguinal repair** - Lichtenstein, plug and patch, sandwich technique
        - **Open preperitoneal repair** - for repair of recurrent inguinal hernias, sliding hernias, femoral hernias and some strangulated hernias
        - **Laparoscopic repair:**
            - <u>Classification</u>:
                - Totally extraperitoneal (TEP)
                - Transperitoneal pre-peritoneal (TAPP)
