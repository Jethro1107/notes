# Ch50 Unintentional Weight Loss

- **Definition** - clinically important weight loss is defined as the loss of > 5% of body weight over a period of 6-12 mo
- **Clinical outcomes in UWL:**
	- Not uncommon in those > 65y, but under-recognised in patients who are previously overweight or no documented previous weight
	- Up to 25% cases have no identifiable causes despite extensive investigations
	- Weight loss in elderly a/w various deleterious effects, including 1) falls and fractures, 2) pressure ulcers, 3) impaired immune status, 4) decreased functional status, and 5) increased 1-2y overall mortality rate
	- Those w/ no known causes of weight loss have a better prognosis than those w/ known cause, especially when the source is neoplastic

## Physiology of Weight Regulation with Aging

- **Normal change in body weight and composition in adult life:**
	- _Total body weight_ peaks in the sixth decade of life and generally remains stable until the _ninth decade_, after which it _gradually falls_
	- _Lean body mass_ begins to decline at a rate of 0.3kg per year in the third decade, and increases further beginning at 60y for M, and 65y for F
	- In the healthy elderly, there is an increase in fat tissue that balances loss of lean body mass until very old age
- **Physiological aspects of changes in lean body mass and body composition in adult life:**
	- _Gradual decline in growth hormone secretion_ - resulting in reduced circulating levels of insulin-like growth factor type I (IGF-I), resulting in protein catabolism
	- _Reducing sex hormone levels_ - loss of sex steroids at menopause in F and more gradually in M also contributes to changes in body composition
- **Reduced energy intake in adult life:**
	- Mean energy intake reduced by up to 1200 kcal/d in M and 800 kcal/d in F between 20-80y
	- Due to a decrease in energy demand due to 1) reduced physical activity, and 2) loss of lean body masss
- **Elderly are physiologically predisposed to UWL** - "anorexia of aging" characterised by early satiety and a decline in both appetite and hedonistic appreciation of food:
	- Declining chemosensory function (smell and taste) that reduces appetite
	- Reduced efficiency of chewing
	- Slowed gastric emptying
	- Alterations in the neuroendocrine axis, including changes in level of _leptin_, _cholecystokinin_, _neuropeptide Y_
- **Other social factors predisposing to UWL in the elderly:**
	- Social isolation
	- Poverty
	- Immobility
## Causes of Unintentional Weight Loss
- **DDx of unintentional weight loss** - most causes belonging to one of four categories, including 1) malignant neoplasms, 2) chronic inflammatory or infectious disease, 3) metabolic disorders, and 4) psychiatric disorders:

- **Malignancies and weight loss:**
	- _Epidemiology_:
		- 50% of patients w/ malignancies lose some body weight
		- 33% of patients lose > 5% of total body weight
		- Up to 20% of all cancer deaths are caused directly by cachexia (mediated by immobility, heart failure, or respiratory failure)
	- _Most common malignancies a/w weight loss_ - malignancies that present as weight loss are a/w poorer prognosis:
		- GI and HBP malignancies
		- Haematological malignancies
		- Lung cancer
		- Breast cancer
		- Genitourinary malignancies
		- Ovarian cancer
		- Prostate cancer
- **Gastrointestinal diseases and weight loss** - almost all gastrointestinal diseases can result in weight loss:
	- _Disorders of oral cavity and dental problems_ - poor oral hygiene, xerostomia, inability to chew, non-occlusion, TMJ syndrome, edentulousness, and pain due to caries or abscesses
	- _Upper GI disorders_ - peptic ulcer disorders, atrophic gastritis are common causes
	- _Lower GI disorders_ - inflammatory bowel disease, coeliac disease, and constipation are common causes
	- _Pancreatic disorders_  - chronic pancreatitis
- **Chronic infections and weight loss:**
	- Tuberculosis
	- Subacute bacterial endocarditis
	- Parasitic infections
	- HIV
- **Metabolic disorder:**
	- _Thyrotoxicosis_ - often less prominent sympathomimetic featuers and may present as "apathetic hyperthyroidism" or isolated T3 thyrotoxicosis
	- _Diabetes mellitus_ - associated glycosuria and hypercatabolic state promotes weight loss
- **Chronic conditions:**
	- _Cardiovascular and pulmonary diseases_ - increased metabolic demand and decreased appetiete and caloric intake
	- _Chronic kidney disease_ - uraemia produces anorexia, nausea, and vomiting
	- _Connective tissue disease_ - increased metabolic demand and disrupt nutrtional balances
	- _Medications_ - various medications causing anorexia, GI disturbances may result in UWL
- **Neurological disorders** - all neurological disorders w/ permanent sequelae can result in UWL by impairing caloric intake:
	- _Visceral and autonomic dysfunction_ - results in abnormal motility and reduced caloric intake
	- _Dysphagia_ - results in regurgitation and reduced caloric intake
	- _Visual impairments_ - limits ability to prepare and eat meals
	- _Cognitive impairments_ - limited awareness of the need for eating meals
	- _Functional disability_ - compromises ADLs resulting in preparing, acquiring and eating foods
- **Psychiatric disorders:**
	- _Depression_ - cytokine-mediated inflammatory emtabolic cascade as the cause and manifestation of depression which results in weight loss
	- _Bereavement_ - not an uncommon cause of weight loss, and is often more pronouncced in M
	- _Alcoholism_ - often a due to primacy over food intake, resulting in both weight loss and malnutrition
	- _Other mental illnesses_ - paranoia, schizophrenia often due to the functional aspects of the diseases
## Assessment

- **4 major manifestations accompanying UWL:**
	- Anorexia (loss of appetite)
	- Sarcopenia (loss of muscle mass)
	- Cachexia (syndrome that combines weight loss, loss of muscle and adipose tissue, anorexia, and weakness)
	- Dehydration

## Treatment

- **Principles of Mx:**
	- _Identification of underlying cause_ - identify neoplastic, inflammatory/ infectious, metabolic, psychiatric, or social causes of undernutrition
	- _Nutritional supplementation_ - high-energy drinks can sometimes reverse weight loss, taken between meals rather than w/ meals to help minimise appetite suppression and facilitate overall intake
	- _Pharmacological Mx_ - in those w/ mood component, mirtazapine significantly increased body weight, body fat mass, and leptin concentration
	- _Other measures_ - exercise programmes for patients w/ cachexia to gain muscle protein mass, endurance, and ADL activity
