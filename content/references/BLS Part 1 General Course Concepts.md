# Part 1: General Course Concepts

- **Application of BLS:**
    - Recognize cardiac arrest
    - Activate emergency response system early
    - Respond quickly and confidently
- **Prognosis of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest** - remains poor:
    - <u>Survival</u> - only 10% adult patients with non-traumatic cardiac arrest treated by emergency medical services (EMS) survival to hospital discharge
- **Learning objectives:**
    1.  Importance of high-quality CPR and impact on survival
    2.  Describe and apply BLS concepts of Chain of Survival
    3.  Recognize the signs of someone needing CPR
    4.  Performing high-quality CPR for adult, child and infant
    5.  Importance of use of AED
    6.  Appropriate use of AED
    7.  Effective ventilation by a barrier device
    8.  Teams in multirescuer resuscitation
    9.  Technique for relief of foreign-body airway obstruction for an adult, child and infant
- **Cardiopulmonary resuscitation** (CPR) - lifesaving procedure and improves victim's chance of survival of cardiac arrest:
    - <u>Indications</u> - all victims who has signs of cardiac arrest (i.e. unresponsive, no normal breathing, no pulse)
    - <u>Components of CPR</u> - 1) chest compressions, 2) breaths
    - <u>Concepts of high-quality CPR</u>:
        - **Early CPR** - start compressions within 10 seconds of cardiac arrest
        - **Maintain CO** - push hard and fast, at rate of 100-120bpm, at depth of (\> 5cm in adults, 1/3 of depth in children or infants), with complete chest recoil in between each compression, and minimize interruptions
        - **Maintain ventilation** - effective breaths delivered over 1 second, enough to make victim's chest rise

- **Age definitions in BLS manual:**
    - <u>Infants</u> - \< 1y
    - <u>Children</u> - \> 1y to puberty (signs of secondary sexual characteristics)
    - <u>Adults</u> - adolescents (after onset of puberty) or older
