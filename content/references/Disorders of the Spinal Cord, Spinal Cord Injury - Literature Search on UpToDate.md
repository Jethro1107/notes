# Anatomy and localization of spinal cord disorders

## Spinal cord anatomy

- **External cord anatomy:**
    - **Spinal segments** - 31 spinal segments each with a pair of <u>ventral (motor)</u> and <u>dorsal (sensory)</u> spinal roots, combining to form spinal nerve that exit from the vertebral through the neuroforamina
    - **Emergence of nerve from the spine:**
        - <u>C1-7 nerves</u> - emerge from above their respective vertebral segments (i.e. C1 nerve emerge above C1 vertebrae)
        - <u>C8 nerve</u> - emerge from between C7 and T1
        - <u>Thoracic, lumbosacral nerves</u> - emerge from below their respective vertebral segments
    - **Connus medularis, cauda equina and filum terminalis** - distal features of the external cord owing to differential growth rates of spine and spinal cord:
        - <u>Connus medularis</u> - termination of the spinal cord near the lower largin of L1 vertebral body (i.e. L1/2)
        - <u>Cauda equina</u> - lumbar, sacral, and cocygeal nerve roots below L1 level, resembling a horse tail
        - <u>Filum terminalis</u> - fibrous cord of connective tissue extending from conus medularis and attaches to the dorsal surface of first Co vertebrae
- **Longitudinal organisation** - 31 spinal segments each with a pair of ventral (motor) and dorsal (sensory) spinal roots, combining to form spinal nerve that exit from the vertebral through the neuroforamina:
    - **4 regions of the spinal cord** - cervical (C1-8), thoracic (T1-12), lumbar (L1-5), and sacral (S1-4)
        - **Cervical cord** - sensory and motor innervation to the UL, and motor innervation to diaphragm:
            - <u>C3-C5</u> - innervation to diaphragm via **phrenic nerve**
            - <u>C4-C7</u> - innervate the shoulder and arm
            - <u>C6-C8</u> - innervate forearm musculature
            - <u>C8-T1</u> - innervate intrinsic hand musculature
        - **Thoracic cord** - sympathetic nerve supply and somatic nerve supply via IC nerves
        - **Lumbosacral cord** - sensory and motor innervation of the LL, buttock and anal regions, and parasympathetic outflow via sacral nerve roots, and sympathetic outflow via L1-2
    - **Cauda equina**
    - **Segmental level of spinal cord do not correspond to vertebral levels** - due to differential growth of cord and vertebral column underlying presentation of myelopathy:
        - <u>Cervical segments</u> (C1-8) - lie between C1-7 vertebral levels
        - <u>Thoracic segments</u> (T1-12) - lie between T1-8 vertebral levels
        - <u>Lumbar segments</u> (L1-5) - lie between T9-11 vertebral levels
        - <u>Sacral segments</u> (S1-5) - lie between T12-L1
- **Cross sectional anatomy** - central canal (CSF-filled), central H-shaped grey matter, peripheral white matter tracts:
    - **Gray matter:**
        - **Dorsal horn** - considered the entry point of sensory information, particularly involved in cord reflexes, and spinothalmic tract sensation (i.e. pain and temperature)
        - **Ventral horn** (anterior horn) - consists of **LMN** (alpha and gamma motor neurons), and interneurons that <u>integrate information from descending tracts of the pyramidal system and extrapyramidal system</u>
        - **Lateral horn** - found in T1-L2 segments mediating sympathetic outflow
    - **White matter long tracts:** 
    ![](img/white_matter_tracts_in_spinal_cord.png)
        - **Dorsal column** - fasciculus gracilis (LS), and fasciculus cuneatus (CT) containing axons of 1st-order neurons that convey **ipsilateral sensory information regarding proprioception and vibration** (organised such that cervical sections lies most lateral and sacral sections lies most medially)
        - **Anterior and lateral spinothalamic tracts** - containing axons of 2nd-order neurons that convey **contralateral sensory information regarding pain and temperature** (cervical most medial, and sacral most lateral)
        - **Corticospinal tract** - pyramidal motor control of the **ipsilateral muscles** (cervical most medial, and sacral most lateral)
        - **Other poorly defined tracts:**
            - <u>Descending tracts</u> - tectospinal tract, rubrospinal tracts, reticulospinal tracts, vestibulospinal tracts
            - <u>Ascending tracts</u> - dorsal and ventral spinocerebellar tracts, spinoreticular tracts
- **Blood supply** - single anterior and two posterior spinal artery from vertebral arteries, with radicular branches from aorta:
    - <u>Anterior spinal artery</u> - supplies anterior 2/3 of the cord
    - <u>Posterior spinal arteries</u> - primarily supply dorsal columns

## Clinical manifestation and localisation

- **Clinical suspicion of spinal cord lesion** - bilateral sensory and motor S/S that do not involve the hear or face
- **Clinical manifestation:**
    - <u>Motor deficits</u> - weakness and long tract signs below the segmental level
    - <u>Sensory deficits</u> - normal sensation above and reduced below the segmental level (a spinal level should be sought)

## Spinal cord syndromes

- **Definition** - specialised clinical syndromes with preferential involvement of different structures, pointing towards specific etiologies
- **Patterns of spnal cord syndrome:**
    - **Segmental syndrome** - pathologies affecting all functions of the spinal cord at a certain segmental syndrome
    - **Posterior cord syndrome** - bilateral involvement of the dorsal columns, corticospinal tracts, and autonomic tracts
    - **Anterior cord syndrome** - bilateral involvement of the anterior 2/3 of spinal cord, including spinothalamic tract, corticospinal tract, and descending autonomic tracts
    - **Brown-Sequard syndrome** - lateral hemisection involving ipsilateral dorsal column, CST and spinothalamic tract
    - **Central cord syndrome** - encrouchment of the medial aspects of the grey matter +/- CST, resulting in characteristic worse weakening of UL than LL, and loss of pain and temperature sensory loss of several adjacent dermatomes
    - **Conus medullaris syndrome** - lesion at vertebral level L2 affecting the conus medullaris resulting in sacral involvement
    - **Cauda equina syndrome** - compression of the nerve roots forming the cauda equina

## Clinical features and diagnosis of cervical radiculopathy

- **Definition** - clinical syndrome where pathological process results in inpairments of one ore more cervical nerve roots, often suspected when patient presents w/ neck pain and ipsilateral radicular Sx
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 1.2-5.8/1000
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Common in middle aged adults
        - Higher rates among male
- **Pathophysiology of cervical radiculopathy:**
    - <u>Nerve root or spinal nerve involvement</u> - various pathological process resulting in nerve ischaemia, inflammation, axonal loss and/ or demyelination
    - <u>Effects of abberant nerve transmission</u> - results in sensory dysfunction and weakness of muscles innervated by the affected nerve
- **Sites of radiculopathy:**
    - <u>Lateral recess</u> - compresses on traversing root rather than exiting root, such that it will affect one spinal level below
    - <u>Neuroforamina</u> - compresses on the exiting root
- **Etio-pathoanatomy:**
    - **Degenerative cervical spondylosis** - osteophyte formation at the zygopophyseal, uncovertebral or intervertebral joints:
        - <u>Spondylosis at uncovertebral and zygopophyseal joints</u> - results in narrowing of the neuroforamina and radiculopathy
        - <u>Spondylosis of the intervertebral joints</u> - rarely causes radiculopathy
    - **Disc herniation** - typically caused by herniation into lateral recess or far lateral herniation
- **Other causes mimicking cervical radiculopathy:**
    - <u>Ischaemia</u> - due to vasculitis or DM
    - <u>Nerve root trauma</u> - e.g. root avulsion
    - <u>Infectious cause</u> - herpes zoster, Lyme disease
    - <u>Extrinsic compression</u> - e.g. tumours, vascular lesions, granulomatous tissue
    - <u>Neurological disorders</u> - e.g. inflammatory neuropathies, neurodegenerative disorders (e.g. ALS)
- **Clinical features of cervical radiculopathy** - dependent on level of lesion:
    - **Temporal patterns:**
        - <u>Antecedent events</u> - e.g. physical exertion or C-spine trauma
        - <u>Onset</u>:
            - Causes related to spondylosis have more indolent onset
            - Causes related to disc herniation have a more acute onset
    - **Neck pain** - a common feature in all radiculopathies
    - **Radicular presentation of Sx:** 
    ![](img/symptoms_and_signs_of_cervical_radiculopathy.png)
        - **Sensory manifestations** - pain and parasthesia experienced in a dermatomal pattern:
            - <u>C5</u> - lateral upper arm
            - <u>C6</u> - lateral forearm, and extends to the hand particularly radial 2 digits
            - <u>C7</u> - in the distal hand around the middle finger
            - <u>C8</u> - medial aspects of forearm and hand, particularly the ulnar 2 digits
            - <u>T1</u> - medial aspects of arm and forearm
        - **Motor manifestations:**
            - <u>C5</u>:
                - Shoulder abduction, external rotation
                - Elbow flexion
                - Forearm supination
            - <u>C6</u>:
                - Shoulder abduction, external rotation
                - Elbow flexion
                - Forearm supination and pronation
            - <u>C7</u>:
                - Elbow extension
                - Wrist extension (principally radially)
                - Forearm pronation
                - Wrist flexion
            - <u>C8</u>:
                - Wrist extension (ulnar)
                - MCP extension
                - Distal finger flexion, extension, abduction, adduction
            - <u>T1</u>:
                - Distal finger abduction and adduction
- **P/E:**
    - **Neurological examination:**
        - <u>Muscle power</u> - by mMRC power scale as above
        - <u>Sensation</u> - assessment of sensation in dermatomal pattern
        - <u>Reflexes</u> - deminished jerks if level affected
            - Bicep (C5-6)
            - Brachioradialis (C6)
            - Triceps (C7)
    - **Provacation tests:**
        - **Spuuring maneuver** (Neck compression test):
            - <u>Principles and technique</u> - extension, rotation and lateral flexion to side of pain, followed by applying downward pressure:
                - Extension exacerbates disc herniation
                - Rotation and lateral flexion narrows neuroforamina
            - <u>+ve results</u> - reproduction of neurological Sx (pain, weakness, and parasthesia) in radicular pattern
            - <u>-ve results</u>:
                - No pain or parasthesia produced
                - Neck pain reproduced only (non-specific Sx)
            - <u>Caution</u> - do not perform if suspect C-spine instability
        - **Shoulder abduction relief test:**
            - <u>Technique</u> - abduction of shoulder, and rest extended hand on top of the head
            - <u>+ve results</u> - results in relief of radicular Sx
- **Ix:**
    - **Cervical X-Rays:**
        - <u>Standard orthogonal views</u> - evaluation of bone and disc spaces
        - <u>Oblique views</u> - for assessment of foraminal narrowing
    - **MRI spine** - ton confirm nerve compression
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Conservative Mx</u> - indicated for most patients for physiotherapy and medications
        - <u>Surgical Mx</u> - indicated for patients w/ persistent Sx on conservative Mx, or progressive neurological deficits
    - **Surgical Mx:**
        - **Approaches:**
            - <u>Anterior approach</u> - anterior cervical discectomy w/ fusion or artificial disck replacement
            - <u>Posterior approach</u> - posterior cervical foraminotomy
