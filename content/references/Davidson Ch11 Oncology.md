# Ch11 Oncology

## Clinical examination of the cancer patient

## The ten hallmarks of cancer

- **Hallmarks of cancer** (Hanahan & Weinberg) - characteristic features identified irrespective to cancer types:
    - 1\. Genome instability and mutation
    - 2\. Resisting apoptosis
    - 3\. Sustaining proliferative signalling
    - 4\. Evading growth suppressor
    - 5\. Enabling replicative immortality
    - 6\. Inducing angiogenesis
    - 7\. Activating invasion and metastasis
    - 8\. Reprogramming energy metabolism
    - 9\. Tumour-promoting inflammation
    - 10\. Evading immune destruction
- **Genome instability and mutation** - genomic instability enables accelerated process of accumulating driver mutations:
    - Although mutations occur continuously in all dividing cells of the body, under normal circumstances, surveillance system and <u>cellular DNA repair mechanisms</u> enable spontaneous mutations to be corrected, or induce apoptosis, such that <u>actual mutation rate is kept at low rate</u>
    - Mutations that in itself <u>induce genetic instability</u>, by <u>comprimise surveillance system that maintains genomic integrity</u> can accelerate carcinogenesis
- **Resisting apoptosis and cell death:**
    - **Principle mechanisms of cell death in healthy tissue:**
        - <u>Apoptosis</u> - **programmed cell death** through a cascade of proteolysis, cell disassembly with nuclear fragmentation, chromosomal condensation, shrinking of cell, formation of apoptosis and phagocytosis by neighbouring cells, is tightly regulated by **intrinsic** (p53) and **extrinsic** **pro-apoptotic signals**
        - <u>Autophagy</u> - **internal catabolic process** during which cellular constituents are degraded by lysosomal machineries **in response to catabolic stress**, and are found as a **cytoprotective measure for malignant cells** (e.g. during RT/CT)
    - <u>Necrosis</u> - **premature death of cells** that brings about an inflammatory response, and release of stimulatory factors that **promote proliferation of neighbouring cells** (i.e. promote rather than inhibit carcinogenesis)
- **Sustaining proliferative signalling** - growth and proliferative response that is beyond what is expected in normal cells:
    - **Normal cell cycle:** 
    ![](img/cell_cycle_and_regulation.png)
        - **Phases of cell cycle** - most cells remain in G0 (quiescent phase) in absence of cell signal or when they become confluent:
            - <u>G1</u> (gap 1) - period of commitment into cell cycle, where cell is receptive to growth signals and prepares for DNA replication, but may arrest in response to anti-growth signals, but will be locked in S phase once past restriction point
            - <u>S</u> (DNA synthesis) - DNA replication
            - <u>G2</u> - further growth and DNA repair with checkpoints prior to commit to mitosis
            - <u>M</u> (mitosis) - actual phase of cell regulation
        - **Regulation of cell cycle:**
            - <u>Cyclin and cyclin-dependent kinases</u> - kinases that are necessary in progression of cell cycle in checkpoints (G1/S, or G2/M transitions), where tumour suppressor genes (e.g. TP53, TP21, TP16) deactivate cyclin-CDK complexes when necessary
        - **Stimulation of cell cycle** - dependent on growth-factor-receptor interaction
    - **Mechanisms of acquiring sustained proliferative signals:**
        - <u>Autokine release of growth factors</u> - +ve feedback release of TGF-alpha, and PDGF
        - <u>Expression of constituitively active tyrosine kinase receptors</u> - e.g. acquisition of mutated EGFR (lung, GI) and HER2 (stomach, breast)
        - <u>Mutation of downstream signalling proteins</u> - e.g. activation of RAS protein, P3CK etc.
- **Evading growth suppressors:**
    - **Normal inhibitory factor on priliferation:**
        - <u>Cell-to-cell contact</u> - dense cell populations in healthy tissues acts as an inhibitory factor on proliferation (but contact inhibition is absent in cancer cell population)
        - <u>Growth inhibitory factors</u> - factors that modulate the cell cycle regulators, particularly via activation of cyclin-depedent kinases (CDK)
    - **Loss of tumour suppressor genes:**
        - pRB regulation
        - p21/ 27, p53
- **Enabling replicative immortality** - immortality of cells necessary for cancer cells to develop into macroscopic tumours:
    - **Normal limits of replicative potential by telomeres:**
        - <u>Telomeric DNA sequences</u> - DNA sequences that protect and stabilise chromosomal ends, are progressively shortened as they are difficult to replicate
        - <u>Loss of telomeres</u> - prevents cell replication when completely loss, akin to a 'mitotic hourglass'
    - **Acquisition of immortality in cancer cells:**
        - <u>Telomerase</u> - exclusively expressed in cancer cells at significant levels, enables adding of nucleotides to telomeres and <u>prevents premature arrest of cellular replication</u>
- **Inducing angiogenesis** - tumour growth requires a functional vascular supply:
    - <u>Increased expression of pro-angiogenic stimuli</u> - expression of **vascular endothelial growth factor** (VEGF), and **platelet derived growth factor** (PDGF)
    - <u>Loss of anti-angiogenic stimuli</u> - loss of expression of thrombospondin-1 (TSP-1) inhibits suppressive signals on endothelial cells
    - <u>Mutations of HIF-1 pathway</u> - see lecture slides
- **Activating invasion and metastasis** - complex steps that enable transport of malignant cells to distant sites, which forms micrometastases that eventually develop into macroscopic metastatic lesions:
    - **Normal barrier to invasion and metastasis:**
        - <u>Presence of basement membrane</u> - intact basement membrane prevents haematogenous and lymphatic access
        - <u>Cell-cell adhesion</u> - cell-cell adhesive glycoproteins (e.g. Cadherin-1 \[CDH1\]) enables organisation of cells into sheets of tissue, and increased expression is recognised as an **antagonist to invasion or metastasis**
    - **Mechanisms for activating invasion and metastasis:**
        - <u>Down-regulation of CDH1</u> - low CDH1 levels a/w down-regulation, or occasional mutational inactivation of CDH1 gene is observed in metastatic cancer, while in-situ tumours typically retain CDH1 production as evident by their organised architecture
        - <u>Expression of protease</u> - increased secretion of protease, and tissue metalloproteinase (TMP) by macrophages at periphery is associated w/ local invasion and subsequent metastasis
- **Reprogramming energy metabolism** - altered cellular energetics as to provide sufficient raw material for proliferation:
    - <u>Preferred pathway of cellular metabolism</u> - **aerobic glycolysis**, i.e. limiting ATP production to glycolysis even in the presence of O2:
        - 1\. Limit overall energy production (18-fold lower than in normal cells) as ATP is not typically necessary during carcinogenesis
        - 2\. Increased glycolytic intermediates that is fed into other biosynthetic pathways (e.g. amino acids, nucleotides that are necessary for biosynthesis)
    - <u>Mechanisms of altering cellular metabolism</u>:
        - 1\. Upregulation of GLUT1
        - 2\. Inhibition of downstream enzymes
- **Tumour-promoting inflammation** - presence of immune cells on histology historically thought to represent an attempt to erradicate cancer, but now known that tumour-promiting inflammation <u>promotes tumour formation</u> and <u>cancer progression</u>
- **Evading immune destruction** - disabling components of the immune system (e.g. immunosuppression against actions of cytotoxic lymphocytes)

## Environmental and genetic determinants of cancer

- **Cancer as a result of interaction between genetic factor and exposure to environmental hormones** - hence the majority of cancers do not have a single attributable cause
- **Environmental factors** - association is often weak, with evidence of a multifactorial pathogenesis even when their is a principal environmental cause (i.e. not all smokers develop bronchial carcinoma):

### Ectopic hormone production

- **Definition** - metabolic disturbances resulting from ectopic hormone production by cancer cells, including insulin, ACTH, ADH, fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23), erythropoietin, and PTHrP
- **Malignancies commonly associated w/ ectopic hormone production:**
    - Bronchial carcinoma - SCLC (ADH, ACTH), NSCLC (PTHrP)
    - Breast carcinoma (PTHrP)
    - Renal cell carcinoma (erythropoietin, insulin etc.)
- **Clinical features** - may be first presentation of malignancy:

### Neurological paraneoplastic syndromes

- **Definition** - group of conditions a/w underlying malignancy thought to be due to an immunological response to the tumour that results in damage to the nervous system or muscles
- **Cancers most commonly implicated w/ neurological paraneoplastic syndromes:**
    - Bronchial carcinoma (SCLC, NSCLC)
    - Pancreatic adenocarcinoma
    - Breast carcinoma
    - Prostate Cancer
    - Ovarian Cancer
    - Lymphoma
- **Peripheral neuropathy** - immune-mediated axonal degeneration or demyelination
- **Encephalomyelitis** - perivascular inflammation and selective neuronal degeneration a/w malignancy:
    - <u>Etiology</u> - most commonly SCLC (75%)
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - variable focal neurological deficits
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - **LP** - lymphocytic pleocytosis, elevated protein, absence of infection, carcinomatous cells in flow cytometry of CSF
        - **MRI** - meningeal enhancement (esp. at brainstem)
        - **Blood** - occasionally +ve Anti-Hu Ab
- **Cerebellar degeneration** - rapid onset of cerebellar ataxia:
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - rapid onset of immune-mediated cerebellar degeneration
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - limb, truncal and gait ataxia
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - **Neuroimaging** - CT or MRI shows <u>cerebellar atrophy</u>
        - **Blood** - circulating anti-Yo, Tr and Hu Ab (absence does not exclude Dx)
- **Retinopathy:**
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - immune response against retina
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - variable:
        - Blurred vision
        - Episodic vision loss
        - Impaired colour vision
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - Electroretinogram
        - Anti-retinal Ab
- **Lambert-Eaton syndrome** (60% attributable to malignancy):
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - Ab against pre-synaptic Ca channels
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - proximal muscle weakness that improves after exercise
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - EMG - shows low-amplitude compound muscle AP that enhances to near normal following exercise
- **Dermatomyositis and polymyositis** - rheumatological manifestation a/w malignancy

## Emergency complications of cancer

### Spinal cord compression

- **Definition** - compression of the spinal cord commonly as a result of posterior extension of vertebral body, or rarely, intrathecal spinal cord metastases
- **Epidemiology** - complicates 5% of all cancer, but most common in:
    - Multiple myeloma
    - Prostate cancer
    - Breast cancer
    - Lung cancer
- **Clinical features of spinal cord compression:**
    - **Back pain** - earliest sign is back pain particularly on coughing and lying flat
    - **Sensory and motor deficits** - often follows onset of back pain:
        - <u>Muscle weakness</u> - symmetrical, e.g. bilateral LL weakness with increased (or absent) jerks
        - <u>Sensory deficits</u> - sensory level w/ sensory changes developing in dermatomes below the level of compression
    - **Urinary and bowel Sx:**
        - <u>Acute retention of urine</u> (AROU) - results in acute suprapubic pain and oliguria, risking post-renal AKI
        - <u>Faecal incontence</u> - detected clinically by lost of anal tone
- **Signs of spinal cord compression:**
    - <u>Sensory</u> - symmetrical w/ sensory level
    - <u>Power</u> - symmetrical and profound loss
    - <u>Reflexes</u> - increased (or absent) knee and ankle reflexes w/ extensor plantar reflex
    - <u>Anal tone</u> - late loss

### Superior vena cava obstruction

- **Definition** - common complication of cancer resulting in obstruction of the superior vena cava, occuring through extrinsic compression or intrinsic compression
- **Etiology of SVCO:**
    - <u>Extrinsic compression</u> - lung cancer, lymphoma, metastatic tumour
    - <u>Intrinsic blockade</u> - in a/w central venous catheter, thrombophilia secondary to malignancy
- **Clinical features of SVCO:**
    - <u>Dyspnoea</u> (63%) - caused by oedema of the upper airway, pleural effusion, or rarely due to pulmonary embolism if related to thrombophilia
    - <u>Oedema</u> - due to impaired venous return of the upper limbs and H&N area, manifesting as **facial swelling** (50%), and **arm swelling** (15%), a/w signs of venous distension
    - <u>Chest pain</u> (15%) - only if complicated w/ pleurisy or pulmonary embolism secondary to thrombophilia
    - <u>Headache</u> - as a result of raised ICP and cerebral oedema arising from the backflow pressure, and characteristically aggravated by bending forward, stooping or lying down
    - <u>Dysphagia</u> (9%) - due to oedema of the esophagus

  
  ![](img/clinical_features_of_svco.png)
- **Signs of SVCO:**
    - <u>Venous distension</u> - venous distension of the neck and of the chest wall
    - <u>Oedema</u> - facial oedema manifesting as puffiness of the face (46%) and arms (19%)
    - <u>Plethora of the face</u> - due to accumulation of venous blood in the face
    - <u>Pemberton's sign</u> - plethora of face, dyspnoea, venous distension provoked by narrowing the thoracic inlet from raising the arm
- **Ix:**
    - <u>CT thorax</u> - diagnostic and enables distinguishing between extravascular and intravascular cause
    - <u>CT brain</u> - if cerebral oedema suspected
    - <u>Bx of tumour</u> - if unknown primary tumour
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Symptomatic relief</u> - by chemotherapy, palliative mediastinal RT, or stenting
        - <u>Workup and Mx of primary tumour</u> - long-term outcome is strongly dependent on the Tx of the underlying cancer
    - **Symptomatic relief:**
        - <u>Chemotherapy</u> - isolated chemotherapy can be aplied for chemo-sensitive tumours (e.g. lymphoma, germ cell tumours)
        - <u>Mediastinal palliative radiotherapy</u> - reduction of tumour load to reduce obstructive symptoms (Sx relief in 50-90% within 2 weeks)
        - <u>Stenting</u> - maintaining intravascular patency by introduction of stent in SVC, good if primary unknown or tumour is chemo- or radio-resistant

### Hypercalcaemia of malignancy

- **Definition** - most common metabolic disturbance in patients w/ cancer often caused by bone metastasis (and ocassionally as a paraneoplastic phenomenon)
- **Epidemiology** - most common metabolic disturbance in patient's w/ cancer (and most common cause of hypercalcaemia overall):
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 15-20/100000 in overall population
    - <u>Incidence</u> - variable among different cancers:
        - **Cancers w/ highest risk** (up to 40% patients) - multiple myeloma, breast cancer
        - **Cancers w/ intermediate risk** - NSCLC
        - **Cancers w/ relatively low risk** - CRC, prostate cancer, SCLC
- **Pathophysiology of hypercalcaemia of malignancy** - most commonly caused by bone-metastasis:
    - <u>Bone metastasis</u> - bone resorption resulting in release of Ca into blood
    - <u>Over-production of PTHrP</u> - results in elevated Ca by 1) stimulating osteoclastic bone resorption, and 2) increasing tubular resorption of Ca
    - <u>Lymphokine production for osteoclastic activation</u> - release of RANKL to stimulate osteoclast activation, a mechanism that is commonly involved in **multiple-myeloma**
    - <u>Extra-renal activation of vitamin D</u> - occurs in adult T-cell lymphomas
- **Clinical features of hypercalcaemia of malignancy** - note clinical presentation may be dominated by that of the underlying malignancy:
    - <u>Non-specific Sx</u> - fatigue, drowsiness, confusion
    - <u>GI Sx</u> - constipation due to functional ileus
    - <u>Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus</u> - polyuria, polydipsia, and dehydration
- **Ix** - note that total Ca on laboratory report may be <u>falsely low</u> due to <u>underlying cachexia</u>:
    - **LFT** - hypoalbuminaemia if cachexic with poor feeding
    - **Ca** - elevated and often underestimated (may require correlation w/ Alb):
        - Adjusted Ca = Ca level + 0.02 x \[40 - Alb\]
    - **RFT** - derranged especially if dehydrated
    - **Additional Ix** - Ix of cause esp. presenting w/ skeletal Sx:
        - Skeletal survey +/- XR of C-spine
        - +/- MRI spine (if suspecting cord compression)
        - +/- SPE (if suspecting myeloma)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Rehydration</u> - restore renal function and increase urinary Ca excretion
        - <u>Medical therapy</u> - IV zoledronic acid +/- calcitonin for life-threatening hypercalcaemia
        - <u>Assess disease progression</u> - HyperCa is often a frequent sign of disease progression hence further workup and review of anti-cancer therapy is required

### Neutropenic fever

- **Definition** - common complication of malignancy usually secondary to chemotherapy
- **Etiology of neutropenic fever** - noting last dose of chemotherapy in Hx is important:
    - <u>Chemotherapy</u> - neutropenia reaches a nadir in 7-10d which is when patient is most susceptable to infection
    - <u>Radiotherapy</u> - possible especially if large amounts of marrow irradiated
    - <u>Bone marrow metastasis</u> - manifests as part of pancytopenia due to bone marrow infiltration (more common in haematological malignancies compared w/ carcinomas)
- **Clinical features of neutropenic fever:**
    - <u>Fever</u> - often high and unremitting w/ marked systemic upset
    - <u>Localising Sx</u> - Sx may be non-specific with no foci of infection
- **Ix** - septic workup:
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, ESR/CRP
    - **Microbiological screen:**
        - <u>Blood cultures</u> - x2 both from peripheral and from central lines
        - <u>Urine culture</u> - obtain MSU for routine microscopy, C/ST
        - <u>CXR</u> - detection of chest infections
        - <u>Swabs for culture</u> (throat, central line, wounds)
- **Complications of neutropenic fever:**
    - Sepsis and septic shock (Hypotension at presentation is an adverse prognostic factor)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Other supportive therapy</u> - e.g. IV fluids, inotropes, ventilation, haemofiltrations etc.
        - <u>Empirical IV ABx to cover most likely organisms</u> - options include:
            - Broad-spectrum Cephalosporin (ceftazidime, cefotaxime)
            - Meropenem
            - Combination of anti-pseudomonal penicllin w/ aminoglycoside
            - Metronidazole if suspected anaerobic infection
            - Vancomycin if suspected G+ infection (esp. wound or central line infections)
        - <u>Consider antifungal if no clinical response after 36-48h</u> - amphotericin B or voriconazole

## Metastatic disease

### Brain metastases

- **Definition** - distant metastasis of a primary to the brain, being the most common brain tumours, and attribute to increasing morbidity
- **Epidemiology:**
    - 10-30% of adult cancer patients
    - 6-10% of paediatric cancer patients
- **Common primaries that metastasise to the brain:** 
![](img/primary_tumour_sites_that_metastasize_to_the_brain.png)
- **Common sites of metastasis within the brain** - most commonly the cranial parenchyma:
    - White matter of cerebral parenchyma
    - Cranial nerves
    - Intracranial vasculature
- **Clinical features of brain metastasis:**
    - <u>Headaches</u> (40-50%) - as a result of raised ICP from mass lesion +/- associated **vasogenic oedema** (maybe a/w N/V)
    - <u>Focal neurological S/S</u> (20-40%) - dependent on site of tumour, but typically presents as -ve Sx
    - <u>Cognitive dysfunction</u> (35%) - especially if affecting areas of cognitive function, memory etc.
    - <u>Seizures</u> (10-20%) - may result in epilepsy
    - <u>Papilloedema</u> (\< 10%) - frankly raised ICP
- **Ix and Dx** - Dx confirmed by CT or MRI
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Mx of raised ICP</u> - immediate Mx of raised ICP if predominant feature
        - <u>Tx of vasogenic oedema</u> - high-dose dexamethasone (4mg qid)
        - <u>Tx of seizures</u> - anticonvulsants
        - <u>Palliative Tx</u> - surgery and radiotherapy provides Sx relief but effects on OS is questionable
    - **High-dose corticosteroids:**
        - <u>Regimen</u> - Dexamethasone 4mg qid
        - <u>MOA</u> - reduce tumour-related vasogenic oedema
        - <u>Clinical efficacy</u>:
            - Symptomatic improvement
            - May improve survival for 2-3 mo
    - **Surgery** (often w/ adjuvant RT) - may be deemed curative for single site diseae pending neurosurgical evaluation
    - **Radiotherapy:**
        - <u>Whole brain irradiation</u> - mild prognostic effects (3-6 mo) not proven in RCTs, and reserved for **advanced brain metastasis**
        - <u>SBRT</u> - indicated for solitary disease where surgery is not possible
- **Prognosis** - extremely poor prognosis (in terms of mo), but dependent on primary

### Bone metastases

- **Definition** - metastasis of primary to bone, resulting in significant morbidity, and is the 3rd most common organ involved by metastasis after lung and liver
- **Common primaries that metastasize to the bone** - greater incidence of bone metastasis by tumours that do not target bone due to improved survival:
    - Breast cancer
    - Multiple myeloma
    - Prostate cancer
    - Lung cancer
    - Renal cell carcinoma
    - Thyroid carcinoma
- **Clinical features of bone metastasis** - main presentations include 1) bone pain, 2) pathological fractures, and 3) spinal cord compression:
    - **Bone pain** - characteristic progressive rest pain:
        - <u>Onset and progression</u> - insiduous, gradual onset
        - <u>Quality</u> - rest pain
        - <u>Severity</u> - variable, typically controllable by analgesics
        - <u>Timing</u> - worse at night; movement initially relieves pain, but in late stage provokes pain
    - **Pathological fractures** - acute onset of pain after trauma, typically a/w <u>breast cancers</u> (53%) due to underlying osteoporosis that is exacerbated by hormonal therapy (esp. AI)
    - **Spinal cord compression** - sudden onset of sensory level and bilateral LL weakness and constitutes medical emergency
- **Ix:**
    - **Ca** - detection of hyperCa
    - **Bone scan** - high Sn and inexpensive:
        - <u>Test properties</u> - high Sn
        - <u>Limitations</u>:
            - **FP results** - in healing bone (esp in healing bone)
            - **FN results** - occurs in **multiple myeloma** due to suppression of osteoblast activity
    - **Plain XR** - at any sites of bone pain to detect lytic/ sclerotic bone lesions
    - **PET-CT** - not as Sn as bone scan
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Pain relief</u> - provide analgesia
        - <u>Preservation and restoration of function</u> - IV bisphosphonates to improve pain and reduce skeletal related events (e.g. fractures and hyperCa)
        - <u>Local tumour control</u> - relief of tumour impingement by radiotherapy +/- alter anti-cancer Tx
        - <u>Skeletal stabilisation</u> - consider spinal interventions by orthopaedics
    - **Survical intervention** - indicated when:
        - Evidence of skeletal instability (slippage, e.g. anterior or posterior column fracture)
        - Evidence of impending fractor (e.g. large lytic lesions on a weight bearing bone w/ more than 50% cortical involvement)
    - **IV bisphosphonates:**
        - <u>Selection</u> - zoledronic acid, ibandronate, pamidronate
        - <u>Effects</u>:
            - Improve pain
            - Reduction of further skeletal-related events (e.g. pathological fractures, hyperCa)
    - **Radiotherapy** - modalities include:
        - Palliative external beam therapy
        - Systemic radionucleotide (strontium treatment)
    - **Additional therapy:**
        - Hormonal therapy for prostate or HR+ breast cancer may be important

## Therapeutics in oncology

- **Intent of therapy:**
    - Curative
    - Palliative
- **Intent of chemotherapy:**
    - <u>Palliative chemotherapy</u> - typically used to Tx patients w/ distant metastasis w/ goal of delaying disease progression, improving Sx and QOL (survival increments are secondary)
    - <u>Adjuvant chemotherapy</u> - given after a primary therapy that is designed to cyto-reduce the tumour bulk and remove all macroscopic disease, with an intention of eradicating micrometastastic disease and improve DFS and OS
    - <u>Neoadjuvant chemotherapy</u> - chemotherapy administered before a planned cyto-reductive procedure, increasing likelihood of successful debulking

### Surgical Treatment

- **Role of surgical oncology:**
    - <u>Biopsy</u> - tissue acquired for histological or cytological diagnosis of cancer to assist subsequent management
    - <u>Excision</u> - main curative Mx of most solid cancers in early, localised stages
    - <u>Palliation</u> - quickest and most effective way of palliating Sx

### Systemic Chemotherapy

- **Overview:**
    - <u>Classification by mechanism of action</u> - classified into non-cell cycle specific and cell cycle specific drugs
    - <u>Non-specific to cancer</u> - chemotherapaeutic drugs have greatest activity in proliferating cells, and this provides rationale for their use in Tx of cancer, but also its S/E profile
- **Mode of administration of chemotherapy:**
    - <u>Intravenous</u> - many are vesicant or locally irritant if there is extravastation and thus often given as an infusion to minimise the risk
    - <u>Oral</u> - newly developed but not many have replaced their intravenous counterparts
- **Rationale for combination chemotherapy:**
    - <u>Overcome drug resistance</u> - by targeting several pathways of the cell cycle
    - <u>Limit S/E</u> - by minimizing the dose used, and enable time to recover from short-term toxicity before the next dose
- **Adverse effects of cytotoxic chemotherapy** - narrow therapeutic index: 
![](img/adverse_effects_of_chemotherapy_and_radiotherapy.png)
    - **Myelosuppression** - common to almost all cytotoxics due to suppression of HSC:
        - <u>Implications of myelosuppression</u>:
            - Limits dosing of drug
            - Increased risk of life-threatening infections
        - <u>Timing of neutropenia</u> - nadir at 7-12d, and lasts for 5-7d
        - <u>Precaution</u> - G-CSF prophylaxis in regimens that confer high rates of neutropenia (\> 20%)
    - **Nausea and vomiting** - due to mucositis along upper GI tract resulting in release of neurotransmitters stimulating the CTZ:
        - <u>Precaution</u> - consider combination of **dexamethasone** with **ondasetron** (5-HT3 receptor antagonist)
