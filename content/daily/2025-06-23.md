# Advanced Cardiac Life Support - Literature Search on UpToDate

- **Reference:**
    - [Advanced Cardiac Life Support (ACLS) in Adults](https://www.uptodate.com.eproxy.lib.hku.hk/contents/advanced-cardiac-life-support-acls-in-adults?search=advanced%20cardiac%20life%20support&source=search_result&selectedTitle=1~150&usage_type=default&display_rank=1)
- **Nature of evidence** - few evidence is supported based on randomised control trials due to nature of resuscitation:
    - Observational studies
    - Animal studies
    - Expert Consensus
- **Principles of management:**
    - <u>Basis on excellent BLS</u> - excellent cardiopulmonary resuscitationand early defibrillation for shockable rhythms remain the cornerstone in ACLS, with additional roles of medications and monitoring that enable advanced-life support within hospital scenario
    - <u>Resuscitation team management</u> - principles of Crisis Resource Management (CRM) adapted from aviation industry and introduced into medical care by anaesthesiologists providse algorithmic pathways that **decrease disorgniation and improves patient outcomes**

## Management of cardiac arrest

- **Definition of cardiac arrest** - unresponsiveness and loss of pulse
- **Principles of Mx of cardiac arrest:**
    - <u>Order of importance during resuscitation</u> - circulation assumes more prominent role over airway and breathing (C-A-B) in initial Mx of cardiac arrest as DO2 is primarily limited by absence of cardiac output
    - <u>Circulation</u> - High-quality CPR and defibrillation takes presedence over medications contrary to common belief in their role to achieving ROSC
    - <u>Airway and breathing</u> - airway opening and rescue breathing can initially be achieved through bag-valve-mask ventilation (BVM) or supraglottic airway (SGA) after initiation of excellent CPR; **ET intubation is generally deferred after ROSC during post-arrest care**
- **Airway management:**
    - **Approach to airway Mx:**
        - <u>Bag-valve-mask device</u> (BVM) - compression to ventilation is 30:2 (mechanics of BVM is unable to maintain adequate tidal volume during active compressions)
        - <u>Advanced airway Mx</u> - supraglottic airways (SGA) such as naso-laryngeal and oro-laryngeal airways can be inserted to allow for **non-synchronised ventilation** at rate of 10 breaths per minute (6s per breath)
    - **Effects of over-ventilation:**
        - <u>Gastric inflation</u> - increased risk of regurgitation and aspiration
        - <u>Elevation of intra-thoracic pressure</u> - impedes venous return, ventricular filling and CO by compressions, resulting in inadequate cerebral perfusion (circulation takes precedence)
    - **O2 delivery:**
        - <u>During resuscitation</u> - reasonable to give 100%
        - <u>During post-cardiac arrest care after ROSC</u> - titrated to keep SpO2 \>= 94% (hyperoxia theoretically increase risk of oxidative brain injury after ROSC but observational studies are in favour of titrating to higher SpO2)
- **Medications used during CPR:**
    - **Epinephrine** (Adrenaline) - IV/IO 1 mg:
        - <u>Indications</u> - indicated for all cardiac arrest irrespective of rhythm
        - <u>Timing</u> - every 3-5 min:
            - PEA/ Asystole - 1st dose ASAP
            - VF/pVT - 1st dose after 2 min of CPR in shockable rhythms
        - <u>MOA</u> - vasoconstriction via alpha-1 agonism (increased diastolic BP to maintain coronary and cerebral perfusion at cost of peripheral perfusion)
    - **Antiarrhythmic agent** - amiodarone and lidocaine:
        - <u>Dosing</u>:
            - **Amiodarone** - IV/IO bolus 300mg, with repeated dose of 150mg as indicated
            - **Lidocaine** - Iv/IO bolus 1-1.5mgkg, with repeated dose 0.5-0.75mg/kg
        - <u>Indications</u> - for refractory VF/ pVT
        - <u>Timing</u> - given after 2nd unsuccessful defibrillation in anticipation of 3rd shock (esp. in those w/ witnessed arrest due to shorter time to administration)
    - **Magnesium** - IV/IO bolus 2g, repeated once, followed by maintenance infusion:
        - <u>Indications</u> - routine use not recommended, only inducated for polymorphic VT consistent with torsade de pointes

## Management of symptomatic bradycardia with a pulse

- **Definition and terminology:**
    - <u>Bradycardia</u> - defined as HR \< 60bpm
    - <u>Symptomatic bradycardia</u> - defined as bradycardia that causes patient to exhibit evidence of inadequate perfusion, usually entails HR \< 50bpm:
        - Hypotension
        - Altered mental status - including lightheadedness, pre-syncopal sensation and syncope
        - Signs of shock
        - Ongoing ischaemic chest pains
        - Acute heart failure - evidence of acute pulmonary oedema
    - <u>Etiology of bradycardia</u> - H's and T's:
        - Hypoxia
        - Hyperkalaemia
        - Toxicologic - overdose of beta-blockers, calcium-channel blckers, digoxin
        - Inferior myocardial ischaemia
- **Clinical approach to bradycardia:**
    - <u>General measures</u> - Establish monitoring (Oximetry, cardiac monitoring), O2 if hypoxaemia, vascular access (IV/IO)
    - <u>Focused assessment of haemodynamic instability</u> - check pulse:
        - **Sx** - ongoing ischaemic chest discomfort, lightheadedness, presyncope, syncope, dyspnoea (APO)
        - **Signs** - hypotension, signs of shock (e.g. deminished capillary refill), APO (basal crepitations)
    - <u>Mx options</u> - immediate synchronised cardioversion or 12-lead ECG (see below)
- **Approach to Mx of bradycardia:**
    - <u>Stable bradycardia</u> - monitor and observe
    - <u>Symptomatic bradycardia</u> - atropine, transcutaneous pacing +/- chronotropic agents
- **Atropine:**
    - <u>Dosing</u> - IV 1mg, repeated every 3-5 min to a total dose of 3mg
    - <u>Absolute indications</u> - symptomatic bradycardia due to:
        - High vagal tone - e.g. inferior myocardial ischaemia due to ACS
        - Medication-induced bradycardia - e.g. overdose of beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digitalis
        - High-degree AV block with narrow QRS complex - thought to emanate at or above the AV node as suggested by the QRS width
    - <u>Limitations</u>:
        - Mobitz-II or 3rd-degree AV block - pathology resides below AV node and is not subjected to vagal control
- **Transcutaneous pacing:**
    - <u>Indications</u> - initiated if persistent symptomatic bradycardia after atropine
    - <u>Precaution</u> - appropriate sedation and analgesia for conscious patients
- **Chronotropic agents** - IV dopamine vs epinephrine indicated if atropine administration and pacing not successful in alleviating Sx:
    - **Dopamine:**
        - <u>MOA</u>:
            - beta-1 agonism (selective at lower doses) - positive chronotrophic effect
            - alpha-1 agonism (occurs at higher doses) - vasoconstrictive effect (note initial vasodilatory effects at lower doses)
        - <u>Dosing</u> - 5-20 mcg/kg/min titrated against patient's clinical response
        - <u>Precaution</u> - low dose may precipate hypotension
    - **Epinephrine** - preferred over dopamine in concomittant hypotension:
        - <u>MOA</u> - beta-1 and alpha-1 agonist
        - <u>Dosing</u> - 2-10 mcg/min IV infusion

## Management of symptomatic tachycardia with a pulse

- **Definition and terminology:**
    - <u>Tachycardia</u> - defined as HR \> 100 bpm
    - <u>Symptomatic tachycardia</u> - defined as tachycardia that causes patient to exhibit evidence of inadequate perfusion, usually entails HR \> 150 bpm:
        - Hypotension
        - Altered mental status - including lightheadedness, pre-syncopal sensation and syncope
        - Signs of shock
        - Ongoing ischaemic chest pains
        - Acute heart failure - evidence of acute pulmonary oedema
- **Clinical approach to tachycardia:**
    - <u>General measures</u> - Establish monitoring (Oximetry, cardiac monitoring), O2 if hypoxaemia, vascular access (IV/IO)
    - <u>Focused assessment of haemodynamic instability</u> - check pulse:
        - **Sx** - ongoing ischaemic chest discomfort, lightheadedness, presyncope, syncope, dyspnoea (APO)
        - **Signs** - hypotension, signs of shock (e.g. deminished capillary refill), APO (basal crepitations)
    - <u>Mx options</u> - immediate synchronised cardioversion or 12-lead ECG (see below)

- **Approach to Mx of tachycardia** - based on haemodynamic instability of patient:
    - <u>Unstable tachycardia</u> - treated with immediate synchronized cardioversion unless sinus tachycarida; IV 6mg adenosine for some cases of SVT
    - <u>Stable tachycardia</u> - rhythm analysis on ECG to determine best course of action
- **Approach to unstable tachycardia** - <u>immediate synchronised cardioversion</u>:
    - <u>Indications</u> - all unstable tachycardia that is not sinus tachycardia:
        - Unstable SVT (consider adenosine for SVT)
        - Unstable AF
        - Unstable AFlu
        - Unstable WCT
    - <u>Precaution</u> - pre-medication with sedatives and analgesics for all conscious patients
- **12-lead ECG interpretation** - 3 questions must be answered to guide subsequent Mx:
    - <u>Is the patient in sinus rhythm</u> - no targeted Tx for sinus tachycardia with focus on treating underlying cause
    - <u>Is the QRS complex wide or narrow</u> - decision point between Tx as SVT or WCT
    - <u>Is the rhythm regular or irregular</u> - reflects potential underlying etiology
- **Mx of stable regular NCT:**
    - <u>Etiology of regular NCT</u>:
        - Sinus tachycardia
        - Paroxysmal SVT
        - Non-re-entrant SVT
    - **Trial of vagal maneuvers** - employed on appropriate patients while other therapies are prepared:
        - <u>Efficacy</u> - 25% of SVTs converted to sinus rhythm
        - <u>Types of vagal maneuver</u>:
            - Valsalva maneuver - forced exhalation against a closed clottis (nose pinched, mouth closed, bear down)
            - Carotid sinus massage
    - **Adenosine** - indicated if failed vagal maneuvers:
        - <u>Dosing</u> - lower doses into central vein for heart transplant patients (3mg):
            - 1st dose - IV bolus 6mg followed by 20ml saline flush (+/- elevation of extremity to ensure drug enters central circulation)
            - 2nd dose - IV bolus 12mg
        - <u>S/E</u> - brief but distressing and requires warning the patient:
            - Dysphoria
            - Dyspnoea
            - Chest discomfort
            - Flushing
        - <u>Continuous ECG monitoring after administration</u>:
            - Assessment if SVT is aborted
            - Reveals underlying rhythm (e.g. AFlu) after nodal blockade
    - **Rate control** - non-DHP CCB or BBs:
        - <u>Indications</u> - rate control if adenosine does not convert SVT
        - <u>Selection and Dosing</u>:
            - **Non-DHP CCBs** - diltiazem (10-20mg IV over 2 min, repeat 20-25 mg IV after 15 min), verapamil (2.5-5mg IV over 2 min, repeat 5-10mg IV every 15-30 minutes)
            - **BBs** - metoprolol (5mg IV every 2-5 min for 3 doses, 200mg oral q12h), atenolol, esmolol, blabetalol
        - <u>Precaution</u> - ensure patient remains haemodynamically stable (not in APO)
- **Mx of stable irregular NCT** - most commonly new-onset AF:
    - **No role of vagal maneuvers and adenosine** - these cannot abort rhythm through inducing nodal blockade
    - **Rate control** - non-DHP CCB or BBs:
        - <u>Indications</u> - rate control if adenosine does not convert SVT
        - <u>Selection and Dosing</u>:
            - **Non-DHP CCBs** - diltiazem (10-20mg IV over 2 min, repeat 20-25 mg IV after 15 min), verapamil (2.5-5mg IV over 2 min, repeat 5-10mg IV every 15-30 minutes)
            - **BBs** - metoprolol (5mg IV every 2-5 min for 3 doses, 200mg oral q12h), atenolol, esmolol, blabetalol
        - <u>Precaution</u> - ensure patient remains haemodynamically stable (not in APO)
        - <u>Monitoring</u> - monitor for hypotension (consider lower doses)
- **Mx of regular WCT:**
    - **Adenosine:**
        - <u>Indications</u> - stable, regular, monomorphic WCT (never give if unstable, irregular or polymorphic)
    - **Anti-arrhythmic infustion:**
        - **Procainamide** - Class I anti-arrhythmic:
            - <u>Dosing</u>:
                - 20-50 mg/min until:
                    - Arrhythmia suppressed
                    - Hypotension
                    - QRS duration \> 50%
                    - Maximum dose 17 mg/kg
                - 1-4mg/min maintenance infusion
            - <u>Caution</u> - avoid if prolonged QTc, or CHF
        - **Amiodarone** - Class III anti-arrhythmic:
            - <u>Dosing</u>:
                - First dose - 150mg over 10 min (repeat as needed if VT reccurs)
                - Maintenance infusion - 1mg/min for first 6h
        - **Sotalol:**
            - <u>Dosing</u> - 100mg (1.5mg/kg) over 5 min
            - <u>Caution</u> - avoid if prolonged QT
