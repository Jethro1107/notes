# GC211 Varicose veins, disease of the veins and lymphatics, venous thrombosis

## Anatomy

- Venous system - superficial, deep, communicating
- Drainage into saphenous opening - greater saphenous veins draing into common femoral feins, with tributaries, defect in deep fascia 2cm lateral and below the pubic tubercle
- Greater and lesser saphenous vein

## Chronic venous insufficiency (CVI)

- **Definition** - spectrum of clinical manifestations related to diseased venous valves
    - Sapheno-femoral incompetence (most common cause of CVI) - incompetance of valves in communcation between greater saphenous vein and common femoral vein
- **Etiology:**
    - Primary - congenital (+ve FHx), posture (prolonged standing), other risk factors related to increased intra-abd pressure (straining, weight lifting)
    - Secondary - post-thrombotic (DVT) with destruction of deep vein valves
- **Pathophysiology of CVI:**
    - Incompetence of valves in superficial, deep or perforating systems 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/pathophysiology_of_cvi.png)
    - Reflux via incompetent valves results in accumulation of blood in venous system despite skeletal muscle contraction –\> ambulatory venous hypertension
    - Starling's force - increased venous hydrostatic pressure resulting in oedema
    - Extravastation of RBC –\> RBC dies and haemosiderin deposition –\> venous staining
    - Plasma protein mediated inflammation –\> causes pruritis, thickened skin, and venous ulcers
- **Clinical manifestations:**
    - Varicose veins (mild) - accumulation of pressure in venous system –\> dilated and tortuous veins seen especially as superficial veins
        - S/S:
            - Disfigurement
            - Swelling and ache
            - Complications - bleeding, thrombosis
    - Swelling, eczema, pigmentation (moderate)
    - Ulcers (severe)
- Clinical classification - CEAP classification (Clinical, Etiology, Anatomy, Pathology):
    - C1 - telangiectasia, resticular veins
    - C2 - varicous veins
    - C3 - oedema
    - C4 - pigmentation, eczeema, beta-lipo-dermatosiderosis
    - C5 - healed ulcer
    - C6 - active ulcer
    - Etiology - congenital, primary, secondary
    - Anatomy - superficial, perforators, deep
    - pathophysiology - reflux, obstruction, both
- P/E - tournequet test
- Ix:
    - US of axial veins - demonstrate axial reflux (no reason to perform on tortuous parts) +/- perforators
        - Doppler or Duplex
        - Demonstrate reflux on standing position, compression at calf, then release to detect reflux
- Mx - principle is to reduce venous pressure:
    - Elevation
    - Postural adjustment
    - Graduated compression stockings - creates pressure gradient because tightest at the ankle and loosest near the knee
    - Surgical Mx - ligate incompetent perforators, and removed diseased veins
