## Hallux valgus

- Definition - used to describe any enlargement or deformity of the MTP joint, but first described by Carl Hueter in 1871 as the lateral deviation of the great toe from medial plane of the body (i.e. Bunions)
- DDx
    - Gout
    - Enlarged bursa
    - Overlying ganglion
- Etiology:
    - Juvenile - lateral deviation of articular surface of metatarsal head w/o sublaxation of MPTTJ
    - Acquired:
        - Abnormal foot mechanics
        - Contracted achilles tendon
        - Pes planus
        - Generalised neuromuscular disease
        - CVA
        - Hindfoot deformity
        - Inflammatory arthritis
- Special anatomy of 1st toe:
    - No muscle attachment to 1st metatarsal head
    - A sesamoid complex - contains 2 sesamoid bone containing in each flexor hallucus brevis
    - Attachment to base of proximal phalanx by fibrous plantar plate
    - Tendon and muscles - all cross the 1st metatarsal head and insert to proximal phalynx (i.e. MTP joint is quite unstable)
        - Dorsal group - long and short extensor tendons (EHL, EHB)
        - Plantar group - long and short flexor tendons (FHL, FHB)
        - Medial - abductor hallucis
        - Lateral - adductor hallucis
    - Read key points of pathophysiology
    - Pathogenesis:
        - Intrinsic - gender, genetics, pes planus, ligamentous laxity
        - Extrinsic - shoewear, trauma (e.g. amputation of the 2nd toe)
    - Demographics:
        - Female preponderance - 2-15:1 (type of shoes have effect, e.g. fashionable female shoes are less physiological)
    - Genetics - moderate to high hertability (47.7% with +ve FHx)
    - Pes planus - similar incidence but no correlation of severity
    - Effects of shoewear - symptomatic and painful bunion more common in shoe wearing population
- Read clinical evaluation on slides
- Ix:
    - XR - must be done in weight bearing position:
        - AP, lateral and oblique views are taken
        - Interpretation
            - Hallux valgus angle - angle between bisector of metatarsal and bisector of proximal pharynx
            - 1-2 Intermetatarsal angle - angle between first metatarsal and second metatarsal
            - Hallux valgus interphalangeus angle - angle between bisector of proximal and distal phalynx
            - Distal metatarsal articular angle - relationship between articular surface to axis of 1st MT
            - Congruency - relationship between base of proximal phalanx vs articular surface of 1st MT head (differentiate between juvenile and acquired)
- Dx - based on angles (controversal regarding HVA)
- Mx:
    - Conservative - symptomatic only, no effect on prevention or slow disease progression
        - Avoid tight-fitting/ high-heeled footwear
        - Inserts - Hallux valgus splints, bunion shields, toe spacers, night splints
        - Analgesics for pain relief
        - Exercise - abductor allucis muscle training (low level of evidence)
        - Shoe wear advice - avoid high heel, narrow toe box shoes with proper shoe size
        - Orthosis - toe spreader (mild-to-moderate halux valgus). elastic HV splint, night splints
    - Surgery:
        - Indications - failed conservative treatment, progressive deformity

## Rupture of tendo achilles

- Achilles - thickest tendon of the human body attaching plantaris, gastronemius and soleus to calcaneus (responsible for plantar flexion of foot and flexion of knee)
- Most commonly ruptured tendon of the humon body:
    - Demographic - weekend warrior (enthusiastically engages in activity or hoppy on the weekends), 30-40y, male preponderance
- Anatomy:
    - Blood supply - mesotenon and musculotendinous junction, and osseotendinous junction
    - Watershed area - 4cm from calcaneal insertion (least blood supply)
- Cause of rupture - forceful contraction of muscle at jumping rather than landing (see biomechanics)
- Presentation:
    - Pop sounds and felt like someone kick me from behind (mistakened due to pain from behind)
    - Loss of power and inability to walk (e.g. can't plantar flex the foot)
- P/E - examine in prone position
    - Inspection/ palpation - look for gap of achilles tendon
    - Move:
        - Matles test - loss of rest tension
        - Thompson test - calf squeeze test
- Ix - USG (continuity of tendon), XR to r/o avulsion fracture
- Mx:
    - Principle of tendon healing - same 4 phases of healing:
        - Haematoma and inflamm
        - Proliferation (weeks) - proliferation of fibroblast and deposition of collagen
        - Muturation/ remodelling (mo) - reorganisation of collagen and establish cross linking into longitudinal manner instead of haphazardly deposited collagen fibres
    - 9-12 mo for tendon to heal
    - Immobilisation - principles and evidence (see slides)
    - Surgery - open vs minimally invasive (not always employed)
    - Functional rehab preferred over surgery:
        - Boot - keep foot in equinas position

## Diabetic foot ulcers

- Mortality after ulcer and foot amputation –\> higher mortality than breast cancer
- P/E - ABI –\> but diabetics have calcified arteries (falsly high ABI) and sensory testing:
    - Sensory - loss of protective sensory (predict future risk of complications)
- Mx - IWGDF guidelines (see slides)
    - Sharp debridement
    - Off loading
    - Diabetic control educations
