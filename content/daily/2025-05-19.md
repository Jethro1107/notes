# Recurrent pylogenic cholangitis - Literature Search on UpToDate

- **Definition** - disease characterised by recurrent intra-biliary pigment stone formation, resulting in stricturing of the biliary tree and biliary obstruction w/ recurrent bouts of cholangitis
- **Epidemiology** - Disease of the East:
    - <u>Demographic</u> - peak incidence in 3rd-4th decade of life, no sex predisposition, a/w rural populations and low SES
- **Etiology of RPC** - not fully understood with different stand alone theories:
    - <u>Biliary parasitosis</u> (liver flukes) - Clonorchis sinesis, Opisthorchis species, Fasciola hepatica infections in the liver proposed to cause biliar injury and obstruction, initiating the disease (however not uniformly present \[20-45%\] in observational studies)
    - <u>Bacterial infection</u> - bacterial glucuronidase causes de-conjugation in intra-hepatic bile-ducts resulting in formation of pigment stones, which cause further bile stasis and additional stone formation
    - <u>Stasis</u> - stasis due to stricturing from recurrent cholangitis as the predominant disease mechanism after the pathophysiological cycle is established (see below)
- **Pathophysiology of RPC** - incompletely understood:
    - <u>Initial biliary injury</u> - biliary injury from liver flukes or bactebilia (from transient portal bacteraemia or translocation of gut microbiota) results in secondary stone formation by causing bile stasis
- **Clinical manifestations** - recurrent bouts of cholangitis characterised by Charcot's triad
- **Dx of RPC** - established by imaging of the liver and biliary system in patient w/ combatible Hx:
    - **Trans-abdominal ultrasound** - most useful initial Ix:
        - <u>Findings</u> - intra-hepatic ductal dilatations w/ stones (85-90%) +/- liver abscesses
    - **CT abdomen** - complementary to cholangiography as cholangiography may not fully visualise obstructed segments due to stricturing:
        - <u>Findings</u> - demonstration and localisation of the disease (typically left lobe):
            - Dilated central intrahepatic ducts
            - Abrupt tapering of peripheral ducts
            - Enhancement of duct walls
            - Hepatic abscesses
            - Bilomas
            - Stones

    - **Cholangiography** - MRCP, ERCP, PTC:
        - <u>Findings</u> - detailed visualisation of involvement of biliary system
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Tx of acute complications</u> - episodes of acute cholangitis treated with supportive care and biliary drainage
        - <u>Prevention of long-term complication</u> - periodic clearance or definitive surgery
        - <u>Surveillance for CC</u> - clinical suspicion of deterioration in patient w/ Hx of RPC should raise suspicion of intra-hepatic CC in the atrophied left lobe
    - **Periodic intra-hepatic stone clearance:**
        - <u>Approach</u> - percutaneous (T-tube tract), endoscopic (e.g. ERCP) or surgical
        - <u>Outcomes</u> - stone recurrence in \> 30% patients despitec complete clearance
    - **Hepatic resection** - considered definitive in minority of patients in whom disease is localised
