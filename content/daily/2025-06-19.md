# GC - CCMU Point of Care Ultrasound (CCMU) <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- **Point of care** - focused exam (organ/ symptom/ guides procedures), goal directed (improve outcomes, answer aforementioned pre-set questions, diagnosis or exclude certain conditions)

- **POCUS** - useful bedside tool for rapid diagnosis, guide treatment and assist procedure

- **Equipment PCOUS:**

- **VSCanAir:**

    - Two probes - curvi-linear probe (abdomen, cardiac) and linear probe (lung, vascular)
    - Presets - need to set for specific

- **Echogenicity** - ability of a structure to produce USG echos:

    - <u>Iscoechoic</u> - similar echogenicity as surrounding tissue
    - <u>Hyperechoic</u> - increased echogenicity (e.g. stones, calcification)
    - <u>Hypoechoic</u> - reduced echogenicity (e.g. LN)
    - <u>Isoechoic</u> - no echogenicity (e.g. fluid)

- **Image optimisation:**

    - Sliding
    - Tilting
    - Rotating
    - Rocking

- **Colour flow doppler** - red represents flow towards probe, and blue represents flow away probe

## Cardiac echo

- **Positioning** - 45 degrees or left lateral position
- **4 basic view of cardiac echo:**
    - Parasternal long axis
    - Parasternal short axis
    - Apical 4 chamber
    - Subxiphoid

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/four_views_of_cardiac_echo.png)
- **Parasternal long axis:**
    - <u>Approach</u>:
        - 1\. Cardiac mode
        - 2\. Indicator to R shoulder
        - 3\. Nipple line/ 4th intercostal space

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/parasternal_long_axis_view.png)
    - <u>Rules of three</u>:
        - LA, Aortic outflow tract, RV have similar calibre
- **Parasternal short axis:**
    - <u>Approach</u> - from Parasternal long axis, rotate 90 degrees where indicator points towards L shoulder:
        - Initially shows mid papillary view - fan toward apex
        - Tilt down to view MV in fish mouth view
        - Tilt down to visualise superiorly to view inverted mercedes sign of aortic valve
- **Apical 4 chamber view:**
    - <u>Approach</u> - from parasternal short axis view, move across to apex with indicator pointing towards left shoulder
    - Best for assessing chamber enlargement and valvular functions
    - Note RV always smaller than LV - upper limit of RV is 2/3 size of LV
- **Subxiphoid view** - use liver to cast acoustic shadow on 4 chambers of the heart:
    - <u>Approach</u>:
        - 1\. Indicator to patient's left
        - 2\. Overhand grip on the probe
        - 3\. Subxiphoid region
        - 4\. Probe nearly parallel to skin
- **Inferior vena cava** - assessment of patient fluid status:
    - <u>Approach</u> - rotate counterclockwise from subxiphoid view 90 degrees to view IVC in liver draining into RA
    - <u>Size corresponds to fluid status</u>:
        - Normal - ~ 2cm
    - <u>Caval Index</u> - correlates to CVP:
        - Maximal IVC diameter - minimal diameter / Maximum IVC diameter

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/caval_index.png)
- **Application of the cardiac echo:**
    - Cardiac function - systolic (LVEF) and diastolic:
    - Valvular assessment
    - Fluid status
    - Pericardial effusion +/- tamponade
    - Acute right heart strain
    - Aortic dissection
- **Left ventricular ejection fraction** (LVEF) - assessment of cardiac systolic function:
    - <u>View</u> - parasternal long axis view
    - Eyeballing:
        - Squeeze of LV
        - Distance of anterior MV leaflet to septum on systole
    - Values:
        - Normal (\> 50%) - good squeeze and anterior MV leaflet goes against IVS
        - Moderately depressed (30-50%) - some squeeze, and some MV leaflet motion
        - Severely depressed (\< 30%) - minimal squeeze, reduced MV leaflet motion
- **Valvular assessment:**
    - Morphology of valves - number of leaflets, movement of leaflets, rheumatic heart valves, calcification, vegetation
    - Doppler assessment - Allows antegrade flow and prevent retrograde flow
    - Mitral valve - assess based on parasternal long axis view:
        - Mitral stenosis - indicated by excess turbulent flow during diastole on doppler (mixture of signals within the LV)
        - Can assess roughly the severity of mitral regurgitation by regurgitation jet relative to LA area:
            - Mild MR - \< 20% of LA area
            - Moderate MR - 20-40% of LA area
            - Severe MR - \> 40% of LA area

      
      ![](~/Documents/notes/img/mild_mr_on_parasternal_long_axis_view.png)
    - Aortic valve - best accessed based on parasternal long and short axis view: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/valvular_defect_of_aortic_valve.png)
    - **Calcified aortic valve:**
        - Hyperechoic valves on both views
        - Parasternal long axis reveals minimal motion
        - Parasternal short axis reveals minimal lumen
    - **AR** - shows jet of aortic regurgitation on parasternal long axis view
    - **Tricuspid valve** - best viewed on apical 4 chamber view
- **Pericardial effusion:**
    - Fluid (anechoic) viewed anterior to descending aorta (cf posterior in pleural effusion) in parasternal long axis view
    - Tamponade - earliest sign is the systolic collapse of RA, and in late stage reviews diastolic collapse of RV
- **RV strain:**
    - RV dilatation with bowing of septum during systole into LV resulting in D-shaped LV (on parasternal short axis view)
- **Aortic dissection:**
    - Dilated aortic root on parasternal long axis view (rule of 3rds violated)
    - Dissection flap
    - Aortic regurgitation
- FATE and ACES protocol - for patient in shock (read up)
- Indications for certain prentation:
    - Chest pain
    - Dyspnoea
    - Shock
    - Tachycardia
    - Cardiac arrest

## Lung

- **Probes for lung USG** - both:

    - <u>Linear</u> - deeper structures
    - <u>Curvilinear</u> - assessment of pleural effusion

- **3 standard positions for lung USG** (L&R):

    - 1 - anterior
    - 2 - lateral
    - 3 - posterolateral

- **Structures on lung USG:**

    - Hyperechoic ribs
    - lung parenchyma between two rib shadows (acoustic shadowing)
    - Pleural lines
    - A lines - equal distance from each other caused by reflections of waves from pleura

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/normal_lung_usg.png)

    - Look for lung sliding - rubbing of visceral and parietal pleura

- Absence of normal lung sliding - does not equate pneumothorax:

    - Pneumothorax
    - Pulmonary fibrosis
    - Previous pleurodesis

- Lung point - sonographic sign definitive for pneumothorax:

    - Continue to find a point where there is no lung sliding on one side and lung sliding on the other side

- B lines (Kerley B lines) - presence of interstitial oedema, comet like tail artefact

- PLAPs point - posterior lateral alveolar pleural syndrome (look for pleural effusion):

    - Anechoic and collapsed lung - tranxudative pleural effusion
    - Plankton sign - exudative pleural effusion (bits floating within effusion)
    - Septation - reflects empyema

- Consolidation - increased echogenicity of lung similar to liver (hepatisation)

- BLUE profile (read up)
