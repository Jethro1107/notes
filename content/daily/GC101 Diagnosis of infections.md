# GC102 Fever after chemotherapy, infections in immunocompromised hosts

- Core concepts - chemotherapy attacks rapidly dividing cells but can mimic ID:

    - Sore throat - due to chemotherapy-induced oral mucositis, at 41:14 he talks about CPJ (everytime CPJ opens, cells lost due to stretching and damaging, unable to be replaced)
    - Odynaphagia (retrosternal pain on swallowing) - due to esophagitis
    - Watery diarrhoea - chemotherapy-induced neutropenic enterocolitis/ ileocecaetis
    - RLQ pain - breaking of ICJ as food bolus passes

- Pathophysiology of post-chemotherapy bacteraemia/ neutropenic fever:

    - Mucosal damage by chemotherapy
    - Transient bacteraemia
    - Neutropenia due to chemotherapy cannot mount immune response against bacterial invasion (fever may be only clinical manifestation but may be blunted by steroids, NSAID and chemo itself)

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/sequence_of_events_of_chemo_induced_neutropenia.png)

    - **RHS** - narrowest parts of GI tract susceptible to trauma:
        - Upon opening, they stretch, and damage occurs

- Considerations for fever in a patient w/ haematological malignancies:

    - Central venous catheter may be infected - take blood from CVC and one peripheral to compare <u>time to positivity</u> (diff \> 102h suggests CVC problem)

- What is an immunocompromised host:

    - Alterations in innate and adaptive immunity, as a result of underlying diseases and their therapy, which predispose the host to infections/neoplasia
    - Classification:
        - Congenital (IEI)
        - Acquired:
            - HIV vs non-HIV
            - Transplant vs non-transplant
            - Malignancy vs non-malignancy
            - Haematological vs solid tumour

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/classification_of_immunocomprimised_hosts.png)
    - Pathophysiology predisposing to infections (see light blue slides)
    - Dependent on microbial biomass in mucosal surfaces (see slides)
    - However, extremely predicted pathogen by the specific defect of host - e.g. neutropenia: ![](~/Documents/notes/img/pathogens_predisposed_in_neutropenic_patients.png) ![](~/Documents/notes/img/pathogens_predisposed_in_lymphopenic_patient_with_insufficient_th_cells.png) 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/pathogens_predisposed_in_patient_with_humoral_immunity_defect.png)

- Neutropenic fever - based on ANC:

    - ANC \< 1.5 neutropenia
    - ANC \< 0.5 severe neutropenia
    - ANC \< 0.1 profound neutropenia:
        - Know that Neutrophilic reserves inside the marrow, takes time for ANC to drop; Nadir (7-14d, 10d by experience)
        - Lifethreatening if prolonged (14d)
        - 100% of post-chemoRx patients develop fever if neutropenic for 3w
    - **Pathophysiology of neutropenia:**
        - **Initiation of neutropenia** - as a result of an <u>imbalance in neutrophil kinetics</u>:
            - <u>Insult on mitotic reserves</u> - suppressed mitosis of the myeloid lineage in the synthesis of neutrophils as a result of drug-induced suppression, autoimmune disease, haematological disease, infection etc:
            - <u>Initial compensation by release of non-mitotic reserve</u> - bone marrow typically has a reserve of neutrophils, such that ANC does not drop at onset of bone marrow suppression unless neutrophilic reserve has been depleted (<u>Nadir 7-14d</u> \[median 10d\]): 
            ![](~/Documents/notes/img/neutrophil_kinetics.png)
        - **Risk of infections is a/w severity and duration of neutropenia** - as a rule of thumb, 100% of post-chemoRx patients develop fever if neutropenic for \> 3 weeks: 
        ![](~/Documents/notes/img/risk_of_neutropenic_fever_increases_with_duration_and_severity_of_neutropenia.png)
        - **Counfounding effects predisposing to infection in a neutropenic agent:**
            - <u>Chemotherapy-induced mucositis</u> - physiological trauma in the narrowest part of the GI tract (e.g. CPJ, GEJ, ICJ, anus) cannot be heal properly as a result of mucositis, predisposing to entrance of **G- rods** into blood residing within the GI tract
            - <u>Hickman's catheter</u> - chemotherapy in patients w/ leukaemia is delivered via a central venous catheter, which can be a source of colonisation of **G+ cocci** (skin flora), with **access to haematogenous spread**
            - <u>Use of empirical ABx</u> - use of empirical or prophylactic ABx is common, which affects the normal commensal flora and predisposes to **selection of potential pathogen**

      
      ![](~/Documents/notes/img/model_to_explain_origins_of_infections_in_neutropenic_patients.png)
    - **Natural Hx of neutropenia:**

- **Approach to neutropenic fever:**

    - <u>Initial risk assessment</u> - patients are trained to inform first contact of health care system about recent chemotherapy and risk of neutropenia
    - <u>Timing of ABx</u> - **empirical broad-spectrum ABx** initiated immediately <u>after blood cultures are obtained</u> and before any other Ix have been completed (\< 60 min of presentation)
    - <u>Dx evaluation</u> - detailed Hx, P/E, Ix should be performed after empirical ABx therapy has been initiated

- **Salient points of Hx and P/E** - performed daily to identify a focus of infection:

    - <u>Common source of infections</u> - skin, oral, sinuses, lungs (consider aspergillosis), GE or intra-abdominal abscess, perianal, Hickman exit, BM Bx site
    - <u>Determine nature of immunesuppression</u> - e.g. use of other immunosuppressants, transplants etc.

- **Ix:**

    - **Blood culture** (before initiation of empirical ABx) - 2x blood cultures from both ports of Hickman's +/- peripheral venipuncture if suspected infeted catheter:
        - <u>Indications of CVC infection</u> - based on time to positivity:
            - +ve culture in central venous blood 1-2h prior to peripheral venous blood is indicative of CVC infection
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, ESR/CRP:
        - <u>CBC</u> - lymphopenia
        - <u>LRFT</u> - baseline
        - <u>ESR/CRP</u> - non-specifically elevated
    - **Imaging** - CXR, CT thorax, CT abdomen:
        - <u>CXR</u> - if low risk for complications, and absence of respiratory Sx
        - <u>CT lung</u> - if high risk for complications, or presence of respiratory Sx
        - <u>CT abdomen</u> - if dairhoea, abdominal pain and tenderness +ve
    - **Microbiological Ix** - sputum culture, BAL, urine, skin swab, stool +/- galactomannan/1,3 beta-D glucan for pulmonary aspergillosis

- **Definition and terminology:**

    - <u>Immunocomprimised host</u> - a host with alterations in innate and adaptive immunity as a result of underlying disease and their therapy, which predisposes the host to infections or neoplasia

- **Classification of immunocomprimised hosts:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/classification_of_immunocomprimised_hosts.png)

- **Common mechanisms for alterations in host-defense mechanisms in an immunocomprimised patients:**

    - **Defects in innate immunity:**
        - **Lost of barriers:**
            - <u>Physical barriers</u> - damage to skin/ mucosa, chemotherapy-related mucositis, indwelling devices, Hickman's catheter, medical/ surgical procedures
            - <u>Chemical barriers</u> - gastric hypo-/ a-hydrochlorhydria due to long standing PPI use
            - <u>Microbiological barriers</u> - judacious antimicrobial therapy
        - **Lost of innate cellular immunity** - granulocytopenia (neutropenia)
        - **Lost of innate humoral immunity** - complement deficiency, thrombocytopenia
    - **Defects in adaptive immunity:**
        - <u>Cellular immune dysfunction</u> (T cell dysfunction) - e.g. HIV/ AIDs
        - <u>Humoral immune dysfunction</u> (B cell dysfunction) - e.g. multiple myeloma, chronic leukaemia, splenectomy
    - **Mechanical effects in tumour** - tumour obstruction of conducting system (e.g. MBO, bronchial obstruction, urinary tract obstruction, intestinal obstruction, lymphatics) resulting in increased intraluminal pressure resulting in <u>translocation of pathogens into blood</u>
    - **Major organ dysfunction:**
        - <u>Cirrhosis</u> - liver failure predisposes to infections
        - <u>Chronic kidney disease</u> (uraemia) - various mechanisms influencing adaptive immunity
        - <u>Heaert failure</u> - pulmonary congestion predisposes chest infections
        - <u>Neurological dysfunction</u> (e.g. bulbar palsies) - predisposes to aspiration pneumonia
    - **Miscellaneous:**
        - **Defects in signalling between immune cells** - use of biologic or small molecules, AutoAb against cytokines
        - **Others** - chronic hyperglycaemia (<u>DM</u>), malnutrition, iron overload etc.

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/common_mechanisms_predisposing_to_infection_in_immunocomprimised_host.png)

- **Microbiology** - spectrum of pathogens can be <sub>predicted</sub> by the specific defect of the host:

    - **Neutropenia** - see notes on neutropneic fever
    - **Cellular immune dysfunction** - defects in killing intracellular organisms:
        - <u>Bacterial</u> (usually intracellular pathogens of RES) - Listeria monocytogenes, Salmonella spp, Legionella spp, Mycobacteria (TB, NTB), Nocardia
        - <u>Fungal</u> - Cryptococcus neoformans, Dimorphic fungi, Pneumocystis jirovecii, Microsporidia
        - <u>Viral</u> - Herpes viridae (CMV, HSV, VZV, EBV, HHV-6), HPV, Respiratory viruses
        - <u>Protozoal</u> - toxoplasmosis, cryptosporidium
        - <u>Helminths</u> - strongyloides sterocoralis

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/predisposing_pathogens_in_cellular_immune_dysfunction.png)
    - **Humoral immune dysfunction and post-splenectomy:**
        - <u>Encapsulated bacteria</u> - Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae, Neisseria Meningitidis, Capnocytophaga canimorsus, Salmodella, Plasmodium
        - <u>Protozoa</u> - Babesia microti

- **Prevention of infection in immunocomprimised host:**

    - <u>Tackle iatrogenic risk factors</u> - avoid unnecessary instrumentation or invasive procedures, or antimicrobial therapy
    - <u>Detection and Tx of chronic infections before initiation of immunosuppressive therapy</u> - e.g. carious teeth, chronic sinusitis, old TB
    - <u>Specific immune measures</u>:
        - Immunisation - active (vaccination) vs passive (IVIG)
        - Prophylactic ABx - given prophylactically for splenectomised patients, or at first sign of Ix
        - Prophylactiv antivirals or antivungals
        - Immune-modulatory Tx - e.g. G-CSF, adoptive transfer virus-specific ex-vivo expanded T lymphocytes
