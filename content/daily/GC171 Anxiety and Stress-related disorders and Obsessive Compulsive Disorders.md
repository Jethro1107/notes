- **Definition** - the most commonly treated sustained cardiac arrhythmia associated w/ an irregularly irregular ventricular rhythm and absence of distinct P waves
- **Classification and terminology** - classified based on duration and length of episodes:
    - <u>Paroxysmal AF</u> (PAF) - AF that terminates spontaneously or with intervention within 7d of onset (episodes recurr with variable frequency)
    - <u>Persistent AF</u> - AF that fails to self-terminate within 7d of onset, requiring pharmacologic or electrical cardioversion to restore sinus rhythm
    - <u>Long-standing persistent AF</u> - AF that has lasted for \> 12 mo
    - <u>Permanent AF</u> - persistent AF where a decision is made to no longer pursue a rhythm control
- **Ix** - dependent on clinical presentation of new-onset AF:
    - **12-lead ECG:**
        - <u>Features of AF</u> - irregularly irregular rhythm with no discernable p waves (fibrillation waves present)
        - <u>Evidence of underlying structural heart disease or other arrhythmias</u> - preferably **baseline ECG in sinus rhythm**:
            - Old MI - pathological Q waves
            - Chamber dilatation - e.g. LAE, LVH
            - Risk of anti-arrhythmics - prolonged QTc
            - Evidence of sinoatrial nodal disease
    - **Echocardiogram** - TTE preferred over TEE in initial stage:
        - <u>Findings related to AF</u>:
            - Detection of chamber enlargements - e.g. LAE, RAE, LVH
            - Detection of valvular heart disease - valvular AF in patients w/ rheumatic MS
            - Assessment of ejection fraction - detection of any diastolic dysfunction due to AF
            - Detection of LA appendage thrombi - much less sensitive than TEE
    - **CXR** - typically required if patient presents w/ chest pain, or evidence of CHF:
        - <u>Features of CHF</u> - cardiomegaly, upper lobe cephalisation, Kerley B lines, interstitial oedema, batwing opacity
        - <u>Features suggestive of LAE</u> - features of LAE:
            - Straightening of left atrial appendage
            - Double density sign - right side of enlarged LA creates addition contour superimposed over right heart
            - Splaying of the carina
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, RFT, TFT, FBG, lipid profile:
        - <u>CBC</u> - underlying anaemia aggravating HF
        - <u>RFT</u> - serum electrolytes (esp. K), and baseline renal function
        - <u>TFT</u> - look for thyrotoxicosis in all patients with first episode of AF
        - <u>FBG</u> - assessment of DM
        - <u>Lipid profile</u> - assessment of dyslipidaemia
    - **Other Ix** - depending on clinical situation:
        - <u>TnT</u> - acute MI
        - <u>NT-proBNP</u> - AoCHF
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - **Initial Mx:**
            - **Symptom and haeamodynamic Mx** - rate vs rhythm control
            - **Identify and treat triggers of AF** - e.g. Tx of pneumonia, pulmonary embolism etc.
            - **Anticoagulation** - evaluation of the need of anti-thrombotic therapy to prevent systemic embolisation
        - **Long term Mx:**
            - <u>Prevention of thromboembolism</u>
            - <u>Cardiovascular risk factor and co-morbid disease assessment</u>

# GC171 Anxiety and Stress-related disorders and Obsessive Compulsive Disorders <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- Learning objectives:
    - Epidemiology of anxiety and related disorders
    - Comorbid psychiatric diagnoses related to anxiety
    - Diagnostic criteria for anxiety and related disorders
    - Neuroimaging findings of anxiety disorders
    - Quick question to screen for anxiety disorders
    - Tx of anxiety disorder - psychotherapy and psychopharmacology
- Stress ('fight or flight') response can be irrational because it is intrinsic and physiological - e.g. watching scary movies shouldn't be scary because it is not real
- Anxiety - by definition neutral:
    - Normal anxiety is adaptive to stress
    - Inborn response to threat that can result in cognitive and somatic symptoms
- Pathologic anxiety - anxiety that is excessive and impairs function
- Neural response to stressful stimuli:
    - Amygdala - involved with processing of emotionally salient stimuli
    - Medial prefrontal cortex (including ACC, subcallosal cortex and medial frontal gyrus) - involved in modulation of affect
    - Hippocampus - involved in memory encoding and retrieval
- Classification of anxiety disorders:
    - Phobic anxiety disorders - specific ques:
        - Agaraphobia
        - Social phobias
        - Special phobias
    - Other anxiety disorders:
        - Panic disorder
        - Generalized anxiety disorder
        - Mixed anxiety and depressive disorder
- Epidemiology of anxiety disorder - see lecture slides:
    - Prevalence:
        - Most are around 10% range
        - OCD, panic disorder and agarophobia - 2-5% range
    - Demographic:
        - Age of onset - teens or early twenties
        - Sex - 2:1 female predominance
    - Burden - can be quite functionally limitating (similar to major depression or DM)
- Anxiety Sx - similar for different anxiety disorder:
    - Autonomic arousal Sx - palpitations, sweating, tremors, dry mouth
    - Chest and Abd Sx - e.g. difficult of breathing, feeeling of choking, chest discomfort, nausea or abdominal distress
    - Sx concerning brain and mind - dizziness, derealisation or depersonalisation, losing control, fear of dying
    - General Sx - hot flushes or cold chills, numbness and tingling sensation (?functional hypoCa)
- Assessing whether anxiety is disorder or physiological:
    - Severity (i.e. impact) - e.g. Sx, severity of each Sx
    - Duration - e.g. how long feeling persists, frequency
- Agarophobia:
    - Definition - marked and consistently manifest fear in or avoidance of at least 2 of the following situations:
        - Crowd
        - Public places
        - Travelling alone
        - Travelling away from home
- Panic disorder:
    - Recurrent panic attacks, that are not consistently associated with a specific situation, and often occuring spontaneous (i.e. attacks are unpredictable)
    - No marked exertion or exposure to life-threatening scenarios
    - Features of panic attack - characterised by all of the following:
        - Discrete episode - intense fear or discomfort
        - Abrupt onset - crescendo within few minutes and lasts at least some minutes
        - 1 Sx from each Sx group - autonomic, chest and abdomen, brain and mind, general
- Genearalized anxiety disorder (GAD):
    - Period of \> 6mo with prominent tension, worry, feeling of apprehension about every day events and problems
    - At least 1 Sx from each Sx group
- General Mx approach:
    - Pharmacotherapy - **antidepressants**, (benzodiazapine stricter use due to dependence), antipsychotics, mood stabilizers
    - Psychotherapy - CMT, mindfulness-based therapy
- Trauma and stressor-related disorders:
    - Acute Stress Reaction
    - Post-traumatic stress disorder
    - Adjustment disorder
- Post-traumatic stress disorder:
    - Exposure to actual or threatened death, serious or sexual violence in 1 of 3 ways:
        - Direct experience of the traumatic event
        - Witnessed in person
        - Learning that traumatic event occured to person close to them
        - Experienced repeated or extreme exposure to aversive details of trauma
    - Re-experiencing - Presence of 1 or more after the event:
        - Recurrent intrusive memories of the event
        - Recurrent trauma-related nightmares
        - Dissociative reactions
        - Intense physiological distress or marked physiological reactivity at cue exposure
    - Avoidance - Persistent avoidance by 1 or both:
        - Avoiding of distressing memories, thoughts or feelings of the event
        - Avoidance of external reminders of that arouse memories of events
    - Anxiety Sx in or more domains typically present
    - Negative alterations in cognition and mood a/w traumatic events as evidenced by 2 or more of the following:
        - Inability to remember an important aspect of the traumatic events
        - Persistent distorted cognition about cause or consequence of event that lead to blame of self or others
        - Persistent negative emotional state
        - Persistent inability to experience positive emotion
        - Marked diminished interest
        - Feeling detached from others
    - Hypervigillance cognition - Marked alteration in arousal and reactivity with 2 or more:
        - Irritable behaviour and angry outburst
        - Reckless or self-destructive behavior
        - Hypervigilance
        - Exaggerated startle response
        - Problems with concentration
        - Sleep disturbance
    - Duration of disturbance \> 1 mo AND causes significant impairment in function
    - Specifiers:
        - with dissociative Sx
        - with delayed expression
    - Epidemiology of PTSD:
        - Prevalence - 7-9% of general population, 60-80% of trauma victims, 30% of combat veterans, 50-80% of sexual assaulted
        - Demographic - F, younger people, risk increased w/ dose of trauma, lack of social support, pre-existing psychiatric condition
    - Comorbidities of PTSD:
        - Depression
        - Other anxiety disorders
        - Substance use disorders
        - Somatization
        - Dissociative disorders
    - Etiology of PTSD:
        - Conditioned fear - think conditioning dog experiment
        - Autonomic arousal immediately after trauma predicts PTSD development
    - Neuroimaging in PTSD:
        - Increased amygdala activation
        - Hypoactivation of medial prefrontal cortex - inability to modulate affect
    - Tx of PTSD:
        - Debriefing immediately not necessarily effective
        - CBT
        - Eye movement desensitiztion and Reprocessing (EMDR)
        - Psychopharmacology - **antidepressants**, +/- beta blockers (Sx), mood stabilisers
- Acute Stress Reaction - see slides (principally PTSD but does not satisfy duration)
- Obsessive-compulsive and related disorders:
    - Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder
    - Body Dysmorphic Disorder
    - Hoarding Disorder
    - Trichotillomania
    - Excoriation Disorder
- Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder:
    - Obsession defined by:
        - Recurrent and persistent thoughts, impulses or images that are intrusive and unwanted, that cause marked anxiety or distress
        - With attempts to suppress
    - Compulsion - attempts to ignore or suppression such thoughts, urges or images, or to neutralize them with some other thought or action
    - Compulsion:
        - Repetitive behavior or mental acts that the person feels driven to perform to an obscession
        - The behaviours aimed at reducing distress or preventing some dreaded situation however these acts and behaviors are not connected in a realistic way with what they are designed to neutralize or prevent
