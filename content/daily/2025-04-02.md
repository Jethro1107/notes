# ENT Tutorial - Otology and rhinology

## Otitis externa

- **Defintion** - inflammation of external ear and EAC
- **Microbiology** - bacterial vs fungal (ocomycosis), differentiated clinically:
    - Bacterial - more pain, erythema over ear canal
    - Fungal - less pain, more hearing Sx, spores on tympanic membrane

## Otitis media with effusion

- Otoscopic findings - air fluid level demarkating effusion, loss of cone of light on otoscope (still present on endoscopy)
- Clinical features:
    - Prior URTI with eustachian tube dysfunction - if absent suggestive of nasal pathologies (e.g. polyps), or suspicion of NPC
    - Conductive HL and ear block - can be unilateral or bilateral
- Ix:
    - Nasoendoscopy - r/o NPC
- Mx:
    - Medical Tx
    - Surgical Tx - myringotomy and ventilation tube
    - +/- Eustachian tube balloon dilatation - for recurrent cases to restore eustachian tube patency and function

## Perforated ear drum

- Etiology:
    - Acute blast injury - e.g. diving (\> 90% heal by scarring)
    - Chronic suppurative otitis media (CSOM)
- Clinical features:
    - Hearing loss
    - Otorrhoea
- Pre-treatment evaluation - pure tone audiogram to predict return of hearing
- Mx:
    - Control infection - ?systemic ABx
    - Tympanoplasty - temporalis fascia or tragal cartilage (aim is to prevent infection, secondary is to restore hearing)
- Classification:
    - Pars tensa (central) - safe CSOM
    - Pars flaccida - unsafe CSOM (higher risk of complications, cholesteatoma)
- Cholesteatoma:
    - Pathology - erosion of epithelium (squams in EAC) into middle ear
    - Clinical features - conductive hearing loss and otorrhoea
    - Complications:
        - Facial nerve palsy
        - Labyrinthine fistula - vertigo
        - Meningitis, intracranial abscess
    - Mx:
        - Control infection
        - Mastoidectomy

## Rhinitis

- Inflammation of nasal mucosa
- Clinical features - Discharge, sneezing, nasal obstruction
- Causes:
    - Allergic rhinitis (IgE)
    - Infective - viral vs bacterial
- Allergic rhinitis:
    - Allergens - house dust mites
    - Mx:
        - Allergen avoidance
        - Medical - Nasal douching, oral antihistamines, intranasal steroids
        - Immunotherapy considered for severe cases
- Vasomotor rhinitis
- Infective

## Rhinosinusitis

- Acute - \< 4 weeks (more symptomatic with facial pain)
- Chronic - \> 12 weeks (typically no facial pain)
- Dx - clinical and nasoendoscope
- Role of CT - for patients contemplating surgery for any nasal anomalies
- Mx:
    - ABx
    - Systemic corticosteroids for severe Sx
    - Intranasal corticosteroid/ decongestant - \< 1 weeks to avoid rebound (rhinitis medicamentosa)
    - Functional endoscopic sinus surgery
- Complications:
    - Peri-orbital cellulitis

## Nasal polyps

- Classification - neoplastic vs inflammatory (atopy related) vs allergic (require Bx to confirm)
- Clinical manifestations - very symptomatic chronic rhinosinusitis
- Mx:
    - Surgical - FESS + nasal polypectomy –\> will recur 100%
    - Biologics for eosinophilic polyps

## Epistaxis

- Etiology:
    - Local - septal spur, tummour
    - Systemic - HTN, bleeding tendancy
- Mx:
    - Control bleeding
    - Airway mx
