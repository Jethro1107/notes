# GC147 Heart failure and cyanosis in children, acyanotic and cyanotic congenital heart disease <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- **Differentiating features between adult and children heart failure:**
    - Adult - more likely caused by impaired myocardial contractility (i.e. insufficient supply)
    - Children - more likely caused by overloading of the heart (i.e. excessive demand due to pressure and volume overload)
- **How do infants and children w/ HF present?**
    - Common Sx:
        - Breathlessness, especially on exertion - main exercise of newborn is feeding, so they develop SOB during feeding
        - Reduced ET in older children
        - Poor feeding (longer time to feed same amount, equivalent of reduced ET)
        - Excessive sweating (during feeding)
        - Failure to thrive (know birthweight )
        - Recurrent chest infections - as many congenital heart disease a/w left-to-right shunts, it results in pulmonary congestion, excessive fluid in lungs and infection
    - Signs:
        - Signs of pulmonary venous congestion - tachypnoea, subcostal insucking, **wheezing in infants**
        - Signs of systemic venous congestion - hepatomegaly, distended neck veins, peripheral oedema (rare in children w/ heart failure)
        - Signs of compensatory mechanisms - tachycardia, cardiomegaly
        - Signs of forward failure (unusual, only seen when decompensated) - cool extremities, prolonged capillary refill decreased pulse volume
- Etiology of heart failure in neonates:
    - LVOT obstruction - coarctation of aorta, critical aortic stenosis, aortic atresia (hypoplastic left heart syndrome)
    - Myocardial dysfunction - e.g. asphyxia at birth resulting in transient myocardial ischaemia, myocarditis, cardiomyopathy
    - Abnormalities of hear rate and rhythm - ?maternal SLE
    - Extra-cardiac causes - anaemia, sepsis, hypocalcaemia
- Etiology of heart failure in infants:
    - Large left-to-right shunts - VSD, ASD, PDA (these present later than LVOT lesions)
- Etiology of heart failure in older children:
    - Myocardial disease - myocarditis, cardiomyopathy
    - Unoperated structural heart defects
    - Certain repaired or palliated congenital heart defects
- **Coarctation of aorta:**
    - Narrowing of the aortic isthmus - at the extreme case minimal blood flow to the descending aorta, resulting in bowel ischaemic (necrotising enterocolitis), renal ischaemia (AKI) leading to metabolic disturbance
    - Minimal blood flow through ductus arteriosus before it obliterates
    - At extreme cases - interrupted aortic arch, connection of aortic arch to descending aorta completely interrupted (complete dependence of ductus arteriosus)
    - Most important signs:
        - Absent femoral pulses (radio-femoral delay)
- **Critical AS** - both UL and LL pulses weak (slow-rising)
- **Hypoplastic left heart syndrome:**
    - LV unformed
    - Foramen ovale remains patent
    - Fetal circulation in tact and systemic circulation after birth dependent on duct

- **Ductal dependent systemic circulation** - prostaglandin E to maintain patent duct clinically
- **Left-to-Right Shunt:**
    - Increased pulmonary blood flow results in increased pulmonary venous return, resulting in volume overload of LA and LV (except for ASD)
    - In ASD:
        - Volume overloading of RV, with little overload of LV because little blood goes into LV

- **Cyanosis:**
    - Central vs peripheral
    - Apparent in infant and children w/ 3-5 g/dL of ?reduced haemoglobin?deoxygenated (note confounding influence of anaemia or polycythaemia)
- **Etiology of central cyanosis:**
    - Cyanotic congenital heart disease
    - Respiratory tract disease - airway obstruction and pulmonary parenchymal disease
    - CNS - hypoventilation
    - Congenital methaglobinaemia
- Cardiac vs respiratory:
    - Factors predisposing to respiratory disease - pneumonia, meconium aspiration syndrome
    - Respiratory distress - usually NOT present in cyanotic heart disease
    - CXR
    - Nitrogen washout test (no longer performed)
- Pathophysiology of cyanotic heart disease:
    - Right-to-left shunts w/ RV outflow obstruction:
        - TOF
        - TOF with pulmonary atresia
        - Pulmonary atresia w/ intact ventricular septum
    - Transposition of great vessels
    - Reduced pulmonary flow - POT
    - Common-mixing conditions - left to right shunting
