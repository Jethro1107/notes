# Pre-Assistant Internship - Blood Taking and Intravenous Cannulation Training Course

## Prevention of blood bourne transmission in workplace

- **Prevention of needle stick injury by proper blood taching technique**
- **Blood-borne diseases** - most common include:
    - HBV - 8% are carriers in HK
    - HCV - 0.2-0.3% are sero-positive in HK
    - HIV - \< 0.1% are sero-positive in HK
    - And many more
- Risk of conversion to infection following single needle-stick injury - dependent on pathogen, viral load, volume etc:
    - HBV (18-30%), HCV (1.8%), HIV (0.3%) for 0.000004 ml
- Medical and Nursing most common to have needle stick injury (cf Phlebotomist is low)
- Most common devices involved:
    - 1\. Butterfly needle (\> 50% as hollow-bore resulting in higher blood volume and hence higher viral load)
    - 2\. Suture needle
    - 3\. Syringe
- Most common site of needle-stick injury:
    - OT
    - Medical and surgical wards
- **Standard precautions:**
    - Rationale: Meant to reduce the risk of transmission of blood-boorne and other pathogens from both recognized and unrecognized source
    - Assumption - any patient's blood or body fluids can be source of blood-bourne pathogen
    - Components (find poster):
        - Wear gloves when handling blood, body fluids, secretions, excretions, mucous membrane or non-intact skin
        - Wear surgical mask, face shield and gound to prevent splashing
        - No recapping and handle sharps carefully (puncture resistant box)
        - Perform hand hygiene immediate (WHO 5 moments)
- Puncture resitsant box:
    - No recapping
    - Point sharps downwards when disposing
    - Cover after use
    - Do not fill completely (two-thirds)
- Safety devices - further protection offered only if aforementioned preventitive measures have been addressed
    - Recap - either use one-hand scoop technique or recapping device
    - Proper disposal - ensure puncture resistant box at point of care (avoid need of transfer of sharps)
    - Blood collection tube holder (avoid use of sharp)
    - Personal protective equipment
- 5 moments of hand hygiene:
    - Before touching a patient
    - Before clean/ aseptic procedure
    - After body fluid exposure risk
    - After touching a patient
    - After touching patient surroundings
- Blood taking w/ vacuette (avoid blood culture contamination) - read up again
- What to do after needle stick injury:
    - Apply first-aid on site
    - Report to I/C and complete incident report form
    - Send blood from source patient for HBsAg, HCV, HIV w/ consent
    - Bring along the completed incident report form to attend staff clinic/ A&E for blood screening and PEP as required
    - Report Injury on Duty to HR via AIRS

## Blood taking technique and IV cannulation

- Review standard precaution and take consent
- **Blood taking:**
    - Ensure all equipments are available:
        - Alcohol based hand rub
        - Gloves
        - Tournequet
        - Alcohol swab for disinfection
        - Blood taking tubes
        - Vacuette system
    - Hand hygiene and wear hand gloves
    - Visualise anatomy with arm extended - brachial artery more medially on the cubital fossa which should be avoided, hence select a more lateral vein
    - Tie tournequet (midway in the arm)
    - Disinfect with alcohol swab in circular motion inner to outer
    - Connect vacuette system with butterfly needle
    - Ask patient to hold fist
    - Review vein and orient yourself, then remove cap and proceed with blood taking
    - Holding a butterfly needle - sharp end should point downwards
    - Puncture at 45 degree angle - observe presence of blood in system which indicates successful venepuncture
    - Connect blood taking tube into vacuette system
    - Remove tube, ask patient to relax fist, remove tournequet
    - Hold piece of cottom wool on site and remove needle
    - Throw into sharp box needle pointing down
- **IV cannulation:**
    - Ensure all equipments available:
        - Alcohol swab
        - Tournequet
        - Angiocatheter of appropriate size
        - IV drip set
    - Hand hygiene and wear gloves
    - Tie tournequet in forearm and ask patient to hold fist
    - Visualise dorsum of hand for appropriate vein (other sites include avolar aspect of forearm or cubital fossa)
    - Disinfect with alcohol swab in similar manner
    - Angiocatheter inserted w/ sharp down and 45 angle
    - Blood drawn indicates inside vein, then advance cather into the vein
    - Take out needle and throw in sharpbox
    - Connect into IV drip set
