# GC219 Infections and tumours in pharynx and oral cavity

- **Salient points of Hx** - see slides for overview of ENT Hx
- P/E:
    - **Examination of oral cavity:**
        - Inspection of oral cavity:
            - Lips
            - Buccal mucosa with parotid duct opening (below upper 2nd molar teeth)
            - Teeth and gingival sulcus
            - Hard palate
            - Oral tongue (anterior 2/3)
            - Floor of mouth with submandibular duct opening
        - Inspection of oropharynx:
            - Tonsils
            - Soft palate and uvulae
            - Pharyngeal mucosa
            - Tongue base (posterior 1/3) - difficult to examine
        - Palpation
    - Examination of neck for neck swelling
    - Scalp/ skin

## Acute tonsilitis

- **Definition** - infection and inflammation of the tonsils
- **Organisms:**
    - Viral - influenza, parainfluenza, adenovirus, etnerovirus, rhinovirus
    - Bacterial - beta-haemolytic streptococcus, streptococcus pneumoniae, h. influenzae, mixed
    - Others - Dipthaeria, Candidiasis, symphilis, TB
- **Clinical features:**
    - Sore throat
    - Odynophagia
    - Fever
    - Muffled hot-potato voice
    - Otalgia - referred pain via CN IX
    - Others - abd pain, vomiting
- **Signs:**
    - Hyperaemic tonsils with exudates/ pus
    - Minimal trismus - presence suspect peritonsilar abscess affecting pterygoid?
    - Tender cervical LN
- Ix:
    - CBC
    - Throat swab for culture
- Mx:
    - Principles of Mx:
        - Outpatient Mx - for most patient
        - Inpatient Mx - for severe tonsilitis (esp unable to eat well since cannot tolerate oral ABx)
    - Tx:
        - General measures - Bed rest, analgesic, fluid replacement
        - IV ABx - penicillin, erythromycin
- Read complications - local vs general

## Infectious mononucleosis (glandular fever)

- Definition - acute infection of EBV
- Ix:
    - CBC - lymphocytic leukocytosis, thrombocytopenia
    - PBS - atypical lymphocytes
    - LFT - derranged LFT and clotting profiles
    - Monotest +ve (diagnostic)
- Mx:
    - Supportive Tx
    - Avoid penicillin - causes Rubeliform rash
    - Avoid contact sports for 1mo (prevent splenic rupture)

## Peritonsillar abscess

- Collection of pus between tonsilar capsule and superior constrictor
- Organism - mixed aerobic and anaerobic
- Sx - similar to tonsilitis, differentiated on P/E
- Signs:
    - Unilateral peritonsilar swelling (cf bilateral tonsilar enlargement)
    - Deviation of uvula
    - Trismus
- Mx:
    - Transoral I and D
    - ABx
    - Analgesic, fluid replacement, chart I/O
    - Consider elective tonsillectomy (due to 20% recurrence)

## Acute epiglottitis

- Definition - ENT emergency with airway obstruction
- Key clinical feature - drooling

## Deep neck space infection

- Ludwig's angina
- Parapharyngeal abscess
- Retropharyngeal abscess

## Oral cavity benign lesions

- Benign tumours - lipoma, giant cell tumour, papilloma, haemangioma
- Ranula - Bluish cystic swelling in the floor of the mouth due to cyst formation in blocked sublingual glands
- Salivary duct stones:
    - Submandibular \> Parotid \> Sublingual
    - Clinical features:
        - Colicky postprandial glandular swelling and pain
- Torus palantinus

## Premalignant lesions

- Leukoplakia - whitish patch on oral cavity mucosal membrane that cannot be wiped away
    - Clinical description with various pathological conditions:
        - Lichen planus
        - Candidiasis
        - Linea alba
    - A/w malignancy (5%) - all require Bx
- Erythroplakia - erythromatous patch with granular or nodular lesion
    - Red due to dysplasia without keratosis
    - High malignant potential - 15x increased risk of SCC (90% have CIS or frank SCC at time of Bx)
- Ulcer:
    - Apthous
    - Traumatic
    - Infective
    - Systemic manifestation - e.g. Bechet's disease, Autoimmune, Blood disease, Malignancy
