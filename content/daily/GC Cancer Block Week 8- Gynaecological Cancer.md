## Female Pelvic Imaging

- **Common imaging modalities in gynaecology:**
    - USG - transabdominal vs transvaginal
    - CT pelvis
    - MRI pelvis
- **USG** - 2 modes:
    - TVUSG - transvaginal probe w/ high 10 MHz (no need full bladder, but smaller viewer voew although better resolution, contrast and anatomical details)
    - TAUSG - low frequency probe 4-5 Hz (full bladder (create acoustic shadowing) required w/ paranoramic view and larger coverage \[good for large pelvic mass\])
- **Cross-sectional anatomy of the pelvis** - see slides
- **Common indications of imaging:**
    - Pelvic pain
    - AUB - note that in post-menopausal setting, USG becomes principal Ix of choice as endometrial thickness no longer fluctuates w/ menstrual cycle (USG limited role in pre-menopausal AUB)
    - Plevic mass
    - Infertility/ subfertility
- **Mullerian duct anomalies** - spectrum of congenital uterine malformations:
    - Unicornuate uterus
    - Uterus didelphys
    - Bicornuate uterus
    - Septate uterus

## Pathology of tumours of the lower female genital tract

- **Overview** - cervix, vagina, vulva
- **Cervical cancer** - 4th most common cancer in women globally (top 3 cancers in women aged \< 45y)
- **Tumours of the cervical tract** - see WHO classification of the female genital tumours:
    - Squamous epithelial tumours
    - Other epithelial tumours
    - Mixed epithelial and mesenchyma tumours
    - Adenocarcinomas
    - Melanomas
    - Germ cell tumours
    - Metastasis
- **HPV** - strong risk factor a/w lower genital tract (see slides for actual distribution):
    - 100 types
    - 30-40 in anogenital region
    - 15-20 types known as high risk HPV as they are oncogenic:
        - 16, 18, 31,33,45,51,52,58,59
        - As can lead to high-grade squamous intrapithelial lesion (HSIL) and SCC
    - Rest are low risk HPV and only results in warts and LSIL:
        - 6,11 most common
    - Important facts of HPV:
        - Common infection - so common in fact that all sexually active men and women will get the virus at some point in their lives
        - Only a portion of those infected will progress to cancer
        - HPV is a necessary but not sufficient factor for cervical cancer
- **Squamous cell tumours and percursors of uterine cervix** - note that HPV status can be assessed via ISH or p16 IHC as surrogate marker:
    - Condyloma acuminatum
    - Squamous intraepithelial lesions
    - Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-associated
    - Squamous cell carcinoma, HPV-independent
    - Squamous cell carcinoma NOS
- **Note robbins textbook of cervical lesions** - LSIL to HSIL to carcinoma
- **Adenocarcinomas:**
    - Adenocarcinoma in situ, HPV-associated
    - Adenocarcinoma in situ, HPV-independent
    - Adenocarcinoma, HPV associated
    - Adenocarcinoma, HPV independent, gastric type (most AD of HPV-independent adenocarcinoma are gastric types, see details in slides particularly p53 in 50% and are extremely aggressive cancer)
    - Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, clear cell type
    - Adenocarcinoma, HPV-independent, mesonephric type
    - Other adenocarcinomas
- **3 pathways of carcinogenesis for vulval SCC** (see slides):
    - HPV associated (60%) - earlier presentation w/ risk factors
    - HPV-independent - TP53 mutated (30%) - later presentation w/ poor outcomes
    - HPV-independent, TP53 wildtype

## Diagnosis, Investigations and Management of Uterine Cancer

- **Clinical presentations:**
    - AUB (90%) - PMB, HMB w/ new onset and no response to medical Tx (hormonal or tranxemenic acid), persistent IMB or irregularity
    - Postmenopausal vaginal discharge/ myometria
    - Pelvic mass/ pain
    - Urinary or bowel Sx
    - Abnormal cervical smear - esp. atypical glandular cells found
- **See salients point of Hx** - esp for suspicion of endometrial cancer
- **Risk factors:**
    - Age predominantly a disease of postmenopausal women
    - Unopposed estrogen:
        - Endogenous - early menarche, late menopause, nullibarity, obesity, PCOS, estrogen secreting tumours
        - Exogenous - tamoxifen
    - FHx -know lynch syndrome a/w 15-60% lifetime risk (FHx of breast, ovarian, and endometrial cancer)
- **P/E** - may me normal in early stage of disease:
    - General - pallor, other constitutional signs, lymphadenopathy (groin and left SCF)
    - Abdominal examination - e.g. pelvic mass
    - Vaginal examination:
        - Speculum - obtain cervical smear if
        - Bimanual - size and mobility
    - PR - involvement of parametria and rectovaginal septum
- **Dx:**
    - Pipelle sampler w/ blind endometrial aspirate
    - D&C/ diagnostic hysteroscopy + endometrial Bx
- **Ix:**
    - Routine bloods - CBC, LRFT, CA125 (principally for ovarian cancer, but may be raised in endometrial cancer and serve as marker of Tx response and recurrence)
    - Imaging:
        - CXR (+/- CT thorax if CXR abnormal)
        - MRI A+P
        - PET-CT
- **Histological subtypes:**
    - Low grade (G1-2) - 85%
    - High grade (G3) endometrioid
    - Less common histology - e.g. serous carcinoma, clear cell carcinoma, carcinosarcoma (malignant mixed mullerian tumour \[MMMT\])
- **Molecular classification** - as prognostic risk group stratification
- **Route of spread:**
    - Tumour invasion - cervix, vagina, fallopian tube, ovarian
    - LN - internal iliac glands, para-aortic glands
    - Distant metastasis - most common to lung, occassionally to liver, vertebrae or bones, occassionally SCF
- **Slide has 2023 FIGO stage** (cf UpToDate only 2018)
- **Mx:**
    - Surgery
    - Chemoradiation - in pre-operative and post-oeprative setting
    - Immunotherapy
    - Hormonal therapy (selected young patients desiring fertility sparing treatment)
- **Adjuvant therapy:**
    - High risk groups stage I-II consider brachytherapy +/- chemotherapy
    - If LN involvement/ distant metastasis - adjuvant:
        - Chemotherapy - carboplatin and paclitaxel
        - Immunotherapy in MSI-H tumours along with standard chemotherapy
        - RT
- **Hormonal therapy:**
    - Indications - young patients (\< 40y) who desire fertility preservation if:
        - Low-grade endometroid carcinoma
        - no evidence of myometrial invasion on MRI
        - No evidence of LN involvement or distant metastasis
    - Route:
        - Oral progestogen (Megace)
        - Levonorgestrol-releasing IUD (Mirena)
    - Routine endometrial assessment for assessment of Tx response

## Diagnosis, Investigations, and Management of Cervical Cancer

- **Main causes of cervical cancer** - high risk HPV:
    - 16, 18 (main causes)
    - Others include 31, 33, 45, 52, 58

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/hpv_genotypes_in_cervical_cancer.png)
- **HPV-associated malignancies** - only in setting of chronic infections, most clear by themselves:
    - Cervical cancer
    - Vaginal cancer
    - Vulva cancer
    - Anal cancer
    - Oropharyngeal cancer
- HPV infection - \> 50% of sexually active women infected in lifetime:
    - Teenages higher risk
    - May also be acquired in close sexual contact
- **Risk factors of cervical cancer:**
    - <u>HPV-related</u> - early sex, multiple sexual partners
    - <u>Poor immunity</u>:
        - Smoking
        - Immunosuppression
    - <u>Others</u>:
        - Lower SES
        - OC pills
- **Carcinogenesis of cervical cancer** - only at risk if natural immunity unable to clear HPV infection (most capable to clear infection within 2y):
    - Persistent HPV infection
    - Low-grade lesions (CIN 1)
    - High-grade lesions (CIN2-3)
    - Invasive cervical cancer

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/natural_hx_of_hpv_mediated_cervical_cancer.png)
- **Clinical features of cervical cancer:**
    - <u>Asymptomatic</u> - may be picked up in pre-malignant phase of disease in screening
    - <u>Abnormal uterine bleeding</u> - common patterns include:
        - Post coital bleeding
        - Intermenstrual bleeding
        - Post menopausal bleeding
    - <u>Features of late-stage disease</u> - back pain, leg swelling, pelvic pain
- **Signs:**
    - **General examination:**
        - Constitutional signs
        - LN - groin, supraclavicular
    - **PV examination:**
        - Speculum examination - visualise cervix, and assess extent of vaginal involvement
        - Bimanual examination - feel for mobility of uterus to assess whether tumour extend beyond the cervix
    - **PR examination:**
        - Parametera
        - Recto-vaginal septum
        - Pouch of douglas mass for rectal involvement
- **Ix:**
    - Cervical smear - abnormal cervical cytology
    - Colposcopy and Bx if no visible lesion
    - Bx during speculum examination if gross abnormal lesion

- **Staging Ix:**
    - **Bloods** - CBC, LRFT, tumour markers:
        - <u>tumour markers</u> - dependent on histology (SCC if squams, CA125 if adeno)
    - **Imaging:**
        - MRI abdomen and pelvis
        - CXR for screening (+/- CT thorax)
        - PET-CT
    - +/- invasive measures - e.g. cystoscopy, sigmoidoscopy
- **Mx:**
    - **Surgery** - for early stage I disease:
        - Cone hysterectomy - for selected microscopic (\< 3mm) disease
        - Radical hysterectomy and pelvic lymphadenectomy (Werthim's hysterectomy):
            - Remove uterus, upper vagina, parametria and pelvic LN
    - **Chemorradiation:**
        - Chemotherapy (cisplatin) as radiosensitizer
        - Radiation - EBRT + brachytheraapy
    - **Systemic therapy** - for metastatic disease or recurrence:
        - Chemo + bevacizumab
        - Chemo + pembrolizumab
- **Prognostic factor:**
    - Stage
    - LN metastasis
    - Histology
    - Parametrial involvement
    - Size and stromal invasion
    - Lymphovascular permeation
