# GC176 A large liver, liver cancer <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span> <span class="tag" tag-name="Surgery"><span class="smallcaps">Surgery</span></span>

## Hepatomegaly

- **Definition** - an enlarged liver
- **DDx of hepatomegaly:**
    - **Parenchymal hepatomegaly** - Alcoholic cirrhosis, Haemochromatosis
    - **Neoplastic hepatomegaly:**
        - <u>Malignant hepatomegaly</u> - Primary liver tumour (HCC, intrahepatic CC), secondary liver metastasis (GI primary and others)
        - <u>Benign hepatomegaly</u> - <u>focal nodular hyperplasia</u>, agiomyolipoma, hemangioma, adenoma
    - **Infiltrative hepatomegaly** - haematological malignancies (lymphoma, leukaemia, amyloidosis), sarcoidosis
    - **Vascular hepatomegaly** - RHF, TR, Budd-Chiari syndrome
    - **Cystic hepatomegaly** - Polycystic Kidney Disease, cystadenoma, simple cyst, <u>liver abscess</u>
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - **HPI** - site, onset, progression, quality etc.
        - <u>Site</u> - is it truely hepatomegaly?
        - <u>Onset and progression</u> - since when? progressive?
    - **Associated Sx:**
        - <u>Abdominal pain</u> - characterize site, onset, progression, and duration:
        - <u>Features of obstructive jaundice</u> (e.g. tea-coloured urine, pale stools, generalised pruritis) - suggestive of biliary obstruction
        - <u>Ascites</u> - any prior abdominal distension suggestive of HCC or peritoneal involvement
        - <u>Altered bowel habits</u> (changes in frequency, consistency, colour etc.)
        - <u>Constitutional Sx</u> - weight loss, anorexia
    - **PMH** - assessment of risk factors for cirrhosis and HCC:
        - <u>HBV/ HCV status</u> - ask about use of NRTIs, and control of HBV levels if known
        - <u>Alcoholic liver disease</u> - assessed through social Hx
        - <u>Other liver disease or cirrhosis</u> - cirrhosis is a risk factor of HCC irrespective of cause
        - <u>GI diseases</u> - e.g. inflammatory bowel disease, prior colonoscopy showing polyps
    - **SHx:**
        - <u>Smoking</u> - RF for upper GI malignancy
        - <u>Alcohol</u> - RF of alcoholic cirrhosis or HCC
        - <u>Diet</u> - RF for primary GI malignancy
    - **FHx** - FHx of HBV, liver maligancy, or GI malignancy
    - **Systems review** - Sx of other primary malignancies esp. if no known risk factors:
        - **Review of lower GI Sx** - points towards a primary CRC:
            - <u>Sx of left-sided CRC</u>:
                - PR bleeding
                - Obstructive Sx - e.g. constipation, partial obstruction resulting in colicky abdominal pain
                - Altered bowel habits - change in frequency, pencil-thin stool, alternating constipation and diarrhoea
            - <u>Sx of right-sided CRC</u>:
                - Abdominal mass
                - Anaemic Sx
            - <u>Sx of rectal cancer</u>:
                - Tenesmus
        - **Review of upper GI Sx** - points towards esophageal or gastric malignancy:
            - Epigastric discomfort/ Discomfort
            - Nausea and vomiting
            - UGIB
            - Dysphagia
- **P/E:**
    - **General examination** - pallor, jaundice, LN, <u>stigmata of chronic liver disease</u>
    - **Abdominal examination:**
        - <u>Inspection</u> - abdominal distension, visible mass, skin changes (e.g Sister Mary Joseph Nodule)
        - <u>Palpation</u> - characterise hepatomegaly or other organomegaly:
            - **Characterisation of hepatomegaly** - size, consistency, surface, edge, tenderness, pulsation, bruit:
                - <u>Diffuse smooth firm hepatomegaly</u> - suggestive of alcoholic cirrhosis or haematological malignancies
                - <u>Irregular firm or hard hepatomegaly</u> - HCC (can be quite soft as vascular), intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, secondary malignancy
        - <u>Auscultation</u> - listen for **liver bruits**
        - <u>Test for shifting dullness</u> - any ascites
    - **PR bleeding** - rectal mass, PR bleeding etc.
    - **Examination of other Sx**
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, Clotting profile:
        - <u>CBC</u> - anaemia, thrombocytopenia (pHTN)
        - <u>LFT</u> - dLFT or cholestatic pattern
        - <u>Clotting profile</u> - isolated increased PT/ INR suggesting decompensated liver failure
    - **Viral hepatitis serology** - HBsAg, Anti-HBs, Anti-HBc, Anti-HCV
    - **Tumour markers** - AFP, CEA, CA19-9
    - **Imaging** - CXR, US upper abdomen, contrast CT T+A+P, staging imaging (contrast MRI, PET-CT)
    - **+/- other Ix** - based on clinical suspicion:
        - Endoscopy (if suspected primary GI) - colonoscopy (colorectal primary, +ve PR), upper endoscopy
        - Biopsy - FNAC, Trucut core biopsy (risk of bleeding, and tumour cell seeding)
- **Mx** - dependent on underlying cause

## Hepatocellular carcinoma

- **Diagnosis of HCC:**
    - **AFP** - AFP \> 400 ng/mL is almost diagnostic
    - **Imaging** - USG for clinical suspicion, CT scan, MRI or PET-CT:
        - **Triphasic Contrast CT** (Hepatic protocol) -
            - <u>Test properties</u> - 65% Sn, 96% Sp (size dependent)
            - <u>Diagnostic findings</u> - hyper-enhancing lesion in arterial phase with rapid portal venous washout (because HCC receives blood supply chiefly from hepatic artery):
                - **Plain phase** - isodense +/- hypodense (central necrosis)
                - **Arterial phase** - arterial hyperenhancement relative to liver parenchyma (receives more hepatic artery supply than liver parenchyma)
                - **Portal venous phase** - portalvenous washout, appears hypo-dense relative to liver parenchyma (receives less portal venous supply than liver parenchyma)
                - **Other findings** - growth of lesion, enhancing capsule appearance in delayed phase

        
        ![](~/Documents/notes/img/hcc_ct_findings.png)
        - **Primovist contrast MRI** - better test properties than CT (but theoretically the same)
        - **Dual Tracer PET-CT** - 18-FDG and C-11 Acetate with superior Sn:
            - <u>Principles of dual tracers</u>:
                - Well differentiated HCC - highlighted by C-11 acetate as FDG is simutaneously taken up by HCC and normal liver parenchyma
                - Poorly differentiated HCC - highlighted by FDG as differential uptake by HCC and normal liver parenchyma
    - **Bx** - FNAC or trucut Bx for inoperable cases

# GC51 Fever and confusion, CNS infections <span class="tag" tag-name="Medicine"><span class="smallcaps">Medicine</span></span> <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

## Clinical approach to patients with fever and confusion

- **DDx of fever and confusion:**
    - <u>CNS infections</u> - meningitis, encephalitis, suppurative brain infections
    - <u>Sepsis causing encephalopathy</u> - non-CNS infections +/- systemic involvement
    - <u>Infection/ Sepsis in patient w/ primary psychiatric disorder</u>
    - <u>Autoimmune encephalitis</u> - e.g. anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis
    - <u>Inflammatory disorders of the brain</u> - e.g. cerebral vasculitis, cerebral lupus
    - <u>Thyroid storm</u>
- **Salient points of Hx** - approach to patient w/ fever and confusion:
    - **HPI** - onset, duration, progression and pattern of fever and confusion
    - **Associated Sx:**
        - <u>Headache</u> - characterise site, onset, progression, quality, severity
        - <u>Neck stiffness</u> - Sx of meningeal irritation
        - <u>Photophobia</u> - Sx of meningial irritation
        - <u>Sx of raised ICP</u> - e.g. headache, vomiting
        - <u>Focal neurological Sx</u> - e.g. motor or sensory deficits, bulbar Sx, visual field deficits, dysphasia
        - <u>Sx of other non-CNS infections</u>:
            - **Respiratory infections** - cough and sputum, SOB, haemoptysis
            - **Acute rhinosinusitis** - nasal discharge, nasal obstruction, facial pain
            - **GI infections** - abdominal pain, diarrhoea
            - **Urinary tract infections** - loin pain, haematuria, dysuria, LUTS
    - **PMH** - identify risk factors for infection:
        - Immunocomprimised states - DM, immunosuppressants, HIV, prior splenectomy etc.
        - Hx of tuberculosis
        - Other chronic illnesses
        - Immunisation Hx
    - **TOCC Hx:**
        - <u>Occupation Hx</u> - butcher exposed to Streptococcus suis infection
    - **Drug Hx:**
        - <u>Recent use of ABx prior to presentation</u> (visit to GP) - affects culture results
        - <u>Use of immunosuppressants</u> - steroids, azathioprine, cyclosporine
- **P/E:**
    - <u>General examination</u> - temperature, vital signs, rash
    - <u>Neurological examination</u>:
        - Assessment of conscious level - GCS
        - Assessment of meningism - Kernig's sign, Brudzinki's sign
        - Focal neurological exam - motor and sensory exam of UL and LL
        - Fundoscopic examination - papilloedema suggestive of raised ICP
    - <u>Other systems</u> - respiratory examination, abdominal examination, cardiovascular examination
- **Ix** - quick septic workup before initiation of empirical Tx:
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, Clotting profile, inflammatory markers, ABG, BG, lactate:
        - <u>CBC w/ differentials</u> - leukocytosis or leukopenia
        - <u>LRFT</u> - baseline organ dysfunction (Albumin marker of severity for certain infectioons, e.g. pneumonia)
        - <u>Clotting profile</u> - identify DIC (C/I for LP)
        - <u>Inflammatory markers</u> (CRP, ESR) - non-specifically elevated
        - <u>Lactate</u> - marker of sepsis and shock
        - <u>BG</u> - for pairing w/ CSF analysis
        - +/- <u>ABG</u> - hypoxaemia or hypercapnia
    - **Urinalysis** - dipstick for glucose, protein, blood, WBC, and bacteria
    - **Neuroimaging** - indicated for all patients requiring LP:
        - **Non-contrast CT-brain** - to r/o mass lesions (e.g. neoplastic lesions, brain abscess) before lumbar puncture (esp. if focal neurological deficits or impaired consciousness)
    - **Microbiology** - blood culture, urine culture (must be obtained prior to initiation of ABx)
    - **Lumbar puncture:**
        - <u>Appearance</u> - colourless/ straw coloured, turbid, purulent, xanthochromia, blood stained
        - <u>WBC count and differentials</u>
        - <u>Glucose</u> - compared w/ paired blood glucose
        - <u>Protein</u>
        - <u>Microbiology</u> - G stain, routine C/ST, AFB stain and culture, indian ink stain, fungal culture, viral studies, PCR
    - **Other Ix as indicated** - identify other primary infections:
        - **Imaging:**
            - <u>CXR</u> - pneumonia
            - <u>Contrast CT/ MRI</u> - abscess, meningeal enhancement, tuberculomas (rim enhancement)
            - <u>Echo</u> - suspecting IE
        - **Microbiology:**
            - <u>NPA</u> - for respiratory viruses
            - <u>Sputum</u> - C/ST +/- AFB
            - <u>Bilster fluids</u> - viral studies (e.g. PCR)
        - **EEG** - generalised slow waves (encephalopathic changes) or periodic lateralising epileptiform discharges (e.g. HSV encephalitis)

## Meningitis

- **Etiology of bacterial meningitis** - dependent on age of onset: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/etiology_of_bacterial_meningitis.png)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Meningitic dose ABx</u> - high dose empirical ABx that crosses BBB, with refinement of regimen by culture results
        - <u>Adjunctive dexamethasone</u> - reduction of cerebral oedema although benefits inconclusive
        - <u>Contact tracing</u> - chemoprophylaxis for close-contacts
    - **Empirical IV meningitic dose ABx:**
        - <u>Rationale</u> - coverage for most likely organisms
        - <u>Regimen</u> (houseman handbook) - duration dependent on underlying organism but maintain IV for CNS penetration:
            - Ceftriaxone 2g q12h (or cefotaxime 1.5-2g IV q4h) + vancomycin 500-1000 mg q6-12h target trough concentraiton of 15-20 mcg/ml
            - Add on ampicillin 2g IV q4h if at risk of listeriosis

## Tuberculous Meningitis

- **Definition** - form of subacute meningitis caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis
- **Principles of clinical presentation** - raise suspicion of TB meningitis when:
    - Slower onset than typical bacterial meningitis (fever and headache for days to weeks)
    - LP shows mononuclear pleocytosis w/ reduced CSF/ plasma glucose ratio and elevated proteins (and -ve bacterial culture)
- **Ix:**
    - **Neuroimaging** (CT/ MRI) - variable findings including:
        - Hydrocephalus
        - Meningeal enhancement
        - Intracranial tuberculoma
    - **LP:**
        - <u>WBC and differentials</u> - mononuclear pleocytosis (50-300 cells/mm3)
        - <u>Glucose</u> - reduced CSF/ plasma ratio
        - <u>Protein</u> - elevated
        - <u>Microbiology</u> - -ve for bacteria and low Sn for AFB:
            - **Microscopy** - AFB on Ziehl-Neelsen smear (\< 15%)
            - **Culture** - \< 50% return +ve
            - **PCR** - 50-90% +ve
    - **Additional Ix** - for concomittent for pulmonary TB:
        - Sputum x AFB smear + C/ST
        - CXR
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Anti-TB therapy</u> - extended duration of isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol for 9-12 mo (extended duration \[18 mo\] if immunocomprimised)
        - <u>Dexamethasone</u> - tapering dose of dexamethaxone for all suspected TB meningitis
- **Prognosis** - high mortality and morbidity

## Viral Meningitis

- **Definition** - benign and self-limiting meningitis of viral origin
- **Clinical presentation** - benign and self-limiting illness
- **Etiology of viral meningitis:**
    - <u>Enteroviruses</u> - e.g. echovirus, Coxackievirus, polio virus
    - <u>Herpesviridae</u> - e.g. HSV-1, **HSV-2**, VZV, CMV, EBV
    - <u>HIV</u>
- **DDx of aseptic meningitis:**
    - Acute bacterial meningitis partially treated w/ ABx
    - TB/ cryptococcal meningitis
- **Mx** - Symptomatic Mx unless there is associated encephalitis

## Viral Encephalitis

- **Definition** - infection of the brain parenchyma by viruses, often accompanied w/ meningitis
- **Etiology of viral encaphalitis:**
    - <u>Herpesviridae</u> - HSV-1 (most common), HSV-2, VZV, CMV, EBV
    - <u>Enteroviruses</u> - Coxackie virus, echovirus, poliovirus
    - <u>Respiratory viruses</u> - influenza virus, adenovirus
    - <u>Flaviviruses</u> - e.g. Japanese encephalitis virus
    - <u>Others</u> - Rabies virus, HIV
- **Pathophysiology of viral encephalitis:**
    - **Inflammatory response in the infected tissue** - often resulting in neuronal degeneration and diffuse glial proliferation, w/ <u>cerebral oedema</u>
    - **Distribution of lesions varies w/ types of virus:**
        - <u>Herpes simplex encephalitis</u> - frontal and temporal lobes
        - <u>CMV</u> - ventriculitis
        - <u>Japanese encephalitis virus</u> - thalamus
- **Clinical presentation of viral encephalitis:**
    - Fever
    - Focal neurological signs (e.g. hemiplegia, dysphasia, hemisensory loss visual field defects)
    - Features of raised ICP - headache, N/V
    - Complications of raised ICP:
        - Delirium
        - Altered GC
        - Seizures
- **Ix:**
    - **Neuroimaging** - hypodensity on CT and hyperintensity on MRI (site of lesion suggests etiology): 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/neuroimaging_in_viral_encephalitis.png)
    - **LP:**
        - <u>WBC and differentials</u> - lymphocytic pleocytosis (neutrophilic in early stages)
        - <u>Glucose</u> - normal
        - <u>Proteins</u> - mild elevations
        - <u>Microbiology</u> - PCR +ve for viruses
    - **EEG** - localising periodic slow-waves or lateralisation of epileptiform discharges
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Empirical IV acyclovir</u> - initiated in all patients w/ suspected viral encephalitis
        - <u>ASM prophylaxis</u> - to prevent seizures

## Autoimmune encephalitis

- **Definition** - inflammation of the brain parenchyma a/w underlying malignancies
- **Epidemiology** - rare disorder, most commonly affecting young women w/ ovarian teratomas or children
- **Clinical features:**
    - Viral like prodrome (fever)
    - Neuropsychiatric manifestations
    - Abnormal movements
    - Seizures
    - Altered GC
- **Ix** - anti-NMDA receptors
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>General measures</u> - supportive care, close monitoring +/- ASM
        - <u>Immunosuppression</u> - high dose IV steroids, IVIG or plasma-exchange
