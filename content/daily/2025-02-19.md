# GC Block B IT - H&N Surgery

Patient scenario:

- M/60
- CC - 3cm neck swelling
- HPI - dysphonia and haemoptysis
- P/E - hard mass, mobile

Q1. DDx of neck swelling in M/60

- Thyroid - Graves, thyroiditis, goitre
- Lymphadenopathy:
    - Benign - reactive causes (think infections from drainage basins):
        - Bacterial lymphadenopathy - strep, TB, brucellosis
        - Viral lymphadenopathy - Infective mono-like syndrome (EBV), HIV
        - Protozoal lymphadenopathy - toxoplasmosis (AIDs-defining)
        - Fungal lymphadenopathy - histoplasmosis, coccidiodomycosis
    - Malignant - metastatic H&N carcinoma, lymphoma (e.g. Hodgkin's):
        - Metastatic H&N SCCs - CA hypopharynx, CA esophagus
        - Other metastic cancers - CA lung, breast, thyroid, stomach
    - Others - infiltrative diseases, CTD (RA, SLE), Drug-related (phenytoin)

Q2. DDx of cervical lymphadenopathy Q3. Drainage basins of cervical LN

- Occipital - posterior scalp
- Post-auricular - Temporal and parietal scalp
- Pre-auricular - anterior and temporal scalp, midface, nose, anterior ear canal, pinna, lateral conjunctiva
- Parotid - forehead and temporal scalp, midface, nose, external ear canal, middle ear, gums, parotid glands
- Submandbular - cheek, nose, lips, anterior tongue, submandibular gland, buccal mucossa
- Superficial cervical - skin, lower larynx, lower ear canal, parotid
- Superior deep cervica - tonsil, adenoid, posterior scalp and neck, tongue, larynx, hypopharynx, thyroid, palate, nose, esophagus, paranasal sinuses, nasopharynx
- Inferior deep cervical - dorsal scalp and neck, nasopharynx, superficial pectoral region of arm, superior deep cervical

Q4. Clinical features of CA larynx

- Clinical classification - supraglottic, glottic, subglottic:
    - Glottic - early presentation with dysphonia (direct involvement of vocal cord)
    - Suprglotic - late presentation w/ non specific symptoms (because quite large area), typically presents w/ dysphagia, otalgia, and neck LN (more lymphatic drainage)
    - Subglottic (rare if isolated, typically from glottic, i.e. transglottic) - presents w/ dyspnoea (?stridor)
- Ix:
    - Tissue diagnosis - microlaryngoscopy Bx under GA
    - Staging Ix - CT/MRI scan, USG neck +/- FNAC, panendoscopy (field cancerisation, with similar RF, and r/o synchronous tumour 10%)
- Mx - dependent on early or advanced disease:
    - Early disease - single modality Tx (surgery or RT)
        - Surgery - endolaryngeal surgery (Laser, Cordectomy)
    - Advanced disease - combined modality of Tx:
        - Surgery + post-op adjuvant RT
        - Concurrent chemoRT - esp for early T3, with possible organ preservation
        - Total laryngectomy +/- neck dissection
