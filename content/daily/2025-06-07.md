# GC164 I am depressed: Mood Disorders <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- **Low mood vs depressive episode** - is depression always pathological:
    - **Mood fluctuations are normal if short lived, mild and does not cause functional limitation as:**
        - Normal response to dissapointments, frustractions and losses in every life
        - Serve a survival function in reorientation and muturation of the individual
    - **Major depressive disorder:**
        - <u>Characterised by duration and pervasiveness of Sx</u> - at least 2 weeks duration, where depressed mood persists throughout most of the day, nearly every day
        - <u>Characterised by severity of Sx</u> - 'marked' diminished interest or pleasure, 'significant' weight loss or gain
        - <u>Characterised by functional limitations</u> - Impaired adaptation, and often disabling
- **Epidemiology of depression:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 5% of adults suffer from the disorder (280 million world wide)
    - <u>Geographic variation</u> - little variation between high-income and low-income countries interms of average lifetime risk and 12-mo prevalence
    - <u>Demographic</u> - mean age of onset ~27y, female preponderance (2:1)
    - Data in HK - Hong Kong Metal Morbidity Survey:
        - 6.9% - mixed anxiety and depressive disorder
        - 2.9% - depressive episode
    - <u>Societal burden</u> - one of the leading causes of DALY: 
    ![](../img/daly_causes_in_3_decades.png)
- **Impact of depression** - increased suicidal and non-suicidal comorbidities:
    - <u>Increased suicidal risk</u> - 20 fold increased suicide risk attributable to depression (6% lifetime risk), where suicide was cause of death in 6% patients w/ psychiatric disorders (WHO studies)
    - <u>Increased non-suicidal mortality</u> (RR 1.2-4.0) - data from meta-analysis of 61 reports (Shultz et al. 2002):
        - Behavioural mediators - e.g. increased substance abuse, inactivity, poor adherance to medical Tx for comorbidities
        - Biological mediators - e.g. altered haemostasis
        - Medical mediators - higher prevalence of subclinical diseases (e.g. coronary artery disease)
    - Psychological autopsy demonstrates depression acounts for significant proportion of suicides in adults and elderly in HK
- **Clinical presentation of depression** - presentation can vary:
    - **Younger people:**
        - <u>Anhedonia</u> - loss of interest in friends, or hobbies
        - <u>Functional limitation</u> - as reflected by decline in school performance
        - <u>Self harm</u> - self-injury, bulimia or drug use in a previously stable adolescent
    - **Older people** - may mimic dementia as MCI or deterioration in self-care
- **Diagnostic criteria of major depressive disorder** (DSM-5) - A-C represents a major depressive episode:
    - **A. Symptomatology** - Presence of \>= 5 Sx for 1) 2 week period, and 2) represent change from previous functioning, where at least one of the core Sx, anhedonia, or depressed mood is present:
        - <u>Depressed mood</u> - depressed mood on subjective account or observations made by others that are present <u>most of the day, nearly every day</u>
        - <u>Anhedonia</u> - **markly diminished** interest or pleasure in all, or almost all activities for <u>most of the day, nearly every day</u>
        - <u>Significant weight changes</u> - weight loss when not dieting (\> 5% BW over 1 mo) or weight gain, or changes in appetite
        - <u>Changes in sleep</u> - insomnia or hypersomnia
        - <u>Psychomotor agitation or retardation</u> - observable by others nearly everyday
        - <u>Low energy</u> - fatigue or loss of energy every day
        - <u>Depressive cognition</u> - feeling worthlessness or excessive or inappropriate guilt (which may be delusional) nearly every day (not merely self-reproach or guilt about being sick)
        - <u>Cognitive impairment</u> - diminished ability to think, concentrate or indecisiveness nearly every day noted on subjective account or observations made by others
        - <u>Suicidal risk</u> - recurrent thoughts of death, recurrent suicidal ideations w/ or w/o a specific plan, or a suicidal attempt
    - **B. functional limitation** - symptoms cause significant distress and impairment in social, occupational or other important areas of functioning
    - **C. Rule out other etiologies** - the episode is not attributable to substance (alcohol or drugs) or related to another medical condition
    - **D-E. Not better explained by other psychiatric conditions:**
        - <u>Schizophrenia and related psychosis</u> - e.g. schizoaffective disorder, schizophrenia, schizophreniform, delusional disorders or other psychotic disorders
        - <u>Bipolar disorders</u> - there has not been a manic episode or hyponanic episode
    - **Specifiers:**
        - With anxious distress
        - With mixed features
        - With melancholic features
        - With atypical features
        - With mood-congruent psychotic features
        - With mood-incongruent psychotic features
        - With catatonia
        - With peripartum onset
        - With seasonal pattern
- **Etiology of depressive disorders** - multi-factorial disease:
    - **Biological factors:**
        - <u>Monoamine hypothesis</u> - depletion of serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine where are relevant to mood regulation
        - <u>Genetic factor</u> - 3 fold increased risk of depression among first-degree relatives compared to general population
        - <u>Hormonal factors</u> - adrenal, sex hormones, thyroid axis, BDNF levels
    - **Environmental factors:**
        - <u>Upbringing</u> - parental separation, physical or sexual abuse, non-caring or overprotective parents
        - <u>Social support</u> - lack of supportive networks, poor social integration, poorly functioning relationships
        - <u>Stress and trauma</u> - e.g. recent life events leading to feeling of entrapment and humiliation, long term struggles
    - **Temperamental factors:**
        - <u>Personality</u> - sociotropy, neurotism
        - <u>Cognitive distortions</u>:
            - Selective abstractions - focusing on a detail and ignoring more important features of a situation
            - Overgeneralisation -drawing a general conclusion on the basis of a single incident
            - Personalisation - relating external events to oneself in an unwarranted way
            - Arbitrary inference - drawing a conclusion where there is no evidence for it and even some evidence against it
- **DDx of depressive episodes:**
    - **Psychiatric differential diagnosis:**
        - <u>Sadness</u> - Dx of depression not to be made unless severe (\>5 Sx including cardinal features), prolonged (most of the day, nearly everyday for 2 weeks) and causes significant distress or functional impairment
        - <u>Manic episodes with irritable mood or mixed episodes</u> - requires careful clinical evaluation of manic symptoms
        - <u>Mood disorders related to another medical condition</u> - e.g. neurological (MS, PD), endocrine (hypothyroidism), medication-induced, infectious disease
        - <u>Substance/medication-induced depressive or bipolar disorder</u> - a substance appears to be etiologically related to mood disturbance
        - <u>Attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder</u> - irritability, distractibility and low frustration tolerance are overlapping features in both disorders, esp. if the mood is characterized by irritable rather than depressed and anhedonic
        - <u>Adjustment disorder with depressed mood</u> - occurs in response to psychosocial stressor
    - **Medical differential diagnosis:**
        - <u>Neurological disorders</u> - e.g. Parkinson's disease, dementia, multiple sclerosis etc.
        - <u>Endocrinopathies</u> - e.g. hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, Addisons disease, Cushing's syndrome, hyperparathyroidism
        - <u>Medication-induced depression</u> - e.g. sedatives, methyldopa, alcohol, cocaine, amphetamines etc.
        - <u>Infectious disease</u> - e.g. HIV, Lyme disease, syphilis
- **Assessment of suspected depression:**
    - <u>Hx and MSE</u> - detailed psychiatric Hx, and medical Hx
    - <u>Standardised instrument</u> - objective measures of severity of depression
    - <u>Ix</u> - r/o medical conditions that cause depressive symptoms:
        - **Routines** - CBC, LRFT, TFT
        - **Additional Ix:**
            - Blood alcohol level
            - Toxicology screen
            - HIV test
            - ACTH stimulation test
            - Hormonal profile +/- MRI pituitary for causes of hypopituitarism
- **Standardised instruments for assessment of depression:**
    - Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9)
    - Hamilton Rating Scale for Depression (HAM-D)
    - Montgomery-Asberg Depression Rating Scale (MADRS)
    - Beck Depression Inventory (BDI)
    - Geriatric Depression Scale
    - Edingburgh Postnatal Depression Scale
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - multimodal Tx:
        - <u>Pharmacological Tx</u> - monoamine hypothesis is a prominent treatment avenue
        - <u>Psychosocial Tx</u> - e.g. talk therapy, CBT, problem solving therapy, supportive theray
        - <u>Physical activity</u> - wealth of evidence showing benefits of exercise
        - <u>Other physical therapies</u> - ECT, transcranial magnetic stimulation, transcranial direct current stimulation
