- **Overview:**
    - Jaundice results from bilirubin accumulation in skin and sclera:
        - Yellow - unconjugated
        - Yellow with green hue - conjugated bilirubin
    - Threshold for visible jaundice - when reach 80-100 micromol/L
    - Recall davidson that neonates have much lower secretory capacity for bilirubin, so haeolysis can result in marked jaundice
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Prevalence - 50% of term infants, and 80% of preterm baby will have clincical jaundice (more common in asians)
- **Physiology of NNJ** - why neonates for susceptible to jaundice:
    - Increased breakdown of haem:
        - High Hb load in neonates (mean 17-19 g/dL)
        - Short RBC lifespan due to transition from HbF to HbA (80d)
    - Immature liver metabolic function:
        - Low ligandin - reduced uptake of bilirubin into liver cells
        - Low conjugation enzyme activity (UDGPT) - NB GIbert syndrome, Criggler Najjar syndrome
    - Increased enterohepatic circulation - esp when feeding is minimal in first 2-3d
- **Physiological jaundice of newborn:**
    - Etiology - Due to accumulation of unconjugated bilirubin
    - Natrual Hx - Dx requires fitting w/ natural Hx and r/o other causes of NNJ:
        - No jaundice within first 24h (onset first 24h must be pathological)
        - Apparent from day 2-3 of life
        - Peak at day 4-5
        - Subside at day 7-14
    - Ix - r/o other causes of jaundice:
        - Unconjugated bil - commonly elevated (\> 170 micromol/L)
        - Conjucated bil - absent
        - Normal LFTs except bil
        - Peripheral blood smear - no evidence of haemolysis
- **DDx of NNJ:**
    - Physiological NNJ
    - Haemolysis:
        - Immune - Rhesus incompatibility, ABO incompatibility
        - Non-immune - G6PD deficiency (detected in cord blood screening)
        - Extravastation of blood (e.g. birth trauma) - e.g. cephalhematoma, intraventricular haemorrhage
        - Polycythaemia - causes of in utero hypoxia
    - Inadequate feeding - increased enterohepatic circulation
    - Sepsis
    - Hepatobiliary disease (conjugated hyeperbilirubinaemia)
- **Hazards of NNJ:**
    - <u>Theoretical damage to CNS</u> - hydrophobic substance, cross lipid bilayer and BBB to cause neural tissue damage:
        - Protective factors - bound to albumin
        - Destructive factors - high free bilirubin is high, in context of low albumin, or competitive binding to albumin by drugs
    - <u>Kernicterus</u> - death or long-term neurological morbidity caused by excessive unconjugated bilirubin (dystonic cerebral palsy \[bili likes to deposit in BG and other subcortical tissue\], hearing loss, upward gaze palsy)
        - Risk higher if:
            - Prolonged period of very high levels
            - High free bilirubin
            - Active haemolysis
            - Sepsis
            - Acidosis
            - Hypoglycaemia
            - Prematurity
        - Signs of kernicterus:
            - Early - Hypotonia, poor feeding, lethargy, hypertonia, opisthotonus, fever, convusion, death
            - Late - extrapyramidal signs, hearing loss
    - **Salient points of Hx:**
        - Gestation
        - Prenatal complication (e.g. in utero hypoxia)
        - Birth weight - compare with current weight to access whether excessive weight loss due to inadequate feeding
        - Age of life - phototherapy threshold
        - Feeding details - mode of feeding, amount, frequency
        - Blood group of mother / G6PD status baby
        - Other postnatal complications
    - **P/E:**
        - Severity of jaundice - using transcutaneous bilirubinometer
        - Hydration (feeding status)
        - Pallor (haemolysis) / plethora (polycythaemia)
        - Cephalhematoma/ bruises
        - Neurological state
        - Hepatomegaly and splenomegaly
    - **Mx of NNJ:**
        - **Principles of Mx:**
            - Intervention before reaching risky levels:
                - Term - \< 20 mg/dL (340 micromol/L)
                - Lower threshold in preterm infants
            - Avoid risk factors for kerniecterus
        - **Phototherapy** - note can be in incubator form or bilirubin blanket (for mild cases to promote mother-baby contact)
            - Indicated by phototherapy chart
            - Wave length - 460 nm (blue light spectrum)
            - MOA - photo-isomerisation of bilirubin to less toxic (hydrophilic) and readily excretable forms (in bile and urine)
            - S/E:
                - Corneal and retinal damage (use eye shield)
                - Descication and dehydration
                - Skin rash
                - Loose stool
        - **Exchange transfusion:**
            - Removal of plasma bilirubin by gradual exchange of patients blood
            - <u>Indication</u> - when plasma bilirubin reaches dangerous level (e.g. \> 370 micromol/l) or child already showing signs of neurotoxicity
            - S/E:
                - Risk of blood transfusion
                - Haemodynamic comprimise
        - **IVIG for immune haemolytic anaemia** - e.g. incompatibility
            - <u>MOA</u> - ?binding to Ab to prevent haemolysis
- **Breast feeding jaundice vs breast milk jaundice:**
    - Breastfeeding jaundice - insufficient breast milk supply resulting in unconjugcated hyperbilirubinae due to <u>increased enterohepatic circulation</u>
    - Breast milk jaundice - ingredients in breasst milk slow down conjugation
- **Bresat milk jaundice:**
    - More prolonged but to lower severity - hence benign
    - Evidence supports genetic predisposition (more common in Chinese)
    - Pathophysiology:
        - See above
    - Prolonged - beyond 14d even up to 2-3 mo
    - Unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia
    - Baby otherwise healthy
    - Reassure continue breast milk
- **Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia of newborn:**
    - **Definition:**
        - \> 2 mg/dL (\> 35 micromol/L) or \> 15% of total serum bilirubin
        - a/w:
            - derranged LFT
            - derrange synthetic functions - PT, Albumin
            - derranged detoxification - e.g. hyperammonemia
            - derranged metabolic function - hypoglycaemia
    - **Etiology:**
        - Neonatal period:
            - Neonatal hepatitis
            - Biliary atresia, choledochal cyst (obstructive jaundice)
            - Metabolic disease (?IEM) - e.g. citrin deficiency, galactosemia
            - Parenteral nutrion associated cholestasis
            - Syndromal disorders
            - Neonatal haemochromatosis
        - Beyond neonatal period:
            - Viral hepatitis
            - Drug induced hepatotoxicity
            - Wilson's disease
            - Haemochromatosis and other metabolic disease
            - Hereditary hyperbilirubinaemia (?Rotor, Dubin Johnson syndrome)
    - **Most important to r/o biliary atresia and choledochal cyst**
- **Biliary atresia** - progressive obliterative cholangiopathy due to ductal obstruction (read slides)

- **Definition** - when breathing (oxygenation and ventilation) does not meet the metabolic demand for oxygenation, characterised by dyspnoea, and signs such as tachypnoea, use of accessory muscles, and/or retraction
- **Reason for importance of children w/ SOB** - rapid progression to respiratory failure and demise:
    - Greater oxygen consumption than adults
    - Less respiratory reserve
- **Pathophysiology of dyspnoea** - dyspnoea may develop as a response to:
    - Hypoxaemia
    - Hypercapnia
    - Impaired mechanics of respiration - from airway obstruction, muscle weakness or discomfort
    - Stimulated cortical contro for respiration - e.g. metabolic acidosis, hyperammonaemia, salicylate intoxification
- **Etiology of dyspnoea in children:**
    - **Respiratory causes:**
        - **Upper respiratory tract:**
            - <u>Congenital</u> - laryngomalacia, laryngospasm, tracheoesophageal fistula, tracheal stenosis, tracheal ring or sling
            - <u>Infective</u> - epiglottitis, tonsilitis, peritonsillar abscess, retropharyngeal abscess, croup, tracheitis
            - <u>Foreign body</u> - upper airway obstruction
            - <u>Immune</u> - anaphylaxis
            - <u>Environmental</u> - smoke inhalation, biologic or chemical weapons (e.g. anthrax, tularemia, phosgene, mustard gas, nerve agents, ricin), chemical agent exposure (e.g. chlorine, cyanide)
        - **Lower respiratory tract:**
            - <u>Infective</u> - bronchiolitis, pneumonia
            - <u>Asthma</u> - presentation as acute asthmatic attack
        - **Pleura and chest wall:**
            - <u>Pleural condition</u> - pneumothorax, haemothorax, pleural effusion, empyema
            - <u>Chest wall conditions</u> - flail chest, open pneumothorax, thoracic dystrophy
    - **Cardiovascular cause** - acute decompensated heart failure due to:
        - <u>Congenital heart disease</u> - all congenital heart disease can present w/ HF
        - <u>Arrhythmia</u>
        - <u>Myocardial disease</u> - myocarditis, myocardial infarction
        - <u>Pericardial disease</u> - pericarditis, cardiac tamponade
    - **Metabolic causes:**
        - <u>Metabolic acidosis</u> - DKA, severe dehydration, sepsis, toxic ingestions, IEM
        - <u>Hypocalacaemia</u> - causing laryngospasm
    - **Haematological causes:**
        - <u>Reduced DO2 capacity</u> - anaemia, haemoglobin variants, methemoglobinemia, CO poisoning
        - <u>Sickle cell disease</u> - acute chest syndrome
