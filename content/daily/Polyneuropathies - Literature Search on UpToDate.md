## Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy: Etiology, clinical features, and diagnosis

- **Definition** - an acquired, immune-mediated demyelinating polyneuropathy affecting peripheral nerves and nerve roots (polyradiculoneuropathy) characterised by a relapsin-remitting or progressive course of symmetric weakness of proximal and distal muscles +/- sensory involvement
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 0.7-10.3/100000
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - increasing incidence w/ age (peak incidence suggested at around 6th decade of life)
        - Sex - male preponderance
- **Pathophysiology of CIDP** - not completely understood:
    - **Immune-mediated inflamation** - a/w inflammatory demyelination of peripheral nerve that is mediated by both <u>cellular</u> and <u>humoral</u> immunity
    - **Patchy demyelination of the peripheral nerves** - segmental demyelination and remyelination of peripheral nerves resulting in onion bulb formation:
        - <u>Onion bulb formation</u> - repeated episodes of demyelination and remyelination causes thickening of nerve fibre due to whorls of overlapping and proliferating Schwann cell processes encircling bare axons
        - <u>Pattern of involvement</u> - segmental, with preferentially paranodally at the **node of Ranvier**; such patchy nerve involvement results in **inhomogenous thickness** over the length of the nerve
    - **Axonal degeneration** - some axonal degeration is usually present as well, although it is <u>not the predominating pathogenic mechanism</u>, but may <u>explain failure to return to normal state</u>
    - **AutoAb** - specific Ab (NF, CNTN1) found in 10% cases of CIDP may have <u>specific pathogenic mechanisms</u> rather than causing demyelination, and hence **clinical phenotypes and response to Tx differ** from typical CIDP (see below)
- **Patterns of clinical presentation of CIDP:**
    - **Typical CIDP** (50-60%) - symmetric sensorimotor polyneuropathy w/ proximal and distal muscle weakness that exceeds extent of sensory loss
    - **CIDP variants:**
        - <u>Multifocal CIDP</u> (5-10%) - Lewis-Sumner syndrome, presentation w/ strikingly asymmetric, multifocal picture indistinguishable from other forms of mononeuritis multiplex
        - <u>Focal CIDP</u> - presents w/ sensorimotor deficits isolated to the brachial or lumbosacral plexus
        - <u>Pure motor CIDP</u> - exttremely rare prseentation with symmetric motor weakness and sparing sensory fibres confirmed on electrophysiology testing (requires distinguishing from multifocal neuropathy and ALS \[progressive motor atrophy\])
        - <u>Pure sensory CIDP</u> - sensory dysfunction (e.g. pain, numbness, proprioceptive dysfunction) in a length-independent fashion
        - <u>Distal CIDP</u> - distal and sensory-predominant variant of CIDP often presents w/ length-dependent, symmetrical sensory or sensorimotor dysfunction of the LL
- **Clinical features of typical CIDP** - progression of sensorimotor Sx is classical in typical CIDP:
    - **Muscle weakness** (predominant syndrome) - gradually progressive symmetrical motor weakness in a <u>non-length dependent pattern</u> (affecting proximal and distal muscles in similar degrees):
        - <u>Site</u> - symmetrical, with involvement of proximal and distal muscles (but distribution may be variable):
            - **Proximal LL weakness** - manifests as difficulty in climbing or descending stairs, rising from a seated position
            - **Proximal UL weakness** - manifests as difficulty in reaching cupboards, difficulty in lifting objects overhead
            - **Distal LL weakness** - tripping over feet due to foot drop ('high-stepping gait')
            - **Distal UL weakness** - difficulty in fine motor tasks such as buttoning, opening doors, opening jars
        - <u>Onset and progression</u> - gradual onset w/ gradually progressive symptoms over course of several months or longer (by definition, Dx of CIDP requires progression of disease \> 8w)
        - <u>Pattern</u> - non-length dependent pattern (affecting proximal and distal muscles in a similar manner) that is characteristic of acquired demyelinating neuropathies
    - **Sensory involvement** - features <u>far less prominant than motor features</u> and typically **worse distally**, and is greater for vibration and proprioception compared w/ pain and temperature sensation reflecting <u>large fibre involvement</u>:
        - <u>Sensory ataxia</u> - results in ataxic gait (stomping gait) due to loss of proprioception
        - <u>Painful dysethesia</u> - can occur
    - **Autonomic involvement** - generally mild and limited in distribution, where constipation and AROU are not usually early Sx
- **DDx of CIDP** - consider other immune-mediated demyelinating neuropathies:
    - <u>Acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy</u> (i.e. form of GBS) - CIDP w/ more indolent onset, while GBS a/w more acute onset, with temporal differences where GBS typically reaches nadir within 3-4 weeks, while CIDP continues to progress
    - <u>Multifocal motor neuropathy</u> (MMN) - especially absence of sensory involvement on electrophysiological testing
    - <u>Paraprotein-mediated demyelinating neuropathy</u>:
        - POEMS syndrome
        - IgM-associated demyelinating neuropathies - CANOMAD, DADS with monoclonal IgM gammopathy and anti-MAG
    - <u>Medication S/E</u> - ICIs, TNF-alpha
    - <u>Hereditary polyneuropathy</u> - certain types of CMT disease, HNLPP etc.
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, FBG/ A1c, SPE (r/o other causes)
    - **Electrophysiological studies** - NCS of sensory and motor nerves, EMG (shows primary demyelinating pattern)
    - **LP** - if electrophysiological studies inconclusive; shows <u>albuminocytologic dissociation</u>:
        - **Protein** - elevated (higher threshold a/w higher Sn, e.g. \> 100 mg/dL)
        - **WBC** - normal (i.e. classic albuminocytogenic dissociation)
    - **Nerve USG** - detect hypertrophy of nerves in demyelinating neuropathies but not specific to CIDP
    - **Nerve Bx** - if other studies fail Dx of CIDP
- **Dx** - EAN/PNS criteria requires 1) compatible clinical picture, and 2) compatible electrodiagnostic studies:
    - **Compatible clinical picture** - for typical CIDP:
        - Chronically progressive, stepwise or recurrent <u>symmetrical proximal and distal weakness and sensory dysfunction</u> of at least two limbs, developing over <u>two months</u> or longer; cranial nerves may be affected
        - <u>Absent or reduced</u> jerks in all extremities

## Chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy: Treatment and prognosis

- **Principles of Mx:**
    - **Goals of Mx** - cessation of immune-mediated demyelination to <u>avoid secondary axonal degeneration</u> to minimize long-term disability
    - **Decision to Tx** - dependent on severity and course of disease:
        - <u>Mild disease w/ minimal impact on function</u> - Tx not required
        - <u>More than mild disease w/ impact on function</u> - Tx often indicated
    - **Course of Tx:**
        - <u>Initial immunomodulating therapy</u> - for rapid clinical improvement with assessment of response
        - <u>Maintenance therapy</u> - sustain improvement and promote remission
- **Initial therapy** - for those who are more than mildly affected or rapidly worsening:
    - **Selection of initial immunomodulatory Tx** - equally effective:
        - Intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG)
        - Plasma exchange
        - Glucocorticoids
    - **Factors influencing choice of initial therapy:**
        - Disease severity
        - Concurrent illness
        - Venous access
        - Treatment S/E
        - Availability and cost
    - **IVIG** - considered when rapid response is a priority:
        - <u>Clinical efficacy</u> - meta-analysis of 235 patients showed rate of disability improvement one month favoured IVIG over placebo (44% vs 26%)
        - <u>Dosing regimen</u>:
            - 2g/kg infusion over 4-5d (e.g. 0.4g/kg per day for 5d)
            - Repeat IVIG dosing at 1g/kg every 2-4 weeks depending on clinical course (usually for 2-3 mo \[84% respond within 9 weeks in ICE trial\])
        - <u>S/E</u>:
            - Headache
            - Nausea
            - Fever
            - Aseptic meningitis
            - Rash
            - Acute kidney failure (sucrose-cotaining)
    - **Glucocorticoids** - considered if indolent course, where rapid response is not a priority:
        - <u>Dosing regimen</u>:
            - Pulse-dose glucocorticoids - IV methylprednisolone 1000 mg/d for 3d, followed by one pulse per week for 4 weeks
            - Daily dose glucocorticoids - oral prednisolone 1-1.5 mg/kg/d
        - <u>S/E</u> - standard S/E for chronic use of glucocorticoids
    - **Plasma exchange** - as effective as IVIG but inconvenient:
        - Requires repeated venous access and often indwelling catheter
        - A/w S/E such as sepsis, hypotension and other problems of prolonged intravenous access
- **Assessment of clinical response to therapy** - reassess q3mo after initial therapy or changed therapy and q6mo once on maintenance therapy by application of clinical objective scores (e.g. Grip strength)
- **Maintenance therapy** - taper Tx to minimal dose with successful disease control
