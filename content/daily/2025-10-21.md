## Evaluation of severe asthma in adolescents and adults

- **Definition** - uncontrolled despite prolonged Tx using moderate- to high-dose ICS w/ additional controller agents (i.e. step 4 or 5 therapy)
- **Epidemiology** - 5-10% of asthma population
- **DDx of severe asthma** - differentiate from other causes of severe +/- frequent respiratory Sx:
    - Asthma that is exacerbated by uncontrolled triggers
    - Asthma that is poorly controlled because of non-adherance or poor inhaler technique
    - Asthma that is exacerbated by a comorbid condition
    - A disease that mimics asthma
- **Approach to evaluation of severe asthma:**
    - <u>Hx</u> - characterisation of Sx, exacerbation patterns, severity of exacerbations, triggers, and inhaler adherance and technique
    - <u>P/E</u> - nasal/ sinus evaluation (Samter's triad), neck examination (for neck masses), complete cardiopulmonary examination (r/o asthma-mimics)
    - <u>Ix</u> - PFT, additional laboratory testing, imaging for phenotyping
- **Lung function test** - PFT beyond spirometry often indicated to r/o comorbid respiratory conditions or mimickers:
    - <u>Proocedures</u> - consider bronchoprovocation testing (methacholine challenge) if minimal expiratory airflow limitation:
        - Pre- and post-bronchodilator spirometry w/ inspiratory and expiratory airflow loops
        - Lung volume studies
        - Diffusion capacities
    - <u>Interpretation</u>:
        - **Spirometry:**
            - Expiratory airflow limitations w/ bronchodilator response is consistent w/ asthma diagnosis
            - Persistent airflow limitations seen in \> 50% of patients w/ severe asthma
        - **Lung volume studies** - reduced FVC, increased RV and TLC are evidence of air-trapping where there is significant small airway disease
        - **Diffusion capacity** - normal or increased (N.B. some variants of severe asthma \[e.g. asthmatic granulomatosis\] a/w mild or moderate reduction of DLCO)
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, total serum IgE, specific IgE testing +/- ANCA, alpha-1 antitrypsin level:
        - <u>CBC</u>:
            - Anaemia - cause of dyspnoea
            - Eosinophilia
        - <u>Total serum IgE</u> - to assess for allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA) or identify candidates for anti-IgE therapy
        - <u>Specific IgE testing</u> (as skin test or immunoassay) - for aspergillus sensitization and suspected environmental triggers
        - <u>ANCA</u> - evaluation of Churg-Strauss syndrome
        - <u>A1AT levels</u> - evaluation for A1AT deficiency for those w/ **irreversible obstruction**
    - **Additional immunological testing** - allergy skin test or immuno-assay for specific IgE, Ig patterns, ESR/CRP, autoimmune panel:
        - <u>Skin prick test/ specific IgE assay</u> - if environmental factors are suspected triggers (e.g. animal dander)
        - <u>Ig pattern</u> - indicated for those w/ recurrent infectious triggers to identify immunodeficiency
        - <u>ESR/CRP</u> - screen fo systemic autoinflammatory disease
        - <u>Autoimmune pannel</u> (RF, ANA, ACPA, ANCA) - airway manifestations which can worsen asthma
    - **Chest imaging** - CXR, HRCT:
        - <u>CXR</u> - detection of interstitial opacities to identify asthma mimics
        - <u>Inspiratory and expiratory HRCT</u> - assess for interstitial lung disease and bronchiolitis (mosaic attenuation patterns)
- **Phenotyping asthma** - diverse clinical and pathophysiological features of patients w/ severe asthma characterised into phenotypes:
    - **Clinical phenotypes** - by clinical course:
        - <u>Frequent, severe exacerbations</u> - often atopic and present from childhood, w/ a course characterised by frequent, and often severe exacerbations, but may be completely asymptomatic in between exacerbations
        - <u>Chronic, partially reversible airflow limitations</u> - characterised by severe, airflow limitations that is only partially reversible
    - **Phenotypes by response to biologics:**
        - <u>T2-high phenotypes</u> - characterised by cytokines (IL4, 5, and 13) and range of immune cells that result in consequent downstream **eosinophilic inflammation**
        - <u>T2-low pheontypes</u> - poorly understood but results in consequent downstream **neutrophilic inflammation**

## Characterising severe asthma phenotypes

- **Rationale for characterizing asthma phenotypes** - observation points towards the possibility that asthma is a <u>heterogenous group of disease characterised by extremely variable host response</u>:
    - <u>Variable clinical Hx</u> - severe asthmatics may be characterised by varying clinical courses, atopic Hx, Sx onset, even though they similar respiratory S/S may be present
    - <u>Variable pathophysiological changes</u> - underlying difference between host response beyond degree of airflow limitation (e.g. blood/ sputum eosinophilia)
    - <u>Variable response to pharmacological therapy</u> - as evident by subgroup of patients who are gluco-corticoid resistant, varying response to biologics etc.
- **Type 2 inflammation** - form of airway inflammation characterised by elevations of airway and blood eosinophils, mast cells, basophils, Th2 lymphocytes (expressing IL-4, -5, and -13), and IgE-producing plasma cells:
    - <u>Biomarkers for type 2 inflammation</u>:
        - Blood Eos levels - 200-300/ microL
        - Fractional expired nitric oxide levels - \> 50 ppb in adults, or \> 35 ppb in children, as inducible nitric oxide synthase is expressed by sputum eosinophils
        - Sputum eosinophilia - theoretically gold standard but time-consuming
    - <u>Tx response to T2-directed therapies</u> - evidence of type 2 inflammation (incl. peripheral eosinophilia) appears to be good predictors of response to type-2 targeted therapies:
        - Anti-IL4RA
        - Anti-IL5
- **Severe asthma phenotypes** - note that substantial overlap among groups exists:
    - <u>T2-high phenotype</u> - those characterised by type 2 inflammation
    - <u>T2-low phenotype</u> - those w/ minimal type 2 inflammation
- **Subclassification of Type 2 asthma phenotypes** - most show good response to anti-IgE, anti-IL-4R, anti-IL-5R, and anti-TSLP biologics:
    - <u>Childhood-onset type 2 asthma</u> - arbitrarily defined as onset \< 12y from documented case series, represents a largely <u>homgenous group of patients</u> w/ 1) strong atopic history, and 2) FHx of asthma, and 3) significant peripheral eosinophilia
    - <u>Adult-onset type 2 asthma</u> - arbitrarily defined as \> 12y, with less evidence of atopy from childhood, and exacerbations are often triggered by viral infections, occupational exposures, or ingestion of aspirin rather than traditional allergens
    - <u>Hypereosinophilic adult-onset asthma w/ extrapulmonary disease</u> - subset of T2-high disease not associated w/ atopy, but other autoinflammatory condistions such as **chronic rhinosinusitis w/ naso polyposis**, **aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease** (N.B. Samter's triad), **Churg Strauss syndrome**
    - <u>Asthmatic granulomatosis</u> - rare group of disease w/ granulomatous inflamation unrelated to Churg-Strauss

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/subclassification_of_t2_high_asthma.png)
- **Subclassification of Non-type 2 asthma phenotypes:**
    - <u>Neutrophilic asthma</u> - controversial subtype of asthma characterised by prominent sputum neutrophilia and may be a/w persistent airflow limitations and frequent exacerbations
    - <u>Obesity-associated asthma</u> (IL-6) - complex asssociation between obesity and asthma as obseity can be a cause, but also a consequence of asthma, with various contributing roles of 1) insulin resistance, 2) IL-6 levels, and 3) arginine metabolism
    - <u>Perimenopausal-onset asthma</u> - particular phenotype characterised by onset of asthma within 1y of LMP and is a/w sputum eosinophilia
    - <u>Paucigranulocytic asthma</u> - distinct, poorly understood phenotype of asthma characterised by lack of sputum eosinophilia or neutrophilia

## An overview of asthma management in children and adults

- **Risk factors of asthma exacerbations:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/risk_factors_of_asthma_exacerbation.png)
- **Principles of Mx:**
    - **Goals of Mx:**
        - <u>Optimal control of asthma Sx</u>:
            - Freedom from frequent or troublesome Sx of asthma
            - Freedom from nocturnal Sx resulting in awakening
            - Minimal use of acute relief symptoms
            - Maintenance of normal daily activities
        - <u>Reducing future risks</u>:
            - Preventing asthma exacerbations
            - Prevent loss of lung function (or suboptimal development of lung function in children)
            - Reduce adverse effects from asthma medications (particularly glucocorticoids)
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>General measures</u> - patient education, immunisation, and allergen avoidance
        - <u>Pharmacological therapy</u> - initial pharmacological therapy based on **disease severity**, w/ adjustment based on **level of control**
        - <u>Follow-up monitoring</u> - assessment of severity of asthma and adequacy of control
- **Good asthma control is clinically defined as** - and serves as a marker for step-down therapy if \>= 3 mo:
    - Sx \<= 2d per week
    - Nocturnal awakening due to asthma \<= 1 per month
    - No interference w/ activities
    - Rescue therapy used \<= 2d per week for Sx
    - \<= 1 exacerbation treated w/ systemic glucocorticoids per year
- **Classification of asthma severity** - generally minimal relevance to clinical decision making except for identification of those w/ severe asthma:
    - <u>Mild asthma</u> - minimal Sx and minimal risk of exacerbations in patients not on inhaled therapies, those who use reliever medication alone, or those who use low-dose ICD maintenance therapy along w/ reliever therapy
    - <u>Moderate asthma</u> - good asthma control for those w/ medium-dose inhaled ICS therapy or low-medium-dose inhaled glucocorticoids w/ additional controller therapies
    - <u>Severe asthma</u> - good asthma control w/ high-dose inhaled glucocorticoids w/ additional controller agents, or uncontrollable asthma
- **Patient education:**
    - <u>Nature of asthma</u> - S/S of asthmas, clinical course, known triggers and measures to mitigate allergen exposure
    - <u>Inhaler technique</u> - proper technique and emphasize on role of maintenance therapy for asthma control
    - <u>Asthma action plan</u> - instructions for patients when expierencing asthmatic atacks
- **Pharmacological therapy:**
    - **Approach to pharmacological therapy** - <u>stepwise titration</u> to achieve the best asthma control at the lowest-dose of ICS to minimise adverse effects of systemic absorption of glucocorticoids
    - **Initial pharmacological therapy** - selection of step based on symptom burden: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/initial_pharmacological_therapy_for_asthma.png)
    - **Indications for step-up therapy** - inadequate control of Sx and elevated risk of exacerbations that is not related to:
        - Environmental factors - e.g. inadequate allergen avoidance
        - Patient factor - e.g. poor adherance to maintenance therapy, poor inhaler technique, other comorbidities
    - **Indications for step-down therapy** - good asthma control (see above) for 3-6 mo
    - **Additional therapies for severe asthma:**
        - <u>Long-acting muscarinic agents</u> - e.g. tiotropium
        - <u>Leukotriene modifier</u> - e.g. montelukast
        - <u>Biologic therapies</u> - e.g. Anti-IgE therapy, type 2-targeted therapy, anti-TSLP therapy upon specialist referral and phenotyping of asthma
- **Follow-up monitoring** - pro-active, preventive approach for effective asthma Mx: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/domains_of_assessment_of_asthma_control.png)
    - **Assessment of Sx control:**
        - <u>Daytime Sx</u> - \< 2x per week defined as good control
        - <u>Nocturnal awakening</u> - \< 1x per month defined as good asthma control
        - <u>Activity limitation</u> - no interference w/ normal activities
        - <u>Use of rescue therapy</u> - \< 2x per week excluding prophylaxis for exercise
    - **Assessment of risks factors of exacerbations** - considered independent to Sx control otherwise:
        - <u>Number of exacerbations requiring systemic corticosteroids</u> (or hospitalisation) - risk of exacerbation correlates well w/ number of exacerbations in past year
        - <u>Lung function</u> (Spirometry preferred over PEFR) - poor lung function (esp. FEV1 \< 60% of predicted) a/w higher risk of exacerbations
    - **Evaluation of medications:**
        - Assessment of medication adherance
        - Assessment of inhaler technique
        - Assessment of S/E of medications
    - **Evaluation of co-morbid conditions** - influence asthma control, asthma Sx and potentially other diagnosis: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/comorbid_conditions_worsening_asthma_control.png)
    - **Evaluation of other social factors:**
        - Poor housing conditions
        - Increased exposure to air pollution
        - Smoking or second-handed smoking
