## Pathology of brain tumours

- Classification - primary vs secondary:
    - Primary brain tumours - benign vs intermediate vs malignant:
        - Benign - e.g. meningioma, pituitary adenoma
        - Intermediate - i.e. histologically benign but tendancy to recur or dedifferentiate (e.g. low-grade gliomas, craniopharyngiomas)
        - Malignant - glioblastoma
    - Secondary brain tumours - commonest lesions in adults (lung, breast, colon) and melaenoma (highest propensity but lower incidence)
- Classification of primary brain tumour by cellular origin (see slides)
- Different likelihood based on age and location (see slide):
    - Adults - usually supratentorial (glioma, meningioma)
    - Children - usually erebellar tumours

## CLinical presentation

- Raised ICP - by mass effect, by vasogenic oedema, by CSF flow obstruction (esp 3rd ventricles), by venous congestion
- Focal neurological deficits - LOF due to 1) destruction (resection does not help), 2) Pressure effect, 3) Oedema (reversibility by steroids)
- Seizures - only in supratentorial lesions:
    - Why usually only supratentorial - because it cerebral cortex excitatory neurons, vs cerebellar cortex inhibitory neurons
- Other mass effects (see notes):
    - e.g. CP angle tumour –\> hearing problems +/- facial nerve palsy
    - Pituitary adenoma –\> visual field defect
- Tumour haemorrhage - another mode of tumour presentation –\> mimics haemorrhaging stroke

## Approach

- Ix:
    - Contrast-enhanced CT brain:
        - Contrast enhancement - not just vascularity, but about BBB disruption in CT brain:
            - Meningioma - typically intense dura-based homogenous enhancement since no BBB from ECA supply
            - Low grade glioma - week heterogenous weaker enhancement
            - Malignant glioma - disruption of BBB, better enhancement than low grade glioma
    - MR Spectroscopy - no need to know detail
    - PET-CT - malignant tumour is hypermetabolic
    - Functional MRI and DTI (diffuse tensor imaging) tractography - MRI that outlines major fibre tracts for surgical planning

## Principles of management of brain tumour surgery

- Medical Mx based on presentation:
    - Anticonvulsants - if presenting w/ seizures (rarely prophylatic)
    - Steroids (e.g. dexamethasone 4mg tds, qid) - exclude infection and treats cerebral oedema
    - Tanexamic acid - peripreoperative to reduce bleeding
- Principles of neurosurgical Tx:
    - Obtain histoligical diagnosis
    - Maximal safe removal - preserve life and function (resection margin does not apply as 1cm margin means large brain volume in 3D space)
- Principles of neurosurgery:
    - Craniotomy - skin incision, bone flap and dura opened and replace bone flap
    - Neuronavigation and microsurgery
    - Electrophysiological monitoring - MEP, SSEP etc.
    - Awake craniotomy and speech cortex mapping - for speech preservation as electrophysiological monitoring not working
    - Intra-operative MRI for residual tumour
- Radiation therapy vs radiosurgery (X knife vs gamma knife, cyberknife, not open surgery)

## Brain metastasis

- Epidemiology - commonest intracranial tumour via haematogenous or direct invasion, lymphatic not possible (glymphatic syndrome)
- Common origins:
    - Lung breast, colon, kidney, melaenoma
- May present before the primary disease, or long in remission
- DDx of multiple brain lesions (cannot be presumed to be cerebral metastasis) –\> can be other things (e.g. CNS lymphoma, toxoplasmosis, cysticerosis, abscesses, primary brain tumours)
- Workup:
    - Hx and PE
    - CXR
    - Serum tumour markers - not too helpful
    - SPECT/ PECT
    - Neurosurgery and Bx if in doubt
- Aim of surgery (read slides)
- Prognosis - extremely poor:
    - Untreated - 1mo
    - WBRT - 3-6 mo
    - Surgery + WBRT - 12 mo
    - WBRT less significant for S/E and radiosurgery is preferreed
    - If chemo can cross BBB – improves outcome

## Meningioma

- Pathology - from arachnoid cap cells
- Risks - female, radiation, NF2
- Dura-based lesion with long dural tail and strong homogenous contrast enhancement
