## Defintion and classification

- Dysphagia - difficulty in swalling (clearing food and drinks at appropriate rate and speed)
    - Oropharyngeal dysphagia - difficulty w/ initial phases of a swallow (from mouth to esophagus)
    - Esophageal dysphagia - sensation that food and or liquids are being obstructed in their passage from the mouth to stomach

## Anatomy and Physiology

- Upper 5% of esophagus, upper esophageal, oropharynx, tongue - striated muscles
- Mid 35-40% of esophagus - mixed striated and smooth muscles
- Distal 50-60% of esophagus - smooth muscles (primary peristalsis in response to distention and ?secondary peristalsis)
- 3 phases of swallowing (read up):
    - Oral prepatory and propulsive phase
    - Pharyngeal phase
    - Esophageal phase

## Esophageal dysphagia

- ****DDx of Esophageal dysphagia:****
    - ****Intraluminal**** - foreign body (acute dysphagia)
    - ****Mucosal diseases**** - narrowing of lumen through inflammation, fibrosis, or neoplasia:
        - Benign causes - Esophagitis (irritative, radiation, infective, eosinophilic), esophgeal strictures secondary to GERD, esophageal rings and webs
        - Malignant causes - esophageal tumours, CA stomach, extrinsic compression
    - ****Neuromuscular diseases**** - Achalasia, SSc and MCTD, esophageal spasms (jackhammer esophagus)
    - ****Post-operative**** - after fundoplication, antireflux devices
    - ****Drugs**** - bisphosphonate, K tabs
- ****Salient points of Hx:****
    - Is this true dysphagia:
        - Globus hystericus - something sticking in the throat
    - Distinguishing between oro-pharyngeal vs esophageal dysphagia
    - Distinguishing between mechanical (anatomical) vs funcional (motility disorders)
    - HPI - onset, progression, character (type, response, temperature), associated Sx, severity
        - Site - any point can radiate to cervical region
        - Onset - sudden (mechanical) vs gradual (mechanical or functional)
        - Progression - progressive (mechanical) vs variable (functional)
        - Character:
            - Type of bolus - mechanical causes only obstruct solid bolus, while functional causes obstruct both solid and liquid boluses
            - Response to bolus - mechanical causes cause regurgitation, functional can be relieved by drinking water or repeated swallowing
            - Association w/ temperature - functional causes vary w/ temperature of food (worse w/ cold food)
        - Associated Sx:
            - Heartburn - chronic heartburn a/w complication of GERD (e.g. erosive esophagitis, stricture, or CA esophagus)
            - Weight loss - due to difficult food intake vs malignancy
            - Haematemesis
            - Regurgitation
            - Respiratory Sx - cough, wheeze, SOB etc.
            - Anaemia
        - PMH - GERD, SSc, past repeated pneumonia, radiation therapy of H&N, chest, medication use
        - FHx - FHx of upper GI cancers
        - SHx - smoking and alcohol
- ****Specific Ix:****
    - Upper endoscopy - investigation of choice (allows biopsy-based Diagnosis and therapeutics)
    - Video fluorescence swallowing study (VFSS)
    - Fibreoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing (FEES)
    - Barium swallow, Time barium esophogram (TBE)
    - High resolution esophageal manometry (HRM):
        - Manometry - pressure sensor essentially
        - Conventional manometry - ring of pressure centres 5 cm apart, pressure between sensors unknown
        - Chicago classification for esophageal motility disorders on high-resolution manometry:
            - Disorder of EGI outflow (Achalasia)
            - Disorders of peristalsis
        - HRM - ring of sensors 1 cm apart
    - Endoluminal functional lumen imaging probe (EndoFLIP)
        - Balloon sensor - measuring change in diameter in esophagus, balloon stimulates secondary peristalsis by distending the esophagus (no need for awake procedure initiating active swallowing
        - Physiologically, esophagus will accommodate to distension by balloon -\> requires step up in balloon volume

## Achalasia

- Definition - esophageal disorder w/ failed relaxation of LES and aperistalsis
- OGD findings - dilated esophagus w/ food residues
- HRM - absence of peristalsis and relaxation of LES after initiation of swallowing
- Barium swallowing - dilation proximal to achalasia
- Endoflip:
    - Failed relaxation of LES - high pressure despite increasing balloon volume
    - +/- Esophageal spasms
    - +/- Retrograde peristalsis
- ?Complications - epiphrenic diverticulum
- Clinical score - ECKARDT score, defines clinical features:
    - Dysphagia - always present
    - Retrosternal pain - only occurs in spastic type of achalasia, not in a-peristaltic type
    - Regurgitation
    - Weight loss
- Tx - palliative:
    - Lifestyle modification - important but typically not enough
    - Medical Tx - present but not effective and S/E
    - Surgical Tx - balloon dilatation and surgical myotomy similar efficacy, but multiple procedures for balloon dilatation
        - Botox injection into LES - for frail patients (short lasting and require repeated injection)
        - Balloon dilatation
        - Surgical myotomy
        - Peroral endoscopic myotomy (POEM) - endoscopic submucosal dissection (separate mucosa from muscle, and remove muscle)
        - Esophagectomy - for end-stage achalasia (dilated and S-shaped esophagus)

## Esophageal cancer

- Epidemiology:
    - Not too common but clinically significant
    - Geographically variable - common in SE Asia and Africa
    - Demographic - Sex (M:F ~ 2-3:1)
    - Histological subtype - 85% SCC in Asia (AD more common in the West, GERD related)
- Risk factors different for AD and SCC - completely different diseases
    - GERD
    - Overweight
- Principles Mx:
    - Early disease - endoscopic therapies available
    - Localised disease - upfront oesophagectomy
    - Advanced disease - neo-adjuvant therapy (chemotherapy, chemo-radiation)
- Esophagectomy:
    - Principles - subsequent reconstruction after removing part of stomach, typically directly connect to stomach
        - III phase (Mckeown) - bring up stomach to neck
        - II phase (Lewis-Tanner) - bring up stomach to thorax
    - Field of LN disection:
        - Thorax + abdomen (II field)
        - \+ neck (III field disection)
    - Conduit:
        - Stomach - more physiological, but if not available use colon and jejunum
        - Route - orthotopic, retrosternal, subcutaneous
