## Peripheral artery disease

- **Definition** - Disease of the peripheral (limb) arteries resulting in Sx of arterial insufficency, where the lower extremity vessels are affected more commonly than upper extremity vessels
- **Epidemiology** - estimated global prevalence at 1.52%:
- **Risk factors of PAD** - related to risk factors of atherosclerosis:
    - <u>Non-modifiable risk factors</u> - Age (increases beginning after 40y), Sex
    - <u>Modfiable risk factors</u>:
        - Smoking - appears to be a stronger risk factor (Edingburgh Artery Study) for PAD (OR 2.72) compared w/ CAD (OR 1.61), with a dose-dependent relationship (Framingham Heart Study)
        - DM - powerful risk factor second to cigarette smoking
        - Hyperlipidaemia - increased risk of PAD with a dose-dependent relationship (Framingham study), with possible role of Lipoprotein(a)
        - HTN - increased risk fo atherosclerosis

## Lower extremity chronic artery insufficiency

- **Clinical presentations of PAD:**
    - Asymptomatic (20-50%) - detected based on abnormal ankle-brachial index (ABI) screening in patients having Hx of atherosclerosis at other sites
    - Intermittent claudication (10-35%) - defined as 1) a reproducible discomfort, 2) over a specific group of muscles, 3) induced by exercise, and 4) relieved with rest
    - Atypical leg pain (40-50%) - leg pain that does not fully match the definition of claudication due to other comorbidities (diabetic neuropathy) or physical inactivity
    - Critical limb ischaemia (1-2%) - rest pain and tissue loss as a result of critical stenosis to reduce flow and pressure at rest
- **Clinical approach to suspected PAD:**
    - <u>Assessment of claudication</u>:
        - **Is it really claudication?** - is there a reproducible ischaemic pain over specific group of muscles that is exacerbated by ambulation and relieved by rest
            - Exacerbation with exercise - pain only occurs at ambulation
            - Relieved by rest - pain typically subsides within minutes before the patient can resume walking
            - Reproducibility - pain comes on at a reasonably constant claudication distance
        - **Onset** - when did it first start?
        - **Progression** - is there a reduction in claudication distance (has it altered patients QoL)?
        - **Quality** - a burning pain over specific muscle groups that only comes on upon walking
        - **Radiation** - typically no radiation
        - **Severity** - defined by claudication distance
    - <u>Assessment of critical limb</u>:
        - **Is there rest pain** - is there any pain on rest? Does it affect sleep?
            - Site - a diffuse paedal pain caused by paedal ischaemia
            - Onset - when did it first start
            - Progression - progression of pain to more proximally without sparing the foot
            - Exacerbating and relieving factors - Exacerbated by sitting on a chair, relieved by elevation of foot (hanging legs over the side of the bed)
        - **Is there tissue loss?** - prior non-healing wounds/ ulcers? How long present?
    - <u>Assessment of atherosclerosis</u>:
        - **Is there known PAD?** - any prior interventions?
        - **Any other arterial diseases?**
        - **What are the risk factors?**
- **Symptomatological classification of chronic lower limb ischaemia:**
    - <u>Rutherford classification</u> - historical classification based on Sx of peripheral arterial disease:
        - Stage 0 - asymptomatic
        - Stage 1 - Mild claudication
        - Stage 2 - Moderate claudication
        - Stage 3 - Severe claudication
        - Stage 4 - Rest pain
        - Stage 5 - minor tissue loss with ischaemic non-healing ulcer or focal gangrene with diffuse paedal ischaemia
        - Stage 6 - Major tissue loss extending above metatarsal level (functional foot no longer salvageable)
    - <u>Fontaine</u> - old classification similar to Rutherford classification:
        - Stage 1 - Asymptomatic
        - Stage 2a - Claudication distance \> 200 m
        - Stage 2b - Claudication distance \< 200 m
        - Stage 3 - Rest pain
        - Stage 4 - gangrene of limb
    - <u>WIfI</u> (wound, ischaemia, foot infection) - new classification system devised by Society for Vascular Surgery for classifying the severity of limb threat:
        - Wound - based on degree of tissue loss:
            - Grade 1 - small shallow ulcers on distal leg or foot, gangrene limited to distal toe necessitating only digitial amputation
            - Grade 2 - deep ulcers on distal leg or foot exposing bones w/o involvement of calcaneous
