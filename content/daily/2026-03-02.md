# Delirium in Psychiatry

- **Organic causes of psychiatric Sx:**
    - Acute vs chronic
    - Global vs focal
- Attention vs concentration:
    - Attention be aware of surrounding
    - Concentration = sustained attention
- **Delirium is a global, acute psychiatric presentation:**
    - Disturbance of consciousness with reduced ability to focus, sustain, or shift attention
    - A change in cognition (e.g. memory, **orientation**, language, or perceptual disturbance) not better explained by a pre-existing or evolving dementia
    - The disturbance develoepd over a short period of time that **tends to fluctuate** (i.e. sundowning) over the course of the day
- **Diagnosis of delirium:**
    - DSM V - additional of psychomotor and emotional dysturbances in addition to the aforementioned 3 (J Ger Psychiatry 2018 Maldonado JR img)
    - Confusion assessment method (CAM) in notes
- **Delirium** - similar to fever, not a final diagnosis:
    - Epiphenomenon
    - With an underlying medical causes
    - Causes may be multifactorial
- **Delirium phenotypes** - in fact hypoactive delirium is more common, but possibly easily missed:
    - Hyperactive delirium (DDx from psychosis) - increased motor activities, agitation, hallucinations, and inappropriate behaviour
    - Hypoactive delirium (esp. catatonic delirium, DDx from depression) - reduced motor activity and lethargy, and <u>poorer prognosis</u>
    - Mixed-type delirium - usu. fluctuating between the two
    - Subsyndromal
- Pathoetiological model
- System integration failure hypothesis of delirium (Maldonado JR Int J geriatr psychiatry 2018):
    - Consider it as an acute brain failure
    - Seen precipitant causes (acronym of End Acute Brain Failure in slides)
- Outcomes:
    - Mortality depends on presence and duration of delirium
    - Persistent cognitive impairment (probably background of dementia, i.e. acute on chroncic brain failure)
    - Emotion (esp. PTSD) - risk factors for post-ICU PTSD are **use of benzodiazepines** (which appears paradoxical, but likely due to a ?dream-like state which results in false percepts and PTSD), and duration of sedation:
        - Wade et al 2013 Quantitative study suggesting 27% ICU survivors have PTSD
        - Wade et al 2015 is a qualitative study suggesting that post-ICU PTSD is attributed to more than physical trauma (e.g. disordered consciousness, disordered precepts, dreams)
- **Prevention:**
    - Note risk factors - modifiable and non-modifiable
    - Psychoactive drug use (see box 1 risk of delirium with certain commonly used drugs in slides) - anticholinergic, benzodiazepine, opiate medications (exacerbated by polypharmacy)
    - Additional correctable factors such as sensory deficits, pain relief
- **Early recognition** - esp for hypoactive:
    - Esp if mortality and other poor outcomes a/w duration of duration
    - CAM
    - Confusion Rating Scale
    - Stanfard Proxy Test of Delirium
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - pharmacological as last resort as possibly ?PTSD, rather than at face value "stabilise the patient":
        - Revrsible preventitive causes (e.g. meds, other risk factors mentioned above in prevention section)
        - Tx of underlying cause (note often multifactorial)
        - Optimizing brain function (e.g. ?possible rebalancing of underlying neurotransmitter derangement) - no pharmacologic intervention is FDA-approved for treatment of delirium
    - **Environmental strategies:**
        - Decrease stimuli, regular visitors
        - Minimize sleep deprivation, creates day night routine
        - Consider a night light to assist with orientation
        - Provided planned activities where able
        - Sit person up, ensure visual & hearing aids
    - Safety and orientation to reality
    - **Pharmacological therapy:**
        - See flow chart for pharmacological therapy in slides (read notes):
            - Antipsychotics use lower dose than for psychotic patients
        - Neurotransmitter common to all causes:
            - NE
            - DA
            - GLI

            - ORX

            - No good data from systematic reviews

# Neuropsychopharmacology of Substance Abuse

- Different age group have different pattern of use:
    - MDMA and cannabis common in young
    - Heroin common in old
- **Common substances for substance abuse:**
    - <u>Depressants</u> - alcohol, opioids
    - <u>Stimulants</u> - cocaine, amphetamines, phenycyclidine
    - <u>Hallucinogen</u>
    - <u>Others</u> - e.g. etomidate, cough mixture (mixture of opioids and other stimulants)
- **Terminology:**
    - Problem use/ misuse - use for pleasure but with disregard for the personal or social dangers (no withdrawal Sx, but usually results in functional impairments)
    - Craving - strong and sometimes irresistable desire to use, not necessarily pleasurable
    - Dependence (physical/ psychological) - also a/w physical or psychological withdrawal Sx, and tolerance (extreme end of the spectrum)
    - Addiction - general term
- **Diagnostic categories of psychoactive substances** (ICD11):
    - Episode of harmful psychoactive substance use
    - Harmful pattern of psychoactive substance use (i.e. misuse)
    - Substance dependence
    - Substatce intoxication
    - Substance withdrawal
- **Difficulties in asessing individuals with substance disorders:**
    - Not forthcoming
    - Difficult to form trusting relationship due to stigma or concerns with legality
    - Difficult because we are not familiar with terminology of SA (for now)
    - Very chronic issue (i.e. long duration under substance abuse)
    - Cognitive impairment due to influence of substances
- **Skills in assessing individuals with substance disorders:**
    - Simple questions due to cognitive impairment
    - Tackling long Hx (timeline is often very messy; and difficult to characterise in Hx, e.g. increasing use when, now or ever)
    - Narrative approach (see below)
    - Quantifiable (e.g. changing RoA for tolerance)
- **Approach to clerk a SA case:**
    - Narrative approach (establish time points; first encounter; daily use; first time trying to quit; relapse; key problematic times)
    - First encounter:
        - Not just the current drug, but first time substance use
        - Clarify which substance (usually multiple, but some are often more predominent over it)
        - Clarify motive for SA in intial stage
    - When start daily use (possibly first starting to become dependence) - frame thinking using the diagnostic criteria of dependence:
        - Basically the duration from first encounter and dependence implies very heavy use to build up the tolerance (i.e. 1y to become dependence probably more heavy use than 5y)
        - Assumes withdrawal here… but possibly clarify w/ Sx (most do not remember anymore)
        - Clarify Sx of intoxication and withdrawal
        - Clarify strong desire - possibly define by giving up what one treasures (e.g. relationships, money, time etc.)… e.g. how to obtain when stash is used up?
        - Social impairment
        - Change in route of administration (suggestive of tolerance)
    - First time trying to quit:
        - Motivation - money? relationships? prison? forced?
        - Methods
        - Success or not
        - How long
    - First time relapse:
        - Triggers
        - Issues after relapse (similar to start daily use)
        - Mood, psychotic Sx etc.
    - Key problematic times - against law, divorce, job loss, homelessness, hospitalisations
