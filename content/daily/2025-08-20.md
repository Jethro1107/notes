# GC Cancer Block Week 1: General Principles of Targeted Therapy

- **Principles of targeted therapy:**
    - Target genetic changes correlating to hallmarks of cancer
    - Blockade of signal transduction - transmemrane receptors or downstream signalling proteins
    - MAb - large molecules blockade of transmembrane receptors
    - Small molecule inhibitors - small molecules enter cells and may bound to intracellular parts of receptors, down stream signal transduction molecules or blocking transcription

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/signal_transduction_of_cancer_cell.png)
- **Approaches to targeted therapy** - target commonly acquired hallmarkers of cancer:
    - <u>Growth singnals</u> - e.g. HER2, EGFR, ALK rearrangement
    - <u>Angiogenesis</u> - e.g. use of bevacizumab in mCRC, HCC
    - <u>Evasion of growth suppression</u> - CDK4/6 inhibitors

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/overview_of_targeted_therapy_against_hallmarks_of_cancer.png)
- **Importance of molecular testing** - certain targeted therapy only work if there is a drugable mutation
    - IPASS (Iressa Pan-Asia Study \[Mok et al 2009\]) demonstrates the importance of molecular testing
    - Despite observational studies demonstrating **female never smokers w/ adenocarcinoma generally have better response to TKIs**, giving gefitinib **irrespective of EGFR status do not benefit overall survival**
    - Subgroup analysis subsequently showed that giving targeted therapy for those w/ +ve mutation status is beneficial

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/need_for_molecualr_testing_to_determine_treatment_responsiveness_mok_2009.png)
- **Combinations of targeted therapy** - provide synergistic effects by either:
    - <u>Blockade of different domains of the same receptor</u> - e.g. Transtuzumab + Pertuzumab + Docetaxel allows for greater pCR compared w/ transtuzumab + Docetaxel in the neoadjuvant setting of HER2+ EBC
    - <u>Blockade of different levels of the same signalling pathway</u> - combination of BRAF and MEK inhibitors in metastatic melanoma shows synergistic effect
- Transtuzumab + Pertuzumab for HER2 +ve - binding to different subdomain to ensure more comprehensive blockade
- BRAF and MEK inhibitor together in metastatic melanoma shows synergistic effect

# GC Cancer Block Week 1: Cytoxic Chemotherapy

- **Overview:**
    - <u>Classification by mechanism of action</u> - classified into non-cell cycle specific and cell cycle specific drugs
    - <u>Non-specific to cancer</u> - chemotherapaeutic drugs have greatest activity in proliferating cells, and this provides rationale for their use in Tx of cancer, but also its S/E profile
- **Intent of chemotherapy:**
    - <u>Palliative chemotherapy</u> - typically used to Tx patients w/ distant metastasis w/ goal of delaying disease progression, improving Sx and QOL (survival increments are secondary)
    - <u>Adjuvant chemotherapy</u> - given after a primary therapy that is designed to cyto-reduce the tumour bulk and remove all macroscopic disease, with an intention of eradicating micrometastastic disease and improve DFS and OS
    - <u>Neoadjuvant chemotherapy</u> - chemotherapy administered before a planned cyto-reductive procedure, increasing likelihood of successful debulking
    - <u>Concurrent chemoirradiation</u> - chemotherapy for radio-sensitization
    - <u>Curative chemotherapy</u> - as primary therapy in selected tumours (e.g. germ cell tumour)
- **Cell cycle** - chemotherapy acts on one or all components of cell cycle:
    - **Phases of cell cycle** - most cells remain in G0 (quiescent phase) in absence of cell signal or when they become confluent:
        - <u>G1</u> (gap 1) - period of commitment into cell cycle, where cell is receptive to growth signals and prepares for DNA replication, but may arrest in response to anti-growth signals, but will be locked in S phase once past restriction point
        - <u>S</u> (DNA synthesis) - DNA replication
        - <u>G2</u> - further growth and DNA repair with checkpoints prior to commit to mitosis
        - <u>M</u> (mitosis) - actual phase of cell regulation
    - **Regulation of cell cycle:**
        - <u>Cyclin and cyclin-dependent kinases</u> - kinases that are necessary in progression of cell cycle in checkpoints (G1/S, or G2/M transitions), where tumour suppressor genes (e.g. TP53, TP21, TP16) deactivate cyclin-CDK complexes when necessary
    - **Stimulation of cell cycle** - dependent on growth-factor-receptor interaction
- **Principles of cytotoxic chemotherapy** - targets <u>one or more phase of the cell cycle</u>:
    - **Cytotoxic agents** have <u>greatest activity against rapidly deviding cells</u> (i.e. cancer cells), this forms the rationale for their use in anti-cancer therapy
    - **Normal cells** have a <u>slower rate of cell division</u>, hence are less affected by cytotoxic agents
    - **Myeloid cells, mucous membrane cells, skin, and hair cells** have <u>high proliferation rate</u>, hence are affected by chemotherapy irrespective of class of drug
- **Classification of cytotoxic chemotherapy** - cell cycle specific vs cell cycle independent: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/overview_of_chemotherapy.png)
    - **Cell-cycle independent cytotoxic agents:**
        - <u>Anthracyclines</u> - e.g. daunorubicin, doxorubicin, epirubicin
        - <u>Alkylating agents</u> - e.g. platinum-based compunds, nitrosurea, alkyl sulphonates
    - **Cytotoxic agents acting against G1 phase** - e.g. dactinomycin
    - **Cytotoxic agents acting against S phase** - antimetabolites:
        - Cladribine and Cytarabine
        - 5-FU
        - Hydroxyurea
        - Methotrexate
        - Pentostatin
        - Thiopurines
    - **Cytotoxic agents acting against G2 phase** - topoisomerase inhibitor:
        - <u>Topoisomerase inhibitors</u> - etoposide, teniposide, irinotecan, topotecan
        - <u>Others</u> - bleomycin, bortezomib
    - **Cytotoxic agents acting against M phase** - microtubule inhibitors:
        - <u>Taxanes</u> - e.g. paclitaxel, docetaxel, cabezitaxel
        - <u>Vinca alkaloids</u> - e.g. vinblastine, vincristine, vinorelbine
- **Mode of administration of chemotherapy:**
    - <u>Intravenous</u> - many are vesicant or locally irritant if there is extravastation and thus often given as an infusion to minimise the risk
    - <u>Oral</u> - newly developed but not many have replaced their intravenous counterparts
    - <u>Transarterial</u> - e.g. Transarterial chemoembolisation
    - <u>Intraperitoneal</u>
    - <u>Intrathecal</u> - e.g. for CNS prophylaxis in CLL
- **Role of chemotherapy in current paradigm of cancer Tx** - combination w/ other modes of local and systemic therapy:
    - **Role of combining chemotherapy and radiotherapy** - chemotherapy results in radiosensitization of the tumour
    - **Role of combination chemotherapy:**
        - <u>Increased therapeutic potential</u> - through targetting different parts of the cell cycle (e.g. Xeloda + oxaliplatin for mCRC)
        - <u>Overcome drug resistance</u> - by targeting several pathways of the cell cycle
        - <u>Limit S/E</u> - by minimizing the dose used, and enable time to recover from short-term toxicity before the next dose
    - **Role of combining chemotherapy and targeted therapy** - synergistic effect
    - **Role of combination chemoimmunotherapy** - chemotherapy has immunogenic effects
- **Adverse effects of chemotherapy** - dose-dependent relationship and can be easily Mx by dose-reduction or dose-interruption, but some may be long lasting:
    - **Common S/E for chemotherapy:**
        - Myelotoxicity - e.g. anaemia, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia
        - Dermatological toxicities - e.g. hand foot syndrome, alopecia
        - GI toxicity - e.g. N/V, oral mucositis, diarrhoea
        - Neurotixicity - e.g. neuropathy (may be long lasting)
        - Nephrotoxicity - e.g. AKI
        - Hepatotoxicity - e.g. derranged renal function
        - Allergic reaction
    - **Myelosuppression** - common to almost all cytotoxics due to suppression of HSC: - <u>Implications of myelosuppression</u>:
        - Limits dosing of drug (or dose interruption)
        - Increased risk of life-threatening infections
        - <u>Timing of neutropenia</u> - nadir at 7-12d, and lasts for 5-7d
        - <u>Precaution</u> - G-CSF prophylaxis in regimens that confer high rates of neutropenia (\> 20%) given 48-72h after dosing
    - **Different cytotoxic agents have different toxicity profiles** - for example:
        - <u>Platinum based compounds</u> - ototoxicity and nephrotoxicity
        - <u>Bleomycin</u> - pulmonary fibrosis
        - <u>Anthracyclines</u> - cardiotoxicity
- **Chemotherapy resistance** - typically avoided by combination therapy:
    - <u>Mechanisms</u> - not clinically significant:
        - Drug efflux pumps
        - Drug inactivation
        - Mutation of drug target
        - Increased DNA repair deficiency
    - <u>Identification</u> - disease progrsesion despite receiving chemotherapy:
    - <u>Approach</u> - change to another line of therapy (usually a drug w/ different MOA)
- **Supportive measures during chemotherapy:**
    - <u>Antiemetics</u> - dependent on emetic potential of the chemotherapy regimen: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/asco_guidelines_in_antiemetics_for_patients_on_chemotherapy.png)
    - <u>Primary prophylaxis for neutropenia</u> - e.g. G-CSF if regimen high risk for neutropenia

## Head and Neck Cancer Imaging with Focus on NPC

- **Role of imaging in H&N:**
    - Anatomy and pathology
- Most are SCC
- Imaging modalities:
    - USG
    - CT
    - PET
    - Radionuclide imaging (e.g. thyroid radionuclide scan)
- Usually, imaging is performed when Bx and histological Dx is made:
    - Imaging is not for diagnostic purposes
    - Main role is for acertaining locoregional staging (e.g. submucosal extension and relationship w/ surrounding structures)
    - Subsequent role in detecting of distant metastasis
- **Role of USG:**
    - Role in initial workup of superficial structures (e.g. thyroid, LN)
    - Differentiate between solid and cystic structures
- Approach to interpreting imaging - top to bottom:
    - Nasopharynx - pay attention to fossa of rosenmuller which may hind lesions within the recesses and a/w small structures (pterygoid muscles, parapharyngeal spaces, carotid sheath)
    - Intracranial spread via neuroforaminas - e.g. foramen ovale
    - Identify any soft tissue thickening (areas not smooth)
    - Anatomical spaces in H&N radiology (see slides) - potential spread: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/anatomical_spaces_for_head_and_neck_imaging.png)
- Role of imaging in NPC - TNM staging:
    - T staging:
        - Contrast enhancement of MRI because of superior soft tissue resolution for locoregional staging
        - PET-CT has limited role, but high metabolic activity of adjacent brain makes it difficult for T staging
        - Accurate in differentiation in benign and malignant lesion (complimentary role to endoscopy), e.g. deep submucosal lesion that appear normal on endoscopy
    - N staging:
        - CT nd MRI by morphological standards, now added metabolic information from PET-CT increased diagnostic accuracy of PET
        - Nodal metastasis in NPC common:
        - 87% involve upper jugular group (level II)
        - 75% involve retropharyngeal group
        - 44% involve middle jugular group (level III)
        - Morphological criteria:
            - Size - 5mm in shortest diameter for retropharyngeal, 11mm in shortest diameter for jugulodiagastric, 10 mm in shortest diameter for other nodes
            - Appearance - necrosis or extranodal extension
            - Number - 3 clustered nodes w/ borderline size (8 mm)
        - Role of PET imaging for nodal disease - offered to high risk patients to increase diagnostic accuracy
        - Know false +ve and false -ve
    - Indications for PET-CT:
        - Clinically stage III, IV
        - Patients w/ raised EBV-DNA

## Head and Neck Cancer Pathology

## Diagnosis and Presentation of Head and Neck Cancer

- **Anatomy of the head and neck region:**
    - Upper aerodigestive tract
    - Salivary glands
    - Thyroid gland
    - Soft tissue in head and neck region
- **Prevalence** - 2700 cases of H&N cancer per year (~800 cases are NPC):
    - Typically present in \> 65y
- **Risk factors:**
    - Tobacco
    - Alcohol
    - Poor oral hygiene
    - Previous history of radiation
    - EBV, HBV
    - Age and gender
    - Immunocomprimised state
    - Environmental or occupational exposure
- **Principles of clinical presentation:**
    - Local Sx - depends on the primary site of tumour
    - Regional Sx - nodal metastasis
    - Systemic Sx - general condition
- **Local regional Sx:**
    - Bleeding
    - Obstruction
    - Pain
    - Mass/ ulcer
    - Mass effect on surrounding structures
    - Functional disturbance
    - Nodal metastasis
- **Local regional Sx of nasl cavity, PNS, nasopharyns and eustachian tubes** - nasal cavity tumour is easier to detect, while deeper structures are harder to detect:
    - Blood stained nasal discharge - typically earliest Sx
    - Nasal obstruction
    - Smell disturbance
    - Ear blockage/ conductive HL (eustachian tube)
    - Headache
    - CN palsy
- **Locoregional Sx of oral cavity and oropharynx:**
    - Mass/ non-healing ulcer
    - Bleeding
    - Pain
    - Dysphagia
    - Speech disturbance
    - Difficulty in breathing ()
- **Locoregional Sx of larynx and hypopharynx:**
    - Blood-stained saliva/ haemoptysis
    - Odynophagia
    - Dysphagia/ globus sensation
    - Hoarseness/ voice changes
    - Upper airway obstruction
- **Locoregional Sx of salivary gland:**
    - Mass
    - Invasion to other structures - e.g. nerves, skin, vessels
- **Site of nodal metastasis provides clue of the primary** - know the levels of LN:
    - Level 1 - submental and submandibular LN (likely oral cavity, hence detailed examination of oral cavity)
    - Level 2-4 - jugulo-digastric LN
    - Level 5 - posterior neck
    - Level 6 - central

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/nodal_involvement_informs_primary_tumour.png)
- **Common sites of distant metastasis:**
    - Lung - cough, SOB, haemoptysis, reduced ET
    - Liver - abdominal mass, jaundice, pain, ascites
    - Bone - bone pain, pathological Sx
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - PMH - Hx of malignancy and previous cancer Tx (e.g. radiation), general medical health, cancer-predisposition syndrome
    - FHx - FHx of cancer
    - Social Hx:
        - Occupation - impacts Tx modality
        - Smoking
        - Alcohol
        - Inhalation drug use
        - Dental problems
- **P/E:**
    - General examination - general condition, pallor, jaundice, nutritional status
    - Anterior rhinoscopy
    - Examination of oral cavity
    - Otoscopy (may pick up OME)
    - Cervical LN
    - Endoscopy (e.g. nasoendoscopy, flexible laryngoscopy +/- Bx)
    - +/- neurological examination
- **Ix:**
    - Pathology of specimen - EBER (for NPC), P16 (for oral cavity) status and other immunochemical staining
    - If presenting w/ node - FNA instead of open Bx to avoid complications (see slides)
    - Imaging - for TNM staging:
        - CT with contrast - locoregional staging
        - MRI - better soft tissue delineation (preferred for NPC and when cranial nerve involvement)
        - PET-CT - workup for unknown primary or staging
    - General bloods +/- cancer markers (know EBV DNA screening, know the NEJM article)
- **Read up TNM staging** - in fact different H&N cancer have different staging system

## The Role of Surgery in Head and Neck Cancer

- **Goals of Mx:**
    - Control disease at locoregional level while attempting to improve DFS
    - Organ and function preservation
    - Maintain good QOL w/ minimum morbility
- Options:
    - Surgery
    - RT
    - Systemic therapy
- MDT approach:
    - Tumour factor
    - Patient factor
    - Surgeon factor
- Stage-specific Mx:
    - Early stage - single modality (RT or minimal invasive surgery)
    - Late stage - combined modality (surgery w/ adjuvant Tx)
- Exceptions:
    - Oral cavity and thyroid - surgery over RT for early stage disease
    - NPC - chemoirradiation as first line irrespective of stage
- Indications for H&N surgery - dependent on primary (T) or neck (N) stage:
    - Primary stage:
        - Oral cavity - upfront surgery
        - Sinunasal malignancy - surgery after neoadjuvant Tx
        - NPC - salvage surgery if recurrent or persistent disease
    - Neck stage - neck disection:
        - Prophylactic (dependent on risk of occult Nodal metastasis) or therapeutic
- Types of neck dissection:
    - Selective neck dissection (for usually cN0 disease)
    - Radical neck disection (I-V) or modified radical neck disection (for cN1 disease) - know difference
- Principles of Head and Neck surgery:
    - Good survival w/ good quality of life
    - Resection - adequate surgical margin to reduce recurrence
    - Reconstruction if necessary (restore form and function)
    - Rehabilitation always (swalloing, breathing, communication, hearing)
- Difficult assess regions:
    - Oropharynx, nasopharynx, hypopharynx
    - Conventional surgery - e.g. mandibulotomy and maxillary swiling
    - Endoscopic or robotic assisting surgery enables MIS now
- Principles of reconstruction - by secondary healing, primary closure, skin graft, local flaps, regional flaps, free flaps:
    - Optimise wound
    - Ensure good blood supply
    - Replace w/ similar tissue
    - Place/ hide scars carefully ('lines of election')
    - Account for donor site morbidity

## Non-surgical Management of Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

- Note that 800 new cases every year in HK
- **Stage distribution of NPC:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/stage_distribution_of_npc_at_presentation.png)
- Presenting Sx of NPC:
    - LN (76%)
    - Nasal congestion/ blockade (73%)
    - Impaired hearing/ tinnitus (62%)
    - Headache (35%)
    - Diplopia (11%)
    - Facial paresthesia (8%)
- **Baseline diagnostic evaluation:**
    - CBC, LRFT, CrCl
    - HBV serology (why?)
    - EBV serology - Anti-EBV EA, Anti-EBV VCA
    - Plasma EBV DNA load (as tumour marker)
    - Nasopharyngoscopy and biopsy
    - Pure tone audiography (assess baseline hearing function, e.g. for objective assessment of recurrence)
    - MRI of NP and neck
    - PET-CT, or CT T+A+P + bone scan
    - Dental checkup (molar and premolar will be heavily irradiatted)
- **Role of cancer screening:**
    - NEJM 2017 use of plasma DNA to screen for NPC (CUHK)
- Reason for improved survival:
    - Early detection
    - More adavanced and precise imaging
    - Multimodality
    - Modification of NPC staging system
    - More treatment options for recurrent disease
- **Principles of non-surgical Mx of newly-diagnosed and recurrent NPC:**
    - **Stage 1 or II disease** - RT alone with IMRT:
        - Stage I - 5y OS \> 95%
        - Stage II - 5y OS \> 90%
    - Stage III and IVA - concurrent chemoradiation +/- adjunct chemotherapy (as induction or adjunct):
        - Cisplatin weekly (40 mg/m2) or as bolus on D1D22D43 (100 mg/m2) as the chemosensitizing agent
        - Gemcitabine and cisplatin induction chemotherapy (NEJM Zhang et al 2019) - I think the best trial in the MAC-NPC, yes (quoted in the lecture as well)
        - Induction concurrent chemoradiation w/ sintilimab (CONTINUUM study Lancet 2024) - note shows crossing of the survival curves even though better outcomes
    - Stage IVB disease
        - Initial chemotherapy for up to 6 cycles followed by consolidative radiation
        - Consolidative radiation Chen et al JAMA 2020
        - Palliative chemo +/- ICI
- Read up HKNPCSG 1301 study which is a 2018 report for treatment outcomes of NPC in HK in the modern era:
    - Better OS (\> 70% 5y OS in stage IV)
    - locoregional control is strong
- **Principles of Tx of recurrence** - different for different recurrent pattern:
    - Ix for recurrent NPC - blood tests, EBV DNA, nasoendoscopy and Bx, CT scan, MRI scan, PET-CT
    - If local recurrence - salvage surgery (maxillary swing), or re-irridation (by stereotactic radiosurgery, IMRT, cyberknife or intercavitary brachytheray), study on hyperfractionated (lancet 2023 you et al)
    - If neck recurrence - neck dissection or 2nd course RT (interstitial brachytherapy)
    - Distant:
        - Palliative chemo (gemictabine and cisplatin)
        - Chemoimmunotherapy
        - ICI alone (Toripalimab vs Tislelizumab vs Camrelizumab vs Nivolumab vs Pembrolizumab)
