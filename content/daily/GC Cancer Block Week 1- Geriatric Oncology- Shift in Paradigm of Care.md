# GC Cancer Block Week 1: Geriatric Oncology: Shift in Paradigm of Care <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- **Reason for geriatric oncology** - age cutoff as 65y (\> 60% cancer patients are \> 65y currently):

    - Cancer is the disease of the old
    - Special challenges:
        - 1\. Heterogeneity in the aging process - varying co-morbidities and end-organ dysfunction, geriatric syndromes (e.g. dementia), polypharmacy (pharmacodynamics and pharmacokinetics), varying performance status, socioeconomic aspects
        - 2\. Less evidence on which to treatment decisions for older patients (under-represented in clinical trials, usually retrospective subset analysis with little prospective data)
        - 3\. Under- or over-treatment due to non-standardised Tx

- **Geriatric oncology assessment** - assessment prior to Tx recommended by NCCN, SIOG, ASCO: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/nccn_geriatric_oncology_assessment.png)

    - Life-expectancy
    - Mental capacity
    - Goals and values
    - +/- Care planning options or AD, living will for those refusing curative Tx

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/nccn_geriatric_oncology_assessment_for_those_undergoing_curative_tx.png)

    - Geriatric screening followed by CGA

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/tools_for_assessing_geriatric_domains.png)

    - Identify:
        - Age-related problems not identifiable in routine Hx and P/E
        - Predict oncology Tx related complications and Tx adherance - read up on CARG, CRASH scores
        - Identify geriatric parameters informing prognosis (OS) - e.g. age, functional status, nutritional status, mental health etc.
    - Ultimately aim at - improved clinical outcomes (N.B. <u>GAIN Study</u> and <u>GAP70+ study</u> both demonstrates GA + intervention improves clinical outcomes particularly chemo toxicity) relative to routine oncology workup, but **GAP70+ showed no statistically significant difference in OS**
    - Limitations:
        - Time consuming (30-45 min)
        - Lack of expertise
        - May not be practical to all patients esp if very fit and relatively young, or esp very frail at presentation
        - Lack of consensus in application of GA and intervention
        - Lack of consensus of practical approach - but in general can use screening tool (G8, fTRST, VES-13 most studied, whicle G8, fTRST have high Sn \[\>70%\])

# GC Cancer Block Week 1: Cancer Biology <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- **Definition** - uncontrolled and persistent proliferating population of abnormal cells originating from diverse tissue and cell types, and causes disruption of local tissue architecture, with capability to spread to distant sites
- **Pathogenesis** - using epithelial cells as prototype:
    - Normal epithelium (spontaneous mutation) –\> pre-malignant lesion (hyperplasia, dysplasia) –\> neoplasia (BM invasion and assess to LN and blood)
    - Acquisition of mutations –\> acquire hallmarks of cancer
    - How cancer evolves:
        - Chromosomal abnormalities due to genome instability
        - Driver and passenger mutations:
            - Passenger - does not do anything for cancer
            - Driver - enables clonal expansion and promotes cancer evolution (+ve selection)
- **Persistent proliferation:**
    - Self sufficiency in growth signals and insensitivity to anti-growth signals:
        - Normal cells:
            - Ligand (e.g. EGF) binds to TK receptor (e.g. GEFR)
            - TK receptor pathways activates RAS and PI3K pathway with cross talk
            - Stimulates growth and proliferation (RAS cell cycle progression, PI3K stimulates protein synthesis and cell growth)
        - Malignancy - ligand-independent TK receptor activation (constituitively active):
            - Can occur by mutation in receptor
            - Can occur in mutation in downstream signals (e.g. RAS, RAF, PI3K)
            - Deactivation of growth regulator (e.g. PTEN)
            - Direct influence on cyclin and CDK pathways in cell cycle progression (e.g. amplification of CDKs, usually CDK4/6 in breast cancer, or CDK inhibitors) 
            ![](~/Documents/notes/img/cell_cycle_cdk_and_their_inhibitors.png)
                - Remember role of reticuloblastoma protein (RB)
- **Immortality** - evasion of apoptosis:
    - central role of p53 in response to DNA damage and hyperproliferation and activate pro-apoptotic genes (master of genome)
    - central role of RhoA-ROCK pathway in response to extracellular matrix attachment (anoikis)

# GC Cancer Block Week 1: Cancer Survivorship <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- Cancer survivorship care:
    - Cancer survivorship - focuses on the health and wellbeing of a person with cancer from the time of diagnosis until end of life, includes the physical, mental, emotional, social and financial effects of cancer that begin at diagnosis and continue through treatment and beyond
- Challenges of cancer survivors:
    - Physical challenges - S/E and late effects (neuropathy and sleep disturbances are the most commonly seen challenges in cancer survivors)
    - Emotional challenges - anxiety (36%), depression (26%) and fear of cancer recurrence (33%)
    - Financial challenges - medical bills, loss of income, decreased employment opportunities (39% resume work, 35% remain unemployed)
    - Social and relationship challenges - social isolation and difficulty in reintegration due to alterations of physical appearance, limitations of activities or fear of judgement
- Models of cancer survivorship care:
    - Cancer Survivorship Care Quality Framework (Nekhlyudov et al 2019) - 5 domains of cancer survivorship quality:
        - Prevention and surveillance for recurrence and new cancers
        - Surveillance and mx of physical effects
        - Surveillance and mx of psychosocial effects
        - Surveillance and mx of chronic medical conditions
        - Health promotion and disease prevention
    - Specialist follow-up care model - oncologist led (for surveillance of reccurence)
    - Shared care - oncology +/- primary care
    - Nurse-led care
    - MDT approach
