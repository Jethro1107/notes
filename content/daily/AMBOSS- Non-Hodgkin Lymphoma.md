## Overview of the pathobiology of the non-Hodgkin lymphomas

- **Classification of NHL by cells of origin** - a cell of origin has been proposed for each type of NHL based on morphologic, immunophenotypic and genetic ressemblance of the malignant cells
    - **B cell lymphoma** - arising at any stage of B cell development, but most are derived from cells that have germinal-center reaction:
        - <u>Pre-germinal center</u> - naive mature B cells within secondary lymphoid tissue before antigen exposure, most commonly resulting in **mantle cell lymphoma**
        - <u>Germinal center</u> - host of cells (centroblasts, centrocytes, marginal zone B cells) proliferating upon antigen exposure undergoing class-switch recombination and somatic hypermutation:
            - **Centroblasts** - gives rise to Burkitt lymphoma, germinal centre DLBCL
            - **Centrocytes** - gives rise to follicular lymphoma
            - **Marginal zone B cells** - gives rise to marginal zone lymphoma
        - <u>Post-germinal center</u> - differentiation into plasmablasts and subsequently plasma cells:
            - **Plasmablasts** - gives rise to activated B cell-like (ABC) DLBCL
            - **Plasma cells** - gives rise to multiple myeloma
    - **T cell or NK cell lymphoma** - arises at any stage of normal T or NK cell development
- **Pathogenic mechanisms** - progressive accumulation of mutations involving oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes
- **Oncogenic viruses:**
    - <u>EBV</u> - associated w/ Burkitt lymphoma, DLBCL

- **Etiology:**
    - <u>Chromosomal translocations</u> - most commonly t(14;18)
    - <u>Infections</u>:
        - Ebstein-Barr virus
        - HIV
        - HITLV-1
        - HCV
        - H. pylori (a/ gastric lymphoma, e.g. MALT lymphoma, DLBCL)
    - <u>Autoimmune diseases</u> - Hashimoto thyroiditis, rheumatoid arthritis
    - <u>Immunodeficiency</u> - congenital vs acquired
    - <u>Environmental</u> - radiation, aromatic hydrocarbons
- **Principles of clinical manifestations:**
    - **Clinical presentation depends on aggressiveness of disease:**
        - <u>Low-grade lymphomas</u> - typically presenting w/ rapidly enlarging mass and marked constitutional symptoms, but rarely disseminated disease
        - <u>High-grade lymphoma</u> - associated wth slow-growing or indolent lymphadenopathies, but often disseminated disease w/ hepatosplenomegaly and pancytopenia
    - **Clinical presentation correlates to cell of origin:**
        - <u>B cell lymphomas</u> - extranodal involvement uncommon
        - <u>T/NK cell lymphomas</u> - extranodal involvement common
- **Clinical features of NHL:**
    - **Lymphadenopathy** - typically painless lymphadenopathy, and can involve multiple noncontiguous LN (cf HL)
    - **Extra-nodal disease** - features are highly dependent on the affected tissue:
        - <u>GI tract</u> - early satiety, N/V, GI bleeding
        - <u>CNS</u> - headache, focal neurological S/S
        - <u>Skin</u> - rashes, plaques, or ulcers
        - <u>Thyroid</u> - nodules, goitres
    - **Presentation as oncological emergencies:**
        - <u>Tumour lysis syndrome</u> - constellation of electrolyte disturbances reflecting widespread lysis of tumour cells, resulting in AKI, and cardiac arrhythmias
        - <u>Hypercalcaemia of malignancy</u> - manifestation as nephrogenic DI and neuropsychiatric manifestations, or ocassionally other complications of hyperCa
        - <u>Spinal cord compression</u> - presentation as bilateral LL weakness and a sensory level
        - <u>Superior vena cava syndrome</u> - presentation w/ SOB and facial oedema (Pemberton's sign +ve)
        - <u>Neoplastic meningitis</u> - lymphomatous meningitis resulting in radicular pain, mental status changes, focal neurological deficits and headaches
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, ESR/ CRP, Ca, LDH, uric acid, beta-2 microglobulin:
    - **Viral serologies** - HIV, HBV, HCV
    - **Bx** - selection of appropriate site for Bx:
        - <u>Approach</u> - typically image guided, and dependent on clinical scenario
        - <u>Technique</u> - FNAC of LN is insufficient for confirming Dx:
            - Core needle biopsy
            - Excisional biopsy
            - Incisional biopsy
        - <u>Role</u> - Dx of specific subtype of NHL based on:
            - **Histopathology** - including cytological features and histological architecture
            - **Immunophenotyping** - by IHC or flow cytometry:
                - <u>B-cell lymphomas</u> - CD20 +ve
                - <u>T-cell lymphomas</u> - CD3 +ve
            - **Cytogenetics** (karyotyping, FISH) - to identify translocation mutations specific for a subtype
            - **Molecular studies** - e.g. detection of clonality
- **Staging Ix:**
    - **Cross-sectional imaging** - PET-CT or CT T+A+P depending on FDG-avidity of the subspected subtype of NHL (most are FDG-avid)
    - **Bone marrow examination** - identify bone marrow involvement
    - **Neuroimaging** - CT/ MRI brain:
        - <u>Indications</u> - those w/ high risk of CNS involvement:
            - Focal neurological S/S
            - Primary CNS tumour
            - HIV-associated lymphoma
            - Patients w/ high-risk features (e.g. high LDH, age \> 60y, advanced disease, or extensive extranodal involvement)
