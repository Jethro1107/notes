# Intestinal Obstruction - Literature Search on UpToDate

- **Intestinal obstruction** - occurs when normal flow of intraluminal contents is interrupted (80% mechanical)

## Mechanical SBO

- **Definition** - interruption of normal flow of intraluminal contents which can be caused by intraluminal, (mural,) or extraluminal <u>mechaniacl compression</u>
- **Epidemiology** - a common surgical acute abdomen:
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - bread and butter for surgical abdomen:
        - 2-4% of ED visits
        - 15% of hospital admissions
        - 20% emergency surgical operations for abdominal pain
    - <u>Demographic</u> - M=F
- **Pathophysiology of mechanical SBO:**
    - **Proximal dilatation and distal collapse** - due to accumulation of gas from swallowed air and bacterial overgrowth, and GI secretions
    - **Progressive fluid loss** - oedematous bowel progressive loses absorptiove function, causing fluid sequestration into lumen and transudative fluid loss into peritoneal cavity
    - **Strangulation** - insufficient perfusion to the bowel causing ischaemia, necrosis and perforation
- **Etiology of mechanical SBO:**
    - <u>Extraluminal</u> - **adhesions**, hernias, volvolus, intraperitoneal malignancies
    - <u>Mural</u> - **strictures** (Crohn's disease, radiation enteritis, anastamotic strictures), SB tumour, intramural haematoma
    - <u>Intraluminal</u> - foreign body, gallstone ileus, bezoar, parasites
- **Classification of mechanical SBO:**
    - <u>By chronicity</u> - acute vs chronic
    - <u>By completeness</u> - complete vs incomplete
- **Ix of mechanical SBO** - typical workup for patient presenting w/ acute abdomen and N/V:
    - **Routine bloods:**
        - <u>CBC w/ differentials</u> - neutrophilic leukocytosis (indicates septicaemia), anaemia (points to specific aetiology such as Crohn's, tumour, or Meckel's diverticulum)
        - <u>LFT</u> - underlying liver abnormalities
        - <u>RFT</u> - assess degree of dehydration, and associated electrolyte disturbances:
            - Urea and Cr - degree of dehydration
            - Na - hypoNa
            - K - hyperKa
            - HCO3- - metabolic alkalosis if repeated vomiting, metabolic acidosis if ischaemic
        - <u>Blood gases</u> - detection of acid-base imbalance
        - <u>Lactate</u> - non-specific marker for mesenteric ischaemia
    - **Radiograph:**
        - <u>Erect CXR</u> - r/o pneumoperitoneum
        - <u>Erect and supine AXR</u> - diagnosis of IO and detection of complications:
            - **Dilated loops of bowel with air-fluid levels** - dilated bowels on supine AXR as air is parallel to x-ray plate (for estimation of degree of distension), air fluid levels on upright films
            - **Proximal bowel dilatation and distal collapse** - proximal SB dilated (\> 3cm) with collapse of distal SB

### Management of mechanical SBO

- **Initial general measures:**
    - <u>Diet</u> - keep NBO to limit bowel distension
    - <u>Drip and suck approach</u> - fluid resuscitation and NG decompression:
        - **Fluid resuscitation** - initial IV fluid resuscitation by crystalloids (NS, Lactate Ringers) if dehydrated
        - **NG decompression** - insertion of NG Ryle's tube to reduce proximal distension and improve patient comfort
    - <u>Monitoring</u> - cardiac monitoring, foley catheter
- **Indications for emergency surgery** - bowel comprimise based on clinical, radiological and laboratory features, or if etiology is surgically reversible:
    - **Bowel comprimise** - if impending ischaemia, necrosis and perforation based on clinical, radiological and laboratory features:
        - <u>Clinical features</u> - Tachycardia not responsive to resuscitation, tachypnoea, fever, worsening abdominal pain (and continuous), peritoneal signs
        - <u>Laboratory features</u> - Leukocytosis, metabolic acidosis, lactic acidosis, SIRS
        - <u>Radiological features</u>:
            - **Closed loop obstruction** - U-shaped, distended, fluid filled loops, triangular loop, beak sign
            - **Signs of ischaemia** - pneumatosis, intestinalis, portal venous gas
            - **Pneumoperitoneum** - e.g. liver edge sign, falciform ligament sign, double wall sign, football sign
    - **Non-adhesive surgically correctable cause** - unlikely to resolve spontaneously
- **Conservative Mx of adhesive SBO** - trial of conservative Mx for 3-5d if not C/I (effective in 65-83%):
    - **Serial monitoring** - regular clinical, laboratory, and radiological assessment for progression and resolution of SBO:
        - <u>Clinical assessment</u> - serial abdominal examination:
            - **Features suggestive of resolution** - 1) Decreasing abdominal 2) Decrease in volume and intensity of Ryle's tube 3) Passage of flatus or stool
            - **Features of deterioration** - see indications of emergency surgery
        - <u>Laboratory studies</u> - repeat CBC, LRFT, blood gases, lactate and pro-calcitonin (promising prognostic biomarker)
        - <u>Follow-up imaging</u> - passage of gas into distal segments indicative of resolution of obstruction
    - **Water soluble contrast follow-through** (Gastrografin) - evidence is mixed but first demonstrated to be therapeutic in QMH:
        - <u>C/I</u> - bowel comprimise, non-adhesive SBO (only studied in adhesive SBO)
        - <u>MOA</u> - Hyperosmolarity of gastrografin reduces bowel edema and stimulates peristalsis, encouraging resolution of obstruction
    - +/- **Additional adjuncts** - e.g. laxatives, digestants, defoaming agents
- **Conservative Mx of non-adhesive SBO** - trial of conservative Mx based on etiology if upfront surgery is not indicated
