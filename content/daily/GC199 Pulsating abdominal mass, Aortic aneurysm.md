# GC206 Toe gangrene and leg ulcer, peripheral vascular ischaemia, acute vascular emergencies <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span> <span class="tag" tag-name="Surgery"><span class="smallcaps">Surgery</span></span>

- Anatomy:
    - Femoral artery - gives rise to superficial femoral and deep femoral artery:
        - Deep femoral artery - supplies the thigh, and multiple perforating branches
        - Superficial femoral artery - continuous supply to the leg via popliteal artery
    - Popliteal artery - gives rise to 3 branches:
        - Anterior tibial artery - continues as dorsalis paedis artery of the foot
        - Posterior tibial artery
        - Peroneal artery
- Arterial occlusive disease
    - Acute arterial occlusion - caused by 1) cardioembolism (AF), 2) thrombosis-in-situ, 3) trauma (rare cause)
    - Chronic arterial occlusion (collateral supply developed) - caused by 1) Atherosclerosis, 2) Vasculitis, 3) entrapment
- Presenting problems of peripheral arterial disease:
    - Leg pain - intermittent claudication vs rest pain
    - Ulcer or gangrene (tissue loss) in the foot
- Risk factors:
    - Risk factors of atherosclerosis - Smoking, DM, HTN, Hyperlipidaemia, FHx of atherosclerotic diseases
- Clinical presentation of chronic lower extremity ischaemia:
    - Intermittent claudication - pain in the calf/ thigh caused by muscle ischaemia arising from increased demand
    - Rest pain - in critical ischaemia:
        - Typically in toes/ forefoot (most distal), not in calf
        - Caused by ischaemia of skin and subcutaneous tissues, not muscles
        - Improves on dependency
        - Do not concern rest pain as severe form of claudications
    - Tissue loss - ulcers in pressure areas (resting ischaemic pain), gangrene (dry or wet)
        - Dry gangrene - dry, well demarcated border of gangrenous tissue demonstrating tissue loss
        - Wet gangrene (surgical emergency) - wet, will fluffy, spreading borders caused by overlying bacterial infection
- Diagnostic evaluation:
    - Does the patient have arterial disease?
    - Acute vs chronic?
    - Assessment of severity?
    - How severe?
    - Where is the obstruction?
    - Etiology? Risk Factors?
    - Formulate Mx:
        - Mx of patient risk factors - also assess CVS, pulmonary risk factors, Atherosclerosis
        - Mx of limbs:
            - Non-threatened limb (claudication) - relative indication for surgical intervention
            - Threatened limb (i.e. presentation w/ Sx of critical ischaemia) - definite indication for surgical intervention
- Major levels of arterial occlusion - implicates <u>prognosis</u> (more proximal, better prognosis in Chronic arterial insufficiency, due to more collaterals and more treatment option), also affects clinical presentation (see slides:)
    - Aortoiliac disease - reduced/ absent femoral pulse
    - Femoro-popliteal disease - strong femoral pulse and reduced popliteal pulse
    - Distal/ Infra-popliteal/ small vessel diseases - strong femoral and popliteal pulse, and absent distal pulses
- Assessment of severity - Ankle-Brachial Index:
    - Ankle systolic pressure / arm systolic pressure - use doppler US instad of stethoscope
    - N - \>= 1 (know reference range)
    - Measurement of arterial pressures of LL
    - Trendmill exercise - demonstrate claudication if Hx insufficient, measure ABI before and after (drop if chronic arterial insufficiency)
    - Duplex ultrasound
    - Arteriography
        - Indications - only when surgery is planned, not used for Dx
        - Modalities - Conventional, CT angiography, MR angiography
- Mx of intermittent claudication:
    - Risk factor Mx (improve survival by reducing risk of MI or stroke) - Smoking cessation, HTN, DM, lipids +/- Anti-platelet agents (primary prevention of MI)
    - Symptom relief - moderate exercise (better use of anaerobic respiration by muscles), drugs (Pentoxyphylline, Naftidrofuryl, Cilostazol), severe and unremitting pain can consider endovascular vs open surgical Tx
- Indications of surgery in chronic arterial insufficiency:
    1.  Disabling claudication - affects occupation, ADL, severe and unremitting
    2.  Limb salvage in critical ischaemia
- Principles of amputation of digits - never amputate dry gangrene digits before revascularisation:
    - Poor blood supply –\> causes poor healing
    - Blood flow drawn for wound healing +/- superimposed infections –\> steal blood supply from adjacent digits –\> tissue loss in adjacent didgits
- Endarterectomy - indicated for larger superficial vessel, shorter segments and stenosed
- Arterial bypass:
    - Inflow - inflow artery, aorta, femoral, axilla
    - Conduit - bypass grafts (Dacron graft vs polymer grafts), vs veins
    - Outflow
    - Anatomical (normal pathway anatomically) - aortoiliac, aortofemoral, femoropopliteal
    - Extra-anatomical - femoro-femoral (for ipsilateral disease), axillo-femoral (for bilateral disease)
- Endovascular Tx - Balloon agioplasty +/- vascular stenting
    - Advantages - low risk, repeatable, short recovery, low morbidity/ mortality
    - Limitations - durability (esp for long stenosis), stents and adjunts
    - TASC II - classification of Fem-pop lesions and indicated for different Mx
- Amputation - BKA (better rehabiliation, but risk of stump necrosis due to poor collaterals), above knee amputation (worse rehabilitation, better healing)
- Acute arterial occlusion - arise from emboli, thrombus, and trauma
    - Clinical features - 6 P's of acute arterial occlusion
    - Mx:
        - Dx - clinical
        - Immediate heparinisation (anticoagulate)
        - Surgical Tx - within 6 h

# GC199 Pulsating abdominal mass, Aortic aneurysm <span class="tag" tag-name="Surgery"><span class="smallcaps">Surgery</span></span> <span class="tag" tag-name="Lecture"><span class="smallcaps">Lecture</span></span>

- **Definition of aneurysm** - permanent localised dilatation of an artery (50% increase in diameter)
- **Classification by forms:**
    - Fusiform - expansion in all dimensions
    - Saccular - unequal expansion only in one side
    - Dissecting - false channel in the aortic lumen
- **Classification by structure:**
    - Tru aneurysm - constituets all 3 layers
    - False aneurysm - ?fibrous tissue and thrombus
- **Complications of aneurysms:**
    - Rupture - most feared complication
    - Thrombosis - caused stasis due to slower blood flow
    - Embolism
    - Infection
    - Pressure effects - e.g. thoracic aneurysm compressing on Recurrent larynegal nerve, esophagus

## Abdominal aortic aneurysm

- **Most common site** - infra-renal AAA (note the IMA involvement), common iliac artery
- **Etiology** - multifactorial:
    - Degenerative
    - Increased proteolytic activity - higher MMP levels, reduced
    - Genetic - Marfan's, Ehlers-Danlos
    - Autoimmune
    - Infection
- **Pathology:**
    - Loss of elastin and smooth muscle cells
    - Disruption of extracellular matrix
    - Inflammatory inifiltrate
- **Natural Hx:**
    - Expansion - 5mm/y (Laplace's law, rate of expansion variable with size)
    - Risk of rupture at 5y:
        - \< 5cm - 20%
        - \> 5cm - 50% (10%/y)
- **Clinical presentation:**
    - Asymptomatic - incidental finding of pulsatile abdominal mass on clinical exam or imaging
    - Abdminal pain - indicates impending rupture
- **P/E:**
    - Confirm AAA - Expansile pulsation (typically above umbilicus)
    - Extent of AAA - assessment of size (horizontal diameter), upper and lower border
        - Upper border - no expansion below the thorax, indicates infra-renal lesion
- **Ix:**
    - X Ray - calcification of aortic wall
    - US - assessment of size (maximum diameter)
    - CT +/- contrast
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Gepgraphic variation - more common in the west (screening US 1 time in the States)
- **Operative considerations:**
    - Symptoms - urgent operation
    - Size - \> 5cm (arbitrary cutofff in Asians, vs 6cm in Western)
    - Medical risks - associated disease
    - Life expectancy and age - not a C/I
- Balance risk of operation vs risk of rupture:
    - Principles - assume medically fit patients to have risk of operation at around 3-5%
    - Main operative mortality - MI
- Pre-operative preperations:
    - General - blood, ECG, CXR
    - Cardiac - cardiac assessment/ intervention (angiogram)
    - Preparation - monitors, blood
- Surgical Tx:
    - Open repair - aneurysemectomy + inlay graft
        - Procedure:
            - Mobilise bowel
            - Cross clamp aorta at neck (?what if above renal artery) and common iliac
            - Open aorta and remove thrombus
            - Address back-bleeding lumbar arteries - sucture
            - Sucture graft - two-anastamosis graft, bifurcated graft
            - Release clamp
        - Complications:
            - General complications
            - Operative complications - bowel ischaemia (IMA comprmised), paraplegia + sphincteric dysfunction (cord ischaemia due to lumbar arteries involvement), autonomic dysfunction (retrograde ejaculation/ impotence due to comprimised sympathetic involvement), blue toes syndrome (embolic), renal failure, haemorrhage
            - Cardiac - MI
            - Respiratory
    - Endovascular repair - aortic stent graft (EVAR):
        - Anatomical requirements (patient selection):
            - Neck - \> 1.5cm, diameter \< 32 mm, and no angulation
            - CIA - normal length and diameter
            - Access - \> 7mm diameter
        - Benefits:
            - Lower 30d mortality - EVAR 1.7%, Open 4.7%
            - Lower 1y mortality
            - Benefits of mortality loss at 7y
        - Limitations - higher revascular risk
        - Complications:
            - Endoleaks (durability issues, concerns for younger patients) - 5 types (know which is indicated for conservative and which require repair)
- **Ruptured AAA:**
    - Retroperitoneal (most common), intraperitoneal, into duodenum (aortoduodenal fistula), into IVC
    - Triad of features:
        - Pain - abdomen radiate to back
        - +/- Mass - pulsatile (?absent if BP low)
        - Shock - transient/ profound
    - +/- signs of retroperitoneal haemorrhage - Grey-Turner, Cullen
    - CT - retroperitoneal haematoma
    - Surgical emergency - Operative mortality \> 50%, overall mortality \> 80%
    - **Mx:**
        - Treat haemorrhagic shock - large bore IV, cross match blood/ FFP
        - Permissive hypotension - otherwise increased bleeding
        - Immediate operative - open repair (if unstable), endovascular repair (if stable after CT assessment?)
    - Read up specific complications

# GC215 Common nasal conditions and nasopharyngeal carcinoma

- Presenting problems of rhinology:
    - Congestion/ blockage/ obstruction - unilateral vs bilateral
    - Nasal discharge - nature, anterior/ posterior
    - Sneezing, itching
    - Olfactory disturbance
    - Facial pain
    - Epistaxis
    - Adjacent organs:
        - Eyes - itchiness, visual disturbance
        - Ears - otalgia/ aural fullness, hearing loss
        - Throat - dental pain, snoring
        - Systemic - fever
- PMH - asthma, atopy, immunodeficiency
- Social Hx - think occupational hazard
- Drug Hx
- FHx
- P/E:
    - External nose - frontal view, basal view, lateral view
    - Speculum - anterior rhinoscopy
    - Nasoendoscopy - rigid (also allow forcep and suction) vs flexible (able to visualise to throat)
    - 
- DDx of nasal obstruction:
    - Nasal deformity - crooked nose, deviated nose, saddle nose deformity
        - Injuries to nose:
            - Septal haematoma - collection of blood under pericondrium, complicated by:
                - Septal perforation and collapse
                - Infection –\> meningitis
            - Fracture
            - Fracture-dislocation of the septum
    - Mucosal thickening - acute URTI, chronic rhinosinusitis +/- polyposis, allergic rhinitis, non-allergic rhinitis
    - Nasal mass - unilateral or bilateral
- NSD - rarely exactly in midline (usually asymptomatic if minor):
    - Etiology - trauma (birth, long-forgotten, recent) or nasal surgery
    - Clinical presentation:
        - Nasal obstruction
        - Obstruction of sinus drainage pathway –\> ?sinusitis
        - Epistaxis - due to turbulence of airflow resulting in dry nose
    - Mx - septoplasty or septorhinoplasty if symptomatic
- Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis - same epithelium for sinuses and nose
    - Etiology:
        - URTI - bacterial or viral
        - Dental abscess/ extraction (of pre-molar or molar teeth)
        - Trauma
    - Predisposing factors:
        - Poor immunity
        - Poor drainage - e.g. NSD, turbinate hypertrophy, polyposis
    - **Etiological agents:**
        - S. pneumoniae
        - Haemophilus influenzae
        - Moraxella catarrhalaes
        - Anaerobes (dental source)
    - Distinguishing between normal viral URTI vs acute bacterial rhinosinusitis:
        - Clinical course:
            - Normal URTI - typically subside within a week
            - Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis - \> 10d course or double sickening (worsening after 5-7d)
        - **Clinical features:**
            - Similar as URTI - nasal obstruction, nasal discharge (tends to be thick and purulent)
            - Facial pain
            - Reduction of smell - air cannot reach up olfactory cleft
            - Fever
        - Signs:
            - Facial tenderness
            - Edema, mucopurulent discharge in NP or middle meatus
    - Mx:
        - Analgesics
        - Antibiotics
        - Intranasal steroid spray
        - Short-term nasal decongestants (\< 7d) - e.g. oxymetazoline, ephedrine,
        - Douching
    - Cx:
        - Orbital cellulitis, abscess
        - Carvenous sinus thrombosis
        - Intracranial infections - meningitis, encephalitis, brain abscess
- Chronic rhinosinusitis
    - Clinical features - long-standing:
        - Purulent nasal and post-nasal discharge
        - Nasal obstruction
        - Facial discomfort
        - Headache
        - Halitosis
- Nasal polyp:
    - Differentiating nasal polyp w/ inferior turbinate:
        - Inferior turbinate - sensitive and attached to lateral nasal wall
        - Nasal polyp - pale and greyish (different colour than nasal mucosa), insensitive to touch
    - Associated diseases:
        - Asthma (Samter's triad)
        - Allergic fungal sinusitis
        - CF
    - Aspirin exacerbated respiratory disease (Samter's triad):
        - Pathophysiology:
            - Aspirin - COX inhibitor, resulting shift in arachidonic acid metabolism via leukotriene pathway:
                - Increased leukotrienes - results in asthma and nasal polyposis
    - Mx:
        - Intranasal steroids
        - Nasal douching
        - ABx - shorterm for superimposed infections, long term use for immune modulating effects (macrolide, doxycycline)
        - Anti-histamine for atopy
        - Surgery - endoscopic sinus surgery
- Mucocele - epithelial-lined mucus containing sac
    - Clinical features:
        - Nasal congestion
        - Orbital displacement and proptosis
