# TC: Surgical Endoscopy, Diagnostic therapy, Minimally invasive therapy

## Endoscopy

Examination of the interior of a canal or hollow viscus by means of special instrumentation:

1.  Rigid endoscope Limited reach, angled lens Trade off between therapeutics and tolerability
2.  Flexible endoscope Multiple degree of movement - attached to videoscope
3.  Capsule endoscope

Indications:

- Diagnostic - Examination +/- Biopsy, EUS (+/- FNAC/Tru-cut biopsy, staginf of tumours)
- Therapeutic

Patient preparation: Conset - indication procedure, risk (aspiration, bleeding, perforation, sedation risk)

### Complications of endoscopy:

Sedation related - Cardiorespiratory depression (hypoxia, respiratory arrest, aspiration, cardiac events) Perforation of viscus:

- High rates in colon than oesophagus (more turns)
- Higher rates in trauumatic procedures
- Different in severity - most common Sx is pain
- Risk of sepsis, pneumomediastinum, pneumoperitoneum, and surgical emphysema (must identify and treat early)

Infective complication - not common except if already infected (e.g. cholangitis, pyelonephritis) Retained FB - due to failed removal, breakage of instrument, retained capsule Specific complications:

- ERCP - post-ERCP syndrome (pancreatititis)

## Minimally invasive surgery

MSI - reduce size of incisions that minimize the surgical trauma Advantages:

- Smaller wound
- Less pain, infection, blood loss
- Early return of GI function and resumption of oral intake
- Earlier mobilisation
- Less adhesions
- Earlier mobilisation

Disadvantages - learning curve and longer operating time, higher cost
