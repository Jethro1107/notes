## Epidemiology of Lung Cancer

- **Global epidemiology:**
    - 2nd most common cancer in the world (most common in M, 2nd most common F)
    - Incidence - 2.2 million new cases in 2020
    - Geographic variation (see slides, Leiter et al 2023) - see relation w/ economic development (HDI) correlation w/ both incidence and mortality
- **Local epidemiology:**
    - Most common cancer in HK and leading cause of death
- **Risk factors of lung cancer:**
    - Environmental factors:
        - Smoking and second hand smoke
        - Biomass fuels
        - Arseni
        - Radon
        - Asbestos
        - Diesel exhaust
        - Red meat consumption
        - Low fruit and vegetable consumption
    - COPD
    - HIV
- **Prevention efforts depending on causes:**
    - Tobacco smoking - interesting RCT quoted in slide (E-cigarette vs Nicotine replacement), but not suported by WHO

## Lung Cancers Pathology

- **LOs:**
    - Carcinogenesis of CA lung
    - Principles of histological classification
    - NSCLC vs SCLC in terms of behaviour, Tx and prognosis
    - Mode of spread (implicates lung cancer staging)
    - Role of targeted and immunotherapy
- **Carcinogenesis:**
    - Role of ROS and oxidative stress - causes mutations and lead to cancer
    - Etiologies:
        - Tobacco - high statistical significant relationship (increased w/ no. of cigarette per day, tendancy to inhale smoke, total duration, pack years)
        - Occupational and environmental factors - e.g. heavy metals, benzo\[a\]pyrenes, radon
        - Incomplete combustion of animal fuels
        - Chronic inflammation

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/pathogenesis_of_lung_cancer_via_ros.png)
    - Tobacco:
        - Carcinogens - bind covalently to DNA forming bulky compounds and affect DNA replication and repair
        - Also induces chronic inflammation, and ROS generation
    - Chronic inflammation - due to inflam cells and macrophage activation:
        - ROS and free radicals, damage lung epithelium
        - Activated growth factor pathway - cell proliferation, if persistent predisposes to mutations
- **Role of genetics** - only 15% of chronic smokder develop CA lung:
    - SNP genes in P450 enzyme for carcinogen, GST enzyme for detoxification, DNA repair genes, inflammatory pathway genes
    - Also suggested in geographic variation in prevalence - particularly high prevalence of female non-smokers in SE asia:
        - Cancer susceptibility enzymes
        - Role of more female passive smokers?
        - More Sn than men
        - Cooking habits (e.g. generate toxic cooking fumes)
        - Burning fossil fuels
- **Approach to histological Dx:**
    - Solid bx - from primary tumour (bronchial vs percutaneous needle, vs mediastinal LN), distant metastasis (e.g. liver)
    - Malignant pleural effusion - diagnostic paracentesis
    - Liquid Bx - e.g. cf DNA in blood and malignant pleural effusion
- **Schematics for NSCLC Dx and Tx:**
    - Morphological Dx and staging - assess early or late stage
    - Early stage consider surgery
    - Molecular diagnosis (EGFR, ALK, ROS1) if late stage
- **Schematic for SCLC Dx and Tx** - early or late chemotherapy
- **4 main types of lung cancer:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/four_types_of_histology_in_lung_cancer.png)
    - AD development typically in peripheral (distal, terminal bronchioles and alveoli) - dysplasia, CIS, and AD, most common in both smokers and non-smokers (not histological subtypes, e.g. acinar, papillary, lepidic, solid)
    - SCC development typically centrally - metaplasia, dysplasia, CIS, SCC of <u>basal cells</u>, more associated w/ smokers and entire bronchial epithelium has increased risk of developing cancer (field canceriastion)
    - SCLC (2-4x size of nucleus, high N:C ratio with scanty cytoplasm) - strong a/w smokers and derived from <u>neuroendocrine cells</u> and hence may present w/ biochemical abnormalities due to paraneoplastic Sx
    - LCCs (large poorly differentiated) - no features of SCC, AD, SCLC, high grade, aggresive tumour, poor prognosis (however in asia, may be morphologically a/w NPCs and EBV infections, which have good prognosis)
- **Mode of spread:**
    - Local
    - Lymphatic (common for all types) - hilar, mediastinal, SCN and pleural lymphatics (i.e. pleural effusion)
    - Haematogenous - common in AD and SCLC to liver, adrenals bone and brain
- SCLC vs NSCLC: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/sclc_vs_nsclc.png)
- Staging - different for NSCLC vs SCLC:
    - NSCLC - TNM (see slides)
    - SCLC:
        - Limited (one side of chest and can be Tx w/ single radiation field)
        - Extensive stage (distant metastasis or in both sides of the chest)

## Application of Precision Medicine in Lung Cancer Management

- **Concept of precision medicine:**
    - Conventional chemotherapy affects all actively proliferating tissues
    - Targeted therapy have optimal survical and reduced S/E
    - Tailoring drugs choices matching cancer driver mutation status can optimise survival and spares toxicity from drugs
- **Prevalent Tx target:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/tx_targets_in_targeted_therapy_for_advanced_lung_cancer.png)
    - EGFR mutations - highly a/w the female non-smokers with AD:
        - Occurs in exon 18-21 with different genotypes with different responses to TKI
        - Exon 19 inframe deletion and exon 21 L858R comprises of 80% of EGFR mutation and show good response
        - Exon 20 in frame insertion, or T790M do not respond to TKI
    - ALK translocation - also a/w the non-smokers w/ AD, translocation of 5' part of highly expressed genes gene to 3' of ALK results in expression of an oncogenic protein that is normally not expressed:
        - Translocation partners - many different 5' partners, all lead to high ALK fusion protein
    - KRAS common but not drugable
    - See slides in varying distribution between EGFR and ALK
- **Genetic testing:**
    - Specimen - solid vs liquid
    - Routine in HA to test for EGFR, ALK and ROS1
    - EGFR by PCR sequencing of exon 18-21
    - ALK translocation by FISH or IHC
- **Mechanisms of drug resistance:**
    - EGFR:
        - EGFR T790M (50%) - may be primary resistance, or acquired resistance through selection process (12-14 mo)
        - Oncogenic mutation of alternative signalling genes affecting same downstream pathway (see slides):
            - BRAF
            - HER2
            - PIK3CA
            - MET
            - IGFR
            - PTEN

## Lung Cancer Surgery: Perioperative Aspects

- **Approach to patient w/ early NSCLC:**
    - Confirm Dx and staging - early lung cancer with indication of surgery:
        - CT T+A+P and Whole Body PET-CT mandatory
        - Brain imaging if stage 1B or above
    - Resectability - i.e. complete resection w/ clear margins (R0 section)
    - Operability - resectable with acceptable risk
    - Asses patient's preference
- **Assessment of resectability** - see 2023 world conference on lung cancer: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/resectability_of_lung_cancer_by_stage.png)
- **Assessment of operability** - operative mortality, perioperative complications, longterm survival and QoL:
    - General condition - performance status, nutrition status, exercise tolerance, lifestyle, fraility
    - Comorbidity - cardiac risk and pulmonary HTN
    - Lung function test - at least Spirometry and DLCO and PFT, V/Q scan, CPEX
- **Risk of post-op dyspnoea** - dependent on pre-operative FEV1 and DLCO: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/risk_of_post_op_dyspnoea_by_lung_function.png)
    - Consider focal vs diffuse pathologies - e.g. focal bronchiectasis in resection site -\> does not impact post-op lung function too much
    - Post-operative predictive value of of FEV1 and DLCO - by **segmental counting** (for lobectomy):
        - ppo valuve = pre-op value \* (total - functioning segments to be resected)/total
        - Read BTS 2010 and ACCP for risk assessment for post-op dyspnoea
- **Indications for functional testing** - failed lung function test:
    - e.g. 6-minute walk test, shuttle walk test, and stair climbing test
    - 6 min walk test - regarded low risk if no desaturation and can walk for \> 400m in 6 min
- **Cardiovascular risk assessment** - cardiac consultation if any of the following:
    - Thoracic revised cardiac risk index (ThRCRI) - score \>= 2 (by ACCP)
    - Suspected or pre-existing cardiac conditions requiring medications
    - Exercise tolerance (unable to climb 2 flights of stairs \[FOS\])
- **Post-operative management:**
    - Monitoring - fasting and IV fluids, vitals, serial CXR, ABx, Chest physiotherapy, triflow exercise, pain control and chest drainage
    - **Definition of recovery**- no universal definition (best defined by Francis D Moore)
    - **Enhanced recovery after lung surgery** (ERAS) - also known as fast track surgery:
        - Aim - promote evidence based interventions, and minimise physiological response to surgery
        - Outcome:
            - Reduced complications
            - Hasten recovery to normal function
            - Cost effectiveness
            - Length of stay
        - Principles of prehabilitation and eras - see eras guidelines in slide: 
        ![](~/Documents/notes/img/principles_of_prehabilitation_and_eras.png)
- **Pain control:**
    - Intraoperative regional analgesia - intercostal nerve blockade, serratus anterior plane block
    - Post-operative multimodal analgesia:
        - Minimize opioid use with PCA, as most importantly avoid suppression of cough efforts
        - Acetaminophen, NSAID, Gabapentin (no evidence)
- **Chest drain management:**
    - Single chest tube
    - Avoid routine application suction to drain (why???? mentioned in video)
    - Consider using digital drain
    - Drain removal:
        - Satisfactory lung expansion
        - Absence of significant pleural collection
        - Absence of air leak
        - Low output - traditionally \< 200 ml/d, can be considered if non-chylous \< 450 ml/d
- **Post-operative complications:**
    - General surgical risk:
        - Pain
        - Air leak
        - Cardiac risk - MI, arrhythmia, HF
        - Infective risk - e.g. pneumonia, empyema
    - Procedure-specific risk:
        - Nerve injury
        - Lobar torsion
        - Chylothorax
        - Bronchopleural fistula
- Air leak:
    - Alveolar-pleural fistula
    - 30-60%
    - Manifest as pneumothorax or surgical emphysema
    - Definition of persistent air leak - airleak \> 5d
    - Mx:
        - Prevention
        - Chest tube
        - Chemical pleurodesis
        - Endobronchial intervention
        - Reoperation
- Haemorrhage:
    - Not uncommon - leading cause to return to OT
    - Mx
        - Vitals and drain output
        - CXR, clotting profile
        - Supportive Tx
        - Consider reoperation - if high drain output (arbitrary):
            - \> 10 ml/kg in first hour
            - \> 8ml/kg/h for 2h
            - \> 6 ml/kg/h for 3h
            - \> 4 ml/kg/hr for 4h
            - Sudden increase in amounts (e.g. \> 5ml/kg in 1h)
- Lobar torsion:
    - Rotation of hilar pedicle causing venous infarction (0.09-0.3%)
    - Commonly affects RML after RUL lobetomy
    - Risk factors:
        - Complete fissure between lobes
        - Long hilar pedicles
- Bronchopleural fistula:
    - Communication between a lobar or segmental pulmonary bronchus and pleural space
    - More common after pneumonectomy than operation
    - See slides for presentation and Mx

## Surgical intervention for NSCLC in modern era

- Implications of epidemiology - only 16% present at stage I where surgery is feasible:
    - Stage I w/ curative surgery - 5y survival up to 72%
    - Supgroup of Stage I (Stage 1A1) - 5y survival up to 90%
- Evolution from pneumonectomy to lobectomy:
    - Pneumonectomy - historical interest:
        - Preferred as inferred from practices for other malignancy
        - Graham suggested radical removal of entire lung is preferred as lower risk of recurrence
    - Lobectomy and segmentectomy - emphasis on lung conservation:
        - Lobectomy was considered valid oncoligc resection
        - Salzer 1962 demonstrated similar outcomes for lobectomy and pneumonectomy
        - Limited resection/ Sublobar resection/ Segmentectomy/ wedge resection - for compromised patients w/ insufficient lung function
        - Jensik 1973 T1N0M0 lung cancer for patients who can tolerate lobectomy - Ginsberg and Rubinstein study is a RCT that confirms this:
            - Preservation of lung function
            - Reduced perioperative morbidity and mortality
            - Ability to undergo further lung resection in the future
        - RCT showed that segmentectomy a/w higher locoregional recurrence despite similar overall mortality
        - Hence lobectomy is gold standard
- Screening:
    - Low-dose CT screening (NEMJ) - national lung screening trial and NELSON trial
- Intentional sublobar resection - as LDCT screening allows detection of very small tumours, rekindled interest in small NSCLC:
    - Considered for stage IA \< 2cm, even in low risk
- Role of surgery in personalised medicine:
    - Up to 30-55% will recurr after curative resection:
        - 17% local
        - 44% distant
        - 39% both local and distant
    - Adjuvant therapy for selected cases:
        - Lung adjuvant cisplatin evaluation meta-analysis:
            - Stage II and III A (mostly N2 cases), demonstrated adjuvant therapy improves OS and DFS
            - Targeted therapy in EGFR mutated NSCLC for stage IB to IIIA NSCLC after resection (improved OS and DFS irrespective of stage)
    - Neoadjuvant therapy:
        - neoadjuvant Chemo or chemoRT - no demonstrable benefits in early-stage NSCLC
        - neoadjuvant with nivolumab + chemo (2022 RCT) - IB-IIIA NSCLC
        - Perioperative (neoadjuvant and adjuvant) pembrolizumab (Keynote 671 study)

## Role of radiatiotherapy in lung cancer management

- **LOs:**
    - Indications of RT for every stage of NSCLC and SCLC
    - Recent advances in radiation techniques and radiation dose required for NSCLC
    - SBRT for early stage lung cancer as well as oligo-progression/ oligometastasis for lung cancer
- **Baseline Ix before RT for lung cancer:**
    - Tumour markers - e.g. CEA, CA15.3
    - CT thorax
    - PET-CT to r/o distant metastasis or occult mediastinal regional nodal metastasis
    - Lung function test - especially for those w/ suboptimal premorbid lung functions (e.g. COPD, asthma)
- **Indications of RT for NSCLC:**
    - Stage I or II:
        - No role of adjuvant radiotherapy if R0 resection achieved
        - Radical RT conventional dose fractionation is 66-70Gy in 33-35 fractions over 6.5-7 weeks
        - SBRT are also considered for those who have better lung function and fewer morbidities
    - Stage III:
        - Concurrent chemoradiation for all unresectable stage III tumours (better outcomes than sequential CRT according to Auperin et al. JCO2010)
        - Induction chemotherapy may help shrink the tumour before radical chemoradiation leading to a better tumour volume coverage w/ radical dose of radiation (esp if RT planning is lengthy)
        - Dosing 63Gy in 35 fractions over 7 weeks, or 60-66Gy in 30-33 fractions in 6-6.5 weeks
        - Chemotherapeutic drugs:
            - Weekly paclitaxel and carboplatin
            - 3-weekly vinorelbine/ etoposide/ pemetrexed (only for AD) + platinum
- **Indications of RT for SCLC:**
    - Limited stage SCLC - by definition can be encompassed by a reasonable radiation portal field
    - 60Gy 30 fractions over 6 weeks
    - Preferably early concurrent rather than late sequential chemoradiation (Fried et al 2004 systematic review)
    - Hyperfractionated RT - 45Gy 33 fraction over 3 weeks (twice daily fraction) showed statistically insignificant benefit (rationale being rapidly dividing)
    - Prophylactic cranial irradiation (see slides for indications)
- Lobectomy vs SBRT in stage I NSCLC (De Ruiter et al)
    - Lobetomy statistically significant improved recurrence free survival
    - OS same between two groups (however not statistically significant)
- **RT planning for lung cancer:**
- **Modern radiation techniques for lung cancer:**
    - IMRT - better lung and heart sparing
    - Proton therapy - but require carefully selected patients (beyond curriculum)
    - SBRT with active breathing control technique:
        - ABC technique - mouth piece connected to airflow, deep breath and hold for around 20 seconds and immediate start irradiation
        - Gating technique if ABC technique failed - RT delivered during certain phases of respiratory cycle
            - Regular breathing cycle - amplitude of inspiration and expiration
            - Machine switches on during end expiration phase
            - Machine switches off during start of inspiration
    - Note differential movement of lung tumours during breathing cycle - margins have to be determined by site (see slides)
- SBRT:
    - SBRT indicated for:
        - Stage I lung cancer who are not medically or surgically fit for surgery
        - Oligometastatic or oligoprogressive disease
    - Dose fraction is usually hypofractionated (i.e. larger than 2Gy per fraction) - 4-10Gy per fraction for 3-10 fraction

## Systemic therapy in Stage IV Lung cancer

- Chemotherapy and TKI
- Treatment paradigm:
    - Stage I to III - potentially curative (prevent tumour recurrence, but extremely complex)
    - Stage IV - palliative Sx and prevent disease progression, to extend survival, preserve QOL
- Approach to Tx:
    - If oncogenically driven - sequentially use TKIs until exhaustions to target driver mutations, and use chemo as list line (except HER2 mutant, which has transtuzumab derutecan as 2L after 1L TKI)
    - If not oncogenically driven - consider chemo doublet + immunotherapy?
- Chemotherapy doublet/ chemotherapy with maintenance:
    - Platinum + antimetabolite or taxanes:
        - Docetaxel is often considered as 2nd line
        - Pemetrexed only for AD, SCC not sensitive
- TKI:
    - Gefitinib may be first line (2009) - Mok only enrolled female, asian and little smokers (see slides for subgroup analysis)
    - EGFR mutated lung caner with Erlotinib (Zhou 2011) - doubled PFS (13mo vs 5mo)
- Immunotherapy:
    - Nivolumab and ipilimumab have synergistic effects

## Immunotherapy in Lung Cancer Treatment

- Several phase 3 trials demonstrated improved 5y OS when anti-pd1/ pd-l1 are given in the second line setting
- Tail plateau suggests some achieve long term survival
- Immune-related adverse events in pembrolizumab: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/immune_related_adverse_events_of_pembrolizumab.png)
    - Pneumonitis is of great concern as patients have limited lung function from COPD or CA lung
    - Note that this list is not exhaustive
- Immune-related toxicity - treated empirically by steroids
- Testing PD-L1 expression in mNSCLC by IHC:
    - Negative - no staining
    - Low expression -1-49%
    - High expression - 50-100%
- Those w/ high expression have higher response rate to pembrolizumab than those w/ low or no-expression as expected:
    - However it is important to note that those w/ low or no expression still somehow show responses to pembrolizumab and hence immunotherapy should always be concerned

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/differential_responses_to_pembrolizumab_based_on_pdl1_expression.png)
- Chemoimmunotherapy - immunogenic effect of chemotherapy (Gandhi et al 2018):
    - Pembrolizumab + chemotherapy doublet improves survival irrespective of PD-L1 expression status
- KN024 - demonstrated benefits of of pembrolizumab over chemotherapy
- KN407 - pembrolizumab + chemo still benefits SCC irrespective of PDL1 levels
- CHECKMATE227 study - dual immunotherapy (Anti-PD1 PDL1) showed worse survival in short term although longer subsequent survival: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/cm227_pd1_ctla_dual_immunotherapy_survival.png)
    - Tail plateau observed
- CM9LA study - dual immunotherapy w/ 2 cycles of chemotherapy upfront no longer cross survival curve, but a/w increased immune-related AE:
    - Note this regimen appears to provide greater benefit to squamous non-PDL1 expressing tumours (see slide for comparison)
- PACIFIC trial - improved in unresectable stage III disease by adding immunotherapy
- Resectable NSCLC - 3 cycles of Anti-PD1 neoadjuvant or adjuvant enables higher complete response rate and improves survival
- KN001 also demonstrated that mutant EGFR status a/w poorer response than those w/ wildtype when on pembrolizumab irrespective of PDL1 levels
