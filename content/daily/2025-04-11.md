# GC216 Dysphonia, Laryngitis, voice abuse, tumour and laryngeal cancer

- **Definition and terminology:**
    - <u>Dysphonia</u> - any impairment of voice
    - <u>Aphonia</u> - a loss of voice
    - <u>Hoarseness</u> - rough, or noisy quality of voice that localises to the vocal cord
- **Physiology of speech** - complex, co-ordinated action involving 1) understanding language, 2) and sound generation:
    - **Language functions** - part of cortical function (i.e. Broca's and Wernicke's area)
    - **Generation of speech** - as a result of expiratory effort against a vibrating vocal cord, and subsequent modulation of sounds in the upper-aerodigestive tract:
        - <u>Breath support</u> - mediated by the respiratory muscles and lungs
        - <u>Vibration</u> - mediated by vocal cords (vocal cord palsy results in **dysphonia**)
        - <u>Resonance and articulation</u> - modoulated by pharynx, nasal cavity, oral cavity, tongue, and jaw movements (bulbar dysfunction results in **dysarthria**)
- **DDx of dysphonia** - organic vs functional vs psychogenic dysphonia:
    - **Organic dysphonia:**
        - <u>Local vocal cord pathologies</u>:
            - Benign - acute laryngitis, vocal cord nodule, vocal cord polyp, Reinke's oedema, recurrent respiratory papillomatosis
            - Malignant\_ - laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma
        - <u>Neurological pathologies</u>:
            - Central - Parkinsonism, vocal tremor, spasmodic dysphonia
            - Peripheral - recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy, superior laryngeal nerve palsy (iatrogenic or due to tumour involvement)
        - <u>Chest pathologies</u> (poor expiratory efforts) - small airway diseases
    - **Functional dysphonia** - i.e. muscle tension dysphonia
    - **Psychogenic dysphonia** - i.e. conversion disorder
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - **HPI** - onset, progression, quality, severity, timing:
        - <u>Onset</u> - acute vs chronic
        - <u>Progression</u> - static vs persistent (organic) vs fluctuating (functional)
        - <u>Timing</u> - relations to vocal straining
    - **Associated Sx** - particularly identify red flag Sx for malignancy:
        - <u>Bleeding/ Haemoptysis</u> - suggestive of malignancy
        - <u>Shortness of breath/ stridor</u> - suggestive of impending airway obstruction
        - <u>Dysphagia</u> - suggestive of malignant growth impending onto the esophagus
    - **PMH:**
        - <u>Neurological disorders</u> - e.g. old stroke, Parkinsonism
        - <u>Neck surgery</u> - e.g. thyroidectomy, parathyroidectomy, neck dissections
    - **SHx:**
        - <u>Occupation</u> - occupations related to vocal demand (e.g. teacher, singer)
        - <u>Smoking</u> - major risk factor for SCC
        - <u>Alcohol</u> - major risk factor for SCC
- **P/E:**
    - <u>Neck examination</u> - cervical LN
    - <u>Laryngoscopy</u> - indirect (mirror) vs direct (flexible or rigid endoscopy)
    - <u>Stroboscopy</u> - by principles flashing lights synchronised to the rate of fibration of the vocal cords as patient vocalises, creating 'slow motion' for detection of subtle vocal cord palsy
- Terminology:
    - Dysphonia - any impairment of voie
    - Aphonia - loss of voice
    - Hoarseness - rough or noising quality of voice
- Etiology of dysphonia (voice disorders):
    - Organic:
        - Laryngeal diseases:\`
            - Benign disorders - e.g. laryngitis, vocal cord nodule, vocal cord polyp, Reinke's oedema, recurrent respiratory papillomatosis
            - Malignant - SCC
        - Neurological disorders - central vs peripheral (RLN, SLN)
        - Asthma and COPD
    - Functional - muscle tension dysphonia
    - Psychogenic - conversion disorder
- EtiolDysphagia
    - SHx - Occupation, voice demand, smoking
- PE:
    - Oral cavity
    - Cervical LN
    - Inspection of larynx - indirect, flexible, rigid laryngoscopy
    - Stroboscopy - voice picked up patients voice to dtect subtal vocal cord lesions (read up)

## Benign laryngeal lesions

- **Vocal cord nodules:**
    - **Definition** - benign nodules on the vocal cords classically caused by voice abuse
    - **Classical occupational association:**
        - Singers
        - Teachers
    - **Pathophysiology** - repeated vocal trauma:
        - Localised oedema
        - Fibrosis and nodule formation
    - **Clinical features:**
        - <u>Dysphonia</u> - persistent husky voice
        - <u>Easy fatigue on vocalisation</u> - easy fatigue as vocal cords unable to oppose resulting in air leak
    - **Signs** - always <u>bilateral and symmetrical</u> nodules at the <u>junction of anterior- and middle-third of the vocal folds</u>: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/vocal_cord_nodules_on_laryngoscopy.png)
    - **Mx:**
        - Speech therapy
        - Vocal hygiene
    - Pathophysiology:
        - Vocal trauma (forceful closure of cord durng forceful phonation) –\> localised oedema –\> fibrosis –\> nodules
        - Characteristically occurs in junction between anterior and middle 1/3 of vocal folds bilaterally and symmetrically (because vibrates the most)
    - Clinical features - husky voice and easy fatigue
    - Mx:
        - Speech therapy
        - Vocal hygiene
- **Vocal polyp:**
    - **Definition** - unilateral vocal cord lesion associated w/ vocal trauma
    - **Pathophysiology** - acute vocal trauma:
        - Bleeding from mucosal vessels organised into a haematoma
        - Haematoma transforms to polyp
    - **Clinical features:**
        - <u>Dysphonia</u> - persistent husky voice
        - <u>Easy fatigue on vocalisation</u> - easy fatigue as vocal cords unable to oppose resulting in air leak
    - **Signs** - unilateral vocal cord polyp: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/vocal_cord_polyp.png)
    - **Mx** - microlaryngoscopy + excision
- **Reinke's edema:**
    - **Definition** - fluid collection (or myxoedema) in the Reinke's space, in the superficial lamina propria of the vocal cord
    - **Etiology** - associated with certain medical conditions:
        - Smoking
        - GERD
        - Hypothyroidism (myxoedema deposition)
    - **Clinical features:**
        - <u>Dysphonia</u> - often **effortful**, **low-pitch and rough** voice
    - **Signs:** 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/reinkes_oedema.png)
    - **Mx:**
        - Correct underlying cause
        - Microlaryngoscopy + Excision
- **Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis:**
    - **Definition** - recurrent papilloma formation on vocal cord caused by chronic HPV infection (subtypes 6 and 11)
    - **Aquisition of HPV infection:**
        - In utero transfer
        - Birth tract contact
        - Oral tract
    - **Clinical features:**
        - <u>Dyspnonia</u> - recurrent hoarseness
        - <u>Upper airway obstruction</u> - manifests as stridor/ SOB before complete obstruction
        - <u>Malignant transformation</u> - risk of malignant transformation to SCC
    - **Signs:** 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/benign_respiratory_papillomatosis.png)
    - **Mx:**
        - Microlaryngoscopy + excision
        - HPV vaccine reduces recurrence
        - Adjuvant medical therapy (e.g. avastin, cidovovir, interferon-alpha)

## Malignant laryngeal lesions

- Premalignant lesions - leukoplakia and erythroplakia
- CA larynx
    - Histopathology - SCC predominantly, ocassionally CA of minor salivary glands in larynx
    - SCC a/w smoking
    - Clinical features - dependent on site:
        - Hoarseness
        - Airway obstruction:
            - Direct obstruction
            - Invasion into laryngeal muscle –\> fixation of hemilarynx –\> unilateral cord paralysis
        - Cervical LN - rare due to little lymphatic supply
    - Principles of Ix:
        - Staging - TNM staging
        - Detect synchronous lesion (8-10% esp smoking and drinking) - due to field cancerisation (perform panendoscopy)
        - Ix:
            - Bx for histological diagnosis - by flexible laryngoscopy (LA) or microlaryngoscopy (GA)
            - Panendoscopy
            - USG neck for nodal metastasis
            - Contrast CT neck - locoregional staging
        - Mx:
            - Early stage (single-modality Tx) - radiotherapy, transoral LASER microsurgery
            - Advanced stage (multimodal Tx):
                - Chemo + RT
                - Total laryngectomy + adjuvant RT
        - Total laryngectomy - removal of entire larynx:
            - Requires tracheostome for breathing and phonation
            - Voice rehabilitation:
                - Electropharynx
                - Pneumatic device
                - Tracheo-esophageal speech - single way valve into neopharynx
                - Esophageal speech

## Vocal cord oalsy

- Etiology:
    - Neurogenic - vagus or RLN, stroke
    - Cricoarytenoid joint - fixation (e.g. RA), dislocation
    - Intrinsic muscle - myopathy, infiltration by malignancy
- Unilateral vs bilateral vocal nerve palsy
- Workup for unilateral vocal cord palsy:
    - USG neck - for any malignancy
    - CT skull-base to thorax - along course of vagus nerve
