# GC176 A large liver, liver cancer

## Clinical approach to hepatomegaly

- **DDx of hepatomegaly:**
    - **Malignant hepatomegaly** - Primary liver tumours (HCC, CC, others), Secondary liver metastasis (esp. GI tract), Haematological malignancies (lymphoma, leukaemia)
    - **Benign hepatomegaly:**
        - Benign tumours - haemangioma, adenoma, <u>focal nodular hyperplasia</u>, angiomyolipoma
        - Cysts - cystadenoma, polycystic disease
        - Others - alcoholic cirrhosis, liver abscess
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - Pain - typically absent or mild dull-aching pain in malignant conditions (except in acute complications such as ruptured HCC)
    - Features of obstructive jaundice - tea-coloured urine, pale stools
    - Change in bowel habits
    - Systems review - Sx suggestive of other primary (e.g. cough, haemoptysis etc.)
    - Constitutional Sx - weight loss, anorexia
    - Other relavent Hx - past Hx of chronic HBV/HCV infection, alcohol, family Hx of hepatitis B or malignancy
- **P/E:**
    - General - pallor, jaundice, lymphadenopathy, <u>stigmata of chronic liver disease</u>
    - Abdomen - hepatomegaly (liver span, tenderness, consistency, surface, edge, bruit), other organomegaly (e.g. splenomegaly), mass, ascites, PR exam
        - Characteristic of hepatomegaly:
            - Diffuse, smooth firm hepatomegaly - alcoholic cirrhosis, haematological malignancies
            - Irregular, hard hepatomegaly - HCC (typically soft and vascular), CC, secondary malignancy
    - Systems review
- **Ix:**
    - Routine bloods - CBC, LRFT, Clotting
    - Viral hepatitis serology - HBsAg, Anti-HBs, Anti-HBc, Anti-HCV
    - Tumour markers - AFP, CEA, CA19-9
    - Imaging - CXR, US upper abdomen, CT, staging imaging (MRI, PET-CT)
    - +/- other Ix - based on clinical suspicion:
        - Endoscopy - colonoscopy (colorectal primary, +ve PR), upper endoscopy
        - Biopsy - FNAC, Trucut core biopsy (risk of bleeding, and tumour cell seeding)

## HCC

- **Etiology:**
    - HBV (80%)
    - HCV (5%)
    - Alcholic liver disease
    - Cirrhosis irrespective of cause - NAFLD incidence on the rise
    - Aflotoxin (fungal-derived toxin) - a co-carcinogen through interaction w/ HBV
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Incidence - 5th in incidence, 3rd in mortality
    - Demographic - \> 50y, M:F = 4:1
- **Pathology:**
    - Three macroscopic types - massive, diffuse, nodular (worst prognosis)
    - Histopathological variant - fibrolamellar HCC
    - Properties:
        - **Rapid tumour growth** - average tumour doubling time 3mo
        - **Venous invasion**
        - **Blood supply** - from hepatic artery (cf normal liver parenchyma, 2/3 bloow flow from portal v.)
- **Clinical presentation** - 60% late presentation w/ multifocal lesions or venous invasion:
    - Asymptomatic - subclinical via screening test, for carriers
    - **Non-specific Sx of malignancy** - malaise, anorexia, weight loss
    - **Effects of primary tumour** - mild-dull aching RUQ pain (?due to distension of capsule), hepatomegaly
    - **Decompensation of known cirrhosis** - decompesnsation of liver functions (e.g. worsening ascites, variceal bleeding)
    - **Paraneoplastic features** (rare) - fever, diarrhoea
    - **Complications** - Ruptured HCC
- **Dx:**
    - Tumour markers (non-sensitive, non-specific) - AFP \> 400 ng/ml (almost diagnostic)
    - Imaging - USG, CT and MRI (primovist contrast, w/ hepatobiliary phase), dual tracer PET scan, hepatic arteriogram (seldom used)
    - Biopsy - FNAC, trucut Bx (only for non-resectable cases)
- 3-phase CT scan - arterial enhacement w/ porto-venous washout:
    - Non-contrast phase - hypodense
    - Arterial phase - hyperdense
    - Porto-venous phase - hypodense
    - +/- other features - PV invasion (invasion in main PV can lead to sudden pHTN, hypoDense pV in porto-venous phase), central necrosis (hypodense centre)
- **Dual-Tracer PET Scan:**
    - Dual Tracer - FDG (50% sensitivity) and C-11 acetate (90% sensitivity)
    - Principles - well differentiated HCC will have similar FDG metabolism as normal liver parenchyma (cannot differentiate tumour and hence not identifiable)
- **Principle of Mx:**
    - Curative Tx - hepatic resection, liver transplantation (only for early cancers, immunosuppressant –\> recirrence), thermal ablation
    - Locoregional Tx (palliative) - Transarterial chemoembolisation (TACE), Transarterial radioembolisation (TARE), Stereotatic Body Radiotherapy (SBRT)
    - Systemic Tx (palliative) - chemotherapy (non-effective, very chemo-resistent), targeted therapy (Sorafenib, Levatiib, Bevalizumab) and immunotherapy (Atezolizumab)
- Thermal ablation:
    - Efficacy - similar to resection for early cancers (\< 5cm)
    - Indications - for tumours \< 5cm, \< 4 nodules
    - Approach - percutaneous, laparoscopic, open
    - Energy source - radiofrequency, microwave, cryo-ablation
- Transarterial chemoembolisation:
    - Principles:
        - Lipiodol - contrast selectively uptake by liver (then why not use it for CT)
        - Cisplatin - chemotherapy
        - Gelfoam - embolisation to block off arterial supply
