## Overview of hereditary neuropathies

- **Principles of classification** - differentiation is based on:
    - Clinical characteristic
    - Mode of inheritance
    - Electrophysiological features
    - +/- upon specific genetic foci
- **Dyck classification of hereditary neuropathies** - each are divided into types and subtypes:
    - <u>Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease</u> (Hereditary Motor Sensory Neuropathies \[HMSN\])
    - <u>Hereditary sensory-autonomic neuropathies</u> (HSAN)
- **Conditions outside this classification:**
    - Hereditary neuropathy with pressure palsy
    - Hereditary brachial plexopathy
    - Giant axonal neuropathy
- **Charcot-Marie-Tooth Disease** (CMT) - spectrum of disorders caused by pathogenic variants in genes typically expressed in myelin and/or axons:
    - **Most common genes implicated in CMT:**
        - PMP22 (Peripheral myelin protein 22)
        - MPZ
        - GJB1
        - MFN2
    - **CMT types and subtypes** - over 100 subtypes of CMTs: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/distinguishing_among_cmt_subtypes.png)
- **Hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsy** (HNPP) - a recurrent episodic demyelinating neuropathy a/w PMP22 deletions, and a single nucleotide variants that is alleic to CMT1A:
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - pathogenic deletion in PM22 gene resulting in reduced expression, which corresponds to the duplicated region of PMP22 in CMT1A, resulting in disrupted myelin junctions
    - <u>Onset of Sx</u> - typically appear in 2nd-3rd decade of life
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - presents with **isolated nerve palsies** w/ localization in areas affected by compression, trauma or entrapment:
        - Peroneal nerve palsy (36%)
        - Ulnar nerve palsy (28%)
        - Bracheal plexopathy (20%)
        - Radial nerve palsy (13%)
    - <u>Electrodiagnostic findings</u> - multiple signs of demyelination
    - <u>Nerve Bx findings</u> - focal myelin thickening on LM
    - <u>Mx</u> - conservative by avoiding mild trauma and compression

## Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease: Genetics, clinical features, and diagnosis

- **Definition** - the eponym remains popular but refers to hereditary motor sensory neuropathy (HMSN), heterogenous group of hereditary peripheral neuropathies characterised by progressive motor and sensory functions, including characteristic atrophy of calf muscles in several subtypes
- **Epidemiology** - most common hereditary peripheral neuropathy:
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 40/100000
- **Pathogenesis** - caused by pathogenic variants in genees implicated in myelination and axonal function:
    - <u>Variants of pathogenic genes</u> - majority of the cases are attributed to pathogenic variants in 4 genes:
        - Peripheral myelin protein 22 (PMP22)
        - Myelin protein zero (MPZ)
        - Gap junction protein beta 1 (GJPB1)
        - Mitochondrial fusion protein mitofusin (MFN2)
    - <u>Progressive nerve dysfunction</u> - typically first appear in a length-dependent fashion, but becomes more generalised over time
    - <u>Muscle atrophy and altered biomechanics</u> - results in characteristic secondary skeletal deformities of the feet, ankles, vertebrae and other joints
- **Classification** - divided into specific types and subtypes: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/distinguishing_among_cmt_subtypes.png)
    - <u>CMT1</u> - typically AD disorder (rarely AR) a/w pathological variants in genes expressed in Schwann cells, resulting in abnormal peripheral myelination
    - <u>CMT2</u> - typically AD disorder a/w pathogenic variants in genes resulting in primary axonal damage
    - <u>CMTX</u> - X-linked disorders involving different foci which may be demyelinating or axonal
    - <u>CMT3</u> - severe, early onset peripheral neuropathies that are thought to be caused by inability of Schwann cells to produce normal myelin
    - <u>CMT4</u> - AR disorders a/w pathological variants in structural myelin protein
