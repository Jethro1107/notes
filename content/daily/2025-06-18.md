# On Nepotism, Justice, Hipocrisy, the Interplay between Power and Moral Superiority and the true nature of our desires

I prefer not to speak. If I speak, I am in big trouble.

Why do I wish to speak? There is no rational reason that could ever explain my urge to speak on this matter. I am not a stakeholder. I do not care how it ends. The medicolegal aspect worries me, the accusations of academic dishonesty interests me, but I am not naive: This is not a perfect world. There is a clear understanding that whatever I say, will not ripple beyond my Garden of Eden. I am not driven by a moral impulse - all through my life I have been paralysed by the concept of morality. There is absolutely nothing to gain for me to participate in this dialogue. Yet, there is a desire to speak, to commentate, like many of us. Perhaps there is a temptation to join the commentary: I want to be heard like everyone else. It is a learned behaviour. It is an imitated desire to be heard. It is often easy to observe and perceive of other's desires as inauthentic, but do we ever question the authenticity of our own desires?

I, too, feel the pull of this storm - not as a participant but as someone trying to parse its currents. Like you, I'm drawn to speak, to cut through the noise, yet wary of becoming just another voice in the fray. Girard reminds us that our words, our actions are not driven by rationality, but a spirited (though often mimetic and deceitful) desire for being.

I speak perhaps because of my own aspirations. I am also young, I dabble in machine learning research. I am also part of the future medical community. I do not bash not because I want to pledge allegiance, it might be out of self-preservation - to defend my own path.

I speak perhaps because of my pride in my own philosophy. Time and time again, society dissolves into a mimetic hysteria. Two camps form, invoking ideologies, morality and principles to justify their opposition, but deep down because of an impotent hatred towards each other as they view each other as an obstruction to their true self. The crowd is not seaking truth, but often a cathartic release from this madness. The unproductive frenzy resolves not with time, but when a victim is selected as a scapegoat. The clash of morality, driven by ressentiment, reveals the tendancy of moral posturing. All this could all me rationalising my desire to attain moral superiority over others.

I am reminded of Nietzche's Genealogy of Morals, which reveals Christian Morality as a by product of power dynamics and the will to power. It goes s follows:

- **Premise 1** - Envy, and a profound festering resentment that the weak harbours against the strong arises because they view the strong as an obstruction to what they should be, that is the power, status and vitality of the masters.
- **Premise 2** - Christianity morals simply are reactive inversions of the dominant values. and they adopt a slave mentality as evident in the Beatudes ("Blessed are the poor in spirit…")
- **Premise 3** - Validity of an ideology is dependent on its hidden drive - it is valid if its based on reason, but is suspicious if emtionally driven.
- **Conclusion** - Morality in this sense is a coping mechanism, not a coherent ethical framework.

There are certain properties when attaining to a moral framework:

- **Deceitful** - morality is freely chosen or divinely ordained, ignoring its roots in ressentment - the truth of their motives are masked
- **Projection** - the lack of being is projected onto the masters, and is coped with labelling such being as "evil" to avoid confronting their own lack

The slaves are driven by their innate desire to be the masters.
