## Epidemiology of Prostate cancer

## Prostate Cancer Imaging

- **Role of imaging:**
    - Establish likelihood of clinically significant prostate cancer (by PIRADS)
    - Staging (by definition after histological diagnosis)
    - Detect recurrence
- **Different modalities:**
    - USG
    - MRI
    - PSMA PET-CT
- **Classical approach to Dx of prostate cancer does not employ imaging:**
    - PSA screening
    - DRE
    - Systemic transrectal Bx
- **Novel approach to Dx of prostate cancer** - presumptive Dx on imaging to delay complications related to Bx:
    - PSA screening
    - MRI if elevated
    - Decide watchful waiting vs Bx
- **MRI-first approach** - MRI as a triage tool is documented in PROMIS study:
    - Allow 27% to avoid primary Bx
    - Diagnosis of 5% fewer clinically insigificant cancers
    - Also enables MRI-guided TRUS-Bx enabling up to 18% more cases of clinically significant cancer detected
- **Multiparametric MRI** - combines anatomical T2W sequence w/ functional asssessment (by DWI ADC, and DCE):
    - <u>Findings</u> - low signal/ hypointense areas within high-signal activity tissue background (70% in peripheral zone)
    - <u>Assessment of local spread</u>:
        - Detect extracapsular spread
        - Vascular bundle involvement
        - Seminal vesicle invasion
- **Anatomy of the prostate gland** - inverted pyramid from supreior to inferior (coronal view):
    - Base just below urinary gland
    - Mid-gland
    - Apex
- **PI-RADS score system** - score 1-5 for likelihood of clinically significant prostate cancer:
    - PIRADS 1 - Very low
    - PIRADS 2 - low
    - PIRADS 3 - ntermediate
    - PIRADS 4 - high
    - PIRADS 5 - very high
- **USG** - TRUS has been superceded by MRI, but now to guide procedures such as Bx
- **PSMA PET-CT:**
    - New prostate specific membrane antigen-based PET improves prostate cancer detection
    - Main role is staging and detection of recurrence in patients w/ raised PSA
    - Physiological uptake:
        - Salivary glands
        - Liver
        - Kidneys

## Basic Pathological Assessment of Prostate Specimens

- **Histological Dx** - TRUS-guided Bx or transperineal USG guided Bx
    - Multiple cores of Bx are taken in a systematic manner
    - Transperineal route now preferred as less risk of sepsis
- **Assessment report contains:**
    - Any adenocarcinoma
    - Length and % of core of CA (volume of disease)
    - Gleason score
    - Lymphovascular permeation
    - Perineural invasion
    - Extraprostatic extension
- **Normal prostate histology:**
    - Acinar glandular structure
    - Papillary folds with patent lumen
    - Epithelial cells vs basal cells
- **Histological features of acinar adenocarcinoma:**
    - Lost of lobular architecture w/ clustering of glands
    - Cytological atypia - increased N:C ratio, prominent nucleoli, and loss of basal cells
- **Gleason score grading** - 2 components reflecting aggressive nature (requires pre-treatment for standard evaluation):
    - Components:
        - Primary Gleason grade - based on histological grade of most predominant in terms of surface area of involvement
        - Secondary Gleason grade - based on histological grade of second most predominant
    - Grade groups (ISUP/ WHO grade) - correlates w/ outcome

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/prostate_cancer_grade_group.png)
- **Assessment in prostectomy specimens** - pathological T staging (note that pT1 does not exist):
    - Size and weight
    - Histology and quantification
    - Gleason score
    - Extraprostatic extension
    - Urinary bladder neck invasion
    - Seminal vesical invasion
    - Lymphovascular permeation
    - Perineural invasion
    - Surgical margins

## Screening and Diagnostic Workup for Prostate Cancer

- **Epidemiology** - second leading cause of cancer death among US men:
    - ?The most diagnosed cancer excluding nonmelanoma skin cancer
    - Lifetime risk - 1 in 31 M before 75y
    - Increasing trend
- **Principles of screening** - Wilson and Jungner WHO classic screening criteria (disease and test related)… see slides
- **Prostate cancer screening is one of the most controversial topics in urological literature:**
    - Pinsky and Parnes Screening for Prostate cancer (NEJM clinical practice section)
    - **Known harms from screening:**
        - Serious test-related complications:
            - Immediate - perforation w/ colonoscopy
            - Delayed - potential carcinogenesis from radiation exposure
        - False +ve:
            - Causes anxiety
            - Unnecessary invasive diagnostic procedures
            - Unecessary Bx which have significant associated risk
        - Over diagnosis - detection of cancer that would never become clinically apparent in patient's lifetime in absence of screening
        - Over-treatment - results in complications from treatment, e.g. ED, urinary incontinence, bowel dysfunction
- **PSA** - organ-specific but not disease-specific:
    - No normal PSA level
        - Prostate Cancer Prevention Trial demonstrates that even at low level of PSA, prostate cancer can be detected
        - However, generally a linear relationship between PSA and detection rate
    - PSA cut off - 4 ng/ml w/ acceptable Sn and Sp
    - Derrivatives of PSA may increase test properties (see slides) - 2nd vs 3rd generation and know PHI as recommended by NCCN
    - False +ve:
        - Increasing age
        - Recent ejaculation
        - Vigorous exercise
        - Prostatitis/ prostate stimulation/ BPH
        - Recent catheterisation
    - Approach to false +ve of PSA - recheck for 2-3 weeks
- **Role of DRE** - as screening tool as well:
    - Size
    - Nodularity
    - Obliteration of median groove
- **EAU guidelines for prostate cancer screening** - see slides
- HK Anti-Cancer Society:
    - Against screening \> 70y
    - 55-69y of average risk requires detailed discussion about benefits and risks before undergoing screening
- **Cochrane review on PCa screening** - implementation of screening a/w:
    - Increased diagnosis
    - Detection of more localized disease
    - Detection of less advanced PCa
    - No Cancer-specific survival benefit
    - No OS benefit
- **EAU guidelines for diagnostic workup:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/eau_guidelines_for_prostate_cancer_diagnostic_workup.png)
    - Note that additional image from an NEJM workflow
- Stockkholm-3 model, PSI, 4K score test - calculators w/ biomarkers, and clinical factors +/= genetic markers for STHLM:
    - 4K score - probability of finding high-grade aggressive prostate cancer (Gleason 7+)
    - STHLM3 test - predict risk of aggressive PCa
    - PCA3 test - collection of first 20-30ml of urine following vigorous prostate massage to risk stratify likelihood of prostate cancer
- Multiparametric MRI:
    - Combines T2, DWI, and dynamic contrast-enhanced imaging
    - Ada Ng mentioned that although literature favours it as initial test, but limited in HK setting
- TRUS - no role in Dx as variable sonographic findings:
    - Tumour may be hypo iso or hyperechoic
    - Mainly used for TRUS-guided Bx for transrectal or transperineal Bx
    - Transrectal vs transperipheal Bx:
        - Transrectal - less painful (above dentate line); lower risk of infection
        - Transperineal - more painful; lower risk of infection
    - Fusion image biopsy
    - Complications of TRUS Bx:
        - Bleeding - haemospermia, haematuria, rectal bleeding
        - Infective - prostatitis, epididymitis, fever
        - AROU
        - Hospitalisation

## Management of Localised Prostate Cancer

### Radical Prostatectomy

- **Note use of prostate predict tool (NHS)** - illustration of comparitie AS vs radical Tx:
    - Predicts 10 and 15y mortality
    - Predicts risk of S/E of radical Tx
- **Whole gland intervention:**
    - Radical prostatectomy
    - Radical radiotherapy +/- ADT
    - Brachytherapy
    - Photon therapy
- **Focal therapies** (experimental):
    - Cryotherapy
    - HIFU
    - Photodynamic therapy
    - Electroporation
    - Focal RT by brachytherapy
- **Approach:**
    - Young's prostatectomy - Perineal route (historical)
    - Millin's prostatectomy - Retropubic route
- Walsh:
    - Preservation of neurovascular bundles
    - Anatomic approach to radical prostatectomy (see slides on basic steps)
- Open vs MSI - MSI approaches either extra-peritoneal or transperitoneal
- Pentafecta of Protastectomy:
    - No major complications
    - Negative surgical margins
    - Recurrence free
    - Continence
    - Potency
- **ProtecT Trial** (Hamdy et al 2023) - 3 arms RCT w/ 1) radical prostatectomy arm, radical RT arm, and 3) Active surveillance:
    - Surgery vs radical RT has no difference in all oncological outcomes
    - Radical Tx vs active surveillance:
        - Radical Tx a/w lower metastasis local progression or long-term androgen deprivation therapy
        - However did not translate to OS benefits in 15y follow up

### Watchful waiting and active surveillance

- **Risk stratification of prostate cancer** - D'Amico Classification: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/damico_classification_risk_stratification_of_pca.png)
- **New NCCN risk stratification of prostate cancer** - see slides for details but incorpoprates:
    - Clinical features
    - Pathological features
    - Imaging
    - Molecular testing of tumour
    - Genetic testing of tumour
- **Two trials mentioned in lecture** - conflicting results on role of radical Tx, but now <u>redifine its role</u>:
    - Not to improve survival
    - But to reduce suffering from metastasis
- **Watchful waiting** - follow-up w/o curative intent:
    - <u>Definition</u> - monitoring of prostate cancer to detect Sx arise from prostate cancer and symptomatic Tx, with minimal focus on risk stratification and clinical staging of disease
    - <u>Indications</u> - if disease unlikely to cause reduced survival and suffering:
        - Patients with **asymptomatic disease** as a pre-requisite
        - Patients where **Tx has no prognostic implications** in terms of survival (e.g. life expectancy \< 10y) and unlikely to cause problems during lifetime
    - <u>Approach to FU</u>:
        - Monitor Sx and provide Sx-based interventions (e.g. catheterisation for AROU)
        - Routine PSA, imaging and Bx not mandatory and may be used only if clinically indicated
        - ADT initiated +/- palliative RT in cases of symptomatic metastatic disease
- **Active surveillance** - follow-up w/ curative intent but to defer curative Tx to shorten duration of adverse functional outcomes:
    - <u>Indications</u> - very low-, low- and selected favourable intermediate risk by NCCN schema:
        - Low tumour volume (cT1-cT2a)
        - Low risk disease by PSA and Gleason Grade Group
    - <u>Approach</u> - structured follow-up protocol:
        - Clinical assessment - regular DRE
        - Biochemical assessment - regular DRE
        - Radiological assessment - potential role of regular multiparametric MRI assessment
        - Pathological assessment - regular prostate Bx to detect Gleason Grade Reclassification
    - <u>Indications to opt out of protocol</u>:
        - Patient's wishes - as regular assessment may create significant anxiety and causes some patients to subsequently choose definitive intervention despite absence of progressive disease
        - Disease progression - either biochemically, radiologically or upgrade on repeat prostate biopsy

## Introduction to radiotherapy for prostate cancer

- **Indications:**
    - Radical intent - feasible for all localised prostate cancer irrespective to risk (low-intermediate-high), or as salvage Tx after local recurrence after RP
    - Palliative intent
- **Comparison between RP and EBRT:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/comparison_between_rp_andrt.png)
- **Techniques:**
    - <u>External beam RT</u> - usually CT/ MRI planning w/ IMRT:
        - Conventional dose fraction - 74-78 Gy over 37-39 \# over 7-8 weeks
        - Hypofraction - 60Gy over20# over 4 weeks
        - SABR dose fractionation - 36.25 Gy over 5# over 2 weeks (alternative days)
    - <u>Brachytherapy</u> - at low dose rate or high dose rate:
        - LDR - requires implantation of radioactive seeds
        - HDR - requires insertion of radioactive rods to deliver radiation
- **S/E:**
    - **Short term:**
        - General - Fatigue, skin reaction
        - Bowel Sx - e.g. PR bleeding, mucus discharge, faecal urgency, diarrhoea, flatulence/ cramping
        - Urinary Sx - e.g. frequency, urgency, incontinence haematuria
    - **Long-term:**
        - Bowel - chronic radiation proctitis, fistula
        - Urinary - chronic radiation cystitis, incontinence, urethral stricutres
        - Erectile dysfunction and impotence
        - Second malignancy (theoretical risk but is late S/E, generally do not live long enough to experience this)
    - Infertility
    - ADT S/E in intermediate and high risk patients

## Systemic therapy for metastatic prostate cancer

- \- **Types of systemic therapy:**
    - Hormonal therapy
    - Chemotherapy
    - Targeted therapy
    - Immunotherapy
    - Other novel therapies e.g. radioligand therapy
- **Androgen deprivation therapy:**
    - Androgen synthesis blocking agents
    - Androgen receptor blocker
- Chemotherapy
- Novel therapies (e.g. radio-pharmaceutical agents)
- **Natural Hx of prostate cancer** - due to clonal selection, results in:
    - Progression from metastatic hormone sensitive prostate cancer to metastatic castration resistant prostate cancer
- **Physiology of androgen signaling in prostate cancer:**
    - Androgens (primary testosterone and DHT) drives growth and function of prostate cells
    - Androgen enter prostate cells and bind to AR in cytoplasm, forms transcription factor that binds to androgen response element (AREs) on DNA
    - This results in increased transcription of genes that promotes proliferation, surviva and differentiation
- **Metastatic hormone-sensitive Prostate cancer** (mHSPC):
    - First line is still ADT as many patient will initially respond well to ADT
    - ADT will reduce androgens to pre-pubertal levels
    - Duration of response can vary widely, but some patients remain hormone-sensitive for several years
    - Now earlier intensification of mHSPC settings by adding anti-androgen agents +/- chemo will improve outcomes
- **Metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer** (mCRPC):
    - Subsequently developed from mHSPC after prolonged ADT use
    - Mechanisms for developing resistance to hormonal therapy:
        - AR over-expression - more Sn to low levels of androgens
        - AR mutation - constitutively active and do not reqquire androgen for activation
        - Intratumoral androgen production - synthesize androgen within tumor microenvironment, circumventing the reduced systemic androgen levels achieved by ADT (but potenitally responds to abiraterone)
        - Cross-talk with other signalling pathways
    - mCRPC occurs when prostate cancer progresses despite low testosterone levels achieved by ADT, indicated by:
        - Clinical - new or worsening Sx
        - Radiological - progression on imaging studies
        - Biochemical - Rising PSA levels
    - Treatment options:
        - Androgen receptor pathway inhibitors - second-generation anti-androgens and androgen synthesis inhibitors
        - Chemotherapy (if not used before or rechallenge) - taxane-based (docetaxel and cabazitaxel)
        - Targeted therapy - PARP inhibitors (if BRCA +ve)
        - Radiopharmaceuticals - radium 223 for bone metastasis, lutetium-177 PSMA for PSMA positive disease
- **Hormonal therapy:**
    - <u>Rationale</u> - reduce androgen levels or block their effects on prostate cancer by:
        - Reduced synthesis - affecting pituitary axis, blocking enzyme for synthesis, surgically removing testes
        - Blocking androgen receptor
    - **Androgen deprivation therapy** (ADT) - medical vs surgical:
        - **Surgical** - bilateral orchidectomy
        - **Medical** - as monthly, 3-monthly or 6-monthly injections:
            - **LHRH agonist**:
                - <u>MOA</u> - complex:
                    - Initially stimulate LH and FSH surge resulting in testosterone production (anti-androgen to cover initial 2-4 weeks until testosterone surge subsides and castrate levels of testosterone reached)
                    - Continuous administration after weeks results in densensitization by pituitary (reduced expression of LHRH receptors)
                    - Subsequently low LH and FSH, hence reduced testosterone production
            - **LHRA antagonist:**
                - <u>MOA</u> - blockade of LHRH receptors to reduce FSH and LH levels
        - **S/E:**
            - Hot flashes - sudden feeling of warmth often w/ sweating and reddening of skin
            - Loss of libido
            - ED
            - Fatigue
            - Osteoporosis
            - Weight gain
            - Metabolic changes - lipid alterations, insulin resistance
            - Cardiovascular risk
            - Muscle loss
    - **Androgen synthesis inhibitor:**
        - Abiraterone - inhibits enzyme CYP17A1 crucial for androgen biosynthesis in all sources:
            - Testes
            - Andrenal glands
            - Prostate tumour tissue
        - Note that this enzyme affects cortisol, and results in ACTH and MR excess (hence low-dose prednisolone co-administered)

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/effects_of_androgen_synthesis_inhibitors_on_steroid_synthesis.png)
    - **Androgen receptor inhibitors:**
        - <u>Selection</u> - enzalutamide, apalutamide, darolutamide, flutamide, bicalutamide
        - <u>MOA</u> - prevents binding to androgen receptors on cell surfaces (inhibit androgen-driven growth signals)
- **Chemotherapy:**
    - Commonly used microtubule inhibitors such as docetaxel and cabezitaxel
    - More and more evidenece to suggest early intensification of Tx in mHSPC (superceded ADT alone as standard of care):
        - ADT + AR blocker or synthesis blocker
        - ADT + chemo
        - ADT + ARblocker/synthesis blocker + chemo
    - TITAN study demonstrates early intensification w/ apalutamide in mHRPC a/w improved OS and PFS
- **Bone-targeting radio-pharmaceutical agents:**
    - Radium-223 targeted alpha-emitting radiopharmaceutical used for Tx of mCRPC w/ symptomatic bone metastasis:
        - MOA - calcium mimicry and bone targeting:
            - Calcium analog - chemically similar to Ca
            - Bone affinity - selectively targets bone tissue, particular areas of high bone turnover such as sites of bone metastasiz
            - Integration to bone matrix - incorporates into hydroxyapatite matrix (i.e. in areas where bone formation increased)
            - Emitting alpha radiation - short range to limit damage surrounding normal tissue

## Novel therapies for prostate cancer

- **Novel therapy:**
    - Radioligand therapy
    - Target therapy
    - Immunotherapy
- **Radioligand therapy** - Lu-177 PSMA:
    - <u>Rationale</u> - combines radioactive isotope w/ a MAb that binds to cancer cells:Deliver radiation directly to cancer cells while sparing healthy tissues
    - <u>MOA</u>:
        - Targetting mechanism:
            - Payload (radioactive isotope) bound to PSMA
            - PSMA bond to its receptors on and is subsequently internalised
        - Radiation delivery:
            - Emits localised radiation intracellularly once internalised

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/moa_of_radioligand_therapy.png)
- **Targeted therapy:**
    - Homologous recombination repair (HRR):
        - Homologous recombination repair - process that fixes double-strand break:
            - Key proteins - include BRCA1 and BRACA2 as well as other supportive proteins (ATM)
        - Implications of HRR deficiency - risk of developing breast, ovarian and prostate cancer (25% of mCRPC have this defect)
        - PARP inhibitors - class of drugs that interfere w/ PARP enzymes involved in DNA repair:
            - MOA - inhibit prevent repair of single-strand break:
                - Causes more double-strand breaks during replication and hence more DNA damage (exploitation of this weakness in cancer cells)
                - Accumulation of DNAQ damage leading to cancer cell death (synthetic lethality)

        
        ![](~/Documents/notes/img/moa_of_parp_inhibitor.png)
- **Immunotherapy:**
    - Only small subset w/ MSI-H (2-3%) - may benefit from pembrolizumab
