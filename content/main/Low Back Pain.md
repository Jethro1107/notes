# Low Back Pain

- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - affects 60-80% of people at some time in their lives
	- _Incidence_ - 7% of UK adults consult GP for low back pain
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age - affects all ages, where age of onset determines the likely Dx
- **Etiology of back pain:**
	- 
	![[Pasted image 20260426205832.png]]
	- **Mechanical back pain** - most common cause of acute self-limiting low back pain in patients 20-55y
	- **Spinal pathologies:**
		- _Anatomical spine pathologies_ - lumbar spine stenosis, prolapsed disc, spondylolisthesis, vertebral fractures, Paget's disease of the bone
		- _Inflammatory spine pathologies_ - Seronegative spondyloarthropathies, e.g. AS, SpA, PsA
		- _Destructive spine pathologies_:
			- Infections - spondylodisciitis, spinal epidural abscesses
			- Malignancies - e.g. prostate, lung, breast, myeloma
		- _Other spine pathologies_:
			- Diffuse idiopathic skeletal hyperostosis
			- Arachnoiditis
- **Mechanical low back pain:**
	- **Epidemiology** - most common cause of acute, self-limiting low back pain:
		- Considered in 20-55y
		- Usually more common in manual workers, particular those in occupations that involve heavy lifting and twisting
	- **Clinical features of mechanical low back pain**:
		- _Site_ - confined to the lumbosacral region, buttock, and ocassionally the thigh, often lateralised and asymetrical
		- _Onset_ - usually provoked by lifting or bending
		- _Progression_ - generally good prognosis:
			- 30% w/ symptomatic improvement in 2 days
			- 90% w/ full recovery within 6 weeks
			- 10-15% patients develop chronic back pain which is a/w psychogenic elements
		- _Provacation_ - often exacerbated by activity (and specific ranges of movements) and relieved by rest
		- _Quality_ - mechanical in nature, mildly tender
		- _Radiation_ - never radiates beyond the knee (presence suggests sciatica)

	- **Signs:**
		- Asymmetrical paraspinal muscle spasm
		- Asymmetrical muscle tenderness (not midline tenderness)

- **Ix** - Ix not required for acute mechanical low back pain, but should be considered for those w/ 1) persistent pain (> 6 weeks) or 2) red flag features:
	- **Imaging:**
		- _MRI whole spine_ - demonstration of:
			- Structural changes - e.g. spinal stenosis, cord compression, nerve root compression
			- Inflammatory changes - e.g. infections, metastasis, spondyloarthropathies
		- _Plain X Ray of the lumbar spine_ - role in identification of:
			- Vertebral fractures
			- Degenerative changes
		- _PETCT_ - considered if suspecting malignancies
	- **Blood tests** - CBC, LRFT, ESR/ CRP, SPE, PSA
- **Mx of mechanical low back pain:**
	- _Reassurance and education_ - emphasise on self-limiting nature of condition and that exercise is helpful rather than damaging
	- _Advice_:
		- Emphasise role of exercise
		- Bed rests not helpful and may increase risk of chronic disability
		- Return to work and normal activity should take place as soon as possible
	- _Prescription_ - regular analgesics (e.g. NSAIDs) to improve mobility and facilitate exercise
	- _Referral_ - for physiotherapy aimed at return to normal activities
	- _Investigations and follow up_ - monitoring of progress and consider investigations if persistent back pain
