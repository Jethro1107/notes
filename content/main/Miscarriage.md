# Miscarriage

- **Definition** - a pregnancy that ends spontaneously before 24 weeks of gestation
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - common, and occurs in 10-20% of clinical pregnancies
    - <u>Demographic</u> - risk increases w/ age, and a/w certain etiologies
- **Classification of miscarriages** - nature of presentation:
    - <u>Threatened miscarriage</u> - symptomatic, sonographic evidence of intra-uterine pregnancy w/ FH
    - <u>Inevitable miscarriage</u> - symptomatic, sonographic evidence of intra-uterine pregnancy w/o FH
    - <u>Incomplete miscarriage</u> - symptomatic, presence of retained products of conception within uterus
    - <u>Complete miscarriage</u> - previously symptomatic but resolved, sonographic evidence of empty uterus after natural miscarriage
    - <u>Missed miscarriage</u> - asymptomatic, with sonographic evidence of intra-uterine pregnancy w/o FH

- **Etiology of miscarriages:**
    - Chromosomal abnormalities
    - Medical/ Endocrine disorders
    - Uterine abnormalities
    - Infections
    - Drugs/ chemicals
- **Clinical features of miscarriages** - depends on the type of miscarriage at presentation
    - <u>Asymptomatic</u> - typically occurs in complete miscarriages, where Sx has subsided, or missed miscarriages, which are incidentally discovered on booking visit
    - <u>Vaginal bleeding</u> - onset of vaginal bleeding where volume varies significantly, but patients often report passage of clots and tissue
    - <u>Abdominal pain</u> - cramping in nature and often associated w/ vaginal bleeding
- **Signs of miscarriage:**
    - <u>Primary assessment</u> (ABC) - risk of haemorrhagic shock
    - <u>Abdomino-pelvic examination</u> - assess whether cervical os open on speculum examination to determine type of miscarriage (note that pelvic-examination may be omitted if TVUSS can be arranged first)
- **Ix of miscarriage:**
    - **Pregnancy test** - r/o pregnancy complication
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, Rh status, T&S:
        - <u>CBC</u>:
            - NcNc anaemia of acute blood loss
            - raised MCV should raise suspicion of haemolysis
        - <u>Rh status</u> - consider Anti-D prophylaxis for certain mothers
        - <u>T&S</u> - for blood transfusion
    - **Transabdominal/ transvaginal pelvic USG** - determine the type of miscarriage:
        - <u>Incomplete miscarriage</u> - thick, irregular heterogenous echos in midline of uterine cavity, where maximum AP diameter of \<= 15 mm is suggestive of retained products
        - <u>Complete miscarriage</u> - well-defined regular endometrial line (consider EP especially if symptomatic)
        - <u>Silent miscarriage</u> - gestational sac diameter \>= 25 mm w/ no evidence of embryoo or yolk sac; CRL \>= 7mm w/ no evidence of cardiac pulsation (requires repeat scan 7d to confirm Dx)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - depending on clinical status, and patient preference:
        - <u>Expectant Mx</u> - offered if patient prefers this option to avoid surgery if pain and bleeding subside, but risk unplanned surgery if bleeding heavily (repeat urine pregnancy test after 3w and attend FU if +ve)
        - <u>Medical Mx</u> - Tx w/ mifepristone and misoprostol to induce passage of tissue
        - <u>Surgical Mx</u> - suction evaculation (or rarely vacuum aspiration) offered as day case, and is preferred if excessive bleeding and haemodynamically unstable
        - <u>Counselling</u> - distressing experience and emphasise that most are non-recurrent and mother is not to be blamed
    - **Expectant Mx** - least invasive and most natural approach:
        - <u>Indications</u> - typically reserved for those \<= 13 weeks of gestation as higher chance of spontaneous expulsion and lower risk of complications
        - <u>Duration for time needed for complete miscarriage</u>:
            - 70-80% of all patients within first trimester (\< 12+6 weeks) undergo complete miscarriage by 14d
        - <u>Complications</u> - consider opt out expectant Mx when:
            - Unbearable uterine cramping
            - Significant heavy uterine bleed
    - **Medical Tx** - increasingly applied to enable women to <u>miscarry at home</u>:
        - <u>Regimen:</u>
            - Mifepristone (progesterone antagonist) - esp. if \> 9 weeks' gestation
            - Misoprostol (prostaglandin antagonist) - single/ repeated, vaginal/ sublingual doses
        - <u>S/E:</u>
            - Pain (Tx w/ analgesics)
            - GI disturbance - e.g. N/V, diarrhoea (Tx with antiemetics)
        - <u>Clinical efficacy</u> - consider surgical Tx if continue to bleed excessively:
            - Tx failure - 10% failure rate
    - **Surgical Tx** - predictable course and rapid resolution:
        - <u>Indications</u>:
            - 1\. Patient preference
            - 2\. Excessive bleeding or haemodynamically unstable
        - <u>Technique</u>:
            - Manual vacuum aspiration under LA (only if women not comprimised)
            - Surgical evacuation under GA as day case (more common)
        - <u>Pre-treatment</u> - w/ vaginal or sublingual misoprostol to trigger cervical dilatation
        - <u>Complications</u>:
            - General surgical risks (GA risks)
            - Uterine perforation
            - Uterine trauma and haemorrhage
            - Post-operative pelvic infection
            - Cervical trauma - leading to subsequent cervical incompetence
- **Recurrent miscarriage:**
    - **Definition** - loss of \>= 3 consecutive pregnancies
    - **Epidemiology** - affects 1% of couples
    - **Risk factors:**
        - Advancing maternal and paternal age (?chromosomal abnormalities)
        - Obesity
        - Balanced chromosomal translocation
        - Uterinine structural anomalies (e.g. uterine septum, cervical incompetence)
        - Antiphospholipid syndrome
    - **Ix for recurrent miscarriage:**
        - <u>Anti-phospholipid antibodies</u> - deetction of APLS
        - <u>Imaging of the uterus</u> (e.g. TVUSS) - detection of uterine structural anomalies
        - <u>Cytogenetic analysis of products of examination</u> - assessment of chromosomal abnormalities
    - **Mx** - depending of underlying cause:
        - Antiphospholipid syndrome - ASA and low-dose heparin (reduce risk of miscarriage by 50%)
        - Chromosomal abnormalities - overcome by pre-implantation genetic diagnosis or gamete donation
        - Uterine structural abnormalities - may be ameable by surgery
