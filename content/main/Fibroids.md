# Fibroids

- **Definition** - also known as leiomyoma, a well-demarcated, firm whorled tumour arising in the myometrium
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - estimated present in 40% of women overall (most are asymptomatic)
    - <u>Incidence</u> - more common with advancing reproductive age (but unlikely present after menopause)
- **Risk factors of fibroids:**
    - Nilliparity
    - Obese
    - FHx
    - African descent
- **Sites of fibroids:**
    - Pednuculated
    - Submucosal
    - Intramural
    - Subserosal

  
  ![](img/sites_of_uterine_fibroids.png)
- **Pathophysiology of fibroids:**
    - <u>Estrogen dependent tumour</u> - growth of fibroids dependent on estrogen, and thus can **enlarge during pregnancy** in response to hyperestrogenic state and **shrink after menopause** when estrogen production ceases
    - <u>Increased irregularity of uterine cavity</u> - results in **increased endometrial surface area** resulting in HMB and dysmenorrhoea
- **Natural Hx of fibroids:**
    - **Progressive enlargement of fibroids** - enlargement of fibroids due to accumulative estrogen exposure over reproductive age, resulting in onset of S/S
    - **Acute degenerative changes:**
        - <u>Red degenration</u> - haemorrhage and necrosis in response to outgrowing their blood supply, typically presenting in the second trimester of pregnancy as acute abdomen
        - <u>Hyaline degeneration</u> - asymptomatic softening and liquefaction of the fibroid
        - <u>Cystic</u> - asymptomatic central necrosis leaving cystic spaces at the centre, and rarely a/w malignant or sarcomatous degeneration within the cyst (1:350 cases), typically manifesting as rapid enlargement of fibroid in a post-menopausal women
    - **Torsion** - ischaemia and infarction of a <u>pedunculated fibroid</u>
- **Clinical features of fibroids:**
    - **Asymptomatic** - vasty majority of small fibroids are asymptomatic but may be incidentally detected on abdominal examination revealing a <u>pelvic mass</u> (need not be treated)
    - **Abnormal uterine bleeding** - usually heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) and rarely IMB due to increased endometrial surface area
    - **Pressure Sx:**
        - <u>Urinary frequency</u> - compression of the bladder anteriorly
        - <u>Constipation or tenesmus</u> - compression of the rectum posteriorly
    - **Subfertility** - due to mechanical distortion affecting implantation of blastocysts, or occlusion of the fallopian tube
    - **Pain** - unusual and only occurs in torsion of pedunculated fibroid or red degeneration of fibroid during pregnancy
- **Signs of fibroids:**
    - <u>General examination</u> - pallor
    - <u>Abdominal examination</u> - visible and/or palpable abdominal mass arising from the pelvis
    - <u>Bimanual examination</u> - enlarged, smooth/ **irregular**, non-tender uterus (tenderness indicates red degeneration)
- **Ix:**
    - **CBC** - assess severity of anaemia (invariably indicates presence of significant fibroids)
    - **Abdominopelvic ultrasound** (TAUSS and TVUSS) - mainstay for Dx:
        - <u>TVUSS</u> - good for detecting and locating submucous fibroids and small intramural fibroids, which appears as **well-circumscribed, isoechoic mass within the myometrium**
        - <u>TAUSS</u> - good for larger intramural and subserosal fibroids and exclusion of <u>hydronephrosis</u>
    - **Additional Ix:**
        - <u>Saline infusion sonography</u> - good for detection of submucosal fibroids
        - <u>Hysteroscopy</u> - for detection of other pathologies and surgical planning
        - <u>MRI</u> - indicated prior to UAE by location of fibroids
- **Dx** - sonographic Dx
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Asymptomatic</u> - conservative Mx if asymptomatic or if subfertility only problem (surgical risk of extensive bleeding and hysterectomy as high as 1%)
        - <u>Symptomatic</u> - surgical and radiological Tx, with little role of medical Tx
    - **Medical Tx** - little role for conventional medical Tx for HMB (e.g. Merina, tranexamic acid, NSAID, COCP) as ineffective:
        - **Gonadotrophin-releasing hormone agonist** - to induce menopausal state (but poorly tolerated due to severe menopausal Sx)
    - **Surgical Tx** - depends on presenting complaint and patients aspirations for menstrual function and fertility:
        - **Minimally invasive hysteroscopic removal of submucousal fibroids** - to resolve HMB Sx even in the presence of other types of fibroids
        - **Myomectomy** - removal of fibroids w/ uterine conservtion (small but significant risk of uncontrolled bleeding leading to hysterectomy)
        - **Hysterectomy** - removal of uterus
    - **Radiological Tx** - uterine artery embolisastion (UAE) by interventional radiologists (reduction of fibroid volume by 50%)
