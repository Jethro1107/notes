# Staphyloccus aureus and Other Staphylococcus Species

- **Definition** - group of G+ cocci in clusters that colonise the anterior nares and skin
- **Classification of staphylococcus** - traditionally classified by <u>ability to produce coagulase</u>, but now S. aureus identified by other methods:
    - <u>Coagulase-positive staphylococcus</u> - S. aureus (and S. intermedis)
    - <u>Coagulase-negative staphylococcus</u> - many other species, including S. saprophyticus, S. epidermidis, and S. lugdenesis, S. schleiferi, S. haemolyticus, S. caprae
- **Approach to G+ cocci in clusters from blood culture/ microscopy:**
    - Not to be dismissed as contaminant unless all sources excluded
    - Repeat blood cultures x2 before initiating anti-staphylococcal ABx (e.g. cloxacillin) or vancomycin for severe MRSA infections
    - Add on clindamycin if presenting w/ staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome or staphylococcal toxic shock syndrome if presenting w/ toxin mediated disease
- **Staphylococcus aureus** - accounts for most staphylococcal infections:
    - **Clinical manifestations:**
        - **Genuine invasive infections:**
            - <u>Respiratory infections</u> - pneumonia, empyema, lung abscess
            - <u>Cardiac infections</u> - **infective endocarditis**, pericarditis
            - <u>Skin and soft tissue infections</u> - boils, styes, carbuncles, skin abscesses, cannula-related infections
            - <u>Bones and joint infections</u> - septic arthritis, osteoarthritis
            - <u>CNS infections</u> - brain abscesses, ocassionally meningitis
            - <u>Blood stream infections</u> - S. aureus bacteraemia, metastatic abscess formation

        - **Toxin-mediated disease:**
            - <u>Food poisoning</u> - e.g. by staphylococcal enterotoxin
            - <u>Staphylococcal toxic shock syndrome</u> - toxin-mediated multisystem failure
            - <u>Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome</u> - life-threatening skin condition in paediatric populations
    - **Virulence factor S. aureus:**
        - **Toxins:**
            - <u>Panto-Valentine leukocidin</u> (PVL) - causes dermonecrosis
            - <u>Enterotoxins</u> - results in food poisoning
            - <u>Epidermolyic toxins</u> - results in scalded skin syndrome
            - <u>Toxic shock syndrome tixoin 1</u> (TSST1) - superantigen resulting in significant Th cell activation and massive sytokine release, and a SIRS
    - **Classification of S. aureus** - based on ABx resistance patterns:
        - **Methicillin-sensitive S. aureus** (MSSA) - S. aureus that have yet acquired the penicillin-binding protein mutation and hence are still sensitive to **cloxacillin**
        - **Methicillin-resistant S. aureus** (MRSA) - recognised entity from \>= 30y ago:
            - <u>HA-MRSA</u> - hospital-acquired MRSA infection, which is resistant to many antibiotics, and typically respond to big-gun ABx like vancomycin
            - <u>CA-MRSA</u> - community-acquired MRSA infection, resistant against beta-lactams, but may be Sn to other ABx (often fatal as associated w/ PVL toxin production)
        - **Vancomycin-intermediate or -resistant S. aureus** (VISA/VRSA) - S. aurues with intermediate to complete resistance to vancomycin, require Tx with other big-gun ABx

    - **Staphylococcal toxic shock syndrome** - a/w production of TSST1:
        - <u>Clinical associations</u> - seen in young women w/ highly absorbent intravaginal tampons, but can occur w/ any S. aureus infections
        - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - production of TSST1:
            - Acts as 'superantigen', triggers significant Th cell activation and massive cytokine release
            - Results in systemic inflammatory response syndrome (SIRS)
        - <u>Clinical features</u> - SIRS, with features pointing towards an infection:
            - **Systemic upset** - myalgia, headache, sore throat, vomiting etc.
            - **Fever** - often high fever of abrupt onset
            - **SIRS and shock** - often to the extent of hypotension
            - **Generalised rash** - often generalised erythematous blanchable rash similar to that in scarlet fever
            - **Desquamation** - occurs in recovery period for 7-10d

      
      ![](img/full_thickness_desquamation_after_staph_tss.png)
        - <u>Ix and Dx</u> - can be clinical if on pelvic examination reveals retained tampon which shows G+ cocci in clusters on microscopy; blood culture and toxin detetion are confirmatory
        - <u>Principles of Mx</u>:
            - **General measures** - aggressive fluid resuscitation
            - **IV ABx** - consider IV cloxacillin/ vancomycin with addition of protein synthesis inhibitor (clindamycin)
            - **IVIG** - can be added on the basis that it is of demonstrable efficacy in streptococcal toxic shock
- **Coagulase-negative Staphylococcus:**
    - <u>S. saprophyticus</u> - part of normal vaginal flora, and causes UTI in sexually active young women
    - <u>S. epidermidis</u> - most common commensal and a/w **catheter-related/ implanted prosthetic infections**
    - <u>S. lugdunesis</u> - clinically behaves like S. aureus
