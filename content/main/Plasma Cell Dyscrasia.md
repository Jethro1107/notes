# Plasma cell dyscrasia

## Monoclonal Gammopathy of Uncertain Significance

- MGUS - typically an asymptomatic paraproteinaemia caused by pre-malignant clone of plasma cell or lymphoproliferative disorders, but with the absence of features of multiple myeloma, Waldenstrom macroglobulinaemia or lymphomas.
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Incidence - 5% of population \> 50y, incidence increases with age
- **Clinical definition:**
    - Paraproteinaemia - \< 3g/dL
    - BM plasma cell \< 10% of BM cellularity
    - Absence of target organ damage and defining biomarkers - Hypercalcaemia, renal impairments, anaemia, bone lytic lesions
    - r/o differentials of paraproteinaemia - lymphoma, and AL amyloidosis
- **Types of MGUS:**
    - Non-IgM MGUS (IgG, IgA, IgD) - typically progresses into smouldering myeloma and symptomatic myeloma (1%/y), and less frequently into AL amyloidosis, light chain deposition disease or lymphoproliferative diseases
    - IgM MGUS - typically progresses into Waldenstrom macroglobulinemia, and less frequently lymphomas, AL amyloidosis, and very rarely IgM MM
    - Light chain MGUS - typically progresses into MM and AL amyloidosis
- **Initial evaluation of MGUS** (r/o other ddx):
    - CBC
    - LRFT
    - CaPO4
    - SPE +/- Immunofixation
    - FLC assay
    - Ig Pattern (look for immunoparesis)
- **Risk of progression to MM** - 1%/y
    - Size of paraproteinaemia
    - Type of paraprotein - IgA \> light chain \> IgG
    - FLC ratio - Approaching 100 (bad prognostic marker)
    - Rate of rise of paraproteinaemia - typically requires reassessment of disease
    - Bone marrow morphology

## Multiple myeloma

- **Epidemiology:**
    - 1% of all malignancies, 10% of haematological malignancies
    - Incidence - 4/100000/y
    - Demographic - Median age of Dx 6-7th decade of life, M:F = 2:1
- **Pathogenesis of MM**
    - **Neoplasm of plasma cells** - neoplastic proliferation of a single clone of terminally differentiated plasma cells (post-GC plasma cells that have undergone class switching and somatic hypermutation)
        - Clonal expansion of plasma cells produce:
            - Monoclonal Ig - paraproteins in serum and urine
                - Paraprotein - detected by serum protein electrophoresis
                - Underlies cast nephropathy
            - Cytokines (?RANKL) - causing osteolytic bone lesions and hyperCa
            - Cytokines that suppress haematopoesis
    - **Many genetic events within B cells predispose to mutations** - VDJ rearrangement, somatic hypermutation, class switching
    - **Primary genetic change results in formation of MGUS** - causes clonal expansion of plasma cells
        - IGH translocation on chromosome 14q32 (40-50% cases) - causes juxtaposition of IGH locus with oncogene:
            - 11q13 – cyclin D1 (<u><u>CCND1</u></u>)
            - 6p21 – cyclin D3 (<u><u>CCND3</u></u>)
            - 4p16.3 – multiple myeloma set domain (<u><u>MMSET</u>\_/\_<sub>WHSC1</sub>\_</u>)
            - 16q23 – musculoaponeurotic fibrosarcoma (<u><u>C-MAF</u></u>)
            - 20q11 – musculoaponeurotic fibrosarcoma oncology family, protein B (<u><u>MAFB</u></u>)
        - Hyperdiploidy with trisomies involving odd chromosomes (3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 15, 19)
    - **Secondary genetic changes results in progression of MGUS into MM:**
        - Secondary mutations required (secondary translocations, p16 methylation, Myc abnormalities, NF-kappaB activation mutations) - immortilization of plasma cells
    - Bone marrow environment plays key role in development of MM
        - Cytokines from osteoblast/ osteoclast may play a role in myeloma cell growth.
            - Implications - extra-medullary MM are difficult to treat, becomes independent to bone-derived cytokines
    - **Pathogenesis of end-organ damage**
        - **Osteolytic bone lesions and hypercalcaemia** - caused by stimulation of osteoclast activity (increased RANKL expression and decreased OPG expression) and suppression of osteoblast (increased IL-3, IL7, DKK1)
        - **Renal Insufficiency**:
            - Cast nephropathy (most common mechanism) - precipitation of light chain casts in renal tubules, inducing interstitial nephritis
            - Hypercalcaemia - dehydration and pre-renal AKI
            - Nephrotic syndrome - due to deposition of light/ heavy chains onto glomeruli
        - **NcNc Anaemia** - due to 1) Marrow infiltration, 2) Reduced erythropoietin stimulation, and 3) Haemodilution (characterised by Rouleaux formation)
- **Clinical features of MM**
    - **Myeloma-defining features**
        - **NcNc Anaemia** (73% at presentation, 97% in natural Hx) - ocassionally a/w pancytopenia due to marrow suppression
        - **Bone pain** (58%) - osteolytic bone disease can cause bone pain and pathological fracture
            - **Site** - Typically axial skeleton rather than extremites
            - **Exacerbating factors** - movement, but relieved by rest (less common at night during sleep)
            - **Severity** - typically mild to moderate
        - **Renal insufficiency** (50%) - Varying degree of impairment, only 20% meet myeloma-defining renal impairments (SCr \> 177 micromol/L or eGFR \< 40 ml/min)
        - **HyperCa** (28%) - facticious hyperCa can occur due to binding of Ca to paraproteins, recheck ionised Ca if asymptomatic
    - Other clinical manifestations:
        - **Neurological manifestations**
            - Spinal cord compression (5%) - Severe back pain, bilateral LL weakness, sensory level and autonomic dysfunction, rom 1) vertebral collapse, or 2) extradural mass, likely an extramedullary plasmacytoma
            - Hyperviscosity (due to IgM MM) - oronasal bleeding. blurred vision due to retinal bleeding, HF, and cerebral ischaemia
            - Radiculopathy (most common neurological complications) - caused by paravertebral plasmacytoma or vertebral collapse
            - Peripheral neuropathy - typically co-existing AL amyloidosis, or POEMS syndrome
        - **Infections** - typically due to immunoparesis
- **Evaluation of suspected MM**
    - Hx - Hx eliciting information regarding potential malignancy and end organ damage
    - P/E - detailed neurological exam
    - Ix - see below
- **Ix in MM**
    - **Routine Blood Test**
        - **CBC** - NcNc anaemia (rarely macrocytic) +/- Pancytopenia
        - **PBS** - Rouleaux formation (\> 50%), indicative of paraproteinaemia
        - **LFT** - reversed A:G ratio, ALP (indication of fractures), Albumin (adjsuted Ca, and poor prognostic factors)
        - **RFT** - increased SCr, BUN, reduced eGFR, electroolytes
        - **ESR** - non-specifically elevated
        - **CaPO4** - hyperCa
        - **Ig pattern** - Monoclonal increase in one class, and reduced normal serum Ig levels
    - **Detection and quantification of M-protein**:
        - **Serum protein electrophoresis +/- immunofixation** - SPE for identification of paraproteinaemia and immunofixation for subtype (IgG 52%, IgA 21%, Bence Jones 16%, IgD, Biclonal, IgM, non-secretory MM)
        - **Serum free light chain (FLC) assay** - measurement of kappa and lambda light chains unbounded to heavy chains, detection of kappa- or lambda- restricted MM
        - **Urine protein electrophoresis +/- immunofixation** - 24h urine collection for UPE, immunofixation, and quantification.
    - **Bone Marrow Examination**
        - Clonal BM plasma cell \>= 10% (diagnostic) - not necessary if biopsy proven extramedullary plasmacytoma
            - **Immunophenotyping** (FISH) - +ve for markers of plasma cells e.g. CD38, CD45, CD138
            - **Cytogenetics** - trisomies, IGH translocation
    - **Radiological assessment**
        - **Skeletal survey** - conventional modality of imaging but are replaced by more advanced imaging techniques, now reserved for individuals C/I for other modalities
            - **X Ray at different sites** - PA chest, AP and lateral view of C-spine, thoracic spine, lumbar spine, humeri, femora, AP and lateral view of skull (salt and pepper lesions)
            - **Findings** - punched-out lytic lesions (salt and pepper lesion of skull), Diffuse osteopenia, pathological fractures
        - **Cross-Sectional Imaging** - WB PET-CT, WB MRI, WB low-dose CT (now preferred due to increased sensitivity)
    - **Prognostic markers** at diagnosis (ISS staging):
        - Serum beta-2 microglobulin
        - LDH
        - Albumin
- **Diagnostic criteria of multiple myeloma**
    - **Evidence of paraproteinaemia** - serum and/or urinary M-protein (\>= 3g/dL)
    - **Clonal BM plasma cell expansion** - \>= 10% of BM cellularity is diagnostic, or biopsy proven extramedullary plasmacytoma
    - **\>= 1 myeloma defining events**:
        - **Hypercalcaemia** - \> 0.25 mmol above ULN
        - **Renal insufficiency** - SCr \> 177 micromol/L or eGFR \< 40ml/min
        - **Anaemia** (NcNc) - \< 10g/dL or 2g/dL below LLN
        - **Bone lesions** - Osteolytic bone disease demonstrated on radiological assessment (PET-CT, MRI, CT, skeletal survey)
    - **Myeloma-defining biomarkers** (if no evidence of target organ damage):
        - BM plasma cell \> 60% BM cellularity
        - FLC ratio \>= 100 w/ involved free light chain \>= 100 mg/L
        - One or more focal lesions (\>= 5mm) on MRI
- **Staging** (R-ISS)
    - ISS staging - integrates laboratory findings (beta-2 microglobulin and albumin) only
    - R-ISS staging - integrates laboratory (with addition of LDH), cytogenetic findings
        - Stage I (5yOS 82%) - ISS stage I, normal LDH and standard risk mutations
        - Stage II (5yOS 62%) - Not R-ISS stage I or III
        - Stage III (5yOS 40%) - ISS stage 3 and either high LDH or high-risk mutations on FISH
