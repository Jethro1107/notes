# Benign Ovarian Cysts

- **Definition** - benign tumours of the ovary, manifesting as acute pelvic pain, chronic pelvic pain (CPP) or presence of a pelvic mass
- **DDx of pelvic mass:**
    - **Gynaecological disorders:**
        - <u>Ovarian disorder</u> - benign ovarian cyst, complicated ovarian cyst, para-ovarian cyst, ovarian malignancy
        - <u>Tubal disorder</u> - ectopic pregnancy, hydrosalpinx, pyosalpinx, tubo-ovarian abscess, tubal malignancy
        - <u>Uterine disorders</u> - fibroids, uterine malignancy
        - <u>Pregnancy</u>
    - **Non-gynaeclological disorders:**
        - <u>GI disorders</u> - Intestinal obstruction, diverticular/appendicular abscess, intussusception, malignancy (esp. caecal carcinoma)
        - <u>Urological disorder</u> - hydronephrosis, renal/ bladder malignancy, pelvic kidney
        - <u>Others</u> - pelvic lymphocele, peritoneal cyst, lymphoma, neuroblastoma, aortic aneurysm, psoas muscle abscess
- **Classification of ovarian cysts:** 
![](img/types_of_benign_ovarian_cyst.png)
    - **Functional ovarian cysts** - follicular, corpus luteal and theca luteal cyst that appear as <u>simple unilocular cyst on USG</u>, where aetiology is unknown (reduced incidence w/ COCP):
        - <u>Folicular cysts</u> - arises from follicles growing to \> 3cm but rarely exceed 10cm (normal follicles are 2.5 cm)
        - <u>Corpus luteal cyst</u> - arises from the corpus luteum, and can complicate with ruputure or haemorrhage late in the menstrual cycle
        - <u>Theca luteal cyst</u> - functional ovarian cysts a/w pregnancy, particularly multiple pregnancy, but resolve spontaneously during pregnancy

    
    ![](img/ten_teachers_simple_ovarian_cyst.png)
    - **Inflammatory ovarian cysts** - ovarian cysts a/w pelvic inflammatory disease or endometriosis, and are common in young women:
        - <u>Tubo-ovarian abscess</u> - inflammatory mass with tubal, ovarian and bowel involvement, typically arising from PID, but ocassionally a/w other infective causes (appendicitis, diverticulitis)
        - <u>Endometrioma</u> - chocolate cysts reflecting abnormal deposition of endometrial tissue, having characteristic '**ground-glass**' appearance on USG
    - **Germ cell tumours** - most common ovarian tumours in F 20-40y (50%), particularly dermoid tumours (cystic teratoma) is the most common form
    - **Epithelial tumours** - benign neoplasms arising from epithelial cells and a/w increasing frequency in age and are most common in peri-menopausal women:
        - <u>Serous cystadenomas</u> (20-30% in \< 40y) - typically unilocular and unilateral
        - <u>Mucinous cystadenomas</u> - large multiloculated cysts, and bilateral in 10% of cases
    - **Sex cord stromal tumour** - solid ovarian tumours composed of stromal cells presenting in older women:
        - <u>Ovarian fibroma</u> - most common solid ovarian tumour, typically presenting w/ torsion (due to heaviness of ovary) or ocassionally Meig syndrome (pleural effusion, ascites, ovarian fibroma)
        - <u>Thecoma</u> - benign estrogen-secreting tumour presenting after menopause, resulting in post-menopausal bleeding (capable of inducing endometrial carcinoma)
- **Clinical features of ovarian cyst:**
    - <u>Chronic pelvic pain</u> - intermittent, chronic poorly-localised dull pain felt over the pelvis, which may be lateralised
    - <u>Pelvic mass</u> - patient detects pelvic mass caused by large ovarian cyst
    - <u>Urinary Sx</u> - caused by direct pressure on the bladder
    - <u>Bowel Sx</u> - caused by pressure on the bowel
- **Signs of ovarian cyst:**
    - <u>Abdominal examination</u> - palpable abdominal mass if large
    - <u>Pelvic examination</u> - adnexal mass on bimanual examination which may be tender
- **Complications of ovarian cyst:**
    - Torsion
    - Rupture
    - Haemorrhage
