# Acute Vestibular Failure

- **Definition** - an idiopathic acute onset of vertigo w/ vomiting and unsteadiness
- **Causes of acute vestibulopathy** (acute vestibular failure) - may be idiopathic
	- _Vestibular neuritis_ - vestibular neuronitis or acute neuronitis
	- _Stroke_ - ischaemic stroke in AICA or PICA teritory
- **Vestibular neuritis** - covers both vestibular neuronitis and acute labryrinthitis where both are infectious conditions causing a prolonged attack of vertigo:
	- _Vestibular neuritis_ - infection of the vestibular nerve resulting in classical triad of 1) acute prolonged vertigo, 2) nausea, and vomiting
	- _Acute labyrinthitis_ - infection of the inner ear labyrinth resulting in similar vertigo Sx, along w/ aura Sx, including unilateral SNHL +/- tinnitus

- **Clinical features of vestibular neuritis** - mainly occurs in young adults and middle age:
	- _Prior URTI_ - usually preceded by "ILI"
	- _Vertigo_ - often a single attack of vertigo, generally lasting days to weeks, w/ abrupt onset of associated N/V, seating, and ataxia/ unsteadiness
	- _Aural Sx_ - SNHL and deafness may occur w/ acute labyrinthitis
- **Natural Hx** - self-limiting diseases:
	- Resolves over 5-7 days ocurring weeks if acute labyrinthitis
	- During recovery, sudden head movements may bring about transient vertigo
- **Signs** - HINTS examination to r/o central causes (e.g. stroke):
	- _Nystiagmus_ - lateral or unilateral horizontal nystagmus w/ fast-phase moving away from side of lesion
	- _Caloric tests_ - confirms ipsilateral vestibular dysfunction
	- _Hearing tests_ - SNHL of ipsilateral ear (acute labyrinthitis):
		- Rinne +ve - AC >BC in affected ear
		- Weber test - louder in unaffected ear
- **Dx** - diagnosis by exclusion

- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Reassurance and education_ - educate regarding pathophysiology, natural Hxa, and unlikeliness of sinister pathology
		- _Advice_ - advice regarding bed rest and methods for symptomatic relief
		- _Pharmacological Tx_ - anti-vertigo medications +/- corticosteroids
		- _Referral_ - **vestibular rehabilitation** for patients who fail tor eecover w/ residual sense of disequilibrium
	- **General advices:**
		- Rest in bed, lying very still
		- Avoid driving or moving in general
		- Gaze in direction that eases Sx
	- **Anti-vertigo medications** (vestibular sedatives) - commonly a combination of promethazine and an antiematic is used:
		- _Anti-emetics_ - Prochloperazine (Stemetil) 12.5 mg IM or 5-10 mg PO tds
		- _Antihistamine_ - promethazine 10-25 mg IM followed by 10-25 mg PO x 2 days; betahistine 4-16 mg q8h, cinnarizine (also CCB), betahistine
		- _Benzodiazepine_ - diazepam 5 mg PO tds x 2-3 days
