# Sarcopenia

- **Definition** - a syndrome characterised by progressive and generalised loss of skeletal muscle mass and strength w/ a risk of adverse outcomes such as physical disability, poor quality of lfe, and high mortality
- **Etiology of sarcopenia:**
    - <u>Primary sarcopenia</u> - aging
    - <u>Secondary sarcopenia</u>:
        - Inactivity (bed rest, sedentary lifestyle, deconditioning, zero-gravity)
        - Malnutrition
        - Advanced organ failure
- **Pathophysiology of sarcopenia:**
    - <u>Aging</u>:
        - Progressive loss of muscle mass at 1%/y after 30y, and accelerated at 1.5%/y after 70y
        - Progressive loss of muscle strength at 1-1.5%/y from 50-60y, and increased to 3%/y thereafter
    - <u>Additional pathological changes</u>:
        - Reduced Type II fibre w/ transition to type I fibres - reduced fast twitch fibres results in difficulty in initiating explosive actions and in swallowing
        - Intramuscular and intermusclar fat infiltration
- **Diagnostic criteria for sarcopenia** - Asian working group for sarcopenia defines sarcopenia as:
    - <u>Low-muscle mass</u> - DXA \< 7 kg/m2 in M and \< 5.4 kg/m2 in F; Bioimpedence \< 7 kg/m2 in M and \< 5.7 kg/m2 in F
    - <u>Low muscle strength</u> - handgrip strength \< 28kg for M and \< 18 kg/F
    - <u>Low physical performance</u> - defined by battery of screening tests:
        - 6MWT - \< 1m/s
        - Short Physical Performance Battery score \<= 9
        - Chair Stand test \>= 12s
- **Screening tools for sarcopenia** - simple, easily administered tests adopted to identify patients for further testing (e.g. DXA, Bioimpedence):
    - **SARC-F** - screening tool w/ high Sp and low Sn: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/sarc_f_screen.png)
    - **Grip test** (Take 6 test) - test grip strength 3 times on each hand, and take highest value:
        - <u>Cut-off for M</u> - \>= 28kg
        - <u>Cut-off for F</u> - \>= 18kg
    - **Chair stand test** - stand and sit from chair for 5 times:
        - <u>Normal value</u> - \< 12s
        - <u>Sarcopenia probale</u> - \>= 12s
    - **Calf-circumference:**
        - <u>Cut-off for M</u> - \>= 34cm
        - <u>Cut-off for F</u> - \>= 33cm
- **Approach to screening for sarcopenia** - universal screening is not evidence based:
    - <u>Indications for case-finding</u>:
        - Falls, slowness or weakness
        - Decreased walking mobility and walking speed
        - Unintentional weight loss
        - Decreased ADL
        - Self-reported musce wasting
        - Admission to hospital/ RCHEs
    - <u>Simple algorithm for diagnosis in clinical practice</u>: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/algorithm_for_diagnosis_of_sarcopenia.png)
- **Comorbidities associated w/ sarcopenia** - significant overlap w/ frailty:
    - <u>Sarcopenic obesity</u> - copresence of sarcopenia and obesity w/ excessive mass in the presence of reduced muscle mass
    - <u>Sarcopenic dysphagia</u> - impaired swallowing of the elderly because of aging (i.e. presbyphagia), due to progressive loss of type II fibres that initiate swallowing
    - <u>Osteosarcopenia</u> - co-existence of osteoporosis and sarcopenia, w/ higher chance of falls and fracture than either conditions alone

  
  ![](~/Documents/notes/img/overlap_of_sarcopenia_and_frailty.png)
- **Mx of sarcopenia:**
    - <u>Resistance exercise</u> - most evidence in improving muscle strength, muscle mass and balance
    - <u>Protein</u> - ESCEO recommends intake of 1-1.2g/kg BW of protein per day
    - <u>Vitamin D and calcium</u> - 800 IU/d of vitamin D, and 1000 mg/d of Ca to prevent osteosarcopenia
    - <u>Selective androgen receptor modulators</u> - SARMs are in clinical development w/ hope of agonistic activity against skeletal muscles resulting in gain in muscle mass and strength
