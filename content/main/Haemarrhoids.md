# Haemarrhoids

- **Definition** - Haemarrhoids are physiological vascular cushions that aids in continence and protects sphincter from trauma during defaecation (pathalogical only if engorged or prolapsed)
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Prevalence - 4.4% US population seen by physician for symptomatic haemorrhoid
    - Burden - 49/100,000/y haemorrhoidectomy performed in US
- **Etio-pathophysiology of haemarrhoids** - some role of +ve FHx:
    - <u>Engorgement +/- prolapse of anal haemorrhoidal cushions</u> - related to increased intraluminal pressure from 1) constipation, 2) straining, 3) low-fibre diet, 4) Pregnancy
    - <u>Degeneration of tissue supporting hamarrhoids and venous congestion</u> - predisposes to bleeding, thrombosis, erosion and/or prolapse
- **Classification by haemorrhoids** - anatomical relations w/ the dentate line:
    - <u>External haemorrhoids</u> - distal to dentate line, lined by <u>squams</u> and consists of <u>nerve endings</u>
    - <u>Internal haemorrhoids</u> - proximal to dentate line, lined by <u>intestinal columnar epithelium</u> with <u>no nerve endings</u>
- **Severity of internal haemorrhoids:**
    - <u>1st-degree haemarrhoids</u> - haemarrhoids do not prolapse through the anal canal
    - <u>2nd-degree haemarrhoids</u> - haemarrhoids prolapse through the anal canal but is **reduced spontaneously**
    - <u>3rd-degree haemarrhoids</u> - haemarrhoids prolapse and requires **manual reduction**
    - <u>4th-degree haemarrhoids</u> - haemarrhoids that **cannot be reduced**
- **Clinical features of haemarrhoids:**
    - Bleeding - a/w bowel movement
    - Prolapse
    - Mucus discharge
    - Pruritis
    - Pain - only if complicated (e.g. thrombosis, prolapse)
- **Clinical assessment:**
    - <u>DRE</u> - exclude other rectal lesions
    - <u>Protoscope</u> - typically for eary disease (e.g. 1st-2nd degree haemorrhage)
    - <u>Rigid or flexible sigmoidoscopy/ colonoscopy</u> - for more proximal bowel lesions
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - dependent on severity of internal haemarrhoids:
        - <u>1st-2nd degree haemarrhoids</u> - diet modification, medical Tx, +/- office procedures
        - <u>3rd-4th degree haemarrhoids</u> - surgery
    - **Non-operative Tx** - diet modification and medical Tx:
        - <u>Diet modification</u> - high fibre diet for risk factor control
        - <u>Sitz bath</u> - warm, shallow bath offered for Sx relief of **prolapsed haemarrhoids**
        - <u>Ointments and suppositories</u> - may be steroid containing
    - **Office procedures** - banding, sclerotherapy and infra-red coagulation
    - **Surgical Tx** - haemarrhoidectomy or others:
        - **Surgical Haemorrhoidectomy** - surgical excision of the haemarrhoids:
            - <u>Indications</u>:
                - 1\. Severe haemarrhoids (3rd-4th degree internal haemmorrhoids)
                - 2\. Mixed internal and external haemarrhoids
                - 3\. Failure of other Tx
                - 4\. Patient preference
                - 5\. Performed in conjunction of other anorectal procedures
            - <u>Complications</u>:
                - General complications - pain, bleeding, infection
                - Procedure-specific complications - faecal impaction, anal tags, anal stenosis, urinary retention, incontinence
        - **Stapled haemorrhoidopexy**
        - **Transanal haemorrhoidal artery devascularisation**
