# Valvular Heart Disease

- **A diseased valve may be stenosed, or incompetent:**
    - **Stenosis** - lumen becomes narrowed, impeding blood flow
    - **Incompetence** (regurgitation) - lumen incompetent, enabling regurgitation of blood into proximal chamber
- **Pathophysiology:**
    - **Stenosis** - proximal chamber initially hypertrophies, then dilates as it fails
    - **Regurgitation** - chambers on either side dilates
- **Principle etiology of valvular diseases**
    - \
    ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2Fc9WWx5fxs8.png?alt=media&token=b0e3440c-a255-4c15-99ff-e2906e6fad10>)
    - **Valvular stenosis** - congenital, rheumatic heart disease, senile degeneration
    - **Regurgitation** - congenital, rheumatic heart disease (acute and chronic), IE, dilitation (valvular ring dilitation, syphilitic aortitis), traumatic valve rupture, damage to chordae and papillary muscles
- **Clinical features** - variable for different valvular heart diseases
    - S/S of left HF - chronic exertional dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND, low output Sx (e.g. fatigue)
    - S/S of right HF - ankle oedema, hepatic pain, raised JVP etc.
    - Chest pains - due to myocardial ischaemia
    - Palpitations - typically due to AF
    - Complications - cardioembolism, infective endocarditis, pregnancy complications
- **Ix in valvular heart disease:**
    - **CXR** - detection of HF and chamber enlargements
    - **ECG** - detection of chamber enlargements, AF
    - **Echocardiogram** - assessment of valvular architecture, chamber size, chamber functions (e.g. LVEF)
    - **Doppler echo** - measurement of valvular gradients, demonstration of regurgitation jets
    - **Pharmacological stress echo** (e.g. low-dose dobutamine) - detection of ischaemia, and contractile reserve
    - **Cardiac catheterization** - indicated if valvular surgery is to be performed:
        - Assessment of associated CAD, pressure gradient (stenosis), injected contrast (regurgitation)

## Aortic regurgitation

- **Etiology:**
    - **Congenital** - bicuspid valves, disproportionate cusps, Marfan's syndrome
    - **Acquired:**
        - Rheumatic heart disease
        - Seronegative rheumatoid syndrome - Ankylosing spondylitis, Reiter's syndrome, Psoriatic arthritis
        - Infective endocarditis - occurs in normal valves of drug addict, and diseased valves
        - Aortic dilatation - Aortic aneurysm, syphilitic aortitis
        - Traumatic - aortic dissection, ruptured sinus of Valsalva into LV or RV, external trauma
- **Pathophysiology of AR:**
    - **Haemodynamic changes** - increase in LVEDV due to regurgitation volume compensated by LV dilatation and eccentric hypertrophy
        - Increase in stroke volume - manifests as widening of pulse pressure and various eponymous signs (e.g. Quincke's sign, Duroziez's sign, de Musset sign, Waterhammer pulse)
        - LV decompensation due to volume overload - increase in LVEDP, manifesting as HF
- **Clinical presentation of AR:**
    - Mild-to-moderate AR - often asymptomatic, often palpitation is the first presenting symptom prior to HF
    - Severe AR - similar symptom complex as AS, syncope, angina and dyspnoea
- **Signs in AR:**
    - **General inspection** - Marfan's syndrome, AS or other seronegative arthropathies, wide pulse pressure with low diastolic pressure, Corrigan's sign on the neck
    - **Pulses:**
        - Bounding peripheral pulses (character)
        - Collapsing pulse (pulse pressure \> 50 mmHg)
        - Bisferens pulse - severe AR or AR + AS
        - Quinke's sign - flickering capillary pulsations in the nail bed
        - de Musset sign - head nodding with each heart beat
        - Duroziez's sign - systolic and diastolic bruit over femoral artery when partially compressed
    - **Palpation:**
        - **Apex** - Displaced heaving apex beat
        - **Thrills** (rare) - diastolic thrills over left sternal border (exacerbated on expiration and sitting up)
    - **Auscultation:**
        - **Heart sounds** - A2 soft, S3 (volume overload indicates severe AR)
        - \
        ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2F0b2zNXNC30.png?alt=media&token=07d78f8a-064b-448e-8471-d021bf471f99>)
        - **Early diastolic murmur** - Blowing, decrescendo diastolic murmur immediately following S2, heard best at the LLSB and when pt sits up, leans forward, and at maximal expiration
        - **Ejection systolic murmur** - may be of flow murmur, or associated AS (requires presence of peripheral signs)
        - **Austin-flint murmur** - Low-pitched mid-diastolic murmur best heard at apex, not to be mistaken by MS
            - **Mechanistically functional MS** - regurgitation jets impinges on anterior MV leavelets, distinguished by loudness of S1 (loud in MS, soft in AR)
- **Ix in AR:**
    - **Blood** (in acute AR) - VDRL (syphilis), serial blood cultures (IE)
    - **ECG** - LV hypertrophy
    - **CXR** - Cardiomegaly, aortic dilatation, features of HF
    - **Echo** - LV size and function, aortic size, MV apparatus, doppler for severity of regurgitation
    - **Cardiac catheterization** - assessment of regurgitation jets, aortic dilitation and presence of coexisting CAD
- **Mx in AR:**
    - **Treatment of underlying cause** - e.g. syphilis, IE
    - **Conservative Mx** - indicated for asymptomatic patients w/ chronic AR
        - **Serial evaluation** - Assessment of LV size at least q1y by echocardiogram, and advise patients to immediately report development of Sx
        - **BP control** - Typically treated with vasodilating drugs, e.g. DHP-CCB (nifedipine), ACEi
    - **Surgical Tx** - AVR typically performed in combination of aortic root replacement and CABG
        - **Indications** - 1) Symptomatic severe AR, 2) LV dysfunction (e.g. LVEF \< 50%, LVESD \> 50mm or \> 25 mm/m2 BSA), 3) caused by aortic dilatation, 4) concomitant open heart surgery

## Tricuspid regurgitation

- **Etiology:**
    - Functional - RHF, pHTN, RV infarction
    - Rheumatic - rarely occurs in TR, often a/w other mitral valve disease
    - Congenital - Ebstein's anomaly, Tricuspid valve prolapse
    - Infective endocarditis - right-sided endocarditis in IV drug addicts
- **Pathophysiology of TR:**
- **Clinical presentation of TR**:
    - **Non-specific** - S/S of RHF, and low-output failure (e.g. fatigue)
- **Signs of TR:**
    - **Peripheral signs of RHF** - Peripheral oedema, palpable pulsatile liver
    - **JVP** - elevated, giant V wave
    - **Palpation:**
        - Parasternal heave
    - **Auscultation** - usually inaudible due to low-pressure venous system:
        - Pansystolic murmur best heard over LLSB, and increases on inspiration
    - **Abdomen** - large, tender, and pulsatile liver
    - **Review of other systems** (look for signs of generalised oedema) - ascites, pleural effusion
- **Mx of TR:**
    - **Treatment of underlying cause** - e.g. IE
    - **Symptomatic treatment of RV volume overload** - diuretics +/- spironolactone
    - **Surgical Tx** (Valvuloplasty):
        - **Options** - TV repair, TV replacement
        - **Indications** - Concomitant left-sided surgery for functional TR , or if severe, symptomatic, RV dysfunction

# Infective Endocarditis

- **Definition of IE** - A disease caused by microbial infection of endocardial lining of the heart, usually valvular, but can be found in a cardiac chamber, blood vessel, or a congenital abnormality (e.g. septal defect).
- **Pathophysiology of IE:**
    - **Predisposition:**
        - **Endocardial damage** - due to high pressure jets of blood from preexisting cardiac lesions:
            - Valvular heart disease (regurgitation \> stenosis) - MR \> MS, AR \> AS, VSD
            - Shunts - e.g. VSD, PDA, AV fistulas
        - **Exposure to virulent organisms** (affects normal endocardium) - e.g. S. aureus (e.g. IE of tricuspid valve as prototypical complication of IVDU)
        - **Prosthetic heart valves**
    - **Vegetation formation:**
        - **Propensity to occur at sites of endothelial damage** (prothrombotic nature) - deposition of blood clots allows colonisation by blood-borne organisms
            - Endocardium is avascular - allows for proliferation and evasion from host defence mechanisms
        - **Propagation of vegetations** - cause obstruction and emboli, destruction of adjacent tissue and abscess formation
    - **Cardiac manifestation:**
        - **Valvular regurgitation** developed or increased - due to tissue distortion, cusp perforation (aortic) or disruption of chordae (mitral)
    - **Extracardiac manifestations:**
        - **Systemic infections** - due to continuous bacteraemia
        - **Septic embolisation** - MI, Stroke, Ischaemic limbs
        - **Immune complex deposition** - Clubbing, Osler Node, arthritis, tenosynovitis, immune GN
- **Clinical features of IE:**
    - May be acute or subacute, where clinical pattern is influenced by 1) virulence of organism, 2) site of infection, 3) prior ABx, 4) presence of prothesis.
        - Subacute endocarditis - typically manifests with cardiac and extra-cardiac complications
    - Manifestations of systemic infections:
        - Hx - Persistent pyrexia of unknown origin, chills and rigors, Constitutional Sx (night sweats, weight loss), generalised malaise
        - P/E - Fever, pallor, splenomegaly (long standing endocarditis)
        - Ix - NcNc anaemia, leukocytosis, raised ESR/CRP, +ve blood culture
    - Manifestations of valvular involvement:
        - Hx - Symptoms of HF, chest pains
        - P/E - Signs of HF, New evolving murmur (especially regurgitation murmurs)
        - Ix - Echo (vegetations), ECG (AV block in aortic root lesion, or infarction), CXR
    - Manifestations of septic embolisation:
        - Hx - stroke, brain abscess, ischaemic limbs
        - P/E - Skin petechiae (40-50%), Roth spots, Janeway lesions, Splinter haemorrhage
    - Manifestations of immune complex deposition:
        - Hx - arthralgia, myalgia, tenosynovitis, features of GN (proteinuria, haematuria)
        - P/E - arthritis, Osler's node, Finger clubbing
        - Ix - Urinalysis (protein and RBC casts), Ig pattern and SPE (polyclonal), increased RF, decreased Complements

    - 'Varying' murmur (90%) - murmur that is new and changing
    - Non-specific supportive signs - Clubbing (10%), splinter haemorrhages (10%), Splenomegaly (30-40% in long standing IE), Peticheal rash (40-50%) on extremites, mucous membranes or conjunctiva (2-5%)
    - Rare but specific signs:
        - Janeway lesions - non-tender erythematous macules on palms and soles (septic emboli)
        - Osler Nodes - tender nodules on the pulp of fingertips (immune complex mediated vasculitis)
        - Roth spots - exudative, oedematous haemorrhagic lesions of the retina with pale centres
- **Evaluation of IE:**
    - **Clinical suspicion of IE** - pyrexia of unknown origin in individual with compatible risk factors, persistent bacteraemia
    - **Hx and P/E:**
        - Hx - elicit nature and duration of IE, and risk factors for IE
        - P/E - look for varying murmur and signs related to IE
    - **Blood cultures** - prior to initiation of ABx treatment:
        - 3 venous cultures - taken at 3 different venipuncture site separated by at least half hour
    - **Echocardiogram:**
        - Role - Detection of vegetations, valvular damage and abscess formation (-ve echo does not exclude Dx)
        - Modalities:
            - TTE - initial screen for IE (typically detectable at 2-4 mm)
            - TEE - increased sensitivity (1-1.5mm) especially if initial screen -ve, abscess formation initial screen for prosthetic valves
    - **Ancillary Ix:**
        - Initial blood - CBC, ESR/CRP
            - CBC - NcNc anaemia, leukocytosis
            - ESR - non-specifically increased
            - CRP - better monitoring of progress
        - Urinalysis (dipstick) - proteinuria and microscopic haematuria
        - ECG - detection of conduction abnormalities (e.g. AV block in aortic root abscess formation) and MI
        - CXR - evidence of HF

## Microbiology of IE

- **Native valves** - 3/4 cases of IE are caused by either staphylococcus and streptococcus
    - **Staphylococcus aureus** - currently most common cause world wide
        - **Normal site** - skin
        - **Predisposition** - haematogenous access via skin infections, abscesses or vascular access sites (e.g. IV or central lines), or IVDU
        - **Clinical features** - Extremely virulent, causing florid vegetations, fulminant valve destruction and abscess formation
    - **Streptococcus viridans** (e.g. mutans, sanguis) - used to be leading cause of IE
        - **Normal site** - oropharynx (commensals of upper respiratory tract)
        - **Predisposition** - haematogenous access through dental procedures, chewing/ teeth brushing
        - **Clinical features** - typically subacute
    - **Streptococcus bovis** (e.g. pyogenes, agalectiae, bovis, pneumoniae)
        - **Normal site** - GI tract
        - **Predisposition** - large bowel malignancies
        - **Clinical features** - typically subacute, and a/w underlying large bowel malignancies
    - **Enterococci** (e.g. faecalis, faecium)
        - **Normal site** - GI and GU
        - **Predisposition** - GI/ GU manipulation
    - **HAECK group** - Haemophilus aphrophilus, Actinobacillus actinomycetemcomitans, Cardiobacterium hominis, Eikenella corrodens, Kingella kingae
        - **Normal site** - oropharynx
        - **Sensitivity** - penicillin-resistant
        - **Clinical features** - large vegetations that are difficult to culture
- **Prosthetic valves** - dependent on duration since valvular replacement:
    - Early onset (\< 12 mo) - Coagulase -ve staphylococcus (e.g. S. epidermidis, S. lugdenensis)
    - Late onset (\> 12 mo) - similar to native valves
- **Other special populations:**
    - Animal exposure - e.g. Coxiella burnetii (Q fever from exposure of farm animals), Brucella (goats and cattles), Bartonella henselae (cats)
    - Other rarer etiology - Mycoplasma, Chamydia, Legionella
- **Causes of culture-negative endocarditis**
    - Inadequate technique
    - **Prior antibiotics**
    - Fastidious organism - Mycoplasma, HACEK group, Abiotrophia, Bartonella
    - Fungal
    - Non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis (e.g. Libman-Sacks endocarditis in SLE)

## Diagnostic Criteria of IE

- **Modified Duke's Criteria** - IE is established if either pathological criteria or clinical criteria is met (mostly dependent on clinical criteria)
    - **Pathological criteria** (definitive IE):
        - Microbiological criteria (blood culture not part of pathological criteria) - micro-organisms identified in the context of clinical signs of active endocarditis
        - Histopathological criteria - histopathological findings of active endocarditis
    - **Clinical criteria** - Definite IE (2 major, 1 major and 3 minor, or 5 minor), Possible IE (1 major and 1 minor, or 3 minor)
- **Major criteria:**
    - +ve blood culture to demonstrate persistent bacteraemia
        - Persistently positive blood cultures
            - For organisms that are typical causes of IE - At least two positive blood cultures from blood samples drawn \>12 hours apart
            - For organisms that are more commonly skin contaminants - Three or a majority of ≥4 separate blood cultures (with first and last drawn at least one hour apart)
            - Single positive blood culture for \_<sub>Coxiella</sub> burnetii\_\_ or phase I IgG antibody titer \>1:800
    - Endocardial involvements:
        - Positive echo evidence of vegetations, abscess formations, dishecence of prosthetic valves, and new valvular regurgitations
- **Minor criteria:**
    - Predisposition - Shunts, VHD, IVDU
    - Pyrexia \>= 38 degrees
    - Embolic phenomenon - major arterial emboli, pulmonary infarct, mycotic aneurysm, conjunctival haemorrhage, Janeway lesions
    - Immunological phenomenon - GN, Osler nodes, Roth's spots
    - Blood cultures suggestive of IE but not meeting major criteria
    - Suggestive echocardiographic findings

## IE Antibiotic Prophylaxis

- Biological rationale of antibiotic prophylaxis - invasive procedures (dental, GI, GU) may introduce transient bacteraemia, which can theoretically predispose to IE
- Current guidelines for IE - only for those with at-risk procedures in at-risk individuals
    - At risk individuals:
        - Prosthetic valve or prosthetic material for valvular repair
        - Previous IE
        - Congenital heart disease - unrepaired cyanotic CHD, repaired with residual defects, complete repaired after 1st 6 mo post-op
        - Cardiac transplantation recipients who develop valvuopathy
    - At risk procedures:
        - Dental procedures that manipulate gingival tissues, perforate oral mucosa or manipulate periapical regions of teeth - recommended for most individuals
        - Respiratory tract, skin, or musculoskeletal procedures
        - GI/GU procedures - not recommended routinely except for at-risk individuals
- Rationale for current guidelines:
    - Low-grade bacteraemia occurs in routine activities of daily living
    - Risk of IE following dental procedures is very low
    - Risks of indiscriminate ABx use outweighs the benefits
    - Maintenance of optimal oral hygiene is more important than antibiotic prophylaxis.
