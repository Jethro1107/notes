# Renal Cell Carcinoma

- **Definition** - malignancy arising from the tubular cells of the renal cortex accounting for 80-85% of primary renal tumours
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - 400,000 new cases worldwide/y (16/100000/y)
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - peak incidence in 6-8th decade (uncommon in \< 40y unless hereditary)
        - Sex - male preponderance (M:F 2:1)
    - <u>Mortality</u> - good overall prognosis (5y survival 75% if resectable)
- **Risk factors for RCC:**
    - **Non-modifiable risk factors:**
        - <u>Genetic predisposition</u> - genetic predisposition to RCC is more common in early-onset RCC, bilateral, or multi-focal disease:
            - **Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease** (ADPKD) - often bilateral at presentation (12% vs 4%), multicentric (28% vs 8%) and harbour sarcomatoid features (33% vs 1-5%) compared w/ sporadic RCC
            - **Herdeditary renal cell carcinoma syndromes:** 
            ![](img/hereditary_kidney_cancer_syndrome.png)
        - <u>Cytotoxic chemotherapy</u> - for prior malignancies, autoimmune disorders or bone marrow transplant a/w subsequent development of translocation RCC
        - <u>Dialysis and acquired cystic disease of the kidney</u> - Acquired cystic disease of kidney occurs in 35-50% of patients, where they ar at 30x risk of developing RCC compared to general population due to malignant transformation of renal cysts
    - **Modifiable risk factors:**
        - <u>Smoking</u> - meta-analysis demonstrates all smokers, current smokers and former smokers have higher RR (1.31, 1.36, 1.16 respectively), and also higher risk of locally-advanced or metastatic disease at presentation
        - <u>Hypertension</u> - unknown biological mechanism, but studies demonstrate that it is idependent to use of anti-hypertensive medications and obesity
        - <u>Occupational exposure</u> - to benzene, cadmium and asbestos
        - <u>Obesity</u> - increasing RR for RCC w/ BMI, but in patients w/ newly diagnosed RCC, excess body weight is a/w better prognosis
- **Pathology of RCC** - differentiation of RCC based on histological subtypes:
    - **Clear cell carcinoma** (75-85%) - macroscopically a solid renal neoplasm (ocassionally cystic) arising histologically as clear cells from the PCT
    - **Papillary carcinoma** (10-15%) - arising histologically from the PCT but morphologically and genetically distinct from clear cell carcinomas
    - **Chromophobe carcinoma** (5-10%) - histologically sheets of cells darker than clear cell carcinoma arising from the intercalated cells of the collecting system
    - **Oncocytic carcinoma** (3-7%) - histologically consists a pure population of oncocytes, large well-differentiated neoplastic cells w/ intensely eosinophilic-staining cytoplasm due to mitochondria, arising from the intercalated cell of the collecting duct
    - **Collecting duct carcinoma** (extremely rare)
    - **Molecularly defined RCC** (rare) - rare subgroup of RCCs defined by characteristic molecular features
- **Mode of spread of RCC:**
    - Direct spread - to renal hilum and perinephric tissue
    - Lymphatic spread - to para-aortic LN
    - Haematogenous spread - lung, bone, brains
- **Clinical features of RCC** - increasingly diagnosed through incidental findings radiologically, but can result in a wide-range of clinical features esp. due to paraneoplastic phenomenon:
    - **Asymptomatic** - 'radiologist's tumour', increasing frequency of RCC being diagnosed by workup of incidental finding of suspicious renal mass or renal cysts through imaging for other indications
    - **Constitutional symptoms** - Weight loss, fever, anaemic symptoms
    - **Features of local tumour** - classical triad of haematuria, flank pain, and palpable abdominal kidney mass (9%) often signifies locally advanced disease:
        - <u>Haematuria</u> (60%) - often <u>gross haematuria</u> with <u>blood clots</u> and clot colic, occurs with <u>tumour invasion into the collecting system</u>
        - <u>Flank pain</u> (40%) - typically a constant, draggind discomfort due to distension of the renal capsule, ocassionally a/w colicky pain caused by clot obstructing the PUG
        - <u>Abdominal mass</u> - firm, homogenous, non-tender mass that moves w/ respiration
    - **Features of local invasion:**
        - <u>Scrotal varicocele</u> (11% left-sided RCC) - failure of emptying of (left-sided) varicocele raises suspicion for a primary renal tumour that has obstructed drainage of gonadal vein into the renal vein
        - <u>IVC obstruction</u> - obstruction of IV manifests as bilateral LL oedema, ascites w/ upward draining veins, portal HTN (Budd-Chiari syndrome) and pulmonary embolism
    - **Features of metastastic disease** - most commonly metastasis to the lungs, liver, bones and brain
    - **Paraneoplastic syndrome** - 'internist's tumour', aberrant clinical presentation due to ectopic production of various hormones:
        - Pyrexia of unknown origin (20%) - intermittent, and a/w night sweats, anorexia, and weight loss
        - Anaemia (29-88%) - a disproportionately severe NcNc anaemia with iron profiles compatible that of anaemia of chronic disease (ACD)
        - Polycythaemia (1-5%) - secretion of erythopoietin
        - Hypercalcaemia (15%) - PTHrP production or lytic bone metastasis
        - HTN - renin secretion
        - Hypercortisolism (Cushing's syndrome) - ACTH secretion
        - Hypoglycaemia - secretion of insulin
- **Ix and Dx evaluation** - for patients w/ unexplained haematuria or other Sx, signs or findings suggestive of possible RCC:
    - **Urinalysis** - confirm microscopic haematuria
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, Clotting profile, Ca:
        - <u>CBC</u> - Abnormal Hb (anaemia or polycythaemia) +/- leukocytosis and thrombocytosis (?secondary PV)
        - <u>RFT</u> - baseline renal insufficiency
        - <u>LFT</u> - dLFT if liver metastasis, ALP reflects bone metastasis
        - <u>CaPO4</u> - HyperCa due to PTHrP or lytic bone metastasis
    - **Imaging** - Renal USG, Contrast CT A+P, MRI:
        - **Ultrasound** - typically differentiation between simple cysts and solid tumours
        - **Contrast CT abdomen (renal protocol)** - characteristic findings with high diagnostic accuracy:
            - <u>Size</u> - \> 4cm has high PPV for RCC (although smaller lesions may be RCCs)
            - <u>Description</u> - heterogenous lesion comprising of solid, cystic, necrotic, haemorrhagic areas resulting in variable enhancement, with <u>distortion of renal outlines</u>
            - <u>Enhancement</u> - 10-15 HU hyper-enhancement on post-contrast phase

      
      ![](img/rcc_ct.png)
        - **CT Urogram:**
            - <u>Features</u>:
                - Corticcomedullary phase - variable enhancement usually less than normal cortex
                - Nephrogenic phase - detection of abnormal contrast enhancement
- **Staging system** - AJCC 8th edition TNM staging:
    - **TNM system:** 
    ![](img/tnm_staging_rcc.png)
    - **Implications of prognostic staging** - based on anatomical extent of disease:
        - <u>Early RCC</u> - T1N0M0 (Stage I) and T2N0M0 (Stage II)
        - <u>Locally advanced RCC</u> - T3 disease, N1 disease
        - <u>Metastatic disease</u> - M1 disease
- **Principles of staging and assessment of resectability** - accrual of information that guides Mx:
    - **Patient factor** - patient age and co-morbidities
    - **Tumour factor** - extent of disease:
        - <u>Localised RCC</u> - Stage I, II and III RCC by TNM convention
        - <u>Advanced RCC</u> - Stage IV RCC (including T4 disease w/ invasion beyondd Gerota's fascia or into ipsilateral adrenal gland, or M1 disease)
- **Staging Ix of RCC:**
    - **Contrast CT T+A+P** - for clinical assessment of locoregional involvement (A), and assessment of distant metastasis:
        - <u>Test properties</u> - Extremely accurate for locoregional staging of RCC, although poor Sn for renal v. or perinephric invasion may result in under-staging of RCC:
            - **Test poperties of renal vein invasion** - 78% Sn, 96% Sp
            - **Test properties of nodal metastasis** - 83% Sn, 88% Sp (but largely determined by size criterion for defining suspicious nodes)
            - **Test properties of perinephric invasion** - 46% Sn, 98% Sp
            - **Test properties of adjacent organ invasion** (e.g. adrenals) - 100% Sp
        - <u>Findings</u>:
    - **Bone scan** - reserved for symptomatic bone pain or increased ALP on LFT (FN as RCC usually causes osteolytic lesions rather than osteoblastic lesions)
    - **Gandolinium-based MRI** - for better anatomical information regarding IVC to right atrium involvement
    - **PET-CT** - high Sn and Sp for primary lesion and distant metastasis
    - **Tissue biopsy** - role of tissue biopsy depends on the clinical scenario (no role in Bx of primary tumour due to risk of tumour seeding):
        - <u>Isolated resectable renal mass</u> - upfront partial or complete nephroctomy preferred over pre-operative percutaneous biopsy as it provides more information regarding pathological staging and definitive Tx
        - <u>Metastatic disease</u> - metastatic disease at presentation requires Bx of metastatic site for histological diagnosis prior to initiating therapy
    - **Prognostic factors of RCC:**
        - **Pathological risk factors:**
            - **Anatomical extent of the disease** (i.e. prognostic staging in TNM system): 
            ![](img/stage_based_survival_of_rcc.png)
            - **Histopathological subtype** - controversial
            - **Tumour grade** - 5y survival were 89%, 65% and 46% in grades 1, 2 and 3-4 respectively (independent risk factor)
            - **Histological coagulative tumour necrosis** - independent risk of poor prognosis
        - **Clinical risk factors** - performance status, symptomatology, obesity (see correlation in RF)
        - **Molecular markers** - some markers show promise as prognotstic markers for clear cell RCC
    - **UCLA integrated staging system** (UISS) - validated prognostic model that predicts 5y survival and disease recurrence risk guiding use of adjuvant therapy: 
    ![](img/ucla_integrated_staging_system_for_rcc_reprognosis.png)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - **Factors influencing Mx** - patient and tumour factors:
            - <u>Patient age and comorbidities</u>:
                - Non-surgical candidates w/ localised disease can be managed by ablative techniques rather than surgery, or even active surveillance
                - Selected patients w/ advanced disease may be offered radical nephrectomy including adrenalectomy, cytoreductive nephrectomy, and mastasectomy based on certain indications
            - <u>Tumour size</u> - small renal masses (e.g. \< 3cm) may undergo active surveillance esp. in non-surgical candidates as they are slow growing, have low risk of metastasis, and are non-symptomatic
            - <u>Multicentricity</u> - bilateral localised RCCs should be managed in view of sparing as much renal parenchyma as possible, and monitoring for metachronous RCC
        - **Stage-dependent Tx:**
            - <u>Localised RCC</u> - surgical resection (partial vs radical nephrectomy) vs ablative procedures +/- adjuvant therapy
            - <u>Advanced renal cell carcinoma</u> - different Tx approach dependent on pathological reults on Bx of a distant site (multimodal therapy w/ systemic immunotherapy as backbone):
                - **Clear cell RCC** - initiation of systemic immunotherapy +/- targeted therapy depends on IMDC risk stratification and disease burden
                - **Non-clear cell RCC** - varied and tailored to histological subtype (no high quality data guiding Mx due to infrequency of these tumours)
                - **RCC w/ sarcomatoid features** - clinically responsive to immunotherapy

    
    ![](img/initial_evaluation_and_mx_of_suspected_rcc.png)
    - **Role of active surveillance** - active surveillance of small renal mass suspicious of RCC up to \< 4cm for non-surgical patients w/ comorbidities
    - **Surgical Mx RCC:**
        - **Overview of surgical approaches:**
            - **Approach** - open vs minimally invasive
            - **Radical vs partial nephrectomy:**
                - <u>Radical nephrectomy</u> - complete resection of the kidney, Gerota fascia +/- adrenal gland for T4b disease and LN
                - <u>Partial nephrectomy</u> - resection of renal masses w/ clear margins
        - **Evidence for oncological outcomes between radical vs partial nephrectomy:**
        - **Factors for selection between radical vs partial nephrectomy** - based on 1) disease extent, and 2) risk of renal dysfunction:
            - **Disease extent:**
                - <u>Tumour size</u> - Tumour \> 10cm is indicated for radical nephrectomy, tumour \<= 10cm may contemplate partial nephrectomy
                - <u>Tumour location</u> - centrally located RCC is indicated for radical nephrectomy
                - <u>Tumours w/ high oncological potential</u> - e.g. node +ve disease, renal vein or IVC involvement, or T4 disease:
                    - Nodal involvements - suspected node +ve disease are treated w/ radical nephrectomy and lymphadenectomy
                    - Venous extension - renal vein or IVC extension must be managed w/ radical nephrectomy and cavoatrial management
                    - Extension to ipsilateral adrenal - ipsilateral adrenalectomy and radical nephrectomy is preferred
            - **Comorbidities affecting renal function in the future** - localised disease w/ high risk of CKD from radical resectomy should opt for nephron-preserving techniques:
                - <u>Solitary kidney</u> - absolute indication for partial nephrectomy
                - <u>Multiple or bilateral tumours</u> - absolute indication for partial nephrectomy
                - <u>Baseline renal dysfunction</u> - relative indication for partial nephrectomy if technically feasible
        - **Radical nephrectomy:**
            - **Techniques:**
                - <u>Radical resection of the kidney</u> - Ligation of renal vasculature and en-bloc removal of the entire kidney, Gerota fascia and adrenal glands if ipsilateral adrenal involvement
                - <u>LN management</u> - dependent on LN involvement:
                    - Extended lymphadenectomy - for node +ve disease clinically, or increased risk of nodal involvement (e.g. grade 3-4 tumour, T3-4 tumour, sarcomatoid histology, presence of coagulative necrosis)
                    - Limited lymphadenectomy - for clinically node -ve disease with no features of increased risk of nodal metastasis
                - <u>Cavoatrial tumor involvement</u> - thrombectomy
            - **Outcomes** - 5y survival 80-90% for localised disease
            - **Complications** - renal dysfunction in the future (\> 30%)
        - **Partial nephrectomy** - performed whenever technically feasible as <u>similar oncological outcomes</u> w/ significantly <u>lower risk of renal dysfunction</u> in selected patients:
            - **Indications for partial nephrectomy** - balance between oncological outcomes and risk of CKD development:
                - <u>Absolute indications</u> - 1) solitary kidney, 2) bilateral RCC
                - <u>Relative indications</u> - 1) Multicentric unilateral tumours, 2) baseline renal dysfunction
        - **Metastasectomy** - resection of solitary metastasis can be worthwhile as can remain single for long periods
    - **Adjuvant therapy** - pembrolizumab as gold standard:
        - **Indications** - based on risk of recurrence:
            - **Histopathological criteria** - adjuvant pembrolizumab inducated:
                - <u>Intermediate-high risk</u> - p2 tumours w/ grade 4 or sarcomatoid features; pT3 disease irresepective of grade
                - <u>High risk</u> - pT4 disease irrespective of grade; pN1 disease
                - <u>Metastatic</u> - resection of oligometastatic sites w/ no evidence of disease within one year after metastasis
            - **Prognostic modelling** - UCLA integrated staging system (see above)
        - **Regimen** - Pembrolizumab 200 mg q3w for up to 1y (initiated within 12 weeks of nephrectomy)
        - **Outcomes** - improved DFS and OS (KEYNOTE-464 phase III double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT)
    - **Ablation** - indicated for localised diseases w/ high surgical risks:
        - Radiofrequency ablation
        - Cryotherapy
    - **Chemotherapy or immunotherapy** - indicated for <u>metastatic diseases</u> (RCC is chemo-resistant):
        - <u>Cytokine therapy</u> - INF, IL-2
        - <u>Tyroskine kinase inhibitor</u> - sunitinib, pazopanib, sorafenib, axitinib
        - <u>mTOR inhibhitors</u> - temsirolimus, everolimus
        - <u>PD-1 inhibitors</u> - Pembrolizumab, nivolumab, ipilimumab
