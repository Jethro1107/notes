# Morbid Disorders of Emotional Expression

- **Definition** - emotional expressions that are very different from the average normal reaction (cultural and social norm), but the patient is unaware of the morbidity in emotional expression even though it is apparent to observers
- **Phenomenologically, morbid disorders of emotional expressions may be:**
    - <u>Inadequacy of affect</u> - different degree of loss of emotional life which may manifest as social awkwardness and inappropriateness
    - <u>Incongruency of affect</u> - misdirection of emotions which is incongruent with the emotional relevance of the event
    - <u>Lability of affect</u> - rapid cycling in emotion largely unrelated to external stimuli
- **Inadequacy of affect** (Parathymia by Bleuler) - varying degree of limited range and intensity of emotional expression:
    - <u>Blunting of affect</u> - reduced range and intensity of emotional life, and ocassionally manifests itself as insensitivity to the subtleties of social intercourse (e.g. social inappropriateness, disregarding other's emotion)
    - <u>Flattening of affect</u> - emotional constriction as evident to limitations of usual range of emotion such that individual rarely displays emotion in any direction (but is often in the appropriate direction if displayed)
- **Incongruency of affect** - misdirection of emotion:
    - Some attribute incongruency of affect as a congruence response to the delusional systems and active psychosis (i.e. incongruent in our reality but congruent in their's)
    - Not to be mistakened for - abnormal 'expressions of emotion' that fills social roles:
        - The embarrassed smile when recounting an embarassing topic
        - The smiling depressive, as smiles not only are emotional expressions, but can also be communicatory
    - Stiffening of affect where affect is initially congruent but does not adapt as situation changes
- **Lability of affect** - rapid and abrupt changes in emotion largely unrelated to external stimuli:
    - <u>Emotional lability</u> - difficulty in controlling effect, which may be normal (some people are easily brought to tears), seen in mixed affective states and mania, borderline personality disorders, or in depressives (e.g. transient feeling of unhappiness may cause them to cry and are often made worse by sympathy)
    - <u>Affective incontinence</u> - total loss of control of expression of emotion that is often disconcordant with the subjective feeling (e.g. 'forced laughing' or 'forced weeping' in organic brain disease \[cerebral atherosclerosis, multiple sclerosis\])
