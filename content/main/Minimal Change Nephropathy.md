# Minimal Change Nephropathy

- **Definition** - glomerular injury primarily affecting podocytes ('podocytopathies') that is characterised by normal morphology on light microscopy, being the most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in children
- **Epidemiology** - age dependent:
    - <u>Children</u> - accounts for \> 90% of nephrotic syndrome in children
    - <u>Adults</u> - accounts for ~ 10% of nephrotic syndrome in adults
- **Pathophysiology of MCD** - unclear but accumulating evidence suggests it is a systemic T cell dysfunction resulting in <u>production of glomerular permeability factors</u>
- **Etiology of MCD:**
    - **Primary MCD** - MCD not clearly associated w/ an underlying disease or event, thought to be related to production of circulating glomerular permeability factor that directly targets podocytes
    - **Secondary MCD** - drugs, infections, neoplasms, allergy:
        - <u>Drugs</u> - NSAIDs (most common cause), beta-lactams, Sulfasalazine, pamidronate, lithium, ICI
        - <u>Malignancies</u> - particularly a/w Hodgkin lymphoma (immune dysfunction)
        - <u>Infections</u> - syphilis, TB, Mycoplasma, HCV described
        - <u>Allergy</u> - Hx of allergy described in up to 30% of cases of MCD
- **Clinical features of MCD** - usually preceded by viral infection (URTI or systemic) and is more <u>acute in nature</u> (1-2 weeks):
    - **Nephrotic syndrome** - onset over days to weeks of the S/S of nephrotic syndrome acutely (cf MN and FSGS which is more indolent), characterised often by marked proteinuria (up to 15-20 g/d)
    - **Other renal manifestations** - observed in some patients w/ MCD:
        - <u>Microscopic haematuria</u> (29%) - more common in adults and occurs in 20-25% of children, but is not a/w a difference in clinical course
        - <u>Mild renal impairments and HTN</u> - modest elevation of SCr seen in both adults and children
        - <u>Acute kidney injury</u> (18%) - shown to be more likely with more severe proteinuria possibly because of alterations in glomerular dynamics through affecting Bowman capsule oncotic pressures
- **Histopathological findings** - renal Bx often pursued in adults, but a presumed Dx can be made in children:
    - <u>LM</u> - normal glomeruli
    - <u>IMF</u> - no Ig or complement deposits
    - <u>EM</u> - characteristic findings of diffuse (\> 80%) podocyte foot process effacement
- **MCD and primary FSGS** - debate persists to whether consider MCD and primary FSGS as falling <u>within the spectrum of the same disease</u> or are <u>distinct pathological entities</u>:
    - <u>Rationale for considering them as the same disease</u>:
        - Both disease entities are podocytopathies, defined by diffuse podocyte foot process effacement
        - Selection bias on renal Bx may result in misdiagnosis of MCD as sclerotic lesions in FSGS is focal
        - There are case reports where classical presentation of MCD is initially susceptible to steroids, but becomes refractory, where re-biopsy reveals sclerotic lesions
    - <u>Reasons why they are likely distinct pathological entities</u>:
        - **Different tempo of disease** - MCD typically presents more <u>abruptly</u> and invariably with nephrotic syndrome, while patients w/ primary FSGS may present <u>more indolently</u> w/ isolated subnephrotic proteinuria or nephrotic syndrome
        - **Different treatment responsiveness** - MCD always responds to immunosuppression, while responsiveness of FSGS to immunosuppression is seen in \< 50% of patients
        - **Different prognosis** - progression to ESRD is exceedingly rare in MCD, but quite common in FSGS
- **Minimal change variants:**
    - Idiopathic mesangial proliferative glomerulonephritis
    - IgM nephropathy
    - C1q nephropathy
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - **Aims of Mx** - <u>Prevent complications</u> associated w/ nephrotoxic syndrome (i.e. general measures for nephrotic syndrome)
        - **Approach to Mx:**
            - <u>General measures</u> - general Mx of nephrotic syndrome (e.g. RAAS blockade, statins, SGLT2i, salt and protein restriction +/- diuretics)
            - <u>High-dose glucocorticoid therapy</u> - for most adults w/ primary MCD
    - **High-dose steroids** - capable of inducing complete remission in 80-95% of patients:
        - <u>Indications</u>:
            - Children presenting w/ nephrotic syndrome can undergo a trial of high-dose steroids w/o renal Bx
            - Adults presenting w/ nephrotic syndrome w/ pathological confirmation of MCD
        - <u>Dosing and Duration of regimen</u> - 1 g/kg/d (60-80 mg/d) for at least 4 weeks, and subsequent tapering depending on response
- **Prognosis** - <u>does not progress to CKD</u>, but presents w/ <u>complications related to nephrotic syndrome</u> or its Tx
