# Shock

- **Definition** - generalised hypoperfusion of tissue, defined by a level of oxygen delivery that fails to meet the metabolic requirements of the tissue (not synonymous with hypotension)
- **Defining features of shock** - enables early identification of shock:
    - Increasing base deficit - ?HAGMA
    - Increased blood lactate
    - Reduced urine output or oliguria
- **Etiology of shock** - classified into low-output shock and high-output shock:
    - **Low stroke volume:**
        - <u>Hypovolaemic shock</u> - any condition provoking major shrinkage of intravascular volume resulting reduction in preload (e.g. haemorrhage, severe burns, salt and water depletion)
        - <u>Cardiogenic shock</u> - severe cardiac impairment (e.g. myocardial infarction, acute MR, SAH resulting in catecholamine-mediated myocardial stunning)
        - <u>Obstructive shock</u> - obstruction of blood flow around circulation (e.g. massive PE, tension pneumothorax, cardiac tamponade)
    - **High stroke volume** - usually caused by vasodilatation:
        - <u>Anaphylactic shock</u> - inappropriate vasodilatation triggered by an allergen results in tissue oedema, depleted intravascular volume, and hypoperfusion
        - <u>Sepsis/ SIRS</u> - systemic inflammatory response resulting in widespread endothelial damage, with vasodilatation, AV shuntng, microvascular occlusion etc.
        - <u>Neurogenic shock</u> - disrupted brainstem and neurogenic vasomotor control resulting in inappropriate bradycardia due to combination of loss of noradrenaline-mediated vasoconstriction and adrenaline-mediated chronotropy
- **Clinical features of shock** - dependent of primary pathophysiology:
    - **Features of low-output shock** - reflects reduced CO and physiological compensation:
        - <u>Features of low-stroke volume</u> - cold peripheries, weak or absent peripheral pulses, weak central pulses
        - <u>Features of physiological compensation</u>:
            - Reflex tachycardia
            - Narrow pulse pressure - i.e. increased diastolic pressure and reduced systolic pressure (e.g. 105/95 mmHg)
    - **Features of high-output shock:**
        - <u>Features of increased stroke volume</u> (due to reduced afterload) - warm peripheries, bounding pulses
        - <u>Features of physiological compensation</u>:
            - Widden pulse pressure
        - <u>Delayed features of distributive shock</u> - results in hypovolaemia due to third spacing, and myocardial depression:
            - Cool peripheries
            - Weak pulses
- **Mx:**
    - **Principle of Mx:**
        - Restore global DO2 - correct hypoxaemia, optimise fluid status, optimise Hb, ensure adequate CO
        - Maintain MAP to ensure adequate perfusion of vital organs - target pressure is patient specific depending on pre-morbid factors (60-90 mmHg)
        - Establish monitoring - invasive measures (e.g. CVP measurements), and document trends in haemodynamics, ABG, H+, base deficits, and lactate
        - Treatment of underlying cause - e.g. cardiac tamponade, pulmonary embolism, sepsis

    
    ![](img/principles_of_management_of_circulatory_collapse_davidson.png)
    - **Optimisation of cardiac functions:**
        - **Optimisation of preload** - fluid resuscitation and fluid challenges:
            - **Fluid challenges** - guided by CVP:
                - <u>CVP \< 6mmHg</u> - 250 mL NS or colloid
                - <u>CVP \> 6mmHg or poor ventricular function suspected</u> - 100 mL bolus to avoid precipitating APO
        - **Optimisation of afterload:**
            - **Pharmacological Mx** - arteriole dilators:
                - <u>Selection</u> - nitrates
                - <u>Limitations</u> - can reduce afterload but at a cost of fall of MAP
            - **Mechanical circulatory support** - e.g. intra-aortic balloon pump:
                - <u>Indications</u> - cardiogenic shock with particular value in treating myocardial ischaemia
                - <u>Rationale</u> - counterpulsation enables 1) reduction of LV afterload, while 2) increase in CO, diastolic pressure, and hence coronary perfusion
        - **Optimisation of myocardial contractility** - vasopressors and inotropes can be applied: 
        ![](img/common_vasoactive_agents.png)
    - **Control of rate and rhythm** - target HR between 90-110 bpm, treat reversible causes of arrhythmias, and restore sinus rhythms:
        - **Treatment of reversible causes of arrhythmias** - e.g. hyper-/ hypo-kalaemia, hyper-/ hypo-magnesemia
        - **Treatment of AF in the critically ill** - IV amiodarone (300mg over 30-60 min, 900 mg over 24h) to control ventricular rate and rhythm control
- **Prognosis** - dependent on presence of significant organ failure:
    - <u>Early shock</u> (precipitating cause reversed before organ failure) - good prognosis
    - <u>Late shock</u> (MOF ensues) - poor prognosis, increases w/ number of organs failed (\> 80% if \> 4 organ systems failed), duration of organ failure, and patients age
