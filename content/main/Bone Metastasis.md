# Bone Metastasis

- **Definition** - metastasis of primary to bone, resulting in significant morbidity, and is the 3rd most common organ involved by metastasis after lung and liver
- **Common primaries that metastasize to the bone** - greater incidence of bone metastasis by tumours that do not target bone due to improved survival:
    - Breast cancer
    - Multiple myeloma
    - Prostate cancer
    - Lung cancer
    - Renal cell carcinoma
    - Thyroid carcinoma
- **Clinical features of bone metastasis** - main presentations include 1) bone pain, 2) pathological fractures, and 3) spinal cord compression:
    - **Bone pain** - characteristic progressive rest pain:
        - <u>Onset and progression</u> - insiduous, gradual onset
        - <u>Quality</u> - rest pain
        - <u>Severity</u> - variable, typically controllable by analgesics
        - <u>Timing</u> - worse at night; movement initially relieves pain, but in late stage provokes pain
    - **Pathological fractures** - acute onset of pain after trauma, typically a/w <u>breast cancers</u> (53%) due to underlying osteoporosis that is exacerbated by hormonal therapy (esp. AI)
    - **Spinal cord compression** - sudden onset of sensory level and bilateral LL weakness and constitutes medical emergency
- **Ix:**
    - **Ca** - detection of hyperCa
    - **Bone scan** - high Sn and inexpensive:
        - <u>Test properties</u> - high Sn
        - <u>Limitations</u>:
            - **FP results** - in healing bone (esp in healing bone)
            - **FN results** - occurs in **multiple myeloma** due to suppression of osteoblast activity
    - **Plain XR** - at any sites of bone pain to detect lytic/ sclerotic bone lesions
    - **PET-CT** - not as Sn as bone scan
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Pain relief</u> - provide analgesia
        - <u>Preservation and restoration of function</u> - IV bisphosphonates to improve pain and reduce skeletal related events (e.g. fractures and hyperCa)
        - <u>Local tumour control</u> - relief of tumour impingement by radiotherapy +/- alter anti-cancer Tx
        - <u>Skeletal stabilisation</u> - consider spinal interventions by orthopaedics
    - **Survical intervention** - indicated when:
        - Evidence of skeletal instability (slippage, e.g. anterior or posterior column fracture)
        - Evidence of impending fractor (e.g. large lytic lesions on a weight bearing bone w/ more than 50% cortical involvement)
    - **IV bisphosphonates:**
        - <u>Selection</u> - zoledronic acid, ibandronate, pamidronate
        - <u>Effects</u>:
            - Improve pain
            - Reduction of further skeletal-related events (e.g. pathological fractures, hyperCa)
    - **Radiotherapy** - modalities include:
        - Palliative external beam therapy
        - Systemic radionucleotide (strontium treatment)
    - **Additional therapy:**
        - Hormonal therapy for prostate or HR+ breast cancer may be important
