# Adult Polycystic Kidney Disease

- **Definition** - the most common inherited kidney disorder characterised by development of many small cysts that enlarge slowly and irregularly
- **Genetics:**
    - Inheritance - Autosomal dominant pattern of PKD1 gene (85%) or PKD2 gene (15%)
- **Epidemiology** - the most common hereditary kidney disorder:
    - Prevalence - 1:1000
    - Burden - estimated 5-10% patients on RRT have adult PKD:
        - PKD1 mutations - 50% develop ESRD with earlier onset (median age 52y)
        - PKD2 mutations - only minority develop ESRD with later onset (median age 69y)
- **Clinical features:**
    - **Asymptomatic** - most remain asymptomatic until later in life
    - **Hypertension** - most develop HTN from the age 20y and onwards, and will be present in basically all patients once CKD ensues
    - **Ballotable kidney** - nodular ballotable kidney(s) on one or both sides
    - **Renal manifestations:**
        - Vague abdominal/ loin discomfort - due to increasing mass of renal tissues
        - Acute loin pain and renal colic - due to infections, rupture, or haemorrhage of cysts
        - Haematuria - often with little to no proteinuria
        - Urinary tract and cystic infections
    - **Extrarenal manifestations:**
        - Hepatic cysts (30% patients) - LFT rarely derranged, but can cause massive, symptomatic hepatomegaly
        - Berry aneurysms - 10% patients develop subarrachnoid patients
        - Valvular disease - a/w mild MR, AR
        - Colonic diverticula and abdominal wall hernias
- **Ix:**
    - **Ultrasound kidneys** (95% sensitivity) - demonstration of multiple cystic lesions in patients \> 20y (may miss small cyst in young individuals)
    - **Molecular diagnosis** - possible not not in routine clinical practice
    - +/- Non-contrast MRI abdomen
    - \+*- MR angiography - screening of aneurysm can be done in families w* Hx of subarachnoid haemorrhage (not usually indicated)
- **Dx** - clinical Dx (compatible Hx and P/E, +ve FHx, US findings)
- **Mx** - manage as CKD:
    - BP control - reduced CVS morbidity and mortality, no evidence of effects on renal function deterioration
    - V2 receptor antagonist (tolvaptan) - some evidence in showing slow down in cyst formation
    - RRT - generally good candidates for dialysis and transplantation
