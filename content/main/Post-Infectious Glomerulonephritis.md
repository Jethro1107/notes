# Post-Infectious Glomerulonephritis

- **Definition** - glomerulonephritis occuring in connection with an infection, manifesting as different histological patterns:
    - <u>MPGN</u> - a/w subacute bacterial infections (e.g. infective endocarditis, hospital acquired infections)
    - <u>FSGS</u> - a/w HIV infection
    - <u>Other GNs</u> - HBV, HCV, schistosomiasis, leishmaniasis, malaria
- **Post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis** - specific subtype of glomerulonephritis:
    - **Epidemiology** - most common in children, but now rare in developed world
    - **Pathophysiology:**
        - <u>Immune response against S. pyogenes</u> - Hx of strep throat around 10d ago, or longer after streptococcal skin infection
        - <u>Immune complex deposition into glomerulus</u> - leading to complement activation (reduced C3 and C4) and inflammation
    - **Clinical features** - nephritic pattern:
        - <u>Haematuria</u> = characteristicaly red or smoking appearance
        - <u>Proteinuria</u>
        - <u>Fluid retention</u> - manifests as oedema and severe HTN
        - <u>Renal impairments</u> - reduction of eGFR, but normally spontaneously resolves within 10d
    - **Ix:**
        - <u>RFT</u> - reduction in eGFR
        - <u>Urine dipstick</u> - +ve proteinuria and haematuria
        - <u>Complements</u> - C3 and C4 reduced, reflecting complement consumption
        - <u>Serology for streptococcal infection</u> (Anti-SLO) - evidence of past stretococcal infection
