# Spinal Epidural Abscess

- **Definition** - abscess formation in the epidural space w/ the propensity of rapidly progressing into spinal cord compression which results in permanent sequelae
- **Etiology of spinal epidural abscess:**
    - **Haematogenous spread from infections of other tissues:**
        - <u>Skin infection</u> - classically a/w furunculosis
        - <u>Soft tissue infection</u> - dental abscesses, pharyngeal abscesses, chronic sinusitis
        - <u>Others</u> - infective endocarditis, tuberculosis (Pott's disease)
    - **Direct extension of local infections:**
        - <u>Local predisposing conditions</u> - vertebral osteomyelitis, decubitus ulcers
        - <u>Predisposing procedures resulting in direct inoculation</u> - spinal surgery, lumbar puncture, epidural anaesthesia
- **Risk factors of spinal epidural abscess:**
    - <u>Increased risk of transient bacteremia</u> - IVDU, active skin and soft tissue infection
    - <u>Increased risk of developing an infection</u> - i.e. immunocomprimised states:
        - HIV
        - Diabetes mellitus
        - CKD
        - Alcoholism
        - Malignancy
- **Common microbial agents causing spinal epidural abscess:**
    - <u>GPC</u> - Staphylococcus aureus (including MRSA), Streptococcus
    - <u>GNB</u> - aerobes (e.g. enterobacter), anaerobes
    - <u>Others</u> - fungal or TB
- **Clinical features of spinal epidural abscess:**
    - **Fever** - typical but not invariably present, but elevated WCC, ESR/CRP should raise suspicion
    - **Pain** - midline, aching back or neck pain is invariably present:
        - <u>Site</u> - back or neck +/- radicular pain (depending on site)
        - <u>Onset and progression</u> - usually acute or subacute (\< 2 weeks), but may occassionally be present for months or longer
        - <u>Provacating and relieving factors</u>:
            - Provoked by movement, coughing, or sneezing
            - Not relieved by rest (in fact characteristically awakens patients at night)
        - <u>Quality</u> - non-mechanical, aching quality
        - <u>Radiation</u> - midline and non-radiating, radiation occurs in concomitant radiculopathy
    - **Features of spinal cord compression** (myelopathy) - characteristically sensory Sx (e.g. parasthesia) before paralysis, and w/ early onset of 1) saddle parasthesia, 2) spastic paralysis, and 3) sphincteric disturbances:
        - Irreversible damage ensues once weakness and other myelopathic signs occur
        - Irreversible damage is partly due to abscess expansion but also venous congestion and thrombosis resulting in ischaemia
- **Chronic sterile granulomatous abscess:**
    - Usually after treatment of acute epidural infection
    - Suspected w/ progression of compression despite initiation of Tx
- **Ix:**
    - **Septic workup** - elevated WCC, ESR/CRP, and +ve blood culture in 50% cases (definitive microbiolgoical Dx through aspiration of abscess during surgery)
    - **MRI:**
        - <u>Role</u> - localisation of abscess and exclusion of other causes
        - <u>Characteristic findings</u>:
            - Diffuse gandalonium enhancement of epidural process in T1-weighted imaging
            - Hypointensity on T2-weighted imaging
    - **LP** - only indicated if suspected concomittent meningitis (\< 25%) or signs of encephalopathy:
        - <u>Risks</u> - iatrogenic meningitis (level of puncture planned to minimise passage of infected tissue into subdural space)
        - <u>Findings</u>:
            - WBC - neutrophylic pleocytosis
            - Protein - elevated
            - Glucose - reduced
            - Microbiology - cannot be cultured unless associated meningitis
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Spine intervetions</u> - decompressive laminectomy with aspiration for microbiological diagnosis and debridement
        - <u>Long-duration broad spectrum ABx</u> - empirical therapy requires coverage of GPC, GNB, and anaerobes
    - **Role of spine interventions** - main Tx of choice if not C/I unless abscess limited by spine or does not cause neurological impairment:
        - May prevent development of paralysis and improve or reverse paralysis in evolution
        - Unlikely to improve fixed deficits for more than several days
    - **Long duration broad spectrum ABx:**
        - <u>Duration</u> - up to 6-8 weeks
        - <u>Empirical ABx</u> - coverage of 1) GPC (Staphylococcus \[incl. MRSA\], streptococcus), 2) GNB, 3) anaerobes:
            - GPC coverage - vancomycin (15-20 mg/kg q12h)
            - GNR coverage - ceftriaxone (2g q12h)
            - Anaerobe coverage - metronidazole (30 mg/kg/d divided dose into q6h)
        - <u>Modification of ABx regimen</u> - guided by culture results (blood or surgical specimen)
- **Prognosis** - significant recovery in two-thirds of patients w/ prompt Dx and Tx
