# Bacterial Vaginosis

- **Definition** - replacement of local lactobacilli with anaerobic organisms, and is the commonest cause of vaginal discharge in reproductive-aged women
- **Etiolgical agent** - <u>anaerobes</u> replace lactobacilli to raise vaginal pH (pH ~ 7):
    - Gardnerella vaginalis
    - Prevotella spp.
    - Mycoplasma hominis
    - Mobiliuncus spp.
- **Epidemiology** - most common cause of vaginal discharge in women of reproductive age:
    - <u>Prevalence</u>:
        - 5% in asymptomatic students
        - 12% in pregnant women
        - 30% TOP (termination of pregnancy)
    - <u>Demographic</u> - irrespective of sexual activity
- **Risk factors of bacteria vaginosis:**
    - <u>Non-modifiable risk factors</u> - race (more common in black)
    - <u>Modifiable risk factor</u> - IUD, smokers
- **Clinical features of bacterial vaginosis** - 50% are asymptomatic:
    - <u>Vaginal discharge</u> - thin, white homogenous vaginal discharge coating vaginal wall, often foul smelling and offensive
    - <u>No vulvovaginitis</u> - presence of vulvovagnitis is a/w other infective conditions
- **Complications of bacterial vaginosis** - pregnancy-related vs non-pregnancy related:
    - **Pregnancy related:**
        - Post-TOP endometritis
        - Late miscarriage
        - Pre-term labour
        - PPROM
        - Post-partum endometritis
    - **Non-pregnancy related:**
        - Pelvic inflammatory disease
        - Vaginal cuff cellulitis and abscess after vaginal hysterectomy
- **Ix** - high vaginal swab for microscopy and biochemical testing (see below)
- **Dx** - <u>Amsel's criteria</u>, requires 3 out of the following 4:
    - Thin white homogenous discharge
    - Clue cells on microscopy
    - pH of vaginal fluid \> 4.5
    - +ve Whiff test - release of fishy odour on adding alkali (10% KOH)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Patient education</u> - advice local therapies as they may be detrimental to vaginal microbiome
        - <u>ABx Tx</u> - indicated only if symptomatic, lannic surgery, or pregnant women
    - **Patient education:**
        - No vaginal douching
        - Avoid use of shower gels or antiseptic bath agents
    - **ABx therapy:**
        - <u>Indications</u> - symptomatic, planning surgery, pregnancy
        - <u>Regimen</u>:
            - Oral metronidazole (400 mg bid for 5-7d or 2g single dose)
            - Intravaginal metronidazole gel or clindamycin cream
- **Role of universal screening and Tx in pregnant women:**
    - Insufficient evidence for Tx of asymptomatic pregnant women found to have BV
    - No role of oral therapy in pregnant women and breast feeding
    - Intravaginal treatment preferred for lactating women
