# Chickenpox

- **Definition** - clinical syndrome of primary infection of the varicella zoster virus (VZV) manifesting as blistering, vesicular infections
- **Virology of VZV:**
    - Dermatotrophic and neurotrophic virus
    - Spread by aerosol and direct contact, and extremely infectious in non-immune individuals
    - Incupation period around 11-20d
    - Infectivity lasts for 48h (up to 4d) before the lesion appears until the last vesicle crust over
- **Clinical features of chickenpox:**
    - **Rapid vesicular eruption** - rash progresses from small pink macules, to vesicles, and subsequently into pustules that rupture to form erosions:
        - <u>Distribution</u> - initially occurs in mucosal surfaces, and rapid dissemination in a centripetal distribution (more dense on trunk and sparse on limbs)
        - <u>Rate of development</u>:
            - New lesions every 2-4d and each crop is a/w systemic upset
            - Each lesion undergoes characteristic progression (see above) that lasts for 24h
        - <u>Infectivity</u> - patient remains infective via direct contact until last vesicles crust over

    
    ![](img/chickenpox_rash.png)
    - **Pruritis** - intense itching, where scratching may result in secondary bacterial infection after primary chicken pox
    - **Fever** - fever and systemic upset occurs w/ each crop of new lesions
    - **Visceral involvement** - may occurs in <u>pregnant women</u>, <u>adults</u>, or the immunocomprimised:
        - Pneumonitis
        - Hepatitis
        - Encephalitis
    - **Complications of pregnancy** - different complications if occuring at different stages of pregnancy:
        - Neonatal damage if early maternal infection w/ development abnormalities of the eyes, CNS, and limbs (3% risk)
        - Neonatal varicella if within 5d of infection
- **Ix** - principally supportive:
    - Direct IF for VZV Ag in aspirated vesicular fluid
    - PCR for VZV in aspirated vesicular fluid
- **Dx** - primarily clinical by recognition of rash
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Antiviral therapy</u> - marginal benefit if prescribed irrespectively, but benefits are maximal in selected populations
        - <u>Contact tracing</u> - prophylactic VZIG used to attenuate infections for patients who had significant contact
    - **Antiviral therapy** - aciclovir, valaciclovir, famciclovir:
        - <u>Indications</u> - strong indications for:
            - Patients presenting within 24-48h of onset of vesicles (shorten Sx duration)
            - Presentation w/ complications (e.g. visceral involvement, secondary bacterial infection)
            - Immunocomprimised, including pregnant women
        - <u>Regimen</u>:
            - **Immunocompetent adults and children:**
                - Oral aciclovir 800 mg 5x a day for 5d
                - Famciclovir 500 mg tid for 5d
                - Valaciclovir 1g tid for 5d
            - **Immunocomprimised/ pregnant** - prolonged regimen due to prolonged viral shedding:
                - IV aciclovir 5 mg/kg tid until clinical improvement, followed by oral therapy until crusted lesions over

- **Prevention** - live attenuated VZV caccine
