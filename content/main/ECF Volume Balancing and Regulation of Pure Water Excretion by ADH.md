# ECF Volume Balancing and Regulation of Pure Water Excretion by ADH

- **Normal daily water gain and water loss** - matching of daily water gain and water loss to maintain constancy of <u>body water content</u> and <u>body fluid osmolality</u>:
    - **Daily water gain** - from daily water intake and oxidative metabolism:
        - <u>Daily water intake</u> - extremely variable (0.5 L to 2-3 L/d)
        - <u>Metabolic water</u> (water generated from oxidative metabolism) - approximately 400 ml/d
    - **Daily water loss** - insensible water loss and renal water loss:
        - <u>Insensible water loss</u> - water lost through stool, sweat, and respiratory tract, approximately 800 ml/d
        - <u>Renal water loss</u> - **adjusted water excretion** principally in response to body fluid osmolality (280-295 mOsm) or total body water (TBW) content
- **Physiological mechanisms involved in regulation of ECF volume:**
    - <u>Renal control of Na excretion</u> (major mechanism) - mediated via RAAS, SNS (see notes on renal Na handling)
    - <u>Renal control of 'pure' water excretion</u> (minor mechanism) - mediated via ADH
- **Functional anatomy of the nephron relating to free water retention:**
    - <u>Loop of Henle</u> - counter-current flow in the descending and ascending limbs of loop of henle results in reabsorption of water from descending limbs and reabsorption of solute in ascending limb, which **sets up a hypertonoic renal medulla** (~1200 mOsm)
    - <u>Collecting ducts</u> - hypotnoic filtrate from late DCT traverses the renal medulla and water is reabsorbed across the osmotic gradient (depending on permeability to water)
- **ADH axis and control of serum osmolality:**
    - <u>Receptors</u> - osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus, which:
        - Stimulate secretion of ADH by posterior pituitary under conditions of increased osmolality or hypovolaemia
        - Inhibits secretion of ADH by posterior pituitary under conditions of reduced osmolality or hypervolaemia
    - <u>Effector</u> - principal cells of late DCT and collecting duct responding to ADH:
        - ADH binds to V2 receptors, which enhances collecting duct water permeability through the insertion of AQP-2 channels, enabling reabsorption of water along the osmotic gradient of the renal medulla (with maximum urine osmolality approaching that in themedullary tip)
- **Physiological changes along water and ADH axis under normo- or hyper-volaemia** - prevent excessive free water retention and dilutional hyponatraemia:
    - <u>Adequate solute delivery to ascending loop of Henle</u> - delivery of sufficient solute to create the hypertonic medulla and establish the osmotic gradient
    - <u>Normal functioning of ascending loop of Henle</u> - active reabsorption of solute to create hypotonic filtrate
    - <u>Suppressed ADH levels</u> - collecting duct remains impermeable to water
- **Physiological changes along water and ADH axis under hypo-volaemia** - prevent excessive free water loss and hypernatraemia:
    - <u>Adequate solute delivery to ascending loop of Henle</u> - delivery of sufficient solute to create the hypertonic medulla and establish the osmotic gradient
    - <u>Normal functioning of ascending loop of Henle</u> - active reabsorption of solute to create hypotonic filtrate
    - <u>Increased ADH levels and responsive collecting duct</u> - collecting duct becomes permeable to water
