# Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo

- **Definition** - pathologically defined as cupulolithiasis, that is the presence of otolithic debris from the saccule or utricle affecting the free flow of endolymph in semicircular canals
- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - most common cause of acute vertigo
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age - affects all ages, incidence increases w/ age
		- Sex - female preponderance (F:M = 2:1)
- **Pathophysiology of BPPV:**
    - _Cupolithiasis_ - dislodged otolithic debris from the saccules or utricles into the semicircular cannals
    - _Obstructed endolymph flow_ - endolymph flow within the semicircular canal is obstructed, underlying vertigo w/ head movements
	- _Cervical dysfunction_ - abnormal proprioceptive stimulus from upper C-spine
- **Clinical features of BPPV:**
	- **Acute vertigo attacks:**
		- _Onset_ - each attack brief, usually lasting 10-60s and subsides rapidly (recovery within 1  week)
		- _Frequency_ - recurring vertigo lasting for up to several days (recurrence in clusters)
		- _Provocation_ - transient vertigo that is **always triggered by head movement**:
			- Severe vertigo on getting out the bed
			- On head extension or rapid head turning
	- **Minimal accompanying autonomic or aural symptoms** - nausea may occur but remits rapidly, and attacks are never accompanied by vomiting, tinnitus or deafness
- **Signs:**
	- _Hearing tests_ - normal
	- _Caloric tests_ - normal
	- _Dix Halpicke maneuver_ - from a sitting position to the patients rapidly taken to a head-hanging position 30 degreees below the level of couch, done 3 times, once straight, once turning to thr right, and once to the left:
		- +ve test indicated by onset of acute vertigo and nystagmus
		- Usually a latent period of a few seconds before onset of Sx

- **Dix Hallpike maneuver** - demonstration of transient positional nystagmus accompanied by vertigo:
	- **Technique:**
		- Ask patient to keep eyes open to visualise the nystagmus
		- Swing patient briskly backwards through 120 degrees from sitting to overhang the edge of the couch
		- At the end of the motion, the ear tested should face down to the floor
	      
	      ![](img/dix_halpike_maneuver.png)
        - **Findings:**
            - **Torsional nystagmus** - note that will decrease upon repeated testing due to fatigue:
                - <u>At end of the bed</u> - fast phase jerks towards the lower ear; the nystagmus is delayed for a few seconds, and tends to last for 10-20 seconds
                - <u>Recovery to sitting position</u> - transient nystagmus may occur in the opposite direction
            - **Vertigo** - patient reports vertigo upon the maneuver
- **Conditions a/w BPPV:**
	- Trauma (17%)
	- Viral labyrinthitis (50%)
	- Idiopathic other than age (50%)
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Reassurance and education_ - on self-limiting nature, pathophysiology
		- _Advice_ - avoidance measures, and particle repositional maeuvers
		- _Surgical Tx_ - selective occlusion of posterior semicircular canal
	- **Avoidance measures** - avoid rapid head movements during:
		- Waking up and changing from a recumbent to sitting position
		- Rapid head turning
		- Rapid head extension
	- **Particle repositional maneuvres:**
		- _Patient-performed exercises_ - Brandt and Daroff procedures or the Cawthorne-Cooksey exercises involves repeatedly inducing Sx of vertigo until brainstem homeostasis takes over and Sx subsides
