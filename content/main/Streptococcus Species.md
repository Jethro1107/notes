# Streptococcus Species

- **Definition** - medically important G+ cocci in chains, and are nasopharyngeal and gut commensals
- **Classification of streptococci** - by 1) haemolysis status on blood agar, and 2) serotypes based on lancefield grouping:
    - <u>Haemolysis on blood agar</u> - classified into alpha-, beta-, and gamma-haemolytic streptococci
    - <u>Lancefield grouping</u> - classification of microbes (including streptococci) based on serotypes, irrespective of their haemolytic status, but more important for identifying beta-haemolytic streptococci

  
  ![](img/streptococcus_and_related_infections.png)
- **Medically important streptococcus:**
    - <u>Alpha-/ Gamma-haemolytic streptococcus</u>:
        - S. pneumoniae
        - Viridan's streptococcus (common oral commensals a/w IE)
        - S. anginosus/ milleri
        - S. bovis (Group D streptococcus a/w GI malignancy)
        - S. suis
    - <u>Beta-haemolytic streptocuccs</u>:
        - S. pyogenes
        - S. agalactiae
        - Group C and G streptococci

  
  ![](img/medically_important_streptococcus.png)
- **Skin presentations of streptococcuus** - cellulitis, erysipelas, and impetigo as a result of:
    - <u>Group A streptococcus</u> - major cause of skin and soft tissue infections irrespective of age
    - <u>Group C and G streptococcus</u> - major cause of skin and soft tissue infections in elderly and infirmed, especially if diabetic or immunocomprimised
    - <u>Group B streptococcus</u> - classically a/w maternal-fetal transmission, but is increasingly common in extremes of age
- **Scarlet fever** (common in school children) - clinical syndrome of GAS (ocassionally w/ C or G) caused by infecting strain producing <u>streptococcal pyrogenic exotoxin</u>, resulting in:
    - <u>Sore throat</u> - as a result of pharyngitis, tonsilitis, where examination classically reveals a '**strawberry tongue**'
    - <u>Blanchable erythematous rash</u> - diffuse erythematous rash that is blanchable on palpation, often followed by desquamation
    - <u>Pastila's sign</u> - residual petechial lesions in the antecubital fossa

  
  ![](img/clinical_features_of_scarlet_fever.png)
- **Streptococcal toxic shock syndrome:**
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - associated w/ severe GAS (occassionally C or G) producing pyogenic exotoxin A which acts as a superantigen
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - natural Hx follows as:
        - Influenza-like syndrome with foci of streptococcal infection (in 50% of cases)
        - Faint erythematous rash mainly over the chest
        - Circulatory shock and multi-orgal failure
    - <u>Mx</u>:
        - **General measures** - fluid resuscitation
        - **IV ABx:**
            - Benzylpenicillin (antistreptococcal ABx)
            - Clindamycin (protein synthesis inhibitor to inhibit toxin production)
        - **IVIG** - demonstrated efficacy for severe streptococcal toxic shock syndrome
