# General Anaesthesia

## Induction Agent

- **Definition** - drug which <u>induces LOC in one arm-brain circulation time</u> when given at an appropriate dose
- **Classes of induction agents** - must be rapid:
    - Benzodiazepines
    - Opioids - not generally used as induction agent as does not produce fast LOC with the exception of <u>Ketamine</u>
- **Commonly used induction agents:**
    - Barbiturate
    - Etomidate
    - Propofol
    - Ketamine
- **Barbiturate:**
    - Mechanism
        - Potentiate effect of GABA at GABAA inhibitory receptor
    - Pharmacokinetics
        - Rapid brain uptake, rapid redistribution, hepatic elimination
        - Slow metabolism & prolonged elimination
    - Effects
        - CVS - ↓MAP, ↑HR, myocardial depression
        - Respiratory - depression ventilator centre, retain some airway reflexes
        - CNS - ↓CMRO2, CBF, ICP, anti-convulsant
    - Uses
        - Obstetrics, RSI, epilepsy/ seizures, neurosurgical emergency
- **Etomidate:**
    - Mechanism
        - Potentiate effect of GABA at GABAA receptor
    - Pharmacokinetics
        - Rapid onset of action & redistribution. Hydrolysed by plasma esterases & liver
    - Effects
        - CVS - cardiovascular stability, no effect on contractility, SVR, HR
        - Respiratory - minimal effect on respiration
        - CNS - ↓CMRO2, CBF, ICP
        - Other - inhibits 11-β hydroxylase → adrenocortical suppression
    - Uses
        - Cardiac patients
        - Haemodynamically unstable patients
- **Propofol:**
    - Mechanism
        - Potentiate effect of GABA at inhibitory GABAA receptor
    - Pharmacokinetics
        - Rapid onset, rapid redistribution, metabolised in liver, high clearance
    - Effects
        - CVS - ↓SVR, cardiac contractility, preload
        - Respiratory - ↓resp depression, obtunds laryngeal reflexes
        - CNS - ↓CMRO2, CBF, ICP, burst suppression
        - Others - fast clear headed wake-up, anti-emetic, propofol infusion syndrome
    - Uses
        - Most suitable for infusion of induction agents
- **Ketamine:**
    - Mechanism
        - Inhibits excitatory NT glutamate at NMDA receptors
        - Dissociative anaesthesia rather than hypnosis
    - Pharmacokinetics
        - Rapid onset, slower redistribution, hepatic metabolism to norketamine
    - Effects
        - CVS - ↑HR, SVR, CO by SNS activation
        - Respiratory - little effect on rate, bronchodilator, salivation, reflexes preserved
        - CNS - ↑CBF, ICP, CMRO2, hallucinations, amnesic
        - Others - analgesic
    - Uses
        - Shocked patients
        - As analgesic
- **Length of action of an IV bolus of induction agent:**
    - Appropriate dosed induction agent (other than ketamine) will keep patient asleep for 3-5 min
    - This is explained by the pharmokinetics causing in the fall of effector site (brain) concentration and plasma concentration:
        - Rapid peripheral redistribution to other parts of the body (fat, muscle and skin)
        - In fact, actual half-life of most induction agents is several hours
    - Ketamine is different:
        - Onset time of 30s (slower than other induction agents)
        - LOC for 5-10 minutes

## Maintenance agent

- **ROA:**
    - Inhalation route - volatile anaesthetic agent, nitrous oxide
    - IV route - infusion of induction agent
- **Volatile anaesthetic agents** - halogenated ether compounds (comes in liquid form):
    - <u>Mainstay</u> of anaesthetic maintenace
    - <u>Selection</u>:
        - Sevoflurane
        - Desflurane
        - Isoflurane
    - <u>Delivery</u> - via vaporiser:
        - Adds a known concentration of volatile agents to a gas mixture (usually N2O, O2, or air) which patient enhales via the breathing circuit
        - Concentration added adjusted to **determine appropriate concentration of volatile agent** in the lungs to maintain anaesthesia
        - Equilibirum between delivery by vaporiser and elimination through exhalation
- **Nitrous oxide:**
    - <u>Weak anaesthesia</u> - need to breath 104% to achieve anaesthesia
    - <u>Delivery</u> - given w/ O2 or air in gas mixture to inhale
    - <u>Advantages</u>:
        - Good analgesia
        - Reduced monitored anaesthetic care (MAC)
    - <u>S/E</u> - prominent PONV, effects on bone marrow
- **IV induction agent** - in theory all induction agent if given as infusion or regular boluses:
    - <u>Problems</u>:
        - Drug accumulation - accumulation on continuous or repeated dosing (rember they are not eliminated, just redistributed), and resulting in very prolonged duration of action
        - Difficult dose timing - esp. with bolus technique
        - S*E - e.g. propofol infusion syndrome w* propofol, etomidate-induced adrenocortical suppression
- **Common practices for maintenace** - IV propofol:
    - Used w/ ultra-short acting opioid remifentanil for synergistic effects
    - Dosing facilitated by special pharmacokinetic synringe w/ computer programs:
        - Calculation of cncentration of propofol in plasma and the brain
        - Based on patients weight, ocassionally age, sex and height
        - Adjusts dosing over time for redistribution and elimination
    - Anasthesiologist determines the concentration of propofol appropriate

## Complications of anaesthesia

- **Hypoxia** - due to 1) hypoventilation, 2) VQ mismatch, 3) shunting, 4) diffusion hypoxia
- **Hypotension** - due to cardiac depression of anaesthetic, vasodilation +/- hypovolaemia due to surgery
- **Sedation, confusion or agitation** - due to:
    - Anaesthetic related - residual effect of sedation
    - Hypoxia
    - Hypotension
    - Hypoglycaemia
    - Hypothermia
    - Electrolyte disturbance
- **Headache**
- **PONV**
- **Shivering** - increased O2 consumption (bad for those w/ underlying IHD)
