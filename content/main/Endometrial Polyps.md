# Endometrial Polyps

- **Definition** - focal endometrial outgrowths containing variable amounts of glands, stroma and blood vessels
- **Pathophysiology of endometrial polyps:**
    - <u>Develop from excessive estrogen exposure</u> - focal endometrial proliferation resulting in formation hyperplasia and formation of sessile or frankly pedunculated masses
    - <u>Polyps are non-responsive to estrogen</u> - most polyps are relatively insensitive to cyclical hormonal changes leading them to persist and cause unscheduled vaginal bleeding
- **Risk factors of endometrial polyps:**
    - Obesity
    - Early menarche and late menopause
    - Use of tamoxifen
    - Use of hormone replacement therapy
- **Clinical features of endometrial polyps:**
    - <u>Asymptomatic</u> - common in women a/w increased estrogen exposure but may not cause any Sx
    - <u>Abnormal uterine bleeding</u> - accounts for 20% of women w/ AUB, including heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB), intermenstrual bleeding (IMB) and post-menopausal bleeding (PMB)
    - <u>Subfertility</u> - present in 10% of women w/ subfertility
- **Ix and Dx:**
    - **Transvaginal pelvic ultrasound** - increased sensitivity to detect focal intracavity pathologies by saline infusion sonography (SIS)
    - **Hysteroscopy** - polypectomy indicated in order to alleviate Sx (AUB), optimize fertility and <u>exclude hyperplasia and malignancy</u>
- **Mx** - polypectomy as above
