# Takayasu's Disease

- **Definition** - large vessel vasculitis predominantly affecting the aorta and its major branches, and ocassionally the pulmonary arteries
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - worldwide distribution but most common in Asia
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - peak onset at 25-30y
        - Sex - female preponderance (F:M = 8:1)
- **Pathophysiology of Takayasu disease:**
    - **Distribution of vasculitis** - predominantly affects the <u>aorta and its major branches</u> (carotid, ulnar, brachiaal, radial and axillary arteries), ocassionally the <u>pulmonary arteries</u>, classified into:
        - <u>Type 1 Takayasu disease</u> - localised to the aorta and its branches
        - <u>Type 2 Takayasu disease</u> - localised to the descending thoracic and abdominal aorta
        - <u>Type 3 Takayasu disease</u> - combined features of type 1 and type 2
        - <u>Type 4 Takayasu disease</u> - involvement of pulmonary artery
    - **Granulomatous inflammation of the vessel wall** - leads to:
        - <u>Vessel occlusion</u> - results in coarctation or occlusion on angiography
        - <u>Weakening of the vessel wall</u> - results in aortic incompetence and aneurysmal dilatation
- **Clinical features of Takayasu disease:**
    - <u>Non-specific Sx</u> - fever, arthralgia and weight loss
    - <u>Claudication</u> - UL claudication as as a result of vessel occlusion
- **Signs of Takayasu disease** - examination of the arterial system:
    - **Examination of the arterial system:**
        - <u>Pulses</u> - weak or absent
        - <u>Auscultation</u> - bruits present
    - **Cardiovascular examination** - <u>early systolic murmur best heard at LLSB</u> suggestive of AR
    - **BP measurement** - reveals HTN
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, CRP/ESR:
        - <u>CBC</u> - NcNc anaemia
        - <u>CRP/ESR</u> - non-specifically elevated
    - **Angiography** - diagnostic and distribution of types:
        - Coarctation
        - Vessel occlusion
        - Aneurysmal dilatation
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Immunosuppression</u> - high dose steroids and immunosuppressants
- **Prognosis** - 5y survival up to 83% with appropriate Mx
