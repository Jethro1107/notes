# Functional Anatomy and Physiology of the Skin

- **The skin as the largest organ in the body** - covers just under 2 m2 in the average adult
- **Layers of the skin:**
	- _Epidermis_ - a stratified squamous epithelium consting mainly of keratinocytes
	- _Basement membrane_ - separation between the epidermis and dermis
	- _Dermis_ - less cellular layer, rich in connective tissues and supports blood vessels, nerves, and epidermal-derived appendage (hair follicles, sweat glands)
	- _Subcutis_ - layer of adipose tissue beneath the skin
- **Epidermis** - stratified squamous epithelium composed of keratinocytes (90%) and other cells:
	- **Thickness of the epidermis:**
		- 0.1-0.2 mm all over the body
		- Thicker in the palms and soles, reaching few mm
	- **Layers of the epidermis:**
		- _Basal layer_ - main proliferative compartment of the epidermis
		- _Prickle cell layer and granular layer_ - keratinocytes differentiate as moving towards the keratin layer and secretes various protein (keratin) and lipid products
		- _Stratum corneum_ - keratinocytes undergo apoptosis and loses its nuclei and forms flattened corneocytes, forming a hydrophobic barrier
	- **Function of keratinocytes:**
		- Synthesis of structural proteins such as keratin, loricrin, and filaggrin
		- Lipid production
	- **Cell-cell adhesion between keratinocytes** - mediated by desmosomes; loss of function underlies blistering diseases
	- **Other epidermal cells** (10%):
		- _Langerhan's cell_ - dendritic, bone marrow-derrived cell that circulates between the epidermis and LN, primarily for antigen presentation to lymphocytes
		- _Melanocytes_ - predominantly in the **basal layer** and responsible for synthesis of **melanin** (from tyrosine) and package it into **melanosomes** and transfer to surrounding keratinocytes via dendritic processes
		- _Merkel cells_ - presence in basal layer and play a role in signal transduction of fine touch

- **Dermis:**
	- _Thickness of the dermis_ - variable:
		- Just over 1 mm in the forearm
		- Up to 4 mm in the back
	- _Cell types of the dermis:_
		- Fibroblasts (predominant cell type for synthesis of the acellular components)
		- Immune cells (mast cells, macrophages, T cells, dendritic cells)
		- Neurons
		- Endothelial cells (vasculature)
	- _Acellular components of the dermis:_
		- _Fibres_ - primarily collagen I and III, elastin, and reticulin
		- _Ground substances_ - e.g. glycosaminoglycans, hyaluronic acid, dermatan sulphate (catabolism influenced by UVR and hormonal changes)
	- _Different organisation of fibres of the dermis:_
		- Superficial papillary dermis
		- Deep reticular dermis
- **Blood supply of the skin** - organised into superficial and deep plexuses consisting of arterioles, capillaries, and venules:
	- _Plexuses_ - superfical plexus in the papillary dermis communicates w/ lower plexus at the junction between dermis and subcutis
	- _Role of blood vessels in the skin_:
		- Blood supply of the skin far exceeds metabolic activity, and is highly modulated by autonomic innervation
		- This reflects importance of the skin in **thermoregulation**
