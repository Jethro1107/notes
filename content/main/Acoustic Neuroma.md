# Acoustic Neuroma

- **Definition** - a benign tumour of Schwann cells of the 8th cranial nerve, which may arise in isolation or as part of NF2
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - arises in the 3rd decade of life
        - Sex - slight female preponderance
- **Site of tumour** - accounts for 80-90% of tumours of the cerebropontine angle:
    - On the vestibular division
    - Near the nerve's entry point into the medulla, or arises in the internal acoustic meatus
- **Clinical features of acoustic neuroma:**
    - <u>Unilateral hearing loss</u> - classically presents w/ progressive unilateral SNHL, occasionally a/w **tinnitus**
    - <u>Vertigo</u> - rare presentation despite arising from vestibular division, as slow growth enables compensatory brainstem mechanisms to develop
    - <u>Incoordination or ataxia</u> - incoordination of the ipsilateral limbs due to progressive enlargement leading to distortion of the brainstem and/or ipsilateral peduncle
    - <u>Raised ICP</u> - headache, N/V due to hydrocephalus due to obstruction of 4th ventricle
    - <u>Facial weakness</u> - rare presenting Sx and is more a/w a complication after surgical resection

- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Surgical resection</u> - often complete in many tumours (may complicate w/ deafness and facial weakness)
        - <u>Stereotactic radiosurgery</u> - selected lesions
