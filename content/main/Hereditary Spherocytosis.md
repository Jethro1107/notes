# Hereditary Spherocytosis

- **Definition** - autosomal dominant condition a/w abnormalities of RBC cytoskeleton
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - 1:5000
    - <u>Mode of inheritence</u> - AD
- **Physiology of RBC destruction** - red pulp function:
    - <u>Initial elasticity of the RBC membrane</u> - high malleabilit of RBCs offered by cytoskeletal stapled on the lipid bilayer by special protein complexes, which enables it to pass through the spleen without undergoing destruction
    - <u>The red pulp destroys RBCs that have abnormal structure</u>:
        - RBCs w/ abnormal structures will be trapped within the red pulp (fenestrated capillaries), such that they are subjected to loss of membrane by RE cells
        - Loss of membrane relative to cell volume further results in abnormal cell shape, such that they are even more subjected to destruction the next time it passes through the spleen
- **Pathophysiology of hereditary spherocytosis:**
    - <u>Abnormal cytoskeletal structures</u> - most commonly beta-spectrin or ankyrin
    - <u>Chronic extravascular haemolysis</u> - w/ spherocytes, high MCHC, reticulocytosis and mild hyperbilirubinaemia
- **Clinical features:**
    - <u>Chronic haemolytic state</u> - baseline of mild jaundice a/w anaemia
    - <u>Gallstones</u> - pigmented gallstones may be present in up to 50% of patients
    - <u>Crisis</u> - clinical course may be complicated by crisis (see below)
- **Crisis complicating hereditary spherocytosis:**
    - <u>Haemolytic crisis</u> - occurs when severity of haemolysis occurs (usually with infections)
    - <u>Megaloblastic crisis</u> - occurs when patient is folate depleted, and may be the first presentation of the disease in pregnancy
    - <u>Aplastic crisis</u> - occurs in association of bone marrow suppression by Parvovirus B19 infection, presenting w/ severe haemolytic anaemia but low reticulocyte count
- **Ix:**
    - **CBC:**
        - <u>Hb and MCV</u> - Nc or mild macrocytic haemolytic anaemia due tor eticulocytosis (normal if completely compensated)
        - <u>MCHC</u> - elevated as progressive loss of membrane relative to cell volume within the spleen
    - **Coomb's test** - -ve
    - **Osmotic fragility test** - shows increased Sn to lysis in hypotonic salin solutions
- **Mx:**
    - <u>General measures</u> - folic acid prophylaxis +/- transfusion for severe haemolytic crisis
    - <u>Other Mx</u> - e.g. splenectomy to improve Hb, cholecystectomy if found to have stones
