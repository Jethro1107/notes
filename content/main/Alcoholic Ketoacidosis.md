# Alcoholic Ketoacidosis

- **Definition** - ketoacidosis usually occuring in malnourished patients after an episode of binge alcohol ingestion w/ temporarily cessation of alcohol drinking
- **Pathophysiology of AKA:**
    - **Background of ketosis** - background of malnourishment resulting in a state of fasting resulting in non-pathological ketogenesis
    - **Effects of binge drinking** - ethanol is rapidly oxidised into acetic acid, and converted into acetyl-CoA, temporarily inhibiting peripheral lipolysis such that overt ketoacidosis does occur
    - **Onset of severe ketoacidosis after ethanol ingestation has ceased** - active drinking has stopped often due to **development of abdominal pain, N/V**:
        - <u>Catecholamine surge during alcohol withdrawal</u> - increased level of cortisol from withdrawal state induces **insulin resistance**
        - <u>Low insulin:glucagon ratio</u> - results in marked lipolysis, and ketogenesis from 1) ethanol, and 2) fatty acids
- **Clinical features of AKA:**
    - **Non-specific abdominal Sx** - general 1-2d prior to presentation w/ ketoacidosis attributed to alcoholic gastritis, mild hepatitis or pancreatitis:
        - <u>Nausea and vomiting</u> - typically due to the acute effects of alcohol, which can result in hypovolaemia and K depletion from secondary hyperaldosteronism
        - <u>Abdominal pain</u> - low-grade gastritis, hepatitis, or pancreatitis
    - **Evidence of alcoholic hepatitis** - acute flares of alcoholic hepatitis w/ <u>malnutrition</u> and <u>hepatocellular insufficiency</u> being the predominent features
    - **Increased respiratory rate** - due to respiratory compensation for the metabolic acidosis
- **Signs of AKA:**
    - <u>Kussmaul breathing</u> - non-specific for metabolic acidosis
- **Ix:**
    - **Urine dipstick** - ketonuria
    - **Routines bloods** - CBC, LRFT, RBG, CaPO4, Mg, serum Osm, serum ketones:
        - <u>CBC</u> - macrocytosis (non-specific for alcoholism)
        - <u>LFT</u> - raised transaminase in **reversed De Reiter's ratio** and non-specific rise in GGP suggests chronic alcoholism
        - <u>RBG</u> - typically normoglycaemia (although possible to be hyper- or hypo-glycaemia)
        - <u>RFT</u>:
            - K - hypoK, K depletion as N/V tends to precede AKA while extracellular shift is less likely in ketoacidosis due to ability of H+ and ketoacid anion to enter cells via sodium-organic anion cotransporter
            - HCO3 - metabolic acidosis (often mixed w/ metabolic alkalosis \[N/V\], respiratory alkalosis)
        - <u>PO4</u> - hypoPO4 due to reduced intake
        - <u>Mg</u> - hypoMg due to dietary deficiency and urinary Mg wasting
        - <u>Serum osm</u> - markedly elevated (hyper-osmolar); and elevated serum Osm gap (measured - Na, urea, glucose, ethanol) due to presence of ketones
        - <u>Serum ketones</u> - ketonemia
- **Dx** - clinical based on 1) ketonuria/ ketonemia, 2) HAGMA, and 3) compatible Hx and RBG
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Mx of alcohol use disorder</u> - e.g. thiamine administration
        - <u>Rehydration and reduction in rate of ketogenesis</u> - dextrose and normal saline solution
        - <u>Mx of concominent electrolyte abnormalities</u> - e.g. hypoMg, hypoPO4, hypoK
