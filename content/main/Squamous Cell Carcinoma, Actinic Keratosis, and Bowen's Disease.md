# Squamous Cell Carcinoma, Actinic Keratosis, and Bowen's Disease

- **Definition** - a skin malignancy that arises from epidermal keratinocytes
- **Epidemiology** - the second most common skin cancer:
	- Most common in elderly male
	- Increasd risk w/ smoking
- **Clinical features of squamous cell carcinoma** - variable presentation of keratotic nodules on a background of actinic keratosis or de novo:
	- _Site_ - chronically sun-exposed sites:
		- Bald scalp
		- Top of ear
		- Face
		- Back of hands
	- _Clinical appearance_ - corresponds to the underlying histological grading:
		- Well-differentiated tumours - often present as defined keratotic nodules
		- Poorly differentiated tumours - appears ill-defined, infiltrative, and may ulcerate
- **Mode of spread:**
	- _Local invasion_ - within the skin
	- _Lymphatic spread_ - tumours in immunocomprimised host are more likely to metastasise to draining lymphatic basins
	- _Haematogenous spread_ - to distant organs
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Surgical Tx_ - excision w/ a 3-4 mm margins has a cure rate of 90-95% for invasive SCC
		- _Medical Mx_ - high-risk or metastatic SCCs are typically managed in a MDT
		- _Prevention of recurrence_ - systemic retinoids in those high-risk for recurrence
