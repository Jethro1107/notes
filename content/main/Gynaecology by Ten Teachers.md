# Chapter 4: Disorders of Menstrual Bleeding

## Abnormal Uterine Bleeding

- **Definition and terminology** - AUB reflects structural and functional disorders of menstrual bleeding, and are one of the most common complaints to GP and gynaecologist:
    - <u>Heavy menstrual bleeding</u> (HMB) - previously termed 'menorrhagia', is defined by \> 80 ml blood loss per periods
    - <u>Intermenstrual bleeding</u> (IMB) - bleeding in between periods, often seen w/ endometrial and cervical polyps and endometriosis
    - <u>Post-coital bleeding</u> (PCB) - bleeding after sex, often a/w cervical pathologies
    - <u>Post-menopausal bleeding</u> (PMB) - new uterine bleeding after \> 1y after cessation of periods (requires exclusion of endometrial pathologies and vaginal atrophy)
- **Etiology of AUB** - PALM-COEIN system (FIGO):
    - **PALM** - structural pathologies:
        - Polyps
        - Adenomyosis
        - Leiomyoma
        - Malignancies
    - **COEIN** - functional pathologies:
        - Coagulopathies (including anticoagulation)
        - Ovulatory disorders
        - Endometrial
        - Iatrogenic
        - Not classified

## Heavy menstrual bleeding

- **Defintion** - previously termed 'menorrhagia', is the most common type of AUB, defined as blood loss \> 80 ml per period, but difficult to objectively quantify (poor correlation w/ Hb levels)
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - affects 20-30% of women of reproductive age
    - <u>Burden</u> - 5% of women 30-49y present to primary care w/ HMB, with a substantial number being referred to secondary care
- **Etiology of HMB:**
    - **Stuctural disorders:**
        - Endometrial polyps
        - Adenomyosis
        - Fibroids (30%)
        - Malignancy - e.g. Endometrial/ cervical carcinoma
    - **Functional disorders:**
        - Coagulation disorders and anticoagulation
        - Pelvic inflammatory disease
        - Thyroid disease
        - Intrauterine devices
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - **HPI** - onset, duration, nature of bleeding, severity:
        - <u>Onset and duration</u> - length of time of problems
        - <u>Quality of bleeding</u>:
            - HMB - characterise amount of blood loss (by frequency of changing pads) based on comparison with normal periods
            - IMB - suggests of endometrial or cervical polyps
            - PCB - suggestive of cervical pathology
        - <u>Severity</u>:
            - By characteristic of bleeding:
                - 1\. Comparison with usual periods
                - 2\. Frequency of changing pads or tampons
                - 3\. Presence or blood clots
                - 4\. Heavy sensation
            - By anaemic Sx:
                - SOB/ reduced ET
                - Fatigue
                - Dizziness
            - By functional impairment:
                - Constraints on lifestyle
                - Effects on work

    
    ![](img/deterining_hmb_in_hx.png)
    - **Associated Sx:**
        - <u>Abnormal uterine bleeding</u> - ask about IMB, PCB as above
        - <u>Bleeding tendancies</u> - excessive bruising/ bleeding in other sites, and excessive bleeding in haemostatic challenges (e.g. PPH, post-operative bleeding, during dental extractions) to identify **coagulopathies**
        - <u>Vaginal discharge</u> - unusual vaginal discharge a/w **PID**
        - <u>Urinary Sx</u> - pressure from **fibroids**
        - <u>Thyroid Sx</u> - both hyper- and hypo-thyroidism can result in HMB

    
    ![](img/associated_sx_of_hmb.png)
    - **Menstrual Hx:**
        - <u>Regularity</u> - heavy anovulatory bleeds may be a/w early puberty, PCOS, or perimenopause
    - **Relevant past obstetrics Hx, PMH, SHx, FHx**
- **P/E:**
    - **General examination** - pallor
    - **Abdominal examination** - local masses and tenderness
    - **Pelvic examination:**
        - Visualisation of cervix for polyps/ malignancies
        - Swabs if suspecting pelvic infections
        - Cervical smear taken if one is due
        - Palpation for pelvic masses (e.g. adnexal masses) through bimanual examination
    - **PR examination** - if pelvic examination rejected
- **Ix:**
    - **Blood tests** - CBC, +/- clotting profile, TFT:
        - <u>CBC</u> - identify McHc anaemia (assumed to be related to iron-deficiency)
        - +/- <u>Clotting profiles</u> - identify coagulopathies if present early in menarche, or FHx of coagulation disorders
        - +/- <u>TFT</u> - indicated only if Hx suggestive of thyroid disorders
    - **Transabdominal/ transvaginal pelvic USG** - detection of endometrial thickening, or mass
    - **Endometrial biopsy:**
        - <u>Indications</u> - 1) risk factors of malignancy present, 2) age \> 45y, 3) treatment failure
    ![](img/role_of_eb_in_hmb.png)
    - **Hysteroscopy with guided biopsy:**
        - <u>Indications</u>:
            - 1\. Failed EB biopsy
            - 2\. EB biopsy insufficient for histopathology assessent
            - 3\. TVUSS inconclusive to establish location
            - 4\. Abnormality on TVUSS ameable to treatment (e.g. fibroid or polyps)
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Dx and Tx of underlying cause</u> - depends on underlying pathology
        - <u>Medical Tx</u> - initial Mx of HMB in the absence of structural or histological abnormalities
        - <u>Surgical Tx</u> - indicated only if failed medical Tx or if causing pressure Sx (e.g. prolapse, urinary Sx), but must be warned on effects on fertility
    - **Medical Tx** - NICE guidelines suggest use in the following orders:
        - **Levonorgestrel intrauterine system** (Mirena) - long-term use of at least 12 mo as expected:
            - <u>Efficacy</u> - reduction of blood loss of 95% within 1y, serves as a highly effective alternative to surgical Tx
            - <u>C/I</u> - not suitable in women wishing to conceive
        - **Trenexamic acid** - taken during menstruation
            - <u>MOA</u> - antifibrinolytic to reduce blood loss
        - **Other medical Tx** - e.g. mefenamic acid (NSAID), COC pills, norethisterone
        - **Gonadotrophin-releasing hormone agonsits** - not routinely indicated:
            - <u>MOA</u> - GnRH stimulation on pituitary to inhibit production of estrogen, resulting in amenorrhoea
            - <u>Indications</u> - short term use:
                - 1\. Pre-operative use - e.g. shrink fibroids or cause endometrial suppression to enhance visualisation
                - 2\. Severe HMB - improve Hb levels by providing a respite from bleeding
    - **Surgical Tx** - definitive Tx to HMB, but must be counselled and ensure that family is complete:
        - **Endometrial ablation:**
            - <u>Principles</u> - ablation of endometrial lining to sufficient depth to prevent regeneration of endometrium
            - <u>Indications</u> - suitable for women with uterus \< 10w size and fibroids measuring \< 3cm
            - <u>Modalities</u> - 2nd generation techniques preferred:
                - 1st generation techniques - electrical diathermy, rollerball ablation
                - 2nd generation techniques - Impedence control endometrial ablation, thermal uterine balloon therapy, microwave ablation
            - <u>Clinical efficacy</u> - success in 80% cases:
                - Amenorrhoea in 40%
                - Markly reduced menstrual loss in 80%
                - No difference in menstrual loss in 20%
        - **Umbilical artery embolisation** - useful if a/w fibroids
        - **Myotomy** - sensible option for women with large fibroids w/ pressure Sx while preserving fertility (and are at age where this is realistic)
        - **Transcervical resection of fibroids** - alternative option for large submucosal fibroid
        - **Hysteroscopy** - may be necessary in acute HMB or those non-responsive to other Tx

## Dysmenorrhoea

- **Definition and terminology** - dysmenorrhoea is defined as painful menstruation:
    - <u>Primary dysmenorrheoa</u> - painful periods since onset of menarche and is unlikely to be a/w pathology
    - <u>Secondary dysmenorrhoea</u> - new onset painful periods over time and usually have a secondary cause
- **Etiology of secondary dysmenorrhoea:**
    - Endometriosis
    - Adenomyosis
    - Pelvic inflammatory disease
    - Cervical stenosis and haematometra
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - **HPI** - onset, and duration, Timing w/ menstrual periods, and severity:
        - <u>Onset and duration</u> - length of time of problem (since menarche vs new onset dysmenorrhoea)
        - <u>Timing a/w menstrual periods</u>:
            - Endometriosis - characteristic menstrual pain that precedes the period
            - Pain that only occurs with bleeding (esp. passage of clots)
        - <u>Severity</u> - assessed by 1) pain killers, 2) time taken off work/ school due to pain
    - **Menstrual Hx** - characterise flow and regularity
    - **Associated Sx:**
        - <u>Flushing and nausea</u> - prostaglandin related in primary dysmenorrhoea
        - <u>AUB</u> - characterised during menstrual Hx, where AUB is a/w pathological diagnosis
        - <u>Vaginal discharge</u> - presence of abnormal discharge may be seen in PID
        - <u>Dyspareunia</u> - points towards pathological diagnosis
    - **Sexual and contraceptive Hx** - identify risk of pelvic inflammatory disease
    - **Relevant past gynaecological Hx, PMH, SHx, FHx**
- **P/E:**
    - **Abdominal examination** - any suprpubic tenderness
    - **Pelvic examination:**
        - <u>Signs of PID</u> - cervical excitation, vaginal discharge
        - <u>Signs of endometriosis</u> - pelvic mass, fixed and endometrial, uterus, endometriotic nodules (e.g. pouch of Douglas or on the uterosacral ligaments)
- **Ix:**
    - <u>Microbiology</u> - high vaginal and endocervical swabs
    - <u>TVUSS</u> - useful to detect endometriomas or appearance suggestive of adenomyosis
    - \+*- <u>Diagnostic laparoscopy</u> - detailed discussion w* patient about risks and possibility to reveal no underlying pathology when indicated:
        - 1\. Hx suggestive of endometriosis
        - 2\. Swab and USG normal
        - 3\. Patient preference
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Lifestyle modifications</u> - exercise, low fat, vegetarian diet, heat
        - <u>Medical Tx</u> - symptomatic relief by NSAID, hormonal contraceptives, Merina +/- short-term GnRH agonists
        - <u>Surical Tx</u> - surgical laparoscopy to perform adhesiolysis or Tx of endometriosis

# Chapter 5: Implantation and Early Pregnancy

## Implantation and the Establishment of Pregnancy

- **Physiological process of implantation:**
    - Corpus luteum (CL) secretes large amounts of progesterone to prepare the the endometrium to support a pregnancy
    - Ovum is fertilised by sperm in the Fallopian tube and undergoes division
    - Blastocyst implants into the endometrium at around <u>7d after ovulation</u>
    - Implanted blastocyst secrets HCG (doubling over 48h up to \> 1500IU/L) to rescue CL from luteolysis, to maintain progesterone secretion for 8 weeks until placenteal tissue takes over as the main source of progesterone
- **Clinical detection of pregnancy** - transvaginal pelvic USG:
    - <u>5 weeks</u> - detection of early intrauterine gestational sac
    - <u>5-6 weeks</u> - formation of yolk sac and fetal pole (first sign of embryo) within gestational sac
    - <u>6-8 weeks</u> - presence of fetal heart beat

## Miscarriage

- **Definition** - a pregnancy that ends spontaneously before 24 weeks of gestation
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - common, and occurs in 10-20% of clinical pregnancies
    - <u>Demographic</u> - risk increases w/ age, and a/w certain etiologies
- **Classification of miscarriages** - nature of presentation:
    - <u>Threatened miscarriage</u> - symptomatic, sonographic evidence of intra-uterine pregnancy w/ FH
    - <u>Inevitable miscarriage</u> - symptomatic, sonographic evidence of intra-uterine pregnancy w/o FH
    - <u>Incomplete miscarriage</u> - symptomatic, presence of retained products of conception within uterus
    - <u>Complete miscarriage</u> - previously symptomatic but resolved, sonographic evidence of empty uterus after natural miscarriage
    - <u>Missed miscarriage</u> - asymptomatic, with sonographic evidence of intra-uterine pregnancy w/o FH

  
  ![](img/types_of_miscarriage.png)
- **Etiology of miscarriages:**
    - Chromosomal abnormalities
    - Medical/ Endocrine disorders
    - Uterine abnormalities
    - Infections
    - Drugs/ chemicals
- **Clinical features of miscarriages** - depends on the type of miscarriage at presentation
    - <u>Asymptomatic</u> - typically occurs in complete miscarriages, where Sx has subsided, or missed miscarriages, which are incidentally discovered on booking visit
    - <u>Vaginal bleeding</u> - onset of vaginal bleeding where volume varies significantly, but patients often report passage of clots and tissue
    - <u>Abdominal pain</u> - cramping in nature and often associated w/ vaginal bleeding
- **Signs of miscarriage:**
    - <u>Primary assessment</u> (ABC) - risk of haemorrhagic shock
    - <u>Abdomino-pelvic examination</u> - assess whether cervical os open on speculum examination to determine type of miscarriage (note that pelvic-examination may be omitted if TVUSS can be arranged first)
- **Ix of miscarriage:**
    - **Transabdominal/ transvaginal pelvic USG** - single USG required for sonographic diagnosis of miscarriage
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, Rh status, T&S:
        - <u>CBC</u> - assess severity of anaemia of acute blood loss (NcNc anaemia)
        - <u>Rh status</u> - consider Anti-D prophylaxis for certain mothers
        - <u>T&S</u> - for blood transfusion
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - depending on clinical status, and patient preference:
        - <u>Expectant Mx</u> - offered if patient prefers this option to avoid surgery if pain and bleeding subside, but risk unplanned surgery if bleeding heavily (repeat urine pregnancy test after 3w and attend FU if +ve)
        - <u>Medical Mx</u> - Tx w/ mifepristone and misoprostol to induce passage of tissue
        - <u>Surgical Mx</u> - suction evaculation (or rarely vacuum aspiration) offered as day case, and is preferred if excessive bleeding and haemodynamically unstable
    - **Medical Tx** - increasingly applied to enable women to <u>miscarry at home</u>:
        - <u>Regimen:</u>
            - Mifepristone (progesterone antagonist) - esp. if \> 9 weeks' gestation
            - Misoprostol (prostaglandin antagonist) - single/ repeated, vaginal/ sublingual doses
        - <u>S/E:</u>
            - Pain (Tx w/ analgesics)
            - GI disturbance - e.g. N/V, diarrhoea (Tx with antiemetics)
        - <u>Clinical efficacy</u> - consider surgical Tx if continue to bleed excessively:
            - Tx failure - 10% failure rate
    - **Surgical Tx:**
        - <u>Indications</u>:
            - 1\. Patient preference
            - 2\. Excessive bleeding or haemodynamically unstable
        - <u>Technique</u>:
            - Manual vacuum aspiration under LA (only if women not comprimised)
            - Surgical evacuation under GA as day case (more common)
        - <u>Pre-treatment</u> - w/ vaginal or sublingual misoprostol to trigger cervical dilatation
        - <u>Complications</u>:
            - General surgical risks (GA risks)
            - Uterine perforation
            - Uterine trauma and haemorrhage
            - Post-operative pelvic infection
            - Cervical trauma - leading to subsequent cervical incompetence

## Ectopic Pregnancy

- **Definition** - implantation of a pregnancy outsied the normal uterine cavity
- **Terminology:**
    - <u>Pregnancy of unknown location</u> (PUL) - a working diagnosis characterised by +ve urine pregnancy test but TVUSS reveals empty uterus and absence of adnexal mass, unsure of whether pregnancy is in utero or ectopic
    - <u>Heterotropic pregnancy</u> - simultaneous development of two pregnancies - one within and one outside the uterine cavity
- **Sites of ectopic pregnancy:**
    - Fallopian tube (98%) - fibrae, ampulla (80%), isthmus
    - Fallopian tube interstitium
    - Ovary
    - Cervix
    - Abdominal cavity
    - Caesarean section scars
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Incidence</u> - 1/80 pregnancies (low incidence of heterotropic pregnancy \[1:25000\], but up to 1/100 in IVF)
    - <u>Mortality</u> - accounts for 9-13% maternal deaths in the Western World, and 10-30% of maternal deaths in developing countries
- **Risk factors for ectopic pregnancy:**
    - <u>Tubal damage</u> - previous ectopic pregnancy, pelvic infection (e.g. Chlamydia/ Gonorrhoea), previous tubal surgery (e.g. salpingotomy)
    - <u>Functional alterations of fallopian tube</u> - smoking, increased maternal age
    - <u>Additional risk factors</u>:
        - Subfertility and IVF
        - Conception on COC or morning after pill
        - Use of IUCD
        - Endometriosis
        - Previous abdominal surgery (e.g. caesarean section, appendicectomy)
- **Clinical features of ectopic pregnancy:**
    - <u>Abdominal pain</u> - subacute visceral abdominal pain
    - <u>Vaginal bleeding</u> - any volume of vaginal bleeding
    - <u>Presentation as ruptured abdominal pain</u> - clinical diagnosis if +ve pregnancy test:
        - Sudden diffuse parietal abdominal pain - w/ referred shoulder pain due to diaphragm irritation (i.e. phrenic nerve \[C3-5\])
        - Abdominal tenderness and varying peritoneal signs - due to haemoperitoneum
        - Hypovolaemic shock - due to intra-abdominal haemorrhage
- **Signs of ectopic pregnancy:**
    - <u>Primary assessment</u> (ABC) - assess haemodynamic comprimise in ectopic pregnancy
    - <u>Abdomino-pelvic examination</u> - detection of adnexal mass
- **Ix of ectopic pregnancy:**
    - **Transvaginal pelvic USG:**
        - <u>Findings that r/o ectopic pregnancy</u> - intrauterine gestation sac, yolk sac +/- fetal pole effectively confirms intrauterine pregnancy and r/o ectopic pregnancy (except for the rare heterotopic pregnancy)
        - <u>Findings suggestive of ectopic pregnancy</u> - empty uterus w/ adnexal mass (Sn 90%, Sp 95%)
        - <u>Findings suggestive of ruptured EP</u> - free fluid in pelvis
    - **Urine pregnancy test** - +ve result
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, Rh status, T&S
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Expectant Mx</u> - based on assumption that significant proportion of all EPs will resolve spontaneously w/o Tx (suitable for haemodynamcically stable and asymptomatic patients)
        - <u>Medical Mx</u> - methotrexate for haemodynamically stable, minimally symptomatic patients w/ serial FU
        - <u>Surgical Mx</u> - laparoscopic salpingectomy or salpingotomy
    - **Expectant Mx** - serial measurements of Sx and serum hCG until levels are undetectable
    - **Medical Mx** - IM methotrexate:
        - <u>MOA</u> - folic antagonist, inhibition of DNA synthesis in trophoblastic cells
        - <u>Dosing</u> - based on patient's body surface area (50mg/ m2)
        - <u>C/I</u>:
            - Chronic liver, renal or haematological disorders
            - Active infection
            - Immunodeficiency
            - Breast feeding
        - <u>S/E</u> - impact on cells with rapid turnover:
            - Stomatitis
            - Conjunctivitis
            - Gastrointestinal upset (2/3 patients suffer from non-specific abdominal pain)
            - Photosensitive skin reactions
        - <u>FU</u>:
            - Serial measurements at day 4, 7 (15% between 4-7d), and 11, then weekly thereafter until undetectable
            - Counselling on avoiding conception for 3 mo due to risk of teratogenicity
            - Lifestyle modifications - avoid alcohol and prolonged exposure to sunlight during Tx
    - **Surgical Tx:**
        - <u>Salpingectomy</u> (Tx of choice when contralateral tube viable) - removal of the Fallopian tube and the EP within
        - <u>Salpingostomy</u> (Tx of choice if contralateral tube absent or visibly damaged) - opening over Fallopian tube and extraction of EP (higher risk of subsequent EP)

# Chapter 11: Benign Conditions of the Ovary and Pelvis

## Ovarian Cysts

- **Definition** - benign tumours of the ovary, manifesting as acute pelvic pain, chronic pelvic pain (CPP) or presence of a pelvic mass
- **DDx of pelvic mass:**
    - **Gynaecological disorders:**
        - <u>Ovarian disorder</u> - benign ovarian cyst, complicated ovarian cyst, para-ovarian cyst, ovarian malignancy
        - <u>Tubal disorder</u> - ectopic pregnancy, hydrosalpinx, pyosalpinx, tubo-ovarian abscess, tubal malignancy
        - <u>Uterine disorders</u> - fibroids, uterine malignancy
        - <u>Pregnancy</u>
    - **Non-gynaeclological disorders:**
        - <u>GI disorders</u> - Intestinal obstruction, diverticular/appendicular abscess, intussusception, malignancy (esp. caecal carcinoma)
        - <u>Urological disorder</u> - hydronephrosis, renal/ bladder malignancy, pelvic kidney
        - <u>Others</u> - pelvic lymphocele, peritoneal cyst, lymphoma, neuroblastoma, aortic aneurysm, psoas muscle abscess
- **Classification of ovarian cysts:** 
![](img/types_of_benign_ovarian_cyst.png)
    - **Functional ovarian cysts** - follicular, corpus luteal and theca luteal cyst that appear as <u>simple unilocular cyst on USG</u>, where aetiology is unknown (reduced incidence w/ COCP):
        - <u>Folicular cysts</u> - arises from follicles growing to \> 3cm but rarely exceed 10cm (normal follicles are 2.5 cm)
        - <u>Corpus luteal cyst</u> - arises from the corpus luteum, and can complicate with ruputure or haemorrhage late in the menstrual cycle
        - <u>Theca luteal cyst</u> - functional ovarian cysts a/w pregnancy, particularly multiple pregnancy, but resolve spontaneously during pregnancy

    
    ![](img/ten_teachers_simple_ovarian_cyst.png)
    - **Inflammatory ovarian cysts** - ovarian cysts a/w pelvic inflammatory disease or endometriosis, and are common in young women:
        - <u>Tubo-ovarian abscess</u> - inflammatory mass with tubal, ovarian and bowel involvement, typically arising from PID, but ocassionally a/w other infective causes (appendicitis, diverticulitis)
        - <u>Endometrioma</u> - chocolate cysts reflecting abnormal deposition of endometrial tissue, having characteristic '**ground-glass**' appearance on USG
    - **Germ cell tumours** - most common ovarian tumours in F 20-40y (50%), particularly dermoid tumours (cystic teratoma) is the most common form
    - **Epithelial tumours** - benign neoplasms arising from epithelial cells and a/w increasing frequency in age and are most common in peri-menopausal women:
        - <u>Serous cystadenomas</u> (20-30% in \< 40y) - typically unilocular and unilateral
        - <u>Mucinous cystadenomas</u> - large multiloculated cysts, and bilateral in 10% of cases
    - **Sex cord stromal tumour** - solid ovarian tumours composed of stromal cells presenting in older women:
        - <u>Ovarian fibroma</u> - most common solid ovarian tumour, typically presenting w/ torsion (due to heaviness of ovary) or ocassionally Meig syndrome (pleural effusion, ascites, ovarian fibroma)
        - <u>Thecoma</u> - benign estrogen-secreting tumour presenting after menopause, resulting in post-menopausal bleeding (capable of inducing endometrial carcinoma)
- **Clinical features of ovarian cyst:**
    - <u>Chronic pelvic pain</u> - intermittent, chronic poorly-localised dull pain felt over the pelvis, which may be lateralised
    - <u>Pelvic mass</u> - patient detects pelvic mass caused by large ovarian cyst
    - <u>Urinary Sx</u> - caused by direct pressure on the bladder
    - <u>Bowel Sx</u> - caused by pressure on the bowel
- **Signs of ovarian cyst:**
    - <u>Abdominal examination</u> - palpable abdominal mass if large
    - <u>Pelvic examination</u> - adnexal mass on bimanual examination which may be tender
- **Complications of ovarian cyst:**
    - Torsion
    - Rupture
    - Haemorrhage

## Endometriosis

- **Definition** - common condition characterised by endometrial tissue lying outside the uterine cavity (cf adenomyosis is deposition within myometrium, i.e. within the uterus)
- **Sites of endometrial tissue deposition in endometriosis** - usually found within the pelvis:
    - Pelvic wall - along the peritoneal linings
    - Pouch of Douglas
    - Uterosacral ligaments
    - Bladder
    - Ovaries (Endometrioma with old, altered blood, hence known as 'chocolate cyst')
    - Rare site - umbilicus, old abdominal scars, pleural cavity
- **Epidemiology of endometriosis:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 5-10% of women of reproductive age (found in 33% of women undergoing diagnostic laparoscopy for CPP or infertility)
    - <u>Incidence</u> - estrogen-dependent condition, hence only presents w/ women of reproductive age (resolves after menopause)
- **Etio-pathophysiology of endometriosis** - known to recur but reassure will subside at menopause:
    - **Genetic and immunological predisposition** - increased incidence in first-degree relatives of patients w/ endoetriosis, and racial difference (increased incidence in oriental women)
    - **Deposition of endometrial tissue with pelvis** - patho-mechanism unknown but two competing theories implicated:
        - <u>Sampson's implantation theory</u> - retrograde menstrual regurgitation of viable endometrial glands and tissue along Fallopian tubes and subsequently implanted in pelvic peritoneal surface
        - <u>Meyer's 'coelomic metaplasia' theory</u> - de-differentiation of peritoneal cells into embryonic cells, and subsequent differentiation into endometrial cells under homronal or inflammatory stimulus
    - **Haematogenous or lymphatic spread of pelvic endometrial tissue** - some reports of vascular and lymphatic embolisation to distant sites demonstrated and explains <u>rare findings of endometriosis outside periotoneal cavity</u> (e.g. lung)
    - **Estrogen-responsiveness of endometriotic tissue** - responds to cyclical hormonal changes of the female menstrual cycle, resulting in <u>cyclical bleeding</u> and <u>local inflammatory reactions</u>
    - **Effects of local inflammation** - regular repeated bleeding and healing induces fibrosis and adhesion formation between pelvic organs, resulting in pain, infertility, and in severe cases, 'frozen pelvis'
- **Endometriosis and infertility** - 30-40% of patients w/ endometriosis complain of difficulty in conceiving:
    - **Potential pathomechanisms of subfertility:**
        - Anatomical distortion - due to extensive fibrosis and tethering of pelvic organs
        - Destruction of ovarian tissue - due to inflammatory endometrioma

    
    ![](img/pathomechanism_of_infertility_in_endometriosis.png)
    - **Effects of Tx:**
        - <u>Medical Tx</u> - no effects on fertility and should not be given to patients wishing to conceive
        - <u>Surgical Tx</u> - improved fertility chances (but unsure whether increased natural or IVF pregnancy rates, but current consensus is to leave endoemtrioma alone prior to IVF unless symptomatic)
- **Clinical features of endometriosis:**
    - **Pelvic pain** (dysmenorrhoea) - classically a cyclical, non-colicky pelvic pain restricted pelvic pain, but some report a chronic-non-cyclical pelvic pain:
        - <u>Onset and duration</u> - Sx starts few day before menses, and lasts until end of pelvis
        - <u>Quality</u> - usually non-colicky (rarely presenting as colicky pain around menstrual period, but often a/w IBS)
        - <u>Radiation</u> - depends on the site of involvement (see below)
        - <u>Severity</u> - variable, and well recognised that there is lack of correlation between extent of disease and intensity of Sx
    - **Heavy menstrual bleeding** (HMB) - ocassionally a/w pelvic pain
    - **Pain related to site of deposition:**
        - <u>Deep dyspareunia</u> - key indicator of endometriosis within pouch of Douglas
        - <u>Dyschezia</u> - key indicator of endometriosis within pouch of Douglas

    
    ![](img/sx_of_endometriosis_in_relation_to_site.png)
    - **Infertility** - occurs in 30-40% patients w/ endometriosis postulated to be related to ovarian damage, and anatomical distortion
- **Signs of endometriosis** - non-specific and unable to r/o chronic PID, and malignancies:
    - <u>Vaginal examination</u> - usually normal
    - <u>Bimanual examination</u>:
        - Pelvic tenderness
        - Uterus - fixed and retroverted
        - Adnexal mass
        - Thickening and nodularity of uterosacral ligament
    - <u>PR examination</u> - tenderness in pouch of Douglas
- **Ix** - most Ix are limited where presence of absence of signs are unable to rule in or rule out a diagnosis:
    - **Transvaginal pelvic ultrasound** (TVUSS) - detection of endometrioma and rectal endometriosis:
        - <u>Test properties</u> - limited in diagnosis of endometriosis especially for smaller lesions (**-ve findings do not r/o disease**)
        - <u>Findings suggestive of endometriosis</u>:
            - Endometrioma - classically unilocular, low-level homogenous cystic structure 
            ![](img/endometriosis_usg.png)
            - Kissing ovaries - ovaries fixed together, or fixed to back of uterus
    - **MRI** - can detect lesions \> 5mm in deep tissue, indicated for presurgical planning in difficult cases
    - **Diagnostic laparoscopy** - remains the traditional method for diagnosis:
        - <u>Principles</u>:
            - Visual diagnosis of endometriotic lesion (surgeon-dependent) - red, puckered black matchstick appearance, or white and fibrotic
            - Bx for histological confirmation
            - Therapeutic - surgical diathermy and/or excision of endometriotic lesion
            - Staging of disease

    
    ![](img/endometriosis_in_laparoscopic_view.png)
- **Dx** - presumptive diagnosis clinically and based on normal TVUSS (definitive diagnosis by laparoscopy only if not responding to Tx)
- **Associated conditions:**
    - GI disturbances - IBS, constipation
    - Psychological issues
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Modalities of Tx</u>:
            - Medical Tx - analgesics and hormonal ovarian suppression for effective Tx of cyclical and non-cyclical pain
            - Surgical Tx - only if symptomatic relief not obtained after 3-6mo of medical Tx
        - <u>Considerations for Tx</u>:
            - Patient reassurence - little progression of disease overtime, even if known to recur
            - Individualistic Tx - based on age, Sx, extent of disease, and **desire to have children**
            - Holistic care - Tx of associated psychological issues with CPP, and associated GI comorbidities (e.g. IBS, constipation)
    - **Patient reassurance** - most patients have psychological distress in a/w pain and would benefit from understanding the nature of condition:
        - <u>Recurring disease</u> - known to recur in reproductive life, hence impossible to offer a cure, but will subside w/ menopause as disease is estrogen-dependent
        - <u>Unlikely progression of disease</u> - most patients have little progression of disease over time
        - <u>Role of diagnostic laparoscopy</u> - some patients will be relieved by a +ve laparoscopy that validates their symptoms
    - **Medical Tx** - analgesics and hormonal ovarian suppression:
        - **Analgesics** - for symptomatic relief:
            - <u>NSAIDS</u> - drugs of choice and are helpful in reducing severity of dysmenorrhoea and pelvic pain
            - <u>Codeine/ opiates</u> - <u>avoided</u> as may worsen co-existing IBS symptoms, exacerbating pelvic pain symptoms
        - **Hormonal ovarian suppression** - to induce a pseudomenopausal state:
            - **Combined oral contraceptive pills** - indicated for all patients in absence of contraindications not wishing to conceive:
                - <u>Regimen</u> - continued for several years until pregnancy is desired:
                    - Standard regimen for contraception (taken sequentially w/ 7d pill-break) - offers some degree of symptomatic relief
                    - Tricycling (3 packets taken back-to-back-to-back) - induces amenorrhoea and most effective in inducing symptomatic relief
                - <u>Clinical efficacy</u> - demonstrateed:
                    - Alleivation of endometriosis-associated dyspareunia
                    - Alleviation of dysmenorrhoea
                    - Alleviation of non-menstrual pelvic pain
                    - Cycle control
                    - Effective contraception
            - **Progesterone** - for those where COCP are C/I:
                - <u>Selection</u>:
                    - Long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARCS)
                    - Depot-medroxyprogesterone acetate
                    - Levogestrel intrauterine system (Merina)
                - <u>Clinical efficacy</u> - long-term therapeutic effect particularly **after surgical Tx**
            - **Gonadotrophin-releasing hormone agonst** - established in the diagnosis and treatment of endometriosis (long-term use is precluded due to S/E)
    - **Surgical Tx:**
        - **Fertility sparing surgery** - performed during diagnostic laparotomy:
            - <u>Principles</u>:
                - Excision or ablation of endometriotic deposition
                - Drainage of endometrioma +/- cystectomy - cystectomy a/w damage to functional ovarian tissue (not considered if as an adjunct to fertility Tx)
            - <u>Risk of recurrence</u> - up to 30% hence long-term medical therapy often necessary and started straight after surgery
        - **Hysterectomy and oophorectomy** - indicated if family complete and failed to respond to conservative Tx

## Benign Endometrial Lesions

### Endometrial polyps

- **Definition** - focal endometrial outgrowths containing variable amounts of glands, stroma and blood vessels
- **Pathophysiology of endometrial polyps:**
    - <u>Develop from excessive estrogen exposure</u> - focal endometrial proliferation resulting in formation hyperplasia and formation of sessile or frankly pedunculated masses
    - <u>Polyps are non-responsive to estrogen</u> - most polyps are relatively insensitive to cyclical hormonal changes leading them to persist and cause unscheduled vaginal bleeding
- **Risk factors of endometrial polyps:**
    - Obesity
    - Early menarche and late menopause
    - Use of tamoxifen
    - Use of hormone replacement therapy
- **Clinical features of endometrial polyps:**
    - <u>Asymptomatic</u> - common in women a/w increased estrogen exposure but may not cause any Sx
    - <u>Abnormal uterine bleeding</u> - accounts for 20% of women w/ AUB, including heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB), intermenstrual bleeding (IMB) and post-menopausal bleeding (PMB)
    - <u>Subfertility</u> - present in 10% of women w/ subfertility
- **Ix and Dx:**
    - **Transvaginal pelvic ultrasound** - increased sensitivity to detect focal intracavity pathologies by saline infusion sonography (SIS)
    - **Hysteroscopy** - polypectomy indicated in order to alleviate Sx (AUB), optimize fertility and <u>exclude hyperplasia and malignancy</u>
- **Mx** - polypectomy as above

### Asherman syndrome

- **Definition** - irreversible damae of the single layer thick basal endometrium impairing normal regeneration of endometrium, resulting in fibrosis and intra-uterine adehesion formation
- **Etiology of asherman syndrome:**
    - Uterine infection (e.g. endometritis)
    - Overzealous curettage of the uterine cavity during miscarriage or following secondary PPH
- **Mx:**
    - <u>Hysteroscopic adhesiolysis</u> - often difficult and risk further uterine damage

## Benign Lesions of the Myometrium

### Fibroids

- **Definition** - also known as leiomyoma, a well-demarcated, firm whorled tumour arising in the myometrium
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - estimated present in 40% of women overall (most are asymptomatic)
    - <u>Incidence</u> - more common with advancing reproductive age (but unlikely present after menopause)
- **Risk factors of fibroids:**
    - Nilliparity
    - Obese
    - FHx
    - African descent
- **Sites of fibroids:**
    - Pednuculated
    - Submucosal
    - Intramural
    - Subserosal

  
  ![](img/sites_of_uterine_fibroids.png)
- **Pathophysiology of fibroids:**
    - <u>Estrogen dependent tumour</u> - growth of fibroids dependent on estrogen, and thus can **enlarge during pregnancy** in response to hyperestrogenic state and **shrink after menopause** when estrogen production ceases
    - <u>Increased irregularity of uterine cavity</u> - results in **increased endometrial surface area** resulting in HMB and dysmenorrhoea
- **Natural Hx of fibroids:**
    - **Progressive enlargement of fibroids** - enlargement of fibroids due to accumulative estrogen exposure over reproductive age, resulting in onset of S/S
    - **Acute degenerative changes:**
        - <u>Red degenration</u> - haemorrhage and necrosis in response to outgrowing their blood supply, typically presenting in the second trimester of pregnancy as acute abdomen
        - <u>Hyaline degeneration</u> - asymptomatic softening and liquefaction of the fibroid
        - <u>Cystic</u> - asymptomatic central necrosis leaving cystic spaces at the centre, and rarely a/w malignant or sarcomatous degeneration within the cyst (1:350 cases), typically manifesting as rapid enlargement of fibroid in a post-menopausal women
    - **Torsion** - ischaemia and infarction of a <u>pedunculated fibroid</u>
- **Clinical features of fibroids:**
    - **Asymptomatic** - vasty majority of small fibroids are asymptomatic but may be incidentally detected on abdominal examination revealing a <u>pelvic mass</u> (need not be treated)
    - **Abnormal uterine bleeding** - usually heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB) and rarely IMB due to increased endometrial surface area
    - **Pressure Sx:**
        - <u>Urinary frequency</u> - compression of the bladder anteriorly
        - <u>Constipation or tenesmus</u> - compression of the rectum posteriorly
    - **Subfertility** - due to mechanical distortion affecting implantation of blastocysts, or occlusion of the fallopian tube
    - **Pain** - unusual and only occurs in torsion of pedunculated fibroid or red degeneration of fibroid during pregnancy
- **Signs of fibroids:**
    - <u>General examination</u> - pallor
    - <u>Abdominal examination</u> - visible and/or palpable abdominal mass arising from the pelvis
    - <u>Bimanual examination</u> - enlarged, smooth/ **irregular**, non-tender uterus (tenderness indicates red degeneration)
- **Ix:**
    - **CBC** - assess severity of anaemia (invariably indicates presence of significant fibroids)
    - **Abdominopelvic ultrasound** (TAUSS and TVUSS) - mainstay for Dx:
        - <u>TVUSS</u> - good for detecting and locating submucous fibroids and small intramural fibroids, which appears as **well-circumscribed, isoechoic mass within the myometrium**
        - <u>TAUSS</u> - good for larger intramural and subserosal fibroids and exclusion of <u>hydronephrosis</u>
    - **Additional Ix:**
        - <u>Saline infusion sonography</u> - good for detection of submucosal fibroids
        - <u>Hysteroscopy</u> - for detection of other pathologies and surgical planning
        - <u>MRI</u> - indicated prior to UAE by location of fibroids
- **Dx** - sonographic Dx
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Asymptomatic</u> - conservative Mx if asymptomatic or if subfertility only problem (surgical risk of extensive bleeding and hysterectomy as high as 1%)
        - <u>Symptomatic</u> - surgical and radiological Tx, with little role of medical Tx
    - **Medical Tx** - little role for conventional medical Tx for HMB (e.g. Merina, tranexamic acid, NSAID, COCP) as ineffective:
        - **Gonadotrophin-releasing hormone agonist** - to induce menopausal state (but poorly tolerated due to severe menopausal Sx)
    - **Surgical Tx** - depends on presenting complaint and patients aspirations for menstrual function and fertility:
        - **Minimally invasive hysteroscopic removal of submucousal fibroids** - to resolve HMB Sx even in the presence of other types of fibroids
        - **Myomectomy** - removal of fibroids w/ uterine conservtion (small but significant risk of uncontrolled bleeding leading to hysterectomy)
        - **Hysterectomy** - removal of uterus
    - **Radiological Tx** - uterine artery embolisastion (UAE) by interventional radiologists (reduction of fibroid volume by 50%)
