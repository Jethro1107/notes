# Approach to Gram Negative Rods

- **Overiew:**
    - GNR are classified by aerobic status
    - Most aerobic GNR are typically sensitive to beta-lactams w/ good GN coverage, but there is a rising incidence of ESBL and even CPE, while non-fermenters have extensive antimicrobial resistance
    - Anaerobic GNR may require coverage by Metronidazole
- **GNR** - also classified by aerobic status:
    - <u>Aerobic/ facultative anaerobic GNR</u> - enterobacteriaceae, vibrionaceae and related genera, non-fermenters, others and of clinical significance
    - <u>Anaerobes GNR</u> - bacterioides, fusobacterium
- **Aerobic and facultatively anaerobic GNR:**
    - **Enterobacterales** - many are GI commensals, and are involved in intra-abdominal infections:
        - **Heterogenous group:**
            - <u>Within gut</u> - E. coli, Klebsiella, proteus, enterobacter, citrobacter, morganella, serratia
            - <u>Acquired</u> - Salmonella, Shigella, Yersinia
        - **Clinical associations** - intra-abdominal and urogenital disorders:
            - Urinary tract infections
            - Abdominal infections (e.g. biliary sepsis)
            - Diarrheal syndromes
            - May subsequently spread to distant sites (e.g. meningitis, osteoarticular infections)
        - **ABx resistance** - generally Sn to beta-lactams w/ G- coverage (e.g. 3rd generation cephalosporine), except:
            - <u>ESBL producers</u> - resistant to beta-lactams except carbapenems
            - <u>CPE</u> (carbapenemase producing enterobacterales)
    - **Vibrionaceae and related genera:**
        - **Vibrio species:**
            - <u>V. cholerae</u> - classified into O1 & O139 (causing cholera), and Non-O1, Non-O139 (causing local outbreaks of diarrhoea disease)
            - <u>V. parahaemolyticus</u> - causes outbreaks of food poisoning
            - <u>V. vulnificus</u> - causes necrotising fasciitis
        - **Related generae** - Aeromonas, Plesiomonas
    - **Non-fermenters** - do not ferment glucose or other sugars, most are <u>environmental</u>:
        - **Important ecauses:**
            - Pseudomonas aeruginosa
            - Acinetobacter baumannii
            - Stenotrophomonas maltophlia
            - Burkholderia cepacia complex
            - Burkholderia pseudomallei (mellioidosis)
        - **Clinical associations** - mainly noscocomial infections:
            - Severe pneumonia
            - Wound infections
            - Prosthesis-associated infections
            - Chronic chest infections by P. aeruginosa and Burkholderia spp. in cystic fibrosis and bronchiectasis patients
    - **Other aerobic/ facultatively anaerobic GNR of clinical significance:**
        - Haemophilus influenzae - a/w meningitis (Hib), epiglotitis (Hib), sinusitis, otitis media
        - Legionella pneumophilia - a/w CAP
        - Campylobacter - a/w GE
        - H. pylori - a/w peptic ulcer disease and CA stomach
        - Brucella - systemic bacterial infection or febrile illness
        - HACEK group - fastidious organisms and rare cause of infective endocarditis
- **Anaerobic GNR** - normal oral cavity and gut commensals causing invasive disease when opportunity arises; add on <u>metronidazole</u>:
    - **Fusobacterium** - oral commensals that gains access to blood stream during head and neck infections (e.g. simple tonisillitis):
        - <u>Lemierre's disease</u> - multifocal lung abscesses due to access to deep neck spaces and subsequently internal jugular vein thrombophlebitis
        - <u>Brain abscesses</u> - by access to vessels to intracranial cavity
    - **Bacterioides fragilis** - a/w severe intra-abdominal sepsis
