# Principles of Valvular Heart Disease

- **A diseased valve may be stenosed, or incompetent:**
    - **Stenosis** - lumen becomes narrowed, impeding blood flow
    - **Incompetence** (regurgitation) - lumen incompetent, enabling regurgitation of blood into proximal chamber
- **Pathophysiology:**
    - **Stenosis** - proximal chamber initially hypertrophies, then dilates as it fails
    - **Regurgitation** - chambers on either side dilates
- **Principle etiology of valvular diseases:** 
![](img/principal_etiology_of_valvular_diseases.png)
    - <u>Valvular stenosis</u> - congenital, rheumatic heart disease, senile degeneration
    - <u>Regurgitation</u> - congenital, rheumatic heart disease (acute and chronic), IE, dilitation (valvular ring dilitation, syphilitic aortitis), traumatic valve rupture, damage to chordae and papillary muscles
- **Clinical features** - variable for different valvular heart diseases:
    - <u>S/S of left HF</u> - chronic exertional dyspnoea, orthopnoea, PND, low output Sx (e.g. fatigue)
    - <u>S/S of right HF</u> - ankle oedema, hepatic pain, raised JVP etc.
    - <u>Chest pains</u> - due to myocardial ischaemia
    - <u>Palpitations</u> - typically due to AF
    - <u>Complications</u> - cardioembolism, infective endocarditis, pregnancy complications
- **Ix in valvular heart disease:**
    - <u>CXR</u> - detection of HF and chamber enlargements
    - <u>ECG</u> - detection of chamber enlargements, AF
    - <u>Echocardiogram</u> - assessment of valvular architecture, chamber size, chamber functions (e.g. LVEF)
    - <u>Doppler echo</u> - measurement of valvular gradients, demonstration of regurgitation jets
    - <u>Pharmacological stress echo</u> (e.g. low-dose dobutamine) - detection of ischaemia, and contractile reserve
    - <u>Cardiac catheterization</u> - indicated if valvular surgery is to be performed:
        - Assessment of associated CAD, pressure gradient (stenosis), injected contrast (regurgitation)
