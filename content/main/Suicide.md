# Suicide

- **Suicide risk assessment may takes place in 3 settings:**
    - When an individual has yet to express suicide intent
    - When an individual has expressed suicidal ideations
    - When probing safety and further suicide intent after an attempted suicide
- **Suicide risk assessment generally applies the same principles to other risk assessments:**
    - Temporal assessment based on past, present, and future
    - Standard entry points to smoothen out the interview
    - Open-ended questions w/ progressive narrowing of the questions
- **Assessment or general probing of suicidal ideations in an individual who has yet to express suicide intent:**
    - **Entry point** - finding a good entry point for risk assessment for assessing suicidal intent to make the interview less awkward and increases chance of patient to open up:
        - <u>Insomnia</u> generally is a good entry point, but a pre-requisite is that both physician and patient has to be on the same page regarding the <u>cause of insomnia being low mood, stress, or generally distressing mental phenomenon</u> (e.g. AHs)
        - <u>Evidence of negative cognition</u> (particularly <u>hopelessness</u>) also serves as a goood entry point, for seeing no possible improvements in future life is possibly a risk factor for suicide ideations and attempt
        - The two can be linked together tactfully, i.e. in a depressed patient, insomnia, results in rolling in the bed, and the mind wanders. Further questioning on what the patient is thinking when one cannot falls asleep likely yield evidence of negative cognition
        - Rather than directly saying if the patient feels hopeless regarding the future (i.e. putting words into patient's mouths), ask a <u>general question on how they view the future</u>
        - Other good entry points may depend on the patient's source of suffering, and must be adapted, e.g. sense of entrapment, psychic pain, irresolvable stressors
    - **Eased progression when diving into suicidal ideations** - do not abruptly connect hopelessness to self harm/ suicide, rather use a <u>graded approach progressively explore passive suicidal ideations to active suicidal ideations</u>:
        - The canonical canned phrase is a sudden jump to conclusion that general hopelessness equates the genesis of ideations of self-harm or suicide: "When some people feel sad, they may have fleeting thoughts of harming one-self. Have you had similar thoughts?" Direct quote from modified SCID-5: 事情是否差得以致你常常想到死或覺得也許死了更好？你想到過結束自己的生命嗎?
        - Better to explore the logical connection from hopelessness to the genesis of ideas of self harm, which is expressed as a <u>gradient from passive to active suicidal ideations</u>
        - Passive suicidal ideations refers to <u>fleeting ideas of non-existence</u> w/o an actual plan of self harm, it can be explored again by a graded approach:
            - Always try to address their stressful reality, i.e. the burden of being a living person at that point in time
            - (Assuming patient has claimed to be hopeless) Have you ever felt so hopeless that you do not find the motivation to face the new day? (「你有冇試過覺得絕望到，連迎接第二日嘅動力都冇？」, 「有時人去到好攰、好無助嘅時候，會覺得唔想再面對聽日。你有冇出現過呢種感覺？」)
            - Have you ever thought that it would be easier if you did not have to wake up in the morning to face the day? (你有冇試過覺得，如果聽朝唔使醒嚟面對呢一日，反而會容易啲？」)
            - Have you ever thought that it would be better if you just never woken up from your sleep? (「你有冇試過諗過，如果瞓咗之後唔再醒返，可能仲好？」)
            - I can tell that these are very trying times for you, have you ever had thoughts of no longer wanting to be a person/ not existing in this world? (「有時人去到好攰、好無助嘅時候，會覺得唔想再面對聽日。你有冇出現過呢種感覺？」, 「你有冇試過有一刻，覺得自己唔想再存在？」)
        - Active suicidal ideations refers to ideas of/ or actions (i.e. self harm or suicide) that <u>actively aims of achieving this permanent state of non-existence</u> - when finished exploring passive suicide ideations, it is best to openly ask about EVER having the idea of self harm
        - However some people are more impulsive and the flow from passive to active ideation is less obvious (these are extremely high risk for attempt)
- **Assessment of current suicidal ideations in an individual who has expressed active suicidal ideations:**
    - **Characterise most recent suicidal ideations** - how frequent, most recent, when occur and associations with stressors, contents of thoughts, planning, timing and preparation, wills and getting affairs in place, perceived lethality:
        - <u>Baseline frequency and duration</u> (i.e. very frequent; fleeting vs persistent) - as a baseline
        - <u>Basic timing and associations w/ stressors</u>:
            - Occurs during insomnia or in the morning in a/w EMW and morning depression
            - Associations w/ reminders of the stressors
        - <u>Characterisation of most recent episode</u>:
            - Detailed contents of thought (i.e. what was the thought, how, when)
            - Planning, timing and preparation (i.e. exactly how to perform, what are preparations needed, and if these preparations are done)
            - Getting will and affairs in place
            - Concealment (e.g. directly questioned, or inferred from timing of attempt and location of attempt)
            - Perceived lethality
    - **If patient did not go through with the plan, assess** (otherwise if admitted for attempt, likely better to assess future intent \[see below\]):
        - What exactly stopped patient from doing so
        - What is the perceived chances of going through with it at that moment
    - **Assess currentness of suicidal ideations** - whether the patient feels the same way now
- **Assessment of future suicide intent after a suicide attempt:**
    - **Difficulties in assesment of a patient w/ failed suicide attempt:**
        - <u>Concealment</u> - those w/ attempts are the most determined, and if persistent, they may coonceal their thoughts towards their doctors in hopes of early discharge and repeat the attempt
    - **Approach to assessment** - not in a way to distrust or over-interpret what the patient says, but certain aspects are predictive to future suicide attempt:
        - Initial response to failed suicide attempt - as first question usually as patients are more willing to open up regarding this, on the basis that the initial emotional reaction is usually genuine after realising a failed suicide attempt (N.B. smart patients are capable ot concealing even this aspect thus require additional questions)
        - Detailed changes - ask patient to detailing the reason of abrupt change in suicide intent (do not accept vague answers such as 諗通咗, rather more concrete actionable answers)
        - Future plans - regarding active solving stressors, and detailed planning for future life
        - Social support - family and close friends
