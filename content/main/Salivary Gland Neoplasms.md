# Salivary Gland Neoplasms

- **Spectrum of salivary gland tumours** - uncommon heterogenous group of neoplasms that vary considerably in anatomical site of origin, histology, and biologic behaviour:
    - **Benign vs malignant salivary gland tumours** - 2017 WHO classification of salivary gland tumours:
        - <u>Benign salivary gland tumours</u> - pleomorphic adenoma, Warthin's tumour
        - <u>Malignant salivary gland tumours</u> - mucoepidermoid carcinoma, adenoid cystic carcinoma, acini cell carcinoma, carcinoma ex-pleomorphic adenoma, sqyaniys cell carcinoma, lymphoepithelial carcinoma etc.
    - **Anatomical distribution of salivary gland tumours:**
        - <u>Most common site of salivary gland neoplasm</u> - parotid glands (accounts for 85% of salivary gland neoplasms)
        - <u>Relationship between glandular size and malignancy risk</u> - the larger the gland, the more likely it is benign:
            - **Parotid gland** - 75% benign, 25% malignant
            - **Submandibular gland** - 50% benign, 50% malignant
            - **Sublingual and minot salivary glands** - 25% benign, 75% malignant
- **Epidemiology** - rare (20% of H&N neoplasms)
- **Risk factors of salivary gland neoplasms** - no unifying risk factors:
    - **Radiation exposure** - a/w development of both benign and malignant salivary gland tumours (historically the relationship was discovered due to an increased incidence of salivary gland tumours in atomic bomb survivors in Japan), for prior radiation therapy e.g. Hodgkin lymphoma
    - **Smoking** - Warthin's tumour (benign tumour of salivary glands) has strong association with smoking, while correlation with other salivary gland tumour is less clear
    - **Viral infections:**
        - <u>Epstein Barr Virus</u> (EBV) - a/w lymphoepithelial carcinoma based on data in endemic regions
        - <u>Human papillomavirus</u> (HPV) - some associations w/ mucoepidermoid carcinoma (controversial)
        - <u>Humman immunodeficiency virus</u> (HIV) - generalised increased risk of malignancy, but not an AIDS-defining cancer
    - **Environmental exposures** - e.g. rubber manufacturing, hair dressers etc.
- **Clinical presentation of salivary gland neoplasms:**
    - **Features of local disease:**
        - <u>Salivary gland swelling</u> - typically in a major salivary gland tumour, chronic, progressive, **painless** swelling of the parotid, submandibular or sublingual gland is a chief complaint
        - <u>Facial weakness</u> - peripheral facial nerve palsy is generally indicative of a **malignant tumour**
        - <u>Mucosal ulceration or submucosal mass</u> - typical manifestation of minor salivary gland tumours
    - **Features of local invasion:**
        - <u>Nasal obstruction</u> - extension into the nasal cavity
        - <u>Trismus</u> - extension into maxillary sinuses
        - <u>vision changes or other cranial nerve involvement</u> - intracranial extension by NP minor salivary gland tumours
    - **Features of LN metastasis** - regional lymphadenopathy:
        - <u>Parotid malignancies</u> - intraparotid LN, followed by Level I and II cervical LNs
        - <u>Submandibular malignancies</u> - adjacent perivascular nodes and into cervical region
        - <u>Sublingual or minor salivary malignancies</u> - retropharyngeal LNs, and level I cervical LNs
    - **Features of metastatic disease** - lungs, bones, liver
- **Ix and Dx evaluation** - worked up as a salivary gland swelling:
    - **USG** - timely, costeffective imaging which can facilitate tissue sampling
    - **Cross-sectional imaging** (CT, PET-CT, MRI) - assess local anatomy
    - +/- **Tissue Bx**
