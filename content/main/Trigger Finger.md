# Trigger Finger

- **Definition** - a common work-induced condition characterised by stenosing tenosynovitis of the flexor sheath, eventually resulting in a finger locked in a flexed position
- **Epidemiology** - a common work-induced condition:
	- _Prevalence_ - lifetime risk of 2.6% in the population
	- _Demographic_:
		- Age - most common in 5-6th decade of life
		- Comorbidities - a/w T1DM, RA, gout, hypothyroidism, and amyloidosis
- **Pathophysiology of trigger finger:**
	- _Wear and tear of flexor tendons resulting in inflammation_ - repeated flexion and extension results in attrition wear and tear, fibrillation, and fragmentation, resulting in swelling, oedema, and inflammation
	- _Formation of a nodule_ - chronic inflammation and fibrosis results in formation of a nodule (a thickening in the tendon)
	- _Pulley effect of the fibro-osseous tunnel_ - upon flexion of finger, the thickened nodule becomes trapped proximal to the tunnel, locking the fingers in a flexed position

- **Clinical features of trigger finger:**
	- _Locking of the affected finger in flexed position_ - the finger is trapped due to the pulley effect:
		- Active extension - unable to perform full active extension of the finger
		- Passive extension - the finger can be extended passively w/ the other hand
	- _Triggering_ - palpable crepitus and tenderness when pulp of affected finger rubs over the nodule
- **Anatomical features of trigger finger:**
	- Most commonly affects 1st and 4th digit
	- Nodule typically occurs at the level of the metacarpal hand
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Injections_ - injection of corticosteroids near the nodular swelling under the tendon sheath (70% success rate)
		- _Surgery_ - simple and effective; reserved for when injections unsuccessful
