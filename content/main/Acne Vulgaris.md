# Acne Vulgaris

- **Definition** - an extremely common condition occuring after puberty, chronic inflammation of the pilosebaceous unit
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - affects 90% of adolescents at some point
    - <u>Demographic</u> - adolescence (but can persist into 30-40y)
- **Etiolo-pathophysiology of acne vulagris:**
    - Increased sebum production (increased by androgens and progesterone and reduced by estrogen)
    - Colonisation of pilosebaceous ducts by Propionibacterium acnes
    - Release of inflammatory mediators and local inflammation
    - Hypercornification and occlusion of pilosebaceous ducts
- **Clinical features of acne vulgaris:**
    - **Comedones** - hallmarks of acne vulgaris, which are <u>non-inflammatory lesions</u>:
        - <u>Open comedones</u> (blackheads) - dilated keratin-filled follicle, which appear as black papules due to keratin debris
        - <u>Closed comedones</u> (whiteheads) - caused by accumulation of sebum and keratin deeper in the pilosebaceous ducts
    - **Inflammatory lesions** - inflammatory papules, nodules and cysts may occur and arise from the comedones, which may subsequently scar or become keloidal
- **Clinical variants of acne vulgares:**
    - <u>Acne conglobata</u> - characterised by comedones, nodules, abscesses, sinuses, and cysts usually w/ <u>marked scarring</u>, which is rare and affects addult male
    - <u>Acne fulminans</u> - rare but severe presentation of acne in association w/ systemic inflammatory response characterised by fever, arthralgia, raised neutrophils, plasma viscositis
    - <u>Acne excoriee</u> - self-inflicted excoriation due to compulsive picking of pre-existing or imagined acne lesions
    - <u>Secondary acne</u> - acne with an identifiable cause:
        - **Exposure** - e.g. greasy cosmetics, occupational exposure to oils, tars, or aromatic hydrocarbons
        - **Drugs** - OC pills, lithium, anticonvulsants, and **cetuximab**
        - **Endocrinopathies** - e.g. hyper-androgenism (e.g. PCOS, androgen secreting tumour)
- **Ix** - pursued for work up of secondary causes if other evidence of hirsutism and virilisation
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - **Approach to Mx** - escalation of medical Tx by severity of disease:
            - <u>Mild disease</u> - consider topican therapy (benzoyl peroxide, retinoids, azelaic acid, topical macrolides)
            - <u>Moderate disease</u> - isoretinoin +/- prolonged-course of systemic tetracycline/ erythromycin/ trimetroprim
            - <u>Severe disease</u> - isoretinoin +/- systemic steroids
        - **Modalities of Tx:**
            - <u>Topical therapy</u> - e.g. benzoyl peroxidase, azelaic acid, retinoids, macrolides
            - <u>Systemic thereapy</u> - ABx, isoretinoin, steroids
            - <u>Phototherapy</u> - UVB/ PDT
            - <u>Surgical Mx</u> - incision and drainage of acne nodules or scars
