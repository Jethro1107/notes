# Anorexia Nervosa

- **Definition** - a major eating disorder characterised by 1) significantly low body weight maintained by restriction of energy intake, 2) concerns and fears of gaining weight, and 3) disturbances in experience of body weight and image
- **Epidemiology:**
	- _Prevalence_ - lifetime risk in F ~ 0.9-4% depending on method, ascertainment, country and age range
	- _Incidence_ - ~ 5/100,000 based upon primary care and mental health surveys
	- _Demographic_ - 40% of all incident cases occuring inF 15-19y:
		- Age - usually onset during adolescence or young adulthood, rare in children under age of 13y (in this age group M:F = 1:1), or after 40y
		- Sex - female preponderance
		- Socio-economic status - more common in upper than the lower social classes

- **Subtypes:**
    - <u>Binge-eating/ purging type</u> - those w/ AN who binge eat also purge through self-induced vomiting, or the misuse of laxatives, diuretics, or enemas, while a minority do not binge eat but regularly purge after consumption of small amounts of food
    - <u>Restrictive type</u> - primarily achieving weight loss through dieting, fasting, or excessive exercise w/o evidence of binge eating or purging
- **Diagnostic features** - 5 essential features of AN, 1) persistent energy intake restriction, 2) intense fear of gaining weight or becoming fat or persistent behaviour that interferes w/ weight gain, and 3) a disturbance in self-perceived weight or shape, and 4) a Lack of recognition of the seriousness of low body weight:
    - **Weight loss as a result of persistent energy intake restriction** (Criterion A) - the body weight is significantly low (i.e. less than minimally normal), in the given context of age, sex, developmental trajectory, and otherwise physical health:
        - In adults, <u>severity is defined by the BMI</u>, but no specified cutoff is offered in DSM-5, but the WHO defines severe thinness as \<= 17.0 kg/m2 (while 17.0-18.5 kg/m2 requires clinical judgement)
        - In children and adolescents, <u>failure to make expected weight gain</u> or <u>maintain a normal developmental trajectory</u> instead of actual weight loss (BMI-for-age percentile also used)
    - **Intense fear of gaining weight** (Criterion B) - extreme concerns w/ weight and image, and a desire for thinness, or perceived from either objectively gaining weight, subjectively as becoming fat, which is counteracted by behaviour that interferes with normal weight gain:
        - <u>Concerns for weight gain</u> - usually present although usually not admitted especially in children (as weight gain is expected) and some adults, but **characteristically persistent and not alleviated by weight loss**
        - <u>Behaviour that interferes w/ weight gain</u>:
            - Dieting or fasting
            - Binge-eating w/ immediate purging through self-induced vomiting, misuse of laxatives or enemas
            - Excessive exercise (which may be overt or subtle)
            - Misuse of diuretics, and other weight loss agents
    - **Disturbance in self-perceived experience and significance of body weight and shape** (Criterion C) - various perception of body weight and image:
        - Globally overweight despite objective evidence of weight loss
        - Concerns regarding certain body parts being overweight, e.g. abdomen, buttocks, and thighs, with techniques to evaluate body parts such as measurements and mirror checking
        - Usually harsh on oneself if minute weight gain occurs, and is interpreted as evidence of failure of self control
		- And an _undue influence of weight and image on self evaluation_
	- **A lack  of recognition of seriousness of the current low body weight** (Criterion C) - e.g. ignorance of physical consequences of current low body weight
- **Central psychological features** (Fairburn et al 1999):
	- Overvalued ideas about body shape and weight
	- Observed that there is often a pre-occupation of food, which may manifest as cooking elaborate meals for others, or kleptomania (stealing food by shoplifting or other ways)
	- Often remorseful after overeating with intensified purging behaviours in the future
	- Resentful when people encourage them to eat, and may high food, or purge as soon as meal is over
- **Associated features supporting diagnosis:**
    - **Excessive level of physical activity** - in a subgroup of AN patients, and usually precedes onset of disorder:
        - Aimed at accelerating weight loss
        - May be difficult to control which jeopardizes weight recovering
    - **Medical conditions a/w semi-starvation:**
        - <u>Cardiovascular and respiratory system</u> - overall low BP, tachycardia, rhythm abnormalities, palpitations, dizziness, and subjective reduced ET
        - <u>Haematological abnormalities</u> - anaemia
        - <u>Endocrine abnormalities</u> - amenorrhoea (almost always present), osteopenia
    - **Medical manifestations a/w purging behaviours or other methods of interferring w/ weight gain** - electrolyte abnormalities are most common (hypoK, hypoMg, hypoPO4):
        - Laxatives
        - Diuretics
        - Enemas
    - **Depressive symptoms** - can be caused by the disorder itself in severe malnutrition (AN brain), but can also be a comorbid primary MDD:
        - Low mood (and irritability) and anhedonia
        - Social withdrawal
        - Biological symptoms (insomnia, diminished libido)
    - **Obsessive-compulsive features** (usually prominent) - may or may not be related to food:
        - <u>Obsessions on food</u> - often pre-occupied w/ the thoughts of food (may be exacerbated by under-nutrition), many focus on counting calories and focus on how to eat to minimise food intake, where some may collect recipes or hoard food (+/- binge eating), all of which may be time consuming
        - <u>Obsessions on body weight and image</u> - pre-occupation w/ problems of body weight, appearing fat, or overall body image
        - <u>Obsessions unrelated to food, weight, or image</u> - an aditional diagnosis of OCD may be warranted
    - **Other cognitive features:**
        - Concerns w/ eating in public (?high caloric)
        - Feeling of ineffectiveness
        - Strong desire to control one's environment
        - Inflexible thinking
        - Limited social spontaneity
        - Overly restrained emotional expression
- **Approach to the pursuit of thinness or the ideal body weight and shape** - the distinction is supported by evidence that they have different trajectories and cognitive features (Treasure et al 2015):
	- _Restrictive type_ - eating little and set themselves very low daily calorie limits (~ 600-1000 kcal) +/- excessive exercising
	- _Purging type_ - active approaches to achieving weight loss, such as inducing vomiting, excessive exercise, and misuse of laxatives, which may be accompanied by binge-eating
- **Binge eating** - occurs in a subgroup of patients:
	- Behaviour becomes more frequent with chronicity and increases with age (?difficulty to maintaining control)
	- A binge is an episode of eating when a large amounts of food is consumed in a short period of time, during which there is a _sense of loss of control_
	- Often feels remorse with intensified efforts to lose weight, through induced vomiting, exercising, or use of laxatives/ diuretics etc.
- **Amennorhoea** - one of several physical abnormalities traditionally incorporated in diagnostic criteria but not DSM-5:
	- Occurs early in development of condition, ~20% cases precedes obvious weight loss
	- Can potentially prompt clinical presentation rather than the disordered eating
- **Other symptoms:**
	- Depressive symptoms (75% lifetime history of MDD)
	- Anxiety states
	- Obsessional symptoms
	- Lability of mood
	- Social withdrawal
	- Lack of sexual interest (hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism)

- **Functional consequences of chronic AN:**
	- Chronic psychopathology w/ psychiatric comorbidities (e.g. MDD)
	- Physical health consequences (highest mortality rate of any major psychiatry disorders)
	- Major burden on social functioning and employment
- **Development and course** - often begins as an _ordinary effort of dieting_ or weight loss but _subsequently loses control_:
    - <u>Onset</u> - usually in adolescence and young adulthood (late-onset have been described):
        - Onset a/w a life-stressor that may be interpretted as 'losing control' (e.g. leaving home for college, academic stress, parents divorced)
        - Younger patients usually present w/ somatic complaints, and manifests atypically w/ verbal denying "fear of fat"
		- Onset in childhood or young adolescence is less likely attributable to social factors, but more on familial factors
        - Older patients typically manifest w/ more S/S of a long-standing disorder
    - <u>Course</u> - outcomes of AN usually highly variable (most remission within 5y of presentation):
        - Some usually have changed eating habits for a duration prior to meeting full criteria of AN
        - Some recover fully after single episode
        - Most often run a fluctuating course, w/ exacerbations and periods of partial remission
        - Others experiencing a chronic course over many years
        - Hospitalisations may be frequent to restore weight and address medical presentations
- **Prognosis:**
	- _Recovery_ - ~ 2/3 had largely recovered physically and in terms of eating habits at 5y
	- _Poor prognostic factors for enduring AN_:
		- Onset before puberty or in adulthood
		- Long history (> 3y)
		- Premorbid personality problems
		- Comorbid substance misuse
		- Childhood obesity
	- _Mortality_ - highest mortality rate among all major psychiatric disorders (4-5x risk):
		- Suicide - accounts for 1 in 5 deaths (reported as 12 per 100,000/y)
		- Physical consequences - accounts for rest of abnormalities, notably cardiac events or sepsis
- **Differential diagnosis of AN** - DDx for weight loss and loss of appetite:
	- **Medical disorders:**
		- _Gastrointestinal diseases_ - inflammatory bowel disease, malabsorption syndromes (e.g. chronic pancreatitis)
		- _Endocrinopathies_ - **pituitary failure**, where as other endocrinopathies may result in weight loss but an increased appetite (DM, thyrotoxicosis)
		- _Cachexia and neoplasms_ - gastrointestinal, haematological, hypothalamic
	- **Psychiatric disorders:**
		- _Other eating disorders_ - bulimia nervosa, atypical eating disorders, avoidant/ restrictive food intake disorders
		- _Social anxiety disorder_ - prominent theme relating to fear of negative evaluation by others possibly regarding one's own body image, however, ?absence of disordered perception of body weight/ image
		- _Body dysmorphic disorder_ - usually not better explained by concerns with body fat and weight, rather specific bodily features that cause significant distress
		- _Obsessive-compulsive disorder_ - esp. if pre-occupation not only w/ food, body weight and shape
		- _Substance use disorder_ - body weight and appetite affected by comorbid substance use
		- _Depression_ - melancholia often characterised by loss of appetite and weight loss, but noting that AN often presenting w/ comorbid depressive symptoms
		- _Schizophrenia_ - avoidance of food intake possibly driven by bizarre delusional beliefs (e.g. food being poisoned) with minimal disordered experience of weight and image
- **Risk and prognostic factors:**
    - <u>Temperamental</u> - obsessional traits
    - <u>Environmental</u>:
        - Cross cultural variability where increased prevalence in cultures where thinness is valued
        - Occupational and avocations that encourage thinness (e.g. modelling) a/w increased risks
    - <u>Genetics and physiological</u>:
        - Twin studies and family studies demonstrate heritability
        - Increased risk of bipolar and depressive disorders also found among first-degree relatives
- **Etiology:**
	- **Genetics** - lesser heritability of eating disorders in preadolescent and early adolescent cases:
		- _Familial studies_ - strongly familial, w/ a reported heritability of 28-74% suggesting much of familiality reflects genetic predisposition
		- _GWAS_ - no individual risk genes possibly due to insufficient sample size
		- _Associated conditions_ - genetic risks is shared w/ other psychiatric disorders including OCD, and perhaps more surprisingly schizophrenia (Bulik-Sullivan et al 2015)
	- **Neurobiology** - various neuroimaging or neurobiological studies showed associations but unable to determine whether causal (or are a result of starvation and weight loss):
		- _Structural changes_ - reduction in brain volume, but w/ increased grey matter volume in orbitofrontal cortex and insula (reward and introspection)
		- _Neurochemical changes_ - alterations w/ 5-HT system
		- _Hypothesis_ - one theory that links neuroimaging with neurochemistry is that restrictive eating of AN is a maladaptive attempt to reduce negative affect caused by an imbalance between the aversive (serotonergic), and reward (dopaminergic) systems of food
	- **Cognitive features** - interesting as a/w compulsivity as in OCD:
		- Poor performances in "set-switching", i.e. switching between tasks
		- Relative impairments of strategic planning compared to detailed focusing on task
	- **Socio-cultural factors:**
		- More common in societies with the notion that thinness is desirable and attractive
		- However, those w/ AN had no greater exposures to factors that increase dieting, suggesting the problem is a reaction to dieting than a problem of dieting itself
	- **Individual psychological causes** - a _problem of control_:
		- In the Golden Cage, Brunch (1974), suggested that these patients are engaged in "_a struggle for control, for a sense of identity and effectiveness, with the relentless pursuit of thinness as a final step in this effort_"
		- Epidemiological studies (Fairburn 1999) suggest that premorbid personality traits, such as low-self esteem or perfectionism is implicated in development of AN
	- **Causes within the family:**
		- Epidemiological studies shown AN compared w/ healthy controls are exposed to more childhood adverse events, although this may not be specific to AN but also other psychiatric disorders (possibly causing AN by interacting w/ individual psychological causes)
		- Specific patterns of relationships consisting of enmeshment, overprotectiveness, rigidity and lack of conflict resolution (Minuchin et al 1978)
		- AN in the patient served to prevent dissent within the family (Minuchin et al 1978)
- **Approaches to assessment** - most are reluctant to change behaviour, and a thorough Hx should be tactfully elicited in the 1) development of the disorder, the 2) present pattern of eating and weight control, and 3) patient's ideas of body weight:
	- Assessment of eating behaviours
	- Assessment of psychological issues
- **Assessment of eating:**
	- **Typical eating behaviour:**
		- _Typical day's of eating_ - typical mealtime arrangements at home, and at school/ work, and standard meals
		- _Avoiding particular foods_ - reason for avoiding (e.g. flavour, or high caloric content)
		- _Pattern of eating_ - any approaches to minimising caloric intake, and whether it is variable:
			- Eating foods that are more filling first (e.g. vegetables)
			- Avoid eating foods with higher calories (e.g. carbs, proteins)
			- Unenjoyable meals to avoid over-eating
	- **Restriction** - clarify attempts at restriction:
		- _Objective  measures of restriction_ - e.g. quantification of calories often able to elicit as many count calories
		- _Restriction of food stuff_:
			- Avoiding particular foods due to caloric content or risk of overeating
			- Restriction of fluids (bloating, prone to dehydration)
		- _Severity of fasting_ - has patient fasted for a day or longer
		- _Hunger_ - clarify experience of hunger during restriction and any urge to eat (transitioning towards bingeing)
	- **Bingeing** - bingeing defined as an episode of eating a large amount of food within a short period of time:
		- _Whether the patient binges_ - whether unable to restraint oneself during hunger or urges to eat
		- _Details of a typical bingeing episode_:
			- How do binges begin? How do they stop?
			- Usually what does one binges?
			- Are they objectively large binges?
			- How often do they occur?
		- _Sense of control_ - evaluate whether there is a sense of control, loss of effectiveness, concerns with weight gains or changes in body
		- _Sense of remorse after binge episode_:
			- Clarify sense of remorse
			- Increase in purging activities (induced vomiting, intensive exercising, medications)
	- **Purging** - methods of inducing weight loss usually after a meal:
		- _Induced vomiting_ - characterise timing, method etc.
		- _Exercise_ - characterise timing, method, and intent (e.g. healthy living vs burning off calories)
		- _Medications_ - laxatives, emetics, appetite suppressants, diuretics and their effects
	- **Maladaptive behaviours in the social setting:**
		- _Family meals_ - response to being forced to eat more (e.g. hiding food, purging after-meals), whether special dietary requests are accepted, conflicts
		- _Social meals with friends_ - e.g. avoiding social gatherings where a meal is anticipated, pre-occupation with high caloric contents in meals outside resulting in subsequent purging, intentionally ordering little (e.g. just a salad and water)
		- _General eating in front of others_ - whether able to eat in front of others

- **Additional information in evaluation of childhood or young adolescent AN** - less heritable, and less governed by socioeconomic factors, and more affected by familial concerns on weight and shape:
	- Early feeding and childhood weight
	- Parental weight and shape concerns
- **P/E:**
	- _Vital signs_ - baseline weight and BMI, BP/P, temperature:
		- BP - postural hypotension or frank hypotension
		- Pulse - bradycardia, arrhythmias due to electrolyte abnormalities
	- _General examination_ - evaluation for signs of AN:
		- Body build - thin, emanciation
		- Eyes - pallor
		- Parotid glands - parotid gland swelling
		- Oral cavities -  perimylolysis (erosion of frontal teeth due to frequent induced vomitting), hydration status
		- Hands:
			- Russell's sign (purging type)
			- Lanugo (fine downy hair on back, forearms, and sides of face as means of preserving body temperature)
		- Extremities:
			- Dry skin, and orange discolouration of palms and soles
			- Cold extremities/ hypothermia
			- Peripheral oedema (hypoalbuminaemia)
			- Weak proximal muscles (e.g. difficulty in rising from chair)
	- _Cardiovascular examination_ - conduction defects
- **Mental state examination** - screen for:
	- Depressive symptoms
	- Characteristic psychopathology of AN (e.g. pre-occupations/ delusions with regards to weight, body image, eating, and food)
- **Ix:**
	- **ECG** - conduction defects, esp. QTc prolongation
	- **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, RBG, CaPO4, Mg:
		- _CBC_:
			- Hb - NcNc anaemia
			- WBC with differentials - Mild leukopenia w/ relative lymphocytosis
			- PLT - Thrombocytopenia
		- _Electrolytes_:
			- HypoNa/ HyperNa (reflects dehydration)
			- HypoK (esp w/ repeated vomiting, laxatives, or diuretics)
			- HypoPO4 (exagerated during refeeding)
			- HypoMg
		- _RBG_ - hypoglycaemia
	- **DEXA** - not routinely performed but will reveal evidence of osteopenia and osteoporosis

- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Correction of physical consequences_ - e.g. correcting hydration status, electrolyte abnormalities
		- _Weight restoration and maintenance_ - often w/ dietitian input and a strict dietary/ behavioural regimes
		- _Psychological Tx_ - psychotherapies are mainstay of Tx in anorexia nervosa with certain specialised forms being evidence-based and known for effects on weight restoration and maintenance
		- _Psychopharmacological approaches_ - antidepressants and antipsychotics are prescribed but lack evidence, possibly as psychiatric symptoms are partially caused by starvation ("AN brain")
