# Endometrial Carcinoma

- **Definition** - often referring to endometrial adenocarcinoma, refers to malignancy arising from the endometrium of the uterus
- **Epidemiology** - 2nd most common gynaecological cancer worldwide, most common gynaecological cancer in developed countries:
    - <u>Incidence</u> (HK cancer registry) - increasing incidence; 4th in incidence (F), beyond 10th in mortality
    - <u>Demographic</u> - incidence peaks between 6th-7th decades (some present earlier, rarely before 40y except abundant risk factors)
- **Risk factors for endometrial cancer:**
    - **Chronically increased estrogen activity** - primary risk factor for endometrioid EC:
        - **Exogenous sources:**
            - <u>Unopposed estrogen therapy</u> (RR 1.5-10) - systemic estrogen therapy from any route (oral, transdernal, vaginal ring) w/o **concurrent administration of progestin** results in a marked increase risk of developing EC precursors (endometrial hyperplasia with atypia) and EC
            - <u>Tamoxifen</u> - SERM with variable action on ER based on systemic estrogen level, particularly agonistic action in post-menopausal women, such that post-menopausal F w/ breast cancer Tx with tamoxifen increases risk of EC
        - **Endogenous source:**
            - <u>States of chronic anovulation</u> - chronically anovulatory patients (PCOS, perimenopausal period) continue to produce estrogen but not cyclically, w/o adequate progesterone oposition, resulting in increased risk of endometrial hyperplasia and EC
            - <u>Obesity</u> - increased peripheral conversion of androgens into estrogen +/- reduced sex-hormone binding globulin, resulting in overall increased estrogen activity
            - <u>Estrogen-secreting tumour</u> - most commonly see with granulosa cell tumors of the ovary
            - <u>Early menarche and late menopause</u> - early manarche is considered an independent risk factor for EC in some studies, but less consistent association for late menopause
            - <u>Nuliparity and infertility</u> - may not be an independent risk factor as mediated by chronic anovulatory cycles
    - **Hereditary factors:**
        - <u>Lynch syndrome</u> - autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline mutation of DNA mismatch repair gene a/w increased lifetime risk and earlier presentation of EC
        - <u>+ve FHx</u> - patients w/ 1st-degree relative with history of EC were a/w increased risk of EC
- **Protective factors of endometrial cancer:**
    - <u>Use of hormonal contraception</u> - use of COCP decreases risk of EC, and the risk reduction persisted for years after dicontinuation, attributed to role of progestin
    - <u>Increased gravidity and parity</u> - increased gravidty and parity decreases risk of EC
    - <u>Older age of last birth</u> - decreased risk independent of parity and other factors
    - <u>Breastfeeding</u> - parous patients who opt for breast feeding have lower risk of EC may be related to low estrogen state during breastfeeding
- **Clinico-pathological classification of endometrial carcinoma** - different classification systems:
    - **Tradtional classification systems** - defined <u>2 clinicopathogentic enties</u> with variable pathophysiology and prognosis:
        - <u>Type I EC</u> (endometrioid carcinoma) - estrogen driven, lower grade, have less myometrial invasion, and more favourable prognosis
        - <u>Type II EC</u> (non-endoetrioid carcinoma) - non-estrogen driven, presentation at a more aggressive stage, and poorer prognosis
    - **WHO categorization** - classification of endometrial cancer purely based on histopathology: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/who_classification_of_endometrial_carcinoma.png)
    - **FIGO categorization** - classification of the histological subtypes based on <u>clinical aggressiveness</u>:
        - <u>Non-aggressive subtypes</u> - low grade (grade 1 and 2) endometrioid carcinoma
        - <u>Aggressive subtypes</u> - grade 3 endometrioid carcinoma, serous, clear cell, undifferentiated, mixed, mesonephric-like, gastrointestinal mucinous type carcinomas, and carcinosarcoma
- **Molecular classification of endometrial carcinoma** - further risk stratification for more consistent categorization of endometrial carcinomas:
    - **Limitations of clinical classification systems** - Heterogeneity within clinicopathological subgroups:
        - Certain subgroups defined by clinico-pathological features still exhibit heterogeneity in terms of prognosis and Tx responsiveness (e.g. grade 3 endometrioid carcinoma may show poor response to progestin)
        - It is postulated it is the molecular signals that drive prognosis and treatment responsiveness, where certain aggressive subgroups which are more likely harbour markers of poor-prognosis which appears as an observable phenotype

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/relationship_between_traditional_classification_system_and_molecular_classification_in_ec.png)
    - **4 molecular subtypes** - as identified in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA): 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/molecular_classification_of_endometrial_carcinoma.png)
        - **DNA polymerase episolon (POLE)-mutated subtype** (POLEmut):
            - <u>Pathomechanism</u> - recurrent mutations in the exonuclease domain of POLE, a gene involved in DNA replication and repair
            - <u>Associations w/ pathological entities</u> - often of endometrioid subtype (but not exclusive)
            - <u>Associations w/ clinical profile</u>:
                - Younger and thinner women
                - Misleading aggressive pathological features (e.g. high grade, LVSI)
            - <u>Prognosis</u> - exceptionally well (5y OS \> 96%)
            - <u>Clinical implications</u> - adjuvant therapy may not be required (currently under investigations)
        - **Mismatch repair-deficient subtype** (MMRd):
            - <u>Pathomechanism</u> - high tumour mutational burden and high TIL secondary to MSI-H status often caused by 'epigenetic silencing of MSH1', but also comes in somatic or germline mutations of MMR genes
            - <u>Associations w/ pathological features</u> - LVSI and higher grade
            - <u>Clinical implications</u> - role of ICI if advanced or recurrent
        - **p53 abnormal** (p53 abn):
            - <u>Pathomechanism</u> - high somatic copy number alterations and mutational profiles due to **genomic instability** (often a/w HER2 amplification in 20-25%)
            - <u>Associations w/ pathological entities</u> - usually present in aggressive subtypes:
                - Serous carcinoma (93%)
                - Carcinosarcoma (85%)
                - Clear cell carcinoma (38%)
                - Grade 3 endometrial carcinoma (22%)
                - Grade 1 or 2 endometrioid carcinoma (5%)
            - <u>Associations w/ clinical profile</u> - associations to BRCA1/2 carriers (highest risk in BRCA1)
            - <u>Prognosis</u> - poor
        - **No specific molecular profile** (NSMP):
            - <u>Pathomechanism</u> - genomically stable, MMR proficient, POLEwt, where clinical behaviour appears to be **related to ER status**:
                - High ER status (85% of NSMP) - better clinical outcomes
                - Poor ER status - most are non-endometrioid histologies and unfavourable outcome

- **Clinical features of endometrial cancer:**
    - **Asymptomatic** - absence of Sx, and incidentally discovered by:
        - <u>Abnormal cervical cytology</u> - presence of endometrial cells, atypical glandular cells or frank adenocarcinoma on conventional pap smear (Sn 40-55%) for cervical cancer screening
        - <u>Abnormal finding on imaging</u> - thickened endometrial lining sometimes found incidentally on USG, CT, or MRI performed for another indication
        - <u>Incidental finding after hysterectomy or during abdominopelvic surgery</u> - detection of EC on hysterorectomy for benign disease, or detection of metastasis during abdominopelvic surgery
    - **Abnormal uterine bleeding** (75-90%) - any sort of AUB, where nature of clinical presentation depends on age of patient:
        - <u>Post-menopausal women</u> (most cases) - present with PMB of any amounts warranting full workup
        - <u>Peri-menopausal women</u> (17% cases) - intermenstrual bleeding, manifesting as increasing frequency, HMB, or prolonged menstrual bleeding (e.g. \> 7d)
        - <u>Women \< 45y</u> (5% cases) - persistent AUB on a background of unopposed estrogen exposure (e.g. obesity, chronic anovulation), failed medical Mx of bleeding, or in patient with high risk of endometrial cancer (Lynch syndrome)
- **Signs of endometrial cancer:**
    - **General examination** - vital signs (haemodynamic stability), pallor
    - **Abdominal examination** - usually normal for early stages:
        - <u>Inspection</u> - ?abdominal distension for malignant ascites
        - <u>Palpation</u> - ?pelvic mass
        - <u>Shifting dullness</u> - +ve if malignant ascites present
    - **Pelvic examination** - normal in early stages, but uterus <u>enlarged and fixed</u> if late stages
- **Ix:**
    - **CBC** - McHc anaemia if substantial abnormal uterine bleeding
    - **Transvaginal pelvig USG** - non-invasive and indicated in postmenopausal women:
        - <u>Post-menopausal women</u> - increased risk of endometrial thickness (\> 5mm)
        - <u>Pre-menopausal women</u> - poor correlation of thickness with risk of cancer as varying endometrial thickness along menstrual cycle
    - **Cytology or tissue Bx** - neessary for histological Dx:
        - **Blind-endometrial sampling** (pipelle) - initial diagnostic procedure but most reliable when \>= 50% of endometrium is affected by disease:
            - <u>Test properties</u> - Sn \>= 90%
            - <u>Limitations</u> - FN results in:
                - Personal Hx of colorectal cancer
                - Endometrial polyps
                - BMI \>= 35 kg/m2
            - <u>Findings</u> - may reveal frank invasive endometrial carcinoma, or endometrial hyperplasia w/ atypia (high malignant potential)
        - **Dilatation and curettage** (D&C) +/- **hysteroscopy and tisue Bx** - considered w/ non-diagnostic blind sampling (D&C considered in those w/ heavy bleeding as also therapeutic)
- **Dx** - histological Dx
- **Principles of staging:**
    - Endometrial cancer is surgically stage (limited role of non-invasive staging Ix)
    - Endometrial cancer is pathologically examined, with documentation of 1) grade, 2) histological subtype, and 3) lymphovascular space involvement (LVSI)
- **Staging system** - 2023 International Federation of Gynaecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) staging system for cancer of the endometrium:
    - **FIGO staging** - escalation of stage by depth and extent of locoregional involvement, being more lineant for non-aggressive subtypes, and stricter for aggressive subtypes:
        - <u>Stage I</u> - confined to the uterine corpus and ovary
        - <u>Stage II</u> - invasion of cervical stroma w/o extrauterine extension or w/ substantial LVSI or/ aggressive histological types with myometrial invasion
        - <u>Stage III</u> - local and/or regional spread of tumour of any histological subtype
        - <u>Stage IV</u> - distant metastasis, or invasion into bladder mucosa and/or intestinal mucosa

- **Staging procedure** - total hysterectomy, bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy and LN evaluation:
    - **Role of surgery:**
        - <u>Removal of primary tumour</u> - which may be curative for early endometrial cancer
        - <u>Prognostication</u> - determine whether adjuvant therapy is needed
    - **Choice of surgical approach** - based clinical suspicion of disease extent identified on P/E:
        - <u>Minimally invasive surgery</u> (laparoscopic or robotic) - Two large-scale trials (LAP2, LACE) demonstrated reduced perioperative complications and better QOL w/o comprimise of oncological outcomes
        - <u>Laparotomy</u> - induced for those in whom MIS is not feasible (e.g. known adhesive disease)
        - <u>Vaginal hysterectomy</u> - rarely performed but theoretically for those in whom assessment of the peritoneal cavity and retroperitoneum is not required
    - **Lymph node evaluation:**
        - **Sentinel LN mapping** - to avoid the morbidity of full lymphadenopathy in patients w/ -ve SLN:
            - <u>Indications</u>:
                - No strict indications and can theoretically be used for all patients undergoing surgical staging, as does not show excess False -ve compared to systematic lymphadenectomy
                - Some advocate using SLND for low-risk disease, and standard LN dissection for high-risk disease
            - <u>Technique</u> - injection of radiolabelled colloid (e.g. 99mTc) into 3 and 9 oclock region prior to hysterectomy, and draining LN is retrieved and evaluated
        - **Approach to lymphadenectomy:**
            - <u>Complete pelvic lymphadenectomy</u> (+/- para-aortic lymphadenectomy) - complete dissection of pelvic LN, while additional para-aortic LND is considered especially if +ve pevlic LN to reduce risk of recurrence
            - <u>Selective lymphadenectomy</u> - performing LND only in patients w/ high risk of nodal metastasis (e.g. aggressive neoplasm, deeply invasive through \> 50% of myometrium, \> 2cm tumours on pre-operative tumours)
        - **S/E of lymphadenectomy** - major morbidity a/w <u>LL lymphoedema</u> (8-35%) complicating w/ recurrent cellulitis and lymphangitis
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Staging and surgical Tx</u> - may be curative for early endometrial cancer
    - **Role of adjuvant therapy:**
        - <u>Indications</u> - high risk disease (and high-intermediate risk disease):
            - High-risk histologies - particularly nonendometrioid histologies and selected high-risk G3 endometrioid cancers (p53abn +ve and POLEmut -ve)
            - Pathological stage III/IV disease - irrespective of histology
        - <u>Rationale</u>:
            - **Role in low-risk disease** (i.e. G1-2 early endometrioid cancer limited to endometrium, or \< 50% of endometrium w/ no or focal LVSI) - not indicated as extremely low risk of recurrence
            - **Role in high-risk disease** - generally indicated except for early G3 endometrioid cancer with POLEmut (excellent 5y DFS 98% in PORTEC-3)
            - **Role in inter-mediate risk disease** - further subdivided based where high-intermiediate risks may benefit from adjuvant therapy
- **Prognosis:** 
![](img/five_year_survival_of_ca_corpus.png)
    - **Prognostic factors of resectable endometrial cancer:**
        - <u>Stage</u> - lower stage a/w better prognosis
        - <u>Histology</u> - both grade and histological subtype, where most patients will have favourable prognosis since the majority of patient will have endometrioid histology
        - <u>Lymphovascular space invasion</u> - substantial LVSI is an independent risk factor for LN metastasis and disease recurrence
        - <u>Molecular factor</u> - demonstrable heterogenous group of disease in PORTEC-3: 
        ![](~/Documents/notes/img/proactive_molecular_risk_classifier_for_endometrial_cancer.png)
        - <u>Other prognostic factors</u> - e.g. age, race, +ve peritoneal washings, lower-uterine segment involvement
- **Screening:**
    - <u>Screening for patients w/ cancer predisposition syndromes</u> - regular screening and prevention for those w/ Lynch syndrome and Cowden syndrome
    - <u>No role of screening in patient's with increased risk</u> - e.g. those on tamoxifen for breast cancer
