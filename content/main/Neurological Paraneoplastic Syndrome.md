# Neurological Paraneoplastic Syndrome

- **Definition** - group of conditions a/w underlying malignancy thought to be due to an immunological response to the tumour that results in damage to the nervous system or muscles
- **Cancers most commonly implicated w/ neurological paraneoplastic syndromes:**
    - Bronchial carcinoma (SCLC, NSCLC)
    - Pancreatic adenocarcinoma
    - Breast carcinoma
    - Prostate Cancer
    - Ovarian Cancer
    - Lymphoma
- **Peripheral neuropathy** - immune-mediated axonal degeneration or demyelination
- **Encephalomyelitis** - perivascular inflammation and selective neuronal degeneration a/w malignancy:
    - <u>Etiology</u> - most commonly SCLC (75%)
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - variable focal neurological deficits
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - **LP** - lymphocytic pleocytosis, elevated protein, absence of infection, carcinomatous cells in flow cytometry of CSF
        - **MRI** - meningeal enhancement (esp. at brainstem)
        - **Blood** - occasionally +ve Anti-Hu Ab
- **Cerebellar degeneration** - rapid onset of cerebellar ataxia:
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - rapid onset of immune-mediated cerebellar degeneration
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - limb, truncal and gait ataxia
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - **Neuroimaging** - CT or MRI shows <u>cerebellar atrophy</u>
        - **Blood** - circulating anti-Yo, Tr and Hu Ab (absence does not exclude Dx)
- **Retinopathy:**
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - immune response against retina
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - variable:
        - Blurred vision
        - Episodic vision loss
        - Impaired colour vision
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - Electroretinogram
        - Anti-retinal Ab
- **Lambert-Eaton syndrome** (60% attributable to malignancy):
    - <u>Pathophysiology</u> - Ab against pre-synaptic Ca channels
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - proximal muscle weakness that improves after exercise
    - <u>Ix and Dx</u>:
        - EMG - shows low-amplitude compound muscle AP that enhances to near normal following exercise
- **Dermatomyositis and polymyositis** - rheumatological manifestation a/w malignancy
