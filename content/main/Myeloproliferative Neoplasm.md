# Myeloproliferative Neoplasm

- **Definition** - group of chronic conditions characterised by clonal proliferation of marrow precursor cells, including polycythaemia rubra vera, essential thrombocythaemia, myelofibrosis, and chronic myeloid leukaemia
- **Epidemiology** - rare disease:
    - <u>Incidence</u> - 150 cases per year locally
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - increasing incidence w/ age generally
        - Sex - female preponderance in ET (especially during reproductive ages)
- **Molecular basis of MPN:**
    - **JAK-2 mutation** (chromosome 9):
        - Accounts for 90% of PRV
        - Accounts for 50% of ET and MF
- **Pathophysiology of MPN:**
    - **Clonal prolferation of pluripotent stem cells with normal terminal differentiation:**
        - <u>Growth factor independece</u> - acquisition of characteristic mutations (e.g. JAK-2) resulting in clonal proliferation of pluripotent HSC of myeloid lineages, resulting in expansion of 1) erythroid, 2) myeloid, and 3) megakaryocytic lineages
        - <u>Absence of myelodysplastic changes</u> - normal differentiation to give normal cellular components of blood
    - **Disease progression** - MPN can progress in two ways:
        - <u>Myelofibrosis</u> - excessive growth factor secretion by megakaryocytes results in proliferation of fibroblasts and subsequent marrow fibrosis, subsequently resulting in **extramedullary haematopoiesis**
        - <u>Leukaemic transformation</u> - progression into blast phase after acquisition of additional mutations
- **Classification of MPN:**
    - <u>BCR-ABL +ve MPN</u> - chronic myeloid leukaemia
    - <u>BCR-ABL -ve MPN</u>:
        - Polycythaemia rubra vera
        - Essential thrombocythaemia
        - Myelofibrosis
