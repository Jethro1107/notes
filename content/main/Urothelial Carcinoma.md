# Urothelial Carcinoma

- **Definition** - neoplasms arising from the transitional epithelium (urothelium) of the renal tract from the renal pelvis to urethra, most commonly affects the bladder
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Demographic - increasing incidence with age, male preponderance (M:F = 3-4:1)
- **Risk factors for urothelial carcinoma:**
    - **Smoking** (50% of cases)- exposure to many carcinogens including polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons
    - **Occupational carcinogen exposure** (10-20% of cases):
        - <u>Occupational carcinogens</u> - benzenes, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAH), diesel exhausts
        - <u>Occupations w/ increased exposure</u> - exposure to paint components (e.g. painters, rubber industry), exposure to benzenes (e.g. rubber industrial worker, firefighters etc.)
    - **Iatrogenic risk factors**:
        - <u>Radiation therapy</u> - pelvic radiation for rectal, cervical, prostate and testicular cancer is a/w a small magnitude increased risk of urothelial carcinoma
        - <u>Cyclophosphamide</u> - nine-fold risk of developing bladder cancer with latency period \< 10y
        - <u>Thiazolidinediones</u> - controversial risk factor
- **Clinical features of bladder cancer** - typically presents w/ painless haematuria, but can be preceded by irritative voiding Sx:
    - **Sx of local disease:**
        - **Haematuria** - intermittent gross, painless haematuria that is classically described as <u>present throughout micturation</u>:
            - **Onset and progression** - likely gradual onset of intermittent microscopic haematuria followed by more frequent gross haematuria
            - **Gross vs microscopic haematuria** - gross haematuria is much likely to be bladder cancer than microscopic haematuria:
                - <u>Gross haematuria</u> - incidence of bladder cancer around 10-20% in those presenting w/ gross haematuria (lower-tract workup is mandatory to r/o bladder cancer)
                - <u>Microscopic haematuria</u> - incidence of bladder cancer around 1-5% in those presenting w/ microscopic haematuria (workup dependent on risk stratification by AUA guidelines)
        - **Abdominal pain** - typically caused by muscle-invasive CA and depends on size and location of the primary tumour:
            - **Flank pain** - renal colic-type pain caused by tumour obstructing ureters at any level (e.g. bladder, ureter, renal pelvis)
            - **Suprapubic pain** - mild discomfort caused either by locally-advanced tumour invading into perivesicular tissue and nerves, or causing AROU
            - **Hypogastric, rectal or perineal pain** - caused by disease invading into obturator fossa, perirectal fat, presacral nerves or urogenital diaphragm
        - **Lower urinary tract Sx** - most common in patients w/ bladder CIS and usually causes storage Sx and rarely causes voiding Sx:
            - **Storage Sx** (Urgency, Frequency, Nocturia, Urge incontinence, dysuria) - caused by bladder irritation
            - **Voiding Sx** - weak streams, straining, intermittency caused by tumour at the bladder neck or prostate urethra
    - **Constitutional Sx** (fatigue, weight loss, malaise) - usually signs of advanced or metastatic cancer w/ poor prognosis
    - **Sx of metastatic disease:**
        - <u>Liver</u> - RUQ distending discomfort
        - <u>Bone</u> - bone or back pain
        - <u>Brain</u> - persistent headache or raised ICP
- **Signs:**
    - <u>Abdominal and pelvic examination</u>:
        - Inspection - nodularity in periumbilical region, visible mass
        - Palpation - solid pelvic mass, hepatomegaly
    - <u>DRE</u> - induration of prostate caused by bladder neck CA
- **Ix and Dx evaluation of CA bladder** - workup for gross haematuria to r/o benign causes and confirm CA bladder:
    - **Urinalysis** - dipstick chemical test, urine microscopy, urine cytology:
        - <u>Dipstick</u> - +ve for haematuria
        - <u>Urine microscopy</u> - demonstration normally-shaped RBC (cf casts, and dysmorphic RBC suggestive of glomerular bleeding)
        - <u>Urine cytology</u> (poor Sn) - demonstration of abnormal epithelial cells that are exfoliated from the tumour:
            - **Principles** - high grade tumour results in loss of intercellular adhesiveness and hence rapid exfoliation (cf low-grade tumours)
            - **Test properties** - poor Sn (grade-dependent), excellent Sp (\> 98%)
    - **Lower tract workup** - diagnostic cystoscopy (key in initial Dx and staging of bladder CA):
        - **Procedure** - bimanual examination under anaesthesia (EUA) followed by insertion of flexible cystoscope for detailed inspection and colection of bladder wash specimen
        - **Approach** - Bx or transurethral resection of bladder tumour (TURBT) of any visible tumour or suspicious lesion
        - **Role of fluorescence cystoscopy** - injection of intravesical fluorescence to increase Sn compared to white-light cystoscopy (mainly used for CA surveillence)
    - **Upper tract imaging** - for detection of sites of multifocal disease:
        - **CT A+P** - w/ contrast (CT urogram w/ delayed phase) or w/o contrast is the gold standard for Dx of bladder cancer
        - **Renal USG** - for upper tract evaluation
        - **MRI** - for staging of invasive or locally-advanced disease, graded by VI-RADS to distinguish between NMI CA and MI CA
        - **Retrograde pyelogram, ureteroscopy and Bx** - necessary for CTU suspicious of UTUC:
        - <u>Findings of retrograde pyelogram</u> - Goblet sign (cupping of injected contrast due to obstruction)
- **Staging system** - AJCC 8th edition TNM staging:
    - **Clinical vs pathological staging:**
        - <u>Clinical staging</u> - initial stage derived from bimanual examination and imaging studies, as well as from pathological reports from cystoscopic Bx or TURBT
        - <u>Pathological staging</u> - final staging derived after radical cystectomy and pelvic lymphadenectomy is performed
    - **TNM stages** - applied to bladder cancer irrespective of histology (e.g. urothelial, SCC, undifferentiated) ![](img/tnm_staging_ca_bladder.png) 
    ![](img/tnm_prognostic_groups_ca_bladder.png)
- **Staging Ix:**
    - **Transurethral resection of bladder tumour** (TURBT) - for local staging into NMI and invasive bladder cancer (see details below)
    - **PET-CT/ CT T+A+P** - detection of metastasis in muscle invasive diseases 
    ![](img/CA_bladder_local_staging.png)
- **Prognostic factors and risk stratification of histologically diagnosed MNI CA bladder after TURBT** - predicts risk of disease progression and local recurrence: 
![](img/AUA_risk_stratification_of_nmibc.png)
    - **Prognostic factors:**
        - **Tumour (T) stage:**
            - <u>Ta tumors</u> - papillary disease w/ no evidence of BM infiltration usually a/w low-grade lesions typically recur multiple times before becoming invasive
            - <u>Tis tumors</u> - Tis lesions next to raised lesions a/w increased <u>risk of recurrence as inphasive disease</u>
            - <u>T1 disease</u> - virtually all are high-grade lesions and are a/w high risk of recurrence, disease progression and poorer prognosis
        - **Histological Grade** - papillary urothelial neoplasm of low malignant potential (PUNLMP) \< low-grade urothelial CA \< high-grade urothelial CA
        - **Multicentricity** - multicentric tumours have higher risk of recurrence as NMI and muscle-invasive disease
        - **Tumour size** - \> 3cm tumours have higher risk of recurrence and progression to muscle-invasive diseaes
    - **Other prediction models** - European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) recurrence score
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - dependent on local extent of disease:
        - **Non-muscle invasive urothelial CA of bladder** (Tis, Ta, T1) - transurethral resection of bladder tumour (TURBT) +/- intravescical chemotherapy (regimen depends on risk stratification)
        - **Muscle-invasive urothelial CA of bladder** - cisplatin-based neoadjuvant therapy + radical cystectomy and urinary diversion
    - **Transurethral resection of bladder tumor** - <u>differentiation between NMI and muscle-invasive disease</u>, and potentially therapeutic for NMI CA bladder:
        - **Technical details of initial TURBT** - quality of initial Dx disease is of importance to avoid missing muscle-invasive disease:
            - <u>Bimanual examination under anasthesia</u> (EUA) - presence of induration or palpable mass under EUA is suggestive of invasive disease
            - <u>Complete resection of visible bladdeer tumour</u> - resection of tumour w/ adequate depth including **muscularis propria**, +/- Bx of mucosa suspicious of Tis disease
        - **Indications for restaging TURBT** (4-6 weeks) - for selected patients w/ confirmed pathological Dx NMI Ca of bladder:
            - 1\. T staging - T1 disease or selected high-grade Ta disease
            - 2\. Incomplete resection - visibly incomplete for high volume tumours, MP not present in histological specimen
        - **Rationales of restaging TURBT:**
            - <u>Avoid understaging</u> - avoid misdiagnosis of muscle-invasive disease as T1 disease, which impacts management
            - <u>Accurate risk stratification</u> - more accurate risk stratification of NMI CA bladder, a/w improved outcomes such as lower recurrence rates and improved PFS
    - **Intravesical therapies** - different regimens based on risk stratification:
        - **Regimens based on AUA risk of recurrence**:
            - <u>Low-risk disease</u> - single instillation intravesical chemotherapy (gemcitabine)
            - <u>Intermediate risk diease</u> - induction therapy (6 weekly courses) and maintenance therapy (10 monthly courses) of intravesical gemcitabine
            - <u>High risk disease</u> - induction therapy and maintenance therapy of intravesical BCG
        - **Intravesical BCG:**
            - <u>Indications</u> - most patients w/ high-risk disease but potential alternative for intermediate risk disease
            - <u>MOA</u> - live-attenuated form of Mycobacterium bovis, inducing a local immune response w/ demonstrable **anti-tumour activity**:
                - 1\. Mononuclear inflammatory infiltrates with CD4 T cells and macrophage
                - 2\. Increased INF-gamma expression - increased expression of MHC molecules on cancer cells, inducing cytotoxicity
                - 3\. Direct suppression of tumour growth in dose-dependent fashion
            - <u>Dose and scheduling</u>:
                - **Induction therapy** - Weekly courses for 6 weeks
                - **Maintenance therapy** - weekly for three weeks at months 3, 6, 12, 18, 30, 36
            - <u>Efficacy</u> - reduced risk of disease recurrence, disease progression and improves disease specific survival
            - <u>S/E</u>:
                - **Acute Sx** - fever, malaise, storage Sx, mild haematuria
                - **Infection** - increased risk of systemic or local infection
        - **Radical cystectomy and urinary diversion:**
            - **Indications:**
                - 1\. All muscle-invasive, non-metastatic CA bladder
                - 2\. NMI CA bladder w/ very high-risk features or persistent/ recurrent disease with intravesical BCG
            - **Technical details:**
                - <u>Cystectomy</u> - often radical cystectomy preferred, but other neurovascular bundle-sparing techniques are described
                - <u>Lymphadenectomy</u> - standard bilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy (including external, internal and obturator LNs)
                - <u>Reconstruction</u>:
                    - Ileal conduit - synthesis of neobladder from a segment of ileum, incontinent urinary diversion requiring stoma bag for collection of urine
                    - Continent cutaneous diversions - urinary diversion via a segment of intestine w/ valve mechanism constructed
- **Prognosis** - dependent of T stage and grading:
    - <u>Survival</u>:
        - Muscle invasive CA - 70% 5y survival
    - <u>Progression</u> - dependent in T stage and grading:
        - Low grade superficial bladder cancers - 5% progression into invasive bladder cancer
        - High-grade (HG) superficial bladder cancers - 50% progression into invasive bladder cancers
    - <u>Recurrence</u> - high for NMI CA:
        - Recurrence rate - 20-90% at 1y
- **Follow-up:**
    - Regular surveillance cystoscopy - q3mo at 1y, q6mo at 2y,, q1y there-after
    - Regular surveillance ureteroscopy (for UTUC w/ partial ureterectomy)
