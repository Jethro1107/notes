# Gastritis

- **Definition** - inflammation of the stomach mucosa, which is a <u>histological diagnosis</u>, although it can be sometimes be recognised in endoscopy
- **Acute gastritis** - acute erosive, and haemorrhagic inflammation of the superficial epitheium:
    - <u>Pathology</u> - Neutrophilic infiltration into the gastric mucosa
    - <u>Etiology</u>:
        - Aspirin or NSAIDs (most common cause)
        - Initial H. pylori infection
        - Alcohol
        - Other drugs - e.g. iron supplements
        - Severe physiological stress - e.g. burns, multiorgan failure, CNS trauma
        - Viral infections (in HIV-AIDS) - CMV, herpes simplus virus
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - often asymptomatic:
        - **Non-specific dyspepsia** - can result in anorexia
        - **Nausea or vomiting**
        - **UGIB** - haematemesis or melena
    - <u>Mx</u> - endoscopy and Bx may be required to exclude PUD or gastric cancer:
        - Symptomatic Mx - antacids, short-term PPIs, prokinetics (domperidone), or anti-emetics (metoclopramide)
- **Chronic gastritis:**
    - <u>Pathology</u> - Mononuclear infiltrations (lymphocytes or plasma cells) into the gastric mucosa
    - <u>Etiology</u>:
        - H. pylori infection (Type B Gastritis affecting antrum) - many assymptomatic, but may manifest as dyspepsia warranting workup and H. pylori eradication via test-and-treat approach
        - Autoimmune gastritis (Type A Gastritis sparing antrum) - gastritis itself is asymptomatic, but may lead to pernicious anaemia
        - Rare specific causes:
            - Infections - CMV, TB
            - Inflammatory Bowel Disease - e.g. Crohn's
            - Systemic disease - e.g. GVH disease, sarcoidosis
            - Idiopathic
- **Menetrier's disease** - rare disease of unknown cause resulting gastric pits to be long and tortuous, with <u>replacement of parietal cells and chief cells with mucous secreting cells</u>:
    - <u>Pathology/ Endoscopic Findings</u> - enlarged, nodular and coarse gastric folds
    - <u>Clinical features</u>:
        - Mild UGI Sx
        - Protein-losing enteropathy - due to exudation from gastric mucosa
    - <u>Mx</u>:
        - Anti-secretory drugs - PPI +/- octreotide
        - Partial gastrectomy
