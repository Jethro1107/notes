# Nasal Polyps

- **Definition** - bags of oedematous mucosa most frequently arising from the ethmoid cells and prolapse into the nasal cavity via the middle meatus
- **Pathophysiology** - not well understood:
    - <u>Predisposition in certain populations</u> - e.g. asthma, aspirin hypersensitivity, cystic fibrosis
    - <u>Systemic bilateral process</u> - nasal polyps always bilateral, unilaterality suggestive of neoplasia which requires Bx
- **Clinical features of nasal polyps:**
    - <u>Nasal obstruction</u> - often progressive, bilateral obstruction is the cardinal Sx
    - <u>Rhinorrhoea</u> - frequent and may be a/w ascending infections
    - <u>Disorders of smell</u> (Hyposmia and anosmia) - not uncommon
    - <u>Recurrent rhinosinusitis</u> - occurs if there is blockade of the ostiomeatal complex causing stasis in the dependent paranasal sinuses
    - <u>Otological Sx</u> (otalgia and hearing loss) - caused by eustachian tube obstruction resulting in OME
- **Signs of nasal polyps** - bilateral polyps found on endoscopy:
    - <u>Nasal endoscopic findings</u> - pale grey polypoidal mass that is mobile, and insensitive to palpation, and do not bleed (cf inferior turbinate hypertrophy)
    - <u>Indications for Bx</u> - unilaterality and features suggestive of haemorrhage
- **Ix and Dx** - Dx is purely clinical and addition Ix is ancillary:
    - <u>Nasal endoscopy</u>
    - <u>CT H&N</u> - radio-opacity in the paranasal sinuses, especially in the maxillary antrum
- **Mx:**
    - <u>Medical Tx</u> - short course of systemic steroids followed by long-term use of topical steroids
    - <u>Endoscopic ethmoidectomy</u> - for refractory or recurrent cases
    - <u>Mx of sinusitis</u> - appropriate medical and surgical Mx if sinusitis occurs
    - <u>Mx of associated comorbidities</u> - routine Mx of associated allergy or asthma
- **Antrochoanal polyp** - rare nasal polyp arising from the maxillary sinus:
    - <u>Epidemiology</u> - seen in young adults
    - <u>Pathology</u> - unilateral oedematous polyp in the maxillary sinus that prolapse via the posterior accessory ostium into nasal cavity and extends towards nasopharynx
    - <u>Clinical features</u> - unilateral nasal obstruction and otological Sx (if encrouching on eustachian tube)
    - <u>Mx</u> - pernasal excision with removal of cystic antral portion via antrostomy
