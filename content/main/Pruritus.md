# Pruritus

- **Definition** - an unpleasant sensation that leads to scratching or rubbing
- **Pathophysiology of pruritus in primary skin disease:**
	- _Stimulation of unmyelinated slow-conducting C fibres_ - nerve endings at the **dermo-epidermal junction**, passes via slow-conducting C fibres and ascends via the spino-thalamic tract towards the thalmaus and cortex (i.e. **pain pathway**)
	- _Wall's gate theory_ - underlies role of scratching in soothing pruritis through stimulation of inhibitory interneurons on the first-order neurons of the spino-thalamic tract

		- **Generalised pruritus:**
			- _Dermatitis_ - atopic dermatitis, xeroderma of old age
			- _Immune-mediated diseases_ - urticaria, psoriasis, pre-bullous pemphigoid
			- _Infections_ - scabies
		- **localised pruritus:**
			- _Infections_ - tinea
			- _Immune-mediated diseases_ - eczemas, dermatitis herpatiformis
			- _Inflammatory diseases_ - lichen planus, pediculosis

- **Approach to assessment** - important to distinguish skin changes between:
	- A primary skin disease that is a/w itching
	- Secondary skin changes caused by rubbing and scratching
- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Dx and Tx of underlying causes_ - no consistent effective therapies for suppression of itch regardless of cause, but Tx of underlying conditions is the first step
		- _Pharmacological therapy_:
			- Anti-histamines - symptomatic relief via sedation
			- Counter-irritants - e.g. menthol-containing preparations
			- Tricyclic antidepressants - likely through mechanisms similar for Tx of chronic pain
		- _Phototherapy_ - UVB phototherapy useful in itch stemming fro variety of primary skin diseases, but limited efficacy in pruritus caused by systemic diseases (except CKD)
