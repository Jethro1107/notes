# Candida Vulvovaginitis

- **Definition** - common non-sexually transmitted infection of the vagina and vulva caused candida albicans
- **Etiological agent** - Candida albicans (G+ yeast-like fungus)
- **Epidemiology of vaginal candidiasis** - common in pregnant and non-pregnant women:
    - <u>Incidence</u>:
        - Pregnant women - 10-16%
        - Non-pregnant healthy young women - 20-25%
    - <u>Risk of recurrence</u> - \< 5%
- **Predisposing factors of vulvovaginal candidiasis:**
    - <u>Hyperestrogenic state</u> - pregnancy, COC pills
    - <u>Immunosuppressive state</u> - DM, immunosuppressive agents, other congenital or acquired immunodeficiencies
    - <u>Loss of vaginal microbiome</u> - e.g. use of broad spectrum ABx
- **Clinical features of vulvovaginal candidiasis** - Sx typically <u>worse the week prior to menses</u>:
    - <u>Vulval Sx</u> - **vulvar pruritis** is the dominant Sx, which is accompanied by burning sensation or soreness and an overlying erythema (skin fissures may occur)
    - <u>Vaginal discharge</u> - classically thick, white/ yellow, cheesy discharge
    - <u>Urinary Sx</u> - may be a/w dysuria and dyspareunia that is typically perceived as external rather than urethral/vaginal
- **Signs of vulvovaginal candidiasis** - on inspection of the external genitalia:
    - Erythema over the vulva and vagina
    - Skin fissures may be present
    - Presence of thick, cheesy discharge

- **Ix** - <u>vaginal swab from vaginal fornices</u> for microscopy and culture:
    - <u>Indications</u> - only indicated for complicated infections:
        - Severe Sx
        - Pregnancy
        - Recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis
        - Abnormal host
        - Suspected non-albican infections
    - <u>Findings on microscopy</u> - slings of pseudohyphae of candida albicans srrounded by round vaginal epithelial cells: 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/wet_mount_of_candida_vulvovaginitis.png)
- **Dx** - clinical Dx
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>General measures</u> - genital hygiene for all
        - <u>Antifungal agents</u> - initiate antifungal agents only if symptomatic
    - **Genital hygiene:**
        - Keep clean and dry
        - Vulval moisterisers as soap substitutes and regular skin conditions to avoid fissuring
        - Avoid tight fitting synthetic clothing
        - Avoid local irritants (e.g. perfumed products)
    - **Antifungal agents** - indicated only if symptomatic or complicated (<u>maintenance therapy if recurrent</u>):
        - <u>Selection</u> - clotrimazole, econazole, miconazle (e.g. clotrimazole pessary 200 mg for 3 nights)
        - <u>RoA</u>:
            - Oral (C/I for pregnancy)
            - Topical/ vaginal
    - Antifungal (topical vs oral) - clotrimazole, econazole pessary
    - Maintenance
- **Candidiasis and pregnancy:**
    - Asymptomatic colonisation is common (30-40%)
    - Tx only necessary if symptomatic, as in non-pregnant women
    - Not a/w low birth weight or premature delivery
