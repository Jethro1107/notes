# Airway Assessment

- **Aims of airway assessment** - to assess a difficulty in:
    - Laryngoscopy and endotracheal intubation
    - Bag mask ventilation
    - Rescue techniques (e.g. SGA)
- **Approach to airway assessment:**
    - <u>Hx</u> - based on suggestive Sx, and past anaesthetic Hx
    - <u>P/E</u> - general inspection, dental condition, application of predictive tests for difficult laryngoscopy
    - <u>Ix</u> - reserved for selected patients
- **Conditions a/w difficult airway:**
    - <u>Congenital</u> - Downs syndrome, craniofacial syndromes
    - <u>Acquired</u> - obesity, pregnancy, obstructive sleep apnoea, ankylosing spondylitis
    - <u>Iatrogenic</u> - cervical spine fushion, radiotherapy
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - <u>Sx of OSA</u> - snoring, daytime lethargy, witnessed apnoea
    - <u>Sx of airway pathology</u> - e.g. hoarseness/ voice change, shortness of breath, difficulty in lying flat
    - <u>Past anaesthetic Hx</u> - any known airway difficulties
- **P/E:**
    - **General examination:**
        - <u>Body habitus</u> - obesity a/w difficult intubation and bag mask ventilation
        - <u>Beard</u> - a/w poorer seal on bag mask ventilation
        - <u>Obvious head and neck pathology</u> (e.g. goitre) - a/w difficutl airway
        - <u>Medical equipments</u> - halo traction, neck collar
    - **Examination of dentition:**
        - Dentures/ No teeth
        - Presence of loose teeth
        - Single incisor
        - Buck teeth
    - **Predictive tests for difficult laryngoscopy** - tests are combined as individual tests perform poorly, where combination of MP score and TM distance is more predictive:
        - **Mallampati score** - based on amount of pharyngeal structures visualised on open mouth, directly related to the view on laryngoscopy and airway for ventilation:
            - <u>MP1</u> - faucial pillars, soft palate, uvula completely visible
            - <u>MP2</u> - faucial pillars and soft palate visible, uvula obstructed by tongue
            - <u>MP3</u> - only soft palate is visible
            - <u>MP4</u> - soft palate not visible

      
      ![](img/mallampati_score.png)
        - **Thyromental distance** - distance between thyroid notch and mental prominence when head is fully extended:
            - Distance <u>\> 6.5cm</u> is rarely associated w/ difficulty
            - Distance <u>\< 6.5cm but \> 6cm</u> may be associated w/ difficult laryngoscopy but intubation is usually possible
            - Distance <u>\< 6cm</u> is associated w/ difficult direct laryngoscope
        - **Sternomental distance** - distance between sternal notch and tip of mandible:
            - <u>\< 12.5cm</u> - a/w difficult direct laryngoscopy

      
      ![](img/sternomental_distance.png)
        - **Interincisor gap** - distance between incisors when mouth is fully open, determines patency for instrumentation and is dependent on TMJ and upper C-spine mobility:
            - <u>\< 3cm</u> - direct laryngoscopy and ET intubation more difficult
            - <u>\< 2.5cm</u> - SGA more difficult
        - **Cervical neck movement** - degree of extension of cervical spine
            - <u>Limited cervical neck extension</u> (\< 90 degrees) a/w difficult direct laryngoscopy
- **Predictors of problems with bag mask ventilation** - generally related to the inability to get a good seal:
    - Obesity (BMI \> 26 kg/m2)
    - Beard
    - Absence of teeth
    - Facial abnormalities
    - Receding chin
    - Mallampati 3-4
- **Predictors of problems with SGA:**
    - Incisor gap (mouth opening) \< 2.5cm
    - Intraoral and pharyngeal masses
    - Obesity
    - Poor dentition
- **Ix** - not for all patients:
    - Nasoendoscopy
    - CT/ MRI scan
    - Ultrasound
    - Radiographs (CXR, facial X-rays, C-spine X-rays)
