# Abnormal Clotting Profiles

- See notes on rationale of [[Clotting Profiles]]
- **Approach to abnormal clotting profiles:**
    - Confirm bleeding tendancy - abnormal prolongation of coagulation studies should be interpreted w/ +ve bleeding Hx (N.B. lupus anticoagulant)
    - Further investigation based on pattern of abnormal clotting profile

## Prolonged PT and aPTT

- **Definition** - prolonged PT and aPTT reflects:
    - <u>Deficiency or inhibitor in the common pathway</u> - e.g. X, V, II (prothrombin) or I (fibrinogen)
    - <u>Combined deficiency or inhibitor affecting both intrinsic and extrinsic pathways</u> - e.g. severe vitamin K deficiency, liver disease, Acute DIC
- **DDx of prolonged PT and aPTT:**
    - **Inherited disorders** (rare) - deficiency of factor X, V, prothrombin and fibrinogen (afibrinogenaemia)
    - **Acquired disorder:**
        - <u>Deficiency of the common pathway</u>:
            - Amyloidosis-related factor X deficiency
        - <u>Inhibitors of the common pathway</u>:
            - DOACs - direct thrombin inhibitors (dabigatran), direct Xa inhibitors (apixaban, edoxaban, rivaroxaban)
            - Acquired inhibitors of the common pathway (a/w pregnancy, malignancy or connective tissue disorder)
        - <u>Combined deficiency of intrinsic, extrinsic and common pathway</u>:
            - Decompensated liver failure (deficiency in II, V, VII, IX, X, XI)
            - Severe vitamin K deficiency (required in activation of II, VII, IX and X)
            - Warfarin (some assays may produce isolated prolonged PT, while some will cause prolongation in both PT and aPTT)
            - Disseminated intravascular coagulopathy (DIC)
- **Approach to prolonged PT and aPTT** - further Ix to assess for liver disease, DIC +/- deficiency of the common pathway:
    - LFT
    - Fibrinogen level and D-dimer
    - Mixing test
    - Specific clotting factor assay (X, V, II, fibrinogen)

## Isolated prolonged PT

- **Definition** - prolonged PT reflects deficiency or inhibitor of the extrinsic pathway, particularly factor VII
- **DDx of isolated prolonged PT:**
    - **Inherited disorders** (extremely rare) - congenital factor VII deficiency
    - **Acquired disorder:**
        - <u>Acquired factor VII deficiency</u> - often precedes deficiency of other coagulation factor in common and intrinsic pathway as **factor VII has the shortest halflife**:
            - Decompensated liver disease
            - Mild vitamin K deficiency
            - Warfarin use
            - Disseminated intravascular coagulopathy
        - <u>Acquired inhibitors of factor VII</u> - extremely rare, but r/o by mixing studies
- **Approach to prolonged PT and aPTT:**
    - 1:1 mixing studies (r/o inhibitors)

## Isolated prolonged aPTT

- **Definition** - isolated prolonged aPTT reflects deficiency or inhibitor of intrinsic pathway
- **DDx of isolated prolonged aPTT:**
    - **Inherited disorders:**
        - <u>Inherited haemophilia A</u> (X-linked) - congenital factor VIII deficiency
        - <u>Haemophilia B</u> (X-linked) - congenital factor IX deficiency
        - <u>Factor XI deficiency</u> - rare AD disorder in Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry
        - <u>Von Willebrand disease</u> (Autosomal dominant) - generally a/w normal aPTT but slightly elevated aPTT may be seen in severe disease or type 2N disease
    - **Acquired disorders:**
        - <u>Anticoagulant</u> - heparin typically causes isolated prolonged aPTT, as can certain DOACs though less reliably
        - <u>Acquired haemophilia A</u> - acquired factor VIII diseaes may be seen in pregnancy, malignancy, CTD or in older individuals
        - <u>Lupus anticoagulant</u> - anti-phospholipid syndrome a/w lupus anticoagulant that results in prolonged aPTT, but is **a/w a thrombotic tendancy**
        - <u>Malignancies</u> - resulting in production of heparin-like anti-coagulants
- **Approach to isolated prolonged aPTT:**
    - 1:1 mixing test
    - If mixing corrects:
        - Assay for factor VIII, IX, XI, XII
    - If mixing does not correct:
        - Lupus anticoagulant
        - Factor VIII activity
