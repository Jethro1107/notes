# Respiratory Examination

- **Exposure** - Undressed to the waist
- **Position** - Ideally sitting up, 45 degrees if general condition of the patient is poor
- **General approach to the respiratory examination:**
    - **General examination:**
        - Inspection of the immediate environment - O2 and ventilation support, bronchodilators, sputum mug
        - General impressions - respiratory distress, classical signs of COPD, cough
        - Examination of the eyes and face - Pallor, central cyanosis, Horner's syndrome
        - Examination of the neck - cervical LN, JVP
        - Examination of the hands - tar stains, clubbing, wasting and weakness, Pulse rate, flapping tremor
        - Examination of the legs - ankle oedema
    - **Inspection of the chest** - Shape and deformities, lesions of the chest wall, respiratory distress, stigmata of COPD
    - **Palpation of the chest** - chest wall expansion, tracheal position, apex beat
    - **Percussion of the chest**
    - **Auscultation of the chest** - - quality of breath sounds, intensity of breath sounds, adventitious sounds, vocal resonance

## General Examination

- **Inspection of the general environment** - O2/ ventilatory support (e.g. nasal prongs, masks), inhalers (meter-dose), other medications, sputum mug
- **General impression:**
    - **Respiratory distress** (Dyspnoea):
        - **Normal RR** - 12-18 breaths per minute
        - **Technique** - at the end of the end, count RR based on movement of the nipples
            - Traditionally, count RR superstitiously while affecting to count the pulse.
        - Abnormal rates of breathing:
            - Tachypnoea - RR \> 24 breaths per minute
            - Bradypnoea - RR \< 8 breathes per minute
    - **Stigmata of COPD** - signs that reflect airway obstruction, and hyperinflation of the lungs:
        - **Increased work of breathing** - in-sucking of intercostals and supraclavicular fossa, recruitment of the accessory muscles (e.g. SCM, platysma, straps)
        - **Hyperinflation of the lungs** - Barrel chest, Tracheal tug, Harrison's sulcus, Hoover's sign
        - **Compensation to maintain patent airway** - Tripod position, Dahl's sign
    - **Cough** - character of the cough
        - 'Bovine' cough - lack of usual explosive character of cough, reflects vocal cord paralysis (e.g. L. recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy)
        - Wheezy, muffled, ieffective cough - obstructive lung diseases
        - Loose, productive cough - reflects excessive bronchial excretions characteristic in pneumonia (late), bronchectasis, or chronic bronchitis
        - Dry, irritating cough - pneumonia (initially), asthma, CA lung, ILD, ACEi
        - Barking cough - upper airway infection, or pertussis
- **Examination of the eyes and face:**
    - **Pallor** - reflects anaemia
    - **Central cyanosis** - reflects increased concentration of deoxyhaemoglobin (sign of severe hypoxaemia \< 90% or polycythaemia)
    - **Horner's syndrome** - meiosis (dilated pupil), partial ptosis, anhidrosis
        - Respiratory Ddx - Pancoust tumour, look for other signs suggestive of Pancoust tumour, e.g. facial plethora, Pemberton's sign, wasting of small hand muscles due to cervical plexopathy
- **Examination of the neck:**
    - Cervical lymphadenopathy
    - JVP
- **Examination of the hands:**
    - **Tar staining** - sign of cigarette smoking
    - **Clubbing** - up to 80% patients w/ clubbing have respiratory disease
        - **Grading** - see detailed notes on clubbing
        - **Respiratory Ddx** - Abscesses (Suppurative pneumonia), Bronchiectasis, Cystic fibrosis, Empyema, Fibrosis
    - **Wasting and weakness** - Infiltration into brachial plexus (T1 particularly) causes wasting and weakness of intrinsic hand muscles (particularly finger abduction)
    - **Pulse rate** - often compensation to respiratory diseases. Look for pulsus paradoxus
    - **Flapping tremor** (asterixis) - postural tremor reflecting underlying metabolic encephalopathy:
        - **Technique** - Dorsiflex hands and fingers spread out in an out-stretched hands, observe for tremor with a 2-3s cycle
        - **Non-specific** - T2RF, Decompensated cirrhosis (HE), Uraemia
            - **Respiratory Ddx** - Severe CO2 retention
        - A more clinically relevant sign of CO2 retention includes confusion, bounding pulse and warm peripheries.
- **Examination of the legs:**
    - **Ankle oedema** - reflects cor pulmonale

## Inspection of the Chest

- **Shape and symmetry:**
    - **Barrel chest** - thoracic diameter (AP/ lateral diamter) \> 0.9, reflects hyperinflation of the lung (Emphysema, Severe asthma attack)
    - **Pectus excavatum** - localised depression of sternum (typically aesthetic defect, but can cause restrictive pattern if severe)
    - **Pectus carinatum** - localised prominence of sternum (typically a manifestation of chronic childhood respiratory illness and rickets)
    - **Harrison's sulcus** - linear depression just above the costal margins at site of attachment (signs of chronic respiratory illnesses like COPD and asthma)
    - **Kyphosis** - exxagerated forward curvature of spine
    - **Scoliosis** - lateral bowing of the spine
- **Lesions of the chest wall:**
    - **Scars** - Thoracotomy scars, VATS scars, previous drains
    - **Signs of previous radiotherapy** (e.g. prior lymphoma, breast cancer, lung cancer):
        - Radiotherapy markings - erythema and thickening over irradiated area
        - Rdiotherapy tattoo marks - small tattoo marks indicating the limits of irradiated area
    - **Subcutaneous emphysema** - crackling sensation felt on palpation of skin caused by air trapping subcutaneously
    - **Dilated veins** - typically a sign of SVCO
- **Chest wall expansion**
    - **Technique** - inspect from end of the bed while patient inhales deeply (subsequently confirmed on palpation), often based on the movement of the nipples anteriorly and that of the scapulae posteriorly
    - **Assessment of symmetry**
        - Unilateral - consolidation, unilateral pleural effusion, pneumothorax, collapse, localised fibrosis
        - Bilateral - Severe asthma, COPD, ILD
- Respiration - rate, use of accessory muscles, pattern (see above)

## Palpation of the Chest

- **Chest expansion**:
    - **Principles and technique:**
        - Best visualised if inspiration is from RV to TLC –\> Ask patient to exhale maximally while hands pushes inwards to squeeze away the skin, such that the hands are superimposed onto the chest wall, thumb lifted off the chest wall
        - Ask patient to inhale maximally - thumbs should move away from each other **symmetrically** and **at least 5cm apart**
    - **Demonstrate Hoover's sign if suspected COPD** - paradoxical insucking at lower lobe region
    - \
    ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2F-2gS3ootAN.png?alt=media&token=5c4e379d-ed10-4e1d-8c70-23dcbe003acf>)
- **Mediastinal shift** - demonstrated by tracheal deviation and apex beat
    - **Ipsilateral mediastinal shift** - volume loss lesion (e.g. collapse, fibrosis, pneumonectomy)
    - **Contralateral mediastinal shift** (relatively rare) - volume gain lesion (e.g. MASSIVE pleural effusion, TENSION pneumothorax)
- **Tracheal deviation:**
    - **Technique**:
        - 2nd and 4th fingers placed on the sternoclavicular joints
        - Palpate for paratracheal gutters for their size
        - Run middle finger up and down the trachea
- **Apex beat:**
    - **Definition of apex beat** - most inferior and lateral part felt
    - Apex may be impalpable if lungs are hyperinflated.
- **Tactile fremitus:**
    - Palpable vibrations when patient whispers '一二三'
    - Same significance as vocal resonance

## Percussion of the Chest

- **Principles:**
    - Performed along MCL anteriorly.
    - Performed anteriorly (including axilla) and posteriorly.
    - Performed in upper, middle, and lower zones.
    - Compare between left and right.
- **General flow of percussion:**
    - Clavicle (direct tapping)
    - Bilateral anterior chest wall x3 (typically 1st and 2nd IC space, and select 2 more IC spaces)
    - Bilateral axilla x2
    - Supraclavicular fossa (examination of posterior chest)
    - Posterior chest wall x 4 (ask patient to cross arms to displace the scapulae)
- **Liver and cardiac dullness:**
    - Liver dullness - Right 6th IC space, MCL
    - Cardiac dullness - 5th IC space, MCL
    - Absence of liver or cardiac dullness - reflects hyperinflation of lungs (e.g. Emphysema, Severe Asthma)
- **DDx:**
    - Dull - Consolidation or collapse
    - Stony dull - pleural effusion
    - Hyperresonnant - COPD, pneumothorax

## Auscultation of the Chest

- **Sites** - auscultation symetrically in the anterior, posterior, and axillary regions following that of percussion
- **Note** - quality of breath sounds, intensity of breath sounds, and presence of adventitious breath sounds, vocal resonance
- **Quality of breath sounds**
    - **Normal breath sounds** (vesicular breathing) - louder and longer on inspiration than expiration, with no gaps in between
        - Mechanisms of vesicular breathing - caused by turbulent airflow in the upper airway, not fully deminished on either sides
    - **Bronchial breath sounds** - hollow and blowing quality, audible gap between inspiration and expiration, where expiration may have a higher intensity than inspiration
        - Mechanisms of bronchial breathing - caused by reduced filtering of turbulence in upper airway due to reduction of air-filled lung
        - **DDx:**
            - Consolidation - e.g. lobar pneumonia, haemorrhage, atelectasis, aspiration, lung cancer
            - Pleural effusion - above level of effusion
            - Localised pulmonary fibrosis
            - Collapsed lung
    - \
    ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2FFAg3dk6b3x.png?alt=media&token=a5ab0eb6-46a7-4f2c-b165-78394ca9fd1b>)
- **Intensity of breath sounds**
    - Either normal or reduced.
    - **DDx of asymetrical reduction of breath sounds:**
        - **Bronchial obstruction** - e.g. CA lung
        - **Disorders of the pleura** - e.g. Pleural effusion (below level of effusion), Pneumothorax
        - **Parehnchymal lung disease** - e.g. Pneumonia, collapsed lung

### Adventitious Sounds

- **Types of adventitious sounds:**
    - Wheezes - continuous, musical quality
    - Crackles - interrupted, non-musical quality
- **Wheezes:**
    - **Description** - continuous sounds with a musical quality, described in relation to phase of respiration
    - **Pathophysiology** - continuous oscillation of opposing airway walla implying significant airway obstruction (better heard on expiration because airway is narrower)
    - **DDx:**
        - **Stridor** (wheeze louder on inspiration) - reflects upper airway obstruction (e.g. FB, epiglottitis, croup etc.)
        - **Wheeze**:
            - **Localised expiratory wheeze** - fixed partial bronchial obstruction by CA lung
            - **Diffuse expiratory wheeze** - asthma or COPD
- **Crackles:**
    - **Description** - interrupted, non-musical adventicious sounds
    - **Pathophysiology:**
        - Collapsed small airway - due to instability of airways
        - Sudden opening of small airways and alveoli - due to high inspiratory pressure
    - **Quality - fine vs coarse:**
        - **Fine crackles** - velcro when pulled apart, sound of hair rubbed between fingers
        - **Coarse crackles** - unpleasant gurgling quality
    - **Timing of the crackles:**
        - **Early inspiratory crackles** - small airway disease (e.g. COPD)
        - **Late inspiratory, or pan-inspiratory crackles** - disease with alveolar involvement:
            - **Fine** - ILD, pulmonary oedema
            - **Coarse** - bronchiectasis (changes with posture and coughing), pneumonia
- **Pleural rub:**
    - **Description** - cotinuous or intermittent grating sound
    - **DDx** - pleurisy:
        - **PE and pulmonary infarction**
        - **Parapneumonic effusion**
        - **Pneumothorax**
        - **Malignant involvement of the pleura**

### Vocal Resonance

- **Description** - increased transmission of sound to stethoscope, indicates and confirm suspicion of consolidation
- **Mechanisms:**
    - **Normal lung** - transmission of low-pitched componants, while high-pitched components are attenuated
    - **Consolidated lungs** - increased transmission of high-pitched components, 'e' sounds like 'a'
