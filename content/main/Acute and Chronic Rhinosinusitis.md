# Acute and Chronic Rhinosinusitis

- **Definition** - infection and inflammation of the mucosal lining the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses (same mucosa, hence subjected to the same disease)
- **Clinical entities** - arbitrarily classified into acute rhinosinusitis or chronic rhinositis with subsequent classification:
    - <u>Acute rhinosinusitis</u> - nasal discharge \<= 4 weeks with compatible Hx and nasoendoscopic findings (further classified into viral and bacterial)
    - <u>Chronic rhinosinusitis</u> - nasal discharge \>= 12 weeks with compatible Hx and nasoendoscopic findings
- **Etiology** - complicated from simple infection in individual w/ predisposing factors:
    - **Cause of infection** - usually rhinogenic in origin, ocassionally acquire from dental abscesses:
        - <u>Rhinogenic in origin</u> - prior URTI or rhinitis but ascends into the paranasal sinuses
    - **Predisposing factors of sinusitis** - anatomical deficits near ostiomeatal complex, or poor immunity:
        - <u>Poor drainage</u> - 1) DNS, 2) turbinate hypertrophy, 3) Nasal polyps, 4) Nasal neoplasms
        - <u>Poor immunity</u>

### Acute rhinosinusitis

- **Microbiology of acute rhinosinusitis** - initially viral, but invariably becomes bacterial:
    - <u>Rhinogenic causes</u> - S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis
    - <u>Dental causes</u> - anaerobes
- **Clinical features of acute rhinosinusitis:**
    - **Preceeding URTI** - features of URTI (e.g. nasal discharge, cough), but does not resolve (ABRS defined as Sx of viral URTI \> 10d or double worsening after 5-7d)
    - **Nasal Sx** (diagnostic):
        - <u>Nasal discharge</u> - mucopurulent nasal dischage which may manifest as nasal or post-nasal discharge
        - <u>Nasal obstruction</u>
        - <u>Facial pain</u>
        - <u>Anosmia</u>
    - **Otological Sx** - aural fullness, otalgia
    - **Systemic Sx** - e.g. fever, malaise
- **Signs of acute rhinosinusitis:**
    - <u>Palpation</u> - facial tenderness (periorbital region, upper cheek, upper teeth)
    - <u>Nasoendoscopic findings</u>:
        - Mucosal oedema and narrowing of middle meatus
        - Mucopurulent discharge in the **middle meatus** or nasopharynx
        - +/- Predisposing anatomical deficits - DNS, polyps
- **Dx of acute rhinosinusitis** - clinical:
    - <u>Dx is made when patient presents with</u>:
        - 1\. Purulent nasal discharge (\< 4 weeks)
        - 2\. Nasal obstruction and/or facial pain/ pressure
    - <u>Differentiation between viral and bacterial rhinosinusitis</u>:
        - **Acute bacterial rhinosinusitis** - Sx of ARS persistent for \> 10d or double worsening over the 5-7th day
        - **Acute viral rhinosinusitis** - Sx of ARS \< 10d with no evidence of clinical deterioration
- **Mx of uncomplicated acute rhinosinusitis:**
    - Supportive Tx:
        - Analgesics - pracaetamol or codeine
        - Nasal douching
        - Short term nasal decongestants (\< 7d) - e.g. ephedrine, oxymetazoline
    - ABx - broad spectrum ABx
    - Intranasal steroid spray
- **Cx of acute rhinosinusitis:**
    - <u>Extracranial complications</u>:
        - **Periorbital cellulitis** - cellulitis of the periorbital tissue, resulting in <u>occular pain</u> and <u>eyelid swelling</u> in the <u>absence of visual Sx</u>
        - **Orbital cellulitis** - involvement of orbital tissue, resulting in <u>proptosis</u>, <u>diplopia</u>, or <u>visual changes</u> on top of occular pain and eyelid swelling
        - **Osteomyelitis of the sinus bones** - dull pain over involved site with tenderness, erythema, or swelling
    - <u>Intracranial complications</u>:
        - **Meningitis** - fever, neckstiffness, w/o focal neurological signs
        - **Septic carvenous sinus thrombosis** - CN III, IV, V1 and VI palsies
        - **Intracranial abscess**

### Chronic rhinosinusitis

- **Definition** - clinical syndrome characterised by chronic inflammation involving the mucosa of the nasal cavity and paranasal sinuses lasting \> 12 weeks
- **Clinical features of chronic rhinosinusitis** (4 cardinal Sx) - not as systematically ill as acute rhinosinusitis:
    - **Nasal discharge** - anterior or posterior nasal discharge \> 12 weeks of variable colour (white to yellow), depending if there is active infection
    - **Nasal obstruction** - typically <u>bilateral</u> in most patients although some note that one side to be more severe than the other side (unilateral obstruction suggests anatomical defects or tumours)
    - **Facial discomfort** - vague non-specific discomfort over the face
    - **Disorders of smell** - hyposmia or anosmia, which is much more common in CRS with NP than those without NP
- **Clinical subtypes of chronic rhinosinusitis** - 3 clinical subtypes:
    - Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyps (CRS with NP) - 20-33%
    - Allergic fungal rhinosinusitis (AFRS) - \< 5%
    - Chronic rhinosinusitis without NP (CRS without NP) - 60-65%
- **Mx:**
    - <u>Intranasal steroid</u>
    - <u>Nasal saline irrigation</u>
    - <u>ABx</u>:
        - Short-term ABx - for acute superimposed infections
        - Long-term ABx (e.g. macrolide or doxycycline) - for anti-inflammatory or immunomodulatory effects
    - <u>Antihistamine</u> - for atopy
    - <u>Surgery</u> - Functional endoscopic sinus surgery (FESS)
