# Cardiovascular Examination

- **Position** - patient lying in the bed at 45 degree angle (further positioning of patient during auscultation)
- **General flow:**
    - **General examination** - immediate environment, general appearance, eyes, face, hands, legs
    - **Examination of the arterial pulses** - LL pulses (dorsalis pedis, posterior tibial, popliteal, femoral), radial pulse (rate, rhythm, delays), brachial pulse, carotid pulse (character and volume)
    - **Examination of the venous system** - JVP (height and character)
    - **Examination of the precordium**:
        - Inspection - scars, implants, pulsations, dilated veins
        - Palpation - apex beat position and character, parasternal heave, thrills
        - Auscultations - heart sounds over 4 areas, radiation, maneuvers, basal creps
    - **Systems review:**
        - **Lungs** - basal crepitations, pleural effusions
        - **Abdomen** - liver (pulsatile tender hepatic enlargement in severe RHF), spleen (splenomegaly of IE), ascites (severe RHF)
        - **Thyroid** - if goitre present
- **4 aims of cardiovascular examination:**
    - **Functional diagnosis** - is the patient in clinically significant heart failure?
    - **Rhythm diagnosis** - what rhythm is the patient currently in?
    - **Structural diagnosis** - is there any evidence of structural heart disease?
    - **Etiological diagnosis** - what is the underlying etiology of the current presentation, e.g. IHD, rheumatic, IE?

# General Examination of the Cardiovascular System

- **Immediate environment** - vitals, cardiac monitors, O2/ ventilation support, drips (amiodarone, vasopressors)
- **General impressions:**
    - **Respiratory distress** - rapid and laboured breathing
    - **Body habitus** - obesity, cardiac cachexia, muscle wasting
    - **Syndromal features** - Marfan's syndrome, Down's syndrome, Turner's syndrome
- **General examination of the eyes and face**
    - **Eyes:**
        - **Pallor** - reflects anaemia (precipitating factor of IHD)
            - **Cardiac causes of anaemia** - mechanical haemolytic anaemia (red cell fragmentation syndrome) due to mechanical valve, NcNc Anaemia of IE
        - **Jaundice** - reflects hyperbilirubinaemia
            - **Systematic classification of causes of jaundice** - pre-hepatic, intra-hepatic, post-hepatic
            - **Cardiac causes of jaundice** - Mechanical haemolytic anaemia due to mechanical valve (more common), cardiac cirrhosis (extremely uncommon unless severe TR)
        - **Xanthelasma** - intracutaneous deposition of cholesterol around the eyes
        - **Arcus senilis**
        - **Thyroid eye disease**
    - **Face:**
        - **Mitral facies** - rosey cheeks (CO2 retention in pHTN) with bluish tinge (low CO status)
        - **Central cyanosis** - hypoxaemia from respiratory diseases (T1RF) or cyanotic congenital heart disease
        - **High arched palate** - indicative of Marfan's syndrome
        - Mucosal petechiae in IE
        - Poor dentition in IE
    - **Neck** - inspection for goitre
- **General examination of the hands**
    - **Clubbing** - proper examination involves inspection of fingernails from side at eye level to inspect for loss of hyponychial angle (long-list of DDx)
        - **Pathophysiology** - mechanistically unknown, but PDGF-theory may be a sufficient explanation
        - **Cardiac causes** - IE, cyanotic congenital heart disease
    - **Signs of infective endocarditis:**
        - **Splinter haemorrhages** - described as linear haemorrhages lying parallel to long-axis of fingernails
            - **Non-specific** - can occur in trauma, but can also arise from vasculitic phenomenon of IE, and other vasculitic diseases (e.g. polyarteritis nodosa, rheumatoid arthritis), APLS, sepsis, haematological malignancies, profound anaemia
        - **Janeway lesions** (rare but specific) - non-tender erythematous maculopapular lesions on the palms and soles due to septic emboli
        - **Osler's node** (rare but specific) - tender erythematous nodules on the pulp of fingers, toes, or on the thenar or hypothenar eminences (IC deposition)
    - **Xanthomas** - indicative of hyperlipidaemia:
        - Tendon xanthoma (type II) - yellow/ orange deposition of lipids seen over tendons of the hands, achilles, and elbow
        - Palmar xanthomas (type III) - yellow/ orange deposition of lipids seen on the palmar surface
        - Tuberoeruptive xanthoma (type III) - inflamed xanthomas that appear to have coelasce
- **General examination of the lower limb**
    - **Oedema** - assessment of presence of pitting oedema, whether it is unilateral or bilateral, and the level of oedema
    - **Venous staining**
    - **Venous grafting for CABG** (typically along the great saphenous distribution)

# Examination of the arterial pulse

- **General flow of examination of arterial pulses** - from the leg up, palpate and assess the arterial pulses bilaterally for:
    - Rate - normal, bradycardia, tachycardia
    - Rhythm - regular, regularly irregular, irregularly irregular
    - Delays - radio-radial delay, radio-femoral delay
    - Character (best examined at carotid) - special maneuvers (e.g. collapsing pulse at radial artery), waveforms
    - Volume - increased or deminished
- **Rate and rhythm** - estimate over 15s if regular, 30s if irregular
    - **Rhythm** - based on whether the arrival of the next pulse is anticipated
    - **D/dx of rhythm diagnosis**
        - \

    - **Irregularly irregular rhythm:**
        - **Atrial fibrillation** (most common) - functional AV block causes conduction of fibrillation waves to ventricles irregularly
            - **Character of pulse** - varies from beat to beat
            - **Pulse deficit** - HR on auscultation \> pulse rate because of varying SV (early contractions preceded by short diastolic filling time cannot be palpable peripherally)
        - **AFL or AT with variable block**
        - **Multifocal AT**
        - **Ectopic beats** - arrival of ectopic beats in a non-fixed manner
        - **Mobitz II AV block**
    - **Regularly irregular rhythm:**
        - **Sinus arrhythmia** - increased rate on inspiration, decreased rate on expiration
        - **Fixed ectopic beats:**
            - \
            ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2FDqPqpM13M9.png?alt=media&token=d01b1d04-d51e-4ce1-81cd-1bc00c0c6175>)
            - **Ventricular bigeminy** - 1 ectopic every two beats
            - **Ventricular trigeminy** - 1 ectopic every 3 beats
        - Mobitz type I blocks
- **Delays** - radio-radial delay, radio-femoral delay
    - Radio-femoral delay - noticible delay in the arrival of the femoral pulse
        - Ddx - Coarctation of the aorta, aortic dissection (distal to origin of subclavian artery)
    - Radio-radio delay - difference in timing and volume of radial pulses
        - Ddx - subclavian artery stenosis/ occlusion, dissection of thoracic aorta
- **Character and volume** - best appreciated at carotid pulse (closer and hence resembles more of aortic pressure wave)
    - **Changes in volume of arterial pulses**:
        - **Increased** - bounding pulse
        - **Decreased** - aortic stenosis, cardiac tamponade, forward failure
    - **Changes in character:**
        - Pulsus bisferens - double peak and jerky
            - DDx - HOCM, AS + AR
        - Slow rising pulse (pulses parvus et tardus, anarcotic pulse) - weak, and delayed pulse with slow notched upstroke, slow downstroke
            - DDx - AS
        - Collapsing pulse (Corrigan's sign) - AR, hyperdynamic circulation (Anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, sepsis), peripheral arteriovenous fistula, ruptured sinus of Valsalva, arteriosclerotic aorta
        - Pulsus alternens - alterating strong and weak pulses seen in severe LV failure
        - Pulsus paradoxus - exagerated drop in BP (\> 10 mmHg) on inspiration pathophysiologically due to an obstructive process preventing venous return
            - DDx:
                - Cardiac tamponade (e.g. constrictive pericarditis) - as a result of obstructed venous return to RV
                - Hyperinflation of lungs - increased capacitance of pulmonary circulation, decreasing venous return to LV

# Examination of the precordium

- **Inspection of the precordium:**
    - **Scars:**
        - **Midline sternotomy** - CABG, valvular replacement
    - **Chest wall abnormalities** - Pectus excavatum, kyphoscoliosis (suggestive of Marfan's syndrome)
    - **Implantable devices** - pacemaker (smaller), Implantable cardioverter-defibrillators
    - **Pulsations** - apex beat in thin individuals, hepatic pulsations, pulmonary artery pulsations (pHTN)
- **Palpation of the precordium:**
    - Apex beat - by definition the most lateral and inferior point at which pulsation is detected
        - Location:
            - Normal - Left 5th intercostal space, MCL
            - Displaced (by fingerbreadth) - LV enlargement, occasionally due to chest wall abnormalities, or respiratory diseases (e.g. COPD)
        - Character:
            - Normal - small area (size of small coin), gently lifts the finger
            - Thrusting - displaced, diffuse, non-sustained impulse:
                - Indicative of volume overload - e.g. AR, MR, DCM
            - Heaving - forceful, sustained impulse:
                - Indicative of pressure overload - e.g. HTN, AS
            - Tapping - palpable S1 (Pathognomonic for MS)
            - Double-impulse - two impulses per systole (Pathognomonic for HCM)
    - Parasternal heave - impulse based on heel of hand rested on left sternal border and fingers lifted slightly off the chest
        - Presence of heaving - RVH or severe LA enlargement (e.g. MS)
    - Thrills - palpable thrills over the 4 auscultation spots (presence indicative of at least G4 murmurs)
        - Apical thrills - best felt in left lateral decubitus
        - Basal thrills (aortic and pulmonary area) - best felt when sitting up and leaning forward
- **Auscultation of the precordium** - see detailed notes on auscultation

    - Axilla, diaphragm - radiation of MR
    - Apex, bell - MS murmur

    - LLSB, diaphragm - TR (formality, difficult to hear)
    - LUSB, diaphragm - P2 (loudness), PS/PR, AR
    - Aortic area, diaphragm - AS, AR
    - LSB, diaphragm, full expiration leading forward - accentuation of AR
    - Carotid, diaphragm - radiation of AS
- **Auscultation of back** - basal creps, suspect pleural effusion if silent

## Normal heart sounds

- **Pitch** - both high pitch (diaphragm)
- **S1** - closure of MV and TV, indicative of beginning of ventricular systole
- **S2** (higher pitch than S1) - closure of AV and PV, indicative of end of ventricular systole (typically A2 before P2 because difference in pressures of aorta and pulmonary artery)
    - **Splitting of S2** - typically physiological, but is appreciated best at pulmonary area and along the LUSB and on inspiration (increased capacitance and hence decreased pressure of pulmonary circulation)

## Murmurs

- **Definition of murmurs** - continuous sounds caused by turbulence of blood flow
- **Flow murmurs** - occurs in hyperdynamic circulations (e.g. pregnancy, anaemia, thyrotoxicosis) where turbulence is created by excess velocity (\> 4 m/s) across ortic and pulmonary valves during ventricular systole
- **General descriptors of murmurs** - 1) Timing, 2) Intensity, 3) Where it is heard best, 4) Radiation, 5) Accentuating maneuvers
- **Character and timing of a murmur:**
    - **Pansystolic murmur**
        - **Description** - Murmurs that are of uniform pitch and intensity, beginning at the S1 and extends up to S2
        - **Mechanism** - occurs when ventricles leak into a lower pressure system, where a pressure difference occurs throughout systole (hence 'pan'-systolic)
        - **DDx:**
            - **MR** - best heard at the apex
            - **TR** - best heard at the tricuspid area (LLSB)
            - **VSD** - best heard at left sternal border (exact location depends on the defect), radiates throughout the precordium
    - **Ejection systolic murmur**
        - **Description** - Murmurs that occur during ventricular ejection (i.e. later part of ventricular systole), intensity not uniform, i.e. crescendo-decrescendo.
        - **Mechanism** - Obstruction at the outflow tract, and waxes and wanes in association with changes in intra-ventricular pressure, thus resulting in crescendo-decrescendo/ saw cutting wood nature.
        - **DDx:**
            - **Aortic stenosis** - best heard at the aortic area, radiates to bilateral carotids.
            - **Pulmonary stenosis** - best heard at the pulmonary area
            - **Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy**
        - Note that hyperkinetic (high-output) states in children, anaemia, thyrotoxicosis, pregnancy and fluid overload also cause cardiac murmurs despite normal heart valves.
    - **Early diastolic murmur**
        - **Description** - Murmurs that occur in early-diastole, continuous with S2, often decrescendo in nature
        - **Mechanism** - immediate regurgitation from the aorta valve or the pulmonary valve.
        - **DDx:**
            - **Aortic regurgitation** - best heard at left sternal border, sitting up on full expiration
            - **Pulmonary regurgitation** - best heard at pulmonary area (LUSB)
                - Note that early diastolic murmur from PR arising from pHTN due to MS is called a Graham Stell murmur.
    - **Mid diastolic murmur**
        - **Description** - Murmurs that are low pitch and rumbling (B), begins later in diastole, and often continuous with the next S1.
        - **Mechanism** - Obstruction through the AV valve, resulting in turbulent blood flow during atrial systole.
        - **DDx:**
            - **Mitral stenosis** - best heard at the apex with a bell, in left lateral decubitus position, radiates to the axilla.
            - **Austin flint murmur** in severe AR - concomitant diastolic murmur over LSB and soft S1
    - **Continuous murmur**
        - **Description** - Murmurs that are present throughout the cardiac cycle, during systole and diastole.
        - **Mechanism** - Communication between systemic and pulmonary circulation, resulting in constant turbulent flow between the two systems
        - **DDx:**
            - Patent ductus arteriosus
            - Arteriovenous fistula
            - Ruptured sinus of valsalva
- **Location heard loudest**
    - Not definitive, but usually valvular heart diseases are heard best at their corresponding areas.
- **Radiation:**
    - Mitral valve diseases classically radiates to the axilla.
    - Aortic stenosis classically radiates to the bilateral carotids.
    - \
    ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2F75zQMQNzQo.png?alt=media&token=9e54c192-2e1b-49c3-8d11-06d1674bc829>)
- **Grading** - 1-6 for systolic murmurs, 1-4 for diastolic murmurs
    - **Grade 1** - Very soft, only heard after tuning in
    - **Grade 2** - Soft, but can be heard immediately
    - **Grade 3** - Clearly audible, but thrills not palpable
    - **Grade 4** - Clearly audible, and thrills palpable
    - **Grade 5** - Audible when the stethoscope is only partially touching the chest
    - **Grade 6** - Can be heard without placing stethoscope on the chest
- **Accentuating maneuvers:**
    - **Respiration:**
        - **Inspiration** - results in increased systemic venous return (increased RV preload), and decreased pulmonary venous return (decreased LV preload), hence, <u><u>accentuates right-heart murmurs</u></u>, and <u><u>weakens left heart murmurs</u></u>.
        - **Expiration** - results in decreased systemic venous return (decreased RV preload), and increased pulmonary venous return (increased LV preload), hence **accentuates left-heart murmurs**, and **weakens right heart murmurs**.
    - **Lean forward, deep expiration:**
        - Brings the base of the heart closer to chest wall.
        - Accentuates AR, PR
    - **Left, lateral decubitus position:**
        - Brings the apex closer to the chest wall.
        - Accentuates MDM of MS
    - **Valsalva maneuver:**
        - Force expiration against closed glottis, decrease in systemic venous return.
            - Eccentuates MVP and HOCM
            - Weakens AS and MR
    - **Standing to Squatting:**
        - Promotes venous return, and increased systemic vascular resistance (increased preload, decreased afterload)
        - Accentuates MR (due to increased preload) and AS (due to increased afterload)
        - Weakens MVP and OCM
