# Acquired Coagulopathies

- **Common forms of acquired coagulopathies:**
    - Disseminated intravascular coagulopathy
    - Liver disease
    - Renal failure
- **Bleeding tendancy in liver and biliary disease:**
    - **Pathophysiology of bleeding tendancies in severe liver and biliary disease:**
        - <u>Impaired hepatic synthesis of coagulation factors</u> - factor V, VII (most Sn to testing due to shortest half-life), VIII, IX, X, XI, prothrombin, fibrinogen is impaired impaired synthetic function as a result of hepatocellular insufficiency
        - <u>Thrombocytopenia</u> - may occur if portal hypertension is present resulting in thrombocytopenia
        - <u>Vitamin K deficiency</u> - may occur in biliary obstruction (e.g. MBO)
    - **Clinical manifestations** - genrally increased propensity of GI bleeding:
        - Variceal bleeding
        - Other non-variceal GI bleeding (e.g. peptic ulcers)
    - **Mx** - depending on underlying cause:
        - Parenteral vitamin K - correct vitamin K deficiency
        - Platlet concentrates or plasma products - only reserved for acute bleeds or to cover interventional procedures
- **Bleeding tendancy in renal failure:**
    - **Pathophysiology of bleeding tendancy in renal failure** - proportional to plasma urea concentration:
        - <u>Mild thrombocytopenia</u> - may occur in context of renal failure
        - <u>Platelet dysfunction</u> - accumulation of metabolic wastes (e.g. urea) resulting in inhibition of platelet function
    - **Clinical manifestations** - GI bleeding is excessively common
    - **Mx:**
        - Dialysis
        - DDAVP
        - Platelet concentrate, or red cell transfusion when indicated
