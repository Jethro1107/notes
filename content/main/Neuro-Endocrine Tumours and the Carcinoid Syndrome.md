# Neuro-Endocrine Tumours and the Carcinoid Syndrome

- **Definition** - also known as carcinoid tumours, are malignancies derived from the <u>enterochromaffin cells</u> most commnly in the appendix
- **Sites of carcinoid tumours:**
    - SB (most commonly appendix)
    - rectum
- **Route of spread** - increased risk of local and haematogenous spread if \> 2cm:
    - <u>Direct invasion</u> - to adjacent organs
    - <u>Haematogenous spread</u> - via porto-venous system to the liver
- **Pathophysiology of NETs:**
    - <u>Neoplastic growth of enterochromaffin cells</u> - neoplastic growth of enterochromaffin cells by the primary tumour, which secrete products
    - <u>Hepatic metabolisation of secretory products</u> - if the secretory products are produced by the primary tumour in the SB, they are subjected to liver metabolisation and thus **do not reach systemic circulation**
    - <u>Liver metastasis and onset of Carcinoid syndrome</u> - neoplastic enterochromaffin cells that metastasize to the liver bypasses portal-venous circulation, enabling **release of secretory products directly into systemic circulation**, resulting in 'carcinoid syndrome'
- **Clinical features of NETs** - 'carcinoid syndrome' refers to the systemic Sx resulting from secretory products of NETs, usually indicating <u>hepatic metastasis</u>:
    - **Features of local NET:**
        - <u>Intestinal ischaemia</u> - sudden onset of periumbilical pain +/- PR bleeding w/ mucus due to mesenteric infiltration or vasospasm
        - <u>Mechanical SBO</u> - sudden onset of periumbilical pain, abdominal distension, N/V and obstipation due to tumour mass
    - **Features of carcinoid syndrome** - excess 5-HT, and other vasoactive substances such as bradykinin, histamine, prostaglandin:
        - <u>Heart failure</u> - excessive fibrogenesis w/ cardiac involvement (e.g. TR, PS, RV endocardial plaques)
        - <u>Wheezing</u> - due to excessive histamine resulting in bronchoconstriction
        - <u>Diarrhoea</u> - accelerated gut peristalsis and secretion due to increased serotinergic tone
        - <u>Facial telangiectasia</u> - increased vasoactive substances

- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Surgical Mx</u> - definitive Mx of primary tumour (non-curative if hepatic metastasis has already occured for Sx relief)
        - <u>Interventional radiological therapies</u> - hepatic artery embolisation to retard growth of hepatic deposits
        - <u>Pharmacological therapy</u>:
            - Somatostatic analogue (200 µg 3 times daily by subcutaneous injection) to control hormone secretion
            - Chemothreapy (5-FU, streptozotocin) as rescue therapy if all other therapies have failed
