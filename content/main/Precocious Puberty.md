# Precocious Puberty

- **Definition and terminology** - onset of puberty at younger age than expected than normal population (i.e. \> 2 SD earlier than population mean):
    - <u>Classical age cutoff</u> - onset of pubertal signs occuring before 8y for girls or 9y for boys
    - <u>Paediatric Endocrine Society cutoff</u> - lower cutoff for precocious puberty to \< 7y in white girls and \< 6y in black girls based on observational studies that suggest puberty may be occuring earlier than previously though
- **Epidemiological data** - some data suggest that pubertal development occurs slightly <u>earlier</u> than the <u>clinically imposed norm</u>:
    - **Western data** (PROS network) - 6.7% of white and 27.2% of black girls would have been characterised as having precocious puberty using classical cutoff
    - **Eastern data** - multiple epidemiological study that urban Chinese girls and boys are experiencing earlier pubertal development than currently used norms
- **Approach to grey area defined in PROS study** (not universally accepted) - pubertal onset occuring between 7-8y in white girls and 6-8y in black girls should be evaluated if:
    - Unusual rapid pubertal progression resulting in bone age advancement by \> 2y and a predicted height that is \< 150cm or 2SDs below genetic target height
    - S/S of a CNS lesions
    - Behaviour-based factors suggesting a child's emotional state is adversely affected by early puberty and potential early menses
- **Variations of normal pubertal development** - development of only one secondary sexual characteristic:
    - <u>Premature thelarche</u> - isolated breast development in girls aged between 2-7y and is often tender on palpation with no discharge (bilateral in 50% of cases):
        - Bone maturation rarely accelerated in isolated thelarche
        - Spontaneous remission in 30-60% cases within 18 mo, which do not require Tx
        - Persistent, marked or progression of thelarche should raise posibility of precocious puberty
    - <u>Premature adrenarche</u> - appearance of pubic hair \< 8y in boys or girls:
        - Usually isolated pubic hair growth (ocassionally axillary hair), but often absence of hirsutism
        - Growth velocity and bone age maturation are usually only slightly accelerated
        - As a result of early/ accelerated maturation of adrenal function that precedes puberty (requires a r/o of CAP)
        - Some postulate that it is in fact an early manifestation of steroidogenic dysregulation of PCOS (15-20% to develop PCOS) and is thus not a normal variant, and associated w/ cardiometabolic risk
        - No pharmacological Tx required but should be closely followed throughout puberty
    - <u>Premature menarche</u> - menstruation w/o other signs of puberty in girl \< 9y:
        - Transient estrogen overactivity often observed which may account for isolated premature menarche and accelerated bone maturation
        - May occur w/ thelarche, and frequent presence of functional ovarian cyst
        - Important to r/o intra-uterine pathologies, FB, and McCune-Albright syndrome
        - Pharmacological Tx to arrest puberty is often discussed principally because of risk of height potential (but rarely undertaken)
- **Approach to suspected precocious puberty** - in girls \< 8y and boys \< 9y with accelerated sexual development:
    - <u>Workup</u> - thorough Hx and P/E, assessment of bone age, and close longitudinal follow-up
    - <u>Mx</u> (requires specialist) - not all organic etiologies and certainly not normal variants require Tx
- **Mechanisms of precocious puberty:**
    - <u>Central precocious puberty</u> (10-20% of presentation) - esp. CNS tumours, and progresses rapdly
    - <u>Precocious pseudopuberty</u> (10% of presentation) - adrenal or ovarian cause with autonomous hyperproduction of estrogen resulting in precocious pseudopuberty independent of gonadotropin activation
    - <u>Exogenous precocious puberty</u> - xenoestrogen in chemical pollutants in air, water and food chains
- **DDx of precocious puberty:**
    - **Central precocious puberty** - activation of HPG axis at earlier age, characterised by isosexual pubertal development w/ pubertal changes that is sex-appropriate for the child (i.e. breast budding in F, testicular enlargement in M):
        - **Idiopathic** - 80% idiopathic in girls vs 10% idiopathic in boys
        - **Midline CNS lesions** (particularly in suprasellar region) - this may be a/w concomittant hypopituitarism:
            - <u>Brain tumours</u> - **benign hypothalamic harmatoma** (esp. in young child), glial cell tumours, germ cell tumours, craniopharyngeoma, pinealomas
            - <u>Congenital suprasellar brain defects</u> - subarachnoid cysts, arachnoidoceles, Ranthke cleft cysts
            - <u>Congenital mid-line anolamies</u> - e.g. hydrocephalus, meningomyeloceles, optic nerve hypoplasia, **NF-1**
        - **Traumatic/ iatrogenic** - major head trauma, perinatal insults, cranial irradiation, previous meningoencephalitis
        - **Other endocrinopathies** - e.g. isolated GH deficiency, multiple anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies, hyperprolactinaemia, primary hypothyroidism, undertreated CAP
        - **Genetic** - activating mutation of GPRS4 resulting excessive stimulation of GnRH hormones
    - **Precocious pseudopuberty** - not from activation of HPG axis, such that the process is pituitary independent and adrenal/ovarian or testicular in origin:

- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - **HPI** - onset, progression, and characterisation of Sx:
        - <u>Onset</u> - onset of pubertal features \< 8y in F and \< 9y in M should prompt monitoring
        - <u>Progression</u> - any progression of clinical symptoms from
        - <u>Characterisation of Sx</u> - precise timing of pubertal milestones:
            - **Thelarche** - laterality, progression (remission, persistence, or aggravation), and tenderness
            - **Adrenarche** - pattern (incl. axilla hair), any abnormal perspiration, acne (+ clitoromegaly on P/E)
            - **Menarche** - LMP and cyclicity
    - **Detailed birth Hx:**
        - <u>Birth weight</u> - SGA more prone to precocious pubarche, earlier onset of pubertal development and menarche and faster progression of puberty than children born AGA
        - <u>Perintal insults</u> - e.g. neonatal infections of CNS
    - **PMH** - previous brain trauma, CNS infections
    - **Relevant FHx and SHx**
    - **Parental and child ideas, concerns and expectations** - impact on psychological wellbeing
- **P/E** - focused neurological and endocrine systems:
    - **General examination:**
        - <u>Tanner stages</u> - stage of pubertal maturation
        - <u>Growth velocity</u> - assessment of growth chart
        - <u>General inspection</u> - neurocutaneous markers for NF-1 or McCune-Albright Syndrome, look for scolioisis or body assymetry
    - **Endocrine examination** - examination of thyroid for primary hypothyroidism (inspection and palpation of goitre)
    - **Focused neurological examination:**
        - Fundoscopy
        - Visual field assessment by bedside confrontation
    - **Examination of external genitalia** - esp. in boys where one testis is enlarged (extremly suspicious for an androgen-producing tumour)
- **Ix:**
    - **Hormonal profile** - LH, FSH, E2, testosterone, TFT, prolactin, beta-hCG:
        - **TFT** - screen for primary hypothyroidism
        - **Prolactin** - screen for hyperprolactinaemia
        - **Principles of interpretation of hormonal profile:**
            - **FSH and LH:**
                - <u>Pre-pubertal LH, FSH</u> - suggestive of a peripheral cause
                - <u>Pubertal FSH/ LH</u> - suggestive of **central precocious puberty**
            - **E2, testosterone, beta-hCG:**
                - <u>E2</u> - pubertal E2 will account for thelarche in girls
                - <u>Testosterone</u> - pubertal testosterone will account for andrenarche in girls and increased testicular volume in boys
                - <u>Beta-hCG</u> - important in boys:
                    - Undetectable in central precocious puberty, familial testotoxicosis, or McCune-Albright syndrome
                    - Raised hCG suggestive of a hCG-producing germ-cell tumour
    - **LHRH test** - stimulatory dynamic test:
        - <u>Principles</u> - administration of synthetic LHRH to stimulate pituitary release of FSH and LH:
            - In an **active HCG axis**, administration of synthetic LHRH will result in a **surge in FSH and LH**
            - In an **inactive HCG axis**, administration of synthetic LHRH will **not result in a surge in FSH and LH**
    - **X-Ray** - for assessment of bone age
    - **Imaging:**
        - Pelvic USG and adrenals (if peripheral cause suspected)
        - MRI Brain and Pituitary (if central cause suspected)
    - **Additional Ix:**
        - **Suspicion of CAH:**
            - **17OHP and DHEAs** - indicated if suspected CAP, NCAH or other androgen-producing adrenal tumours:
                - <u>Indications</u> - peripheral cause in:
                    - Virilised girl clinically (adrenarche) and biochemically (elevated testosterones)
                    - Suspected precocious puberty in boy despite symmetrical prepubertal-size
                - <u>Expected result in CAH or NCAH</u> - raised 17OHP and DHEAs
            - **ACTH stimulation test** - short synathen test to detect accompanying <u>cortisol insufficiency</u>
        - **Testicular USG** - indicated to r/o functioning testicular tumour (esp if asymmetrical testes)

- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Central precocious puberty</u> - necessity of Tx (GnRH analogue) dependent on preservation of adult height potential and minimise psychological difficulties of early puberty
        - <u>Peripheral precocious puberty</u> - Tx depends on underlying cause (e.g. adrenal/ ovarian tumours)
    - **Indications and goals of Mx of central precocious puberty** - when structural causes have been r/o:
        - <u>Preservation of adult height potential</u> - decelerate bone maturation and fusion of epiphysis to preserve adult height potential:
            - Efficacy greatest particularly in girls \< 6y with the greates increase in adult height after treatment
            - Little utility to treat girls \> 8y for the purpose of increasing adult height
            - Intermediate ages requires assessment of the rate of pubertal development, with rapid puberty benefiting more from therapy, while slowly progressive therapy yields normal height outcomes w/o treatment
        - <u>Psychological difficulties</u> - if psychological difficulties a/w early puberty and menarche experienced by the child
    - **GnRH analogues:**
        - <u>Selection</u> - IM leuprolide acetate q4w
        - <u>MOA</u> - constant agonism on GnRH, resulting in:
            - Suppression of FSH and LH
            - Subsequent suppression of E2 and testosterone to prepubertal states
            - Temporary arrest of puberty through medical castration
