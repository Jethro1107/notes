# Venous Thromboembolism

- Venous thromboembolism - defined as abnormal blood clot formation in the venous system, typically presents as deep vein thrombosis (DVT) of LL +/- pulmonary embolism.
- Other sites of abnormal venous thromboembolism:
    - \
    ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2FQCS231fDRu.png?alt=media&token=effdfc52-ff22-4c01-80e5-fd8e47e2ad14>)
    - Jugular vein thrombosis
    - Upper limb DVT
    - Cerebral sinus thrombosis
    - Intra-abdominal thrombosis - e.g. mesenteric vein thrombosis, Budd-Chiari syndrome
- Epidemiology of VTE:
    - Western annual incidence of DVT - 1/1000/y
    - HK annual incidence of DVT - 30/100,000/y (lower)
- Pathophysiology of VTE
    - Virchow's triad - Stasis, Hypercoagulability, Endothelial Injury (stasis insufficient to cause VTE alone)
    - Predisposing factors necessary to provoke VTE:
        - General patient factors - Age, obesity, +ve FHx, immobility, oestrogen-containing oral contraceptives
        - Stasis:
            - General medical conditions - HF/ MI, Stroke (immobility), varicose veins, pelvic obstruction
            - General surgical conditions - major surgery, abdominal or pelvic surgery, major LL orthopaedic surgery
        - Hypercoagulability:
            - Inherited thrombophilia - e.g. AT, PC, PS deficiency, factor V Leiden, Prothrombin G20210A mutation, ?Hyperhomocystinaemia
            - Acquired thrombophilia - Anti-Phospholipid Syndrome
            - Other haematological disorders - MPN (MF, PV, ET), Paroxysmal nocturnal haematuria
            - Other medical conditions - Malignancy, Nephrotic Syndrome, Dehydration
        - Endothelial injury - surgery, radiation, tourniquet, sepsis

    - Thrombosis and embolisation
        - Superficial vein thrombosis (superficial thrombophlebitis) - typically presents with more pain due to inflammation, but a low risk for PE
        - Deep vein thrombosis:
            - Proximal DVT (proximal to and including popliteal vein) - high risk for PE (30% asymptomatic PE, further investigations required)
            - Distal DVT (distal to popliteal vein) - low risk for PE
        - Pulmonary embolism (PE) - thrombus formed in venous system, and subsequently emboli to the pulmonary circulation
- Clinical features of VTE
    - Typical clinical presentation - unilateral limb involvement (if in LL) + presence of predisposing factors
    - Features of DVT - typically unilateral pain, leg swelling, erythema, dilatation of superficial veins
    - Features of pulmonary embolism - dependent on size:
        - \
        ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2F0oYrDX84AE.png?alt=media&token=313e4643-d205-49c7-8e64-eff7a5e15d4c>)
        - Acute small/ medium PE - occlusion of segmental pulmonary artery resulting in ischaemia, infarction +/- pleural effusion:
            - Acute pleurisy
            - Dyspnoea - characteristically restricted breathing
            - Haemoptysis
            - Signs - Tachycardia, pleural rub, crackles, effusion, low-grade fever, raised hemidiaphragm
        - Acute massive PE - reduced CO and acute RHF:
            - Symptoms - Crushing central chest pain, severe dyspnoea +/- fainting or collapse
            - Signs - Tachycardia, hypotension, signs of RHF (raised JVP, RV gallop rhythm, loud P2), cyanosis and reduced urinary output
        - Acute pleurisy (SOB + pleuritic chest pains), cough w/ haemoptysis (indicating pulmonary infarction), +/- cardiac chest pains, syncope and SCD if massive saddle emboli
- Initial evaluation of VTE:
    - Hx:
        - HPI - actively look for compatible limb and chest S/S
        - Screen for provocation:
            - Surgical Hx - Recent operation, trauma or immobility
            - Obstetrics Hx - pregnancy, OC pills
            - Constitutional Sx (VTE as 1st manifestation of malignancy, especially if bilateral)
            - FHx for VTE
    - P/E:
        - Examination of LL
        - Look for S/S of P/E (especially those with proximal DVT)
    - Ix:
        - Baseline CBC + clotting profiles - initial screening for haematological disorders predisposing to VTE + ?baseline for Tx
        - RFT - affects modality of PE workup
        - If PE is clinically suspected (e.g. unexplained dyspnoea):
            - CXR
            - ECG + echocardiogram
            - Diagnostic imaging - CT pulmonary angiogram or VQ scan
- Diagnostic evaluation of VTE:
    - \
    ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2Fvj6oqtQPXk.png?alt=media&token=1fda1ffc-96b7-49c2-9032-8fc739a5726a>)
    - Wells score to calculate <u><u>pre-test probability in haemodynamically stable patients</u></u> - stratifies clinical probability of VTE to determine the necessity of D-dimer:
        - Clinical features - Localised tenderness along distribution of deep venous system, entire leg swollen, calf swelling \> 3cm larger than that on asymptoatic side (measured 10cm below tibial tuberosity), pitting oedema confined to symptomatic leg, collateral superficial veins
        - Predisposing factors - Active cancer (patient receiving Tx for cancer within previous 6 mo or currently on palliative Tx), recent immobilisation of LL (including paralysis and paresis), recent bedridden for \>= 3d or major surgery \<= 4 wks
        - Negative factors - alternative diagnosis
    - D-dimer (applied to low-risk DVT) - aims at r/o of VTE:
        - D-dimer -ve - exclude DVT
        - D-dimer +ve - further workup with compressive US
    - Compression USG Doppler of relevant body parts (applied to moderate-to-high-risk DVT)
