# Peritonsillar Cellulitis and Peritonsillar Abscess

- **Definition and terminology:**
    - <u>Peritonsillar cellulitis</u> - inflammatory reaction of the tissue between the capsule of the palantine tonsil and the pharyngeal muscle
    - <u>Peritonsillar abscess</u> - a deep neck infection characterised by collection of pus between the palatine tonsil and the pharyngeal muscle
- **Epidemiology** - most common deep neck space inefction in children and adolescents (30/100000/y)
- **Anatomy of the peritonsillar region** - loose areolar tissue overlying the tonsil and surrounded by the <u>superior pharyngeal constrictors</u>: 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/anatomy_of_peritonsillar_region.png)
    - The palatine tonsil is located in between the palatoglossal and palatopharyngeal arch
    - Peritonsillar tissue is the loose areolar tissue overlying the tonsil and surrounded by the <u>superior pharyngeal constrictor</u>
    - Peritonsillar abscess typically occur in the suprior pole of the tonsils, between the superior constrictor and the palatopharyngeal arch
- **Pathophysiology of peritonsillar cellulitis and PTA:**
    - **Source of infection and abscess formation:**
        - <u>Preceding throat infection</u> - peritonsillar infections generally precedes tonsillitis or pharyngitis and may progresses from pharyngitis to cellulitis to abscess
        - <u>Weber gland obstruction</u> - seldomly, peritonnsilar cellulitis and PTA may occur in the absence of infections due to obstruction of minor salivary glands above the tonsils
    - **Effects of PTA:**
        - <u>Upper airway obstruction</u> - large PTA may result in uvula deviation and subsequently result in upper airway compromise
        - <u>Spread of the infection resulting in complications</u> - spread to the infection to the carotid sheath, masseter or pterygoid muscles, or deep neck spaces
- **Etiological agents of PTA** - often microbiological:
    - **G+ species:**
        - <u>Staphylococcus spp.</u> - S. aureus
        - <u>Streptococcus spp.</u> - GAS, Streptococcus anginosus
    - **G- species** (primarily respiratory anaerobes) - Fusobacterium (a/w Lemierre's disease), Prevotella spp, Veillonella spp.
- **Clinical features of PTA** - extremely <u>septic</u> w/ impending <u>upper airway obstruction</u>:
    - <u>Non-specific Mx</u> - **fever**, as well as fatigue, irritability, and decreased oral intake due to systemic upset and discomfort
    - <u>Soar throat and odynophagia</u> - usually unilateral progressive severe sore throat as it develops from simple pharyngitis to PTA, often a/w **drooling**, and **'hot potato'** voice due to extreme pain on swallowing
    - <u>Neck swelling</u> - **painful neck swelling**, with pain radiating to the ipsilateral ear
    - <u>Trismus</u> (67%) - due to irritation and spasm of the internal pterygoid muscles, and serves as a differentiating factor from severe pharyngitis or tonsillitis
- **Signs in PTA** - note P/E should be performed in a setting where airway immediated protected by ET tube:
    - **General examination:**
        - <u>General apperance</u> - septic looking, anxious, drooling, ipsilateral neck mass
        - <u>Posture</u> - sniffing position to open airway
        - <u>Respiration</u> - suprasternal retraction, rsepiratory distress +/- stridor
        - <u>Temperature</u> - pyrexic
    - **Examination of the oral cavity** - may be limited by ipsilateral trismus:
        - <u>Tonsils</u> - extremely swollen
        - <u>Uvula</u> - deviated to the opposite side

    
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/peritonsillar_abscess.png)
    - **Neck examination** - cervical and submandibular LN
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, RFT:
        - <u>CBC</u> - neutrophilic leukocytosis
        - <u>RFT</u> - assessment of reduced oral intake and dehydration (increased urea)
    - **Blood cultures** - typically not necessary but sensitivity testing may guide ABx selection in complicated cases
    - +/- **USG** - for differentiation of abscess or cellulitis if unable to differentiate on P/E (can be differentiated by resposiveness to ABx therapy if no impending airway obstruction)
    - +/- **CT head and neck** - for evaluation of complications and extension to give deep neck space infections
- **DDx of PTA** - other causes of sore throat, upper airway obstruction and pharyngeal swelling:
    - <u>Severe tonsillopharyngitis</u> - often bilateral involvement, and minimal trismus
    - <u>Retropharyngeal abscess</u> - a/w sore throat, drooling, fever, but trismus is less common
    - <u>Parapharyngeal abscess</u> - typically a/w a bulge behind the palatopharyngeal arch rather than superior to the tonsil (i.e. a medial displacement of the lateral pharyngeal wall)
    - <u>Epiglottitis</u> - classical teaching is that epiglottitis develops **more rapidly** than PTA, although in practice difficult to distinguish
- **Complications of PTA:**
    - **Local complications:**
        - <u>Upper airway obstruction</u> - evident by stridor and respiratory distress due to abscess encrouching onto the airway
        - <u>Aspiration pneumonia</u> - occurs if abscess ruptures into the airway
    - **Complications related to spread to the carotid sheath:**
        - <u>Lemierre syndrome</u> - jugular vein thrombosis w/ suppurative thrombophlebitis with septic embolisation, resulting in **multiple lung abscess**
        - <u>Carotid artery pseudoaneurysm</u> - can complicate w/ carotid artery rupture
    - **Complication related to spread to deep neck spaces** - deep neck space infections and mediastinitis
    - **Systemic complication:**
        - <u>Sepsis</u> - if result in bacteraemia
        - <u>Sequelae of GAS infection</u> - e.g. rheumatic fever, PSGN
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx** - selection of Mx dependent on 1) suspicion on PTA, and 2) degree of airway obstruction:
        - <u>Secure airway</u> - consult airway specialist to secure airway if comprimised (e.g. in respiratory distress or stridor)
        - <u>General measures</u> - analgesics, IV rehydration
        - <u>Empirical IV ABx</u> - indicated for all patients based on likely etiological organisms, where clinical improvement may be seen w/ peritonsillar cellulitis (no abscess neccesary)
        - <u>Abscess drainage</u> - needle aspiration preferred over incision and drainage
    - **Abscess drainage:**
        - <u>Indications</u>:
            - Airway compromise (after airway is securred)
            - Moderate to large abscess clinically or radiologically
            - Patients w/ peritonsillar abscess or cellulitis not responding to ABx and supportive care
        - <u>Choice of procedures</u>:
            - Needle aspiration - often more tolerable as less invasive and less painful
            - Incision and drainage - more invasive, painful and causes more bleeding
            - Tonsillectomy (see below)
    - **Tonsilectomy** - may be performed immediately (quinsy tonsilectomy), or after resolution of acute intervention (e.g. interval tonsilectomy):
        - <u>Indications for quinsy tonsilectomy</u>:
            - Significant upper airway obstruction or other complications
            - Failure of abscess to resolve w/ otehr drainage techniques
        - <u>Indications for interval tonsillectomy</u>:
            - Severe recurrent pharyngitis or previous episode of PTA
            - Chronic S/S of upper airway obstructioon (e.g. snoring)
