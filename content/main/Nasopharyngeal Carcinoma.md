# Nasopharyngeal Carcinoma

- **Definition** - predimonant tumour type of the nasopharynx, differing from other SCCs in epidemiology, histology, natural history, and response to treatment
- **Epidemiology** - geographically variable:
    - <u>Incidence</u> - 25/100,000/y in HK and southern China (cf 0.5-2/100,000/y in Western countries)
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - **Age** - peak incidence in 50-59y (minor incidence peak in adolescence and young adults)
        - **Sex** - male preponderance (M:F = 2-3:1)
- **Etiology and risk factors of NPC:**
    - **Epstein-Barr virus** (EBV) - as primary etiologic agent in the pathogenesis of NPC w/ detection of both EBV DNA and gene expression in precursor lesions and tumour cells
    - **Diet** - diet rich in <u>nitrosamines</u> (e.g. salted fish, feermented foods) are thought to increase risk of NPC
    - **Genetic factors** - evidence of familial clusters w/ increased risk of NPC if first-degree relatives have NPC
- **Histological classification of NPC** - WHO classification:
    - **Keratinizing SCC** - a/w keratinization and is the most common histology seen in sporadic (non-EBV associated) NPC
    - **Non-keratinizing SCC** - non-keratinizing subtype is further subdivied by differentiation:
        - <u>Undifferentiated, nonkeratinizing SCC</u> - most common histology seen in endemic forms of NPC, w/ strong associations w/ Epstein-Barr virus and has a more favorable prognosis
        - <u>Differentiated nonkeratinizing SCC</u> - not the commonest histology seen in NPC
    - **Basaloid SCC** - w/ few reported cases and are notable for aggresive clinical course and poor prognosis
- **Clinical features of NPC** - classical triad of 1) neck mass, 2) nasal obstruction w/ epistaxis, and 3) severe OME is infrequent but extremely suggestive of NPC:
    - **Asymptomatic** - many patients remain asymptomatic for a prolonged period especially for NPC arising from the fossa of Rosenmuller which is clinically occult
    - **Features of local tumour:**
        - <u>Nasal obstruction</u> - typically unilateral, but ocassionally bilateral nasal obstruction if tumour is large
        - <u>Epistaxis</u> - typically mild and usually noticed first thing in the morning
    - **Features of local invasion:**
        - <u>Otitis media w/ effusion</u> - presentation w/ unilateral hearing loss, otalgia w/ evidence of effusion on otoscope as a result of obstruction of the Eustachian tube
        - <u>Tinnitus</u> - due to obstruction resulting in eustachian tube dysfunction
        - <u>Diplopia</u> - due to paracavernous sinus tumour invasion resulting in palsies of CN III, IV or VI
        - <u>Hemifacial numbness</u> - due to paracavernous sinus tumour invasion resulting in CN V palsy
        - <u>Headache</u> - due to intracranial spread via th skull base
    - **Features of nodal metastasis** (75-90% at presentation) - presentation as <u>neck mass</u> (unilateral or bilateral), which are LN metastasis to level 2 and 5 LNs
    - **Features of distant metastasis** (5-11% at presentation) - most common to the bone (75% of distant metastasis), lung, liver, or distant LNs
    - **Paraneopastic phenomenon:**
        - Neutrophilia
        - Pyrexia of unknown origin
        - Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy
        - Dermatomyositis or polymyositis
- **Signs of NPC** - examination of the neck, otoscope, cranial nerves and nasoendoscopy:
    - <u>Otoscope</u> - look for OME bilaterally
    - <u>Cranial nerve examination</u> - assessment of CN V and EOM:
        - Hemifacial numbness if CN V affected
        - Diplopia if CN III, IV or VI affected
    - <u>Neck examination</u> - palpation of of cervical LN (note for LN in level II or V)
    - <u>Nasoendoscopy</u> - visualisation of tumour and endoscopic-guided Bx of the primary tumour for histological Dx
- **Ix:**
    - **Routine bloods** - CBC, LRFT, EBV DNA level
        - <u>LFT</u> - ALP for bone metastasis
        - <u>EBV DNA level</u>:
            - Pretreatment levels are prognostic for survial outcomes
            - High EBV DNA load \>= 4000 copies/mL are suggestive of distant metastasis (warranting PET-CT)
    - **Imaging** - CT/ MRI of the skull base, nasopharynx and neck, +/- CXR or PET-CT
- **Staging system of NPC** - AJCC 9th edition TNM staging:
    - **Principles of staging of NPC:**
        - <u>T-staging</u> - based on involvement and invasion of adjacent anatomical structures
        - <u>N-staging</u> - based on 1) laterality, 2) size, 3) level, and 4) presence of extranodal invasion
        - <u>M-staging</u> - based on presence and number of metastatic lesions

- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - **Stage-specific Mx for initial presenting tumour:**
            - <u>Early stage NPC</u> - RT alone
            - <u>Intermediate stage NPC</u> - based on risk stratification by nodal size, extranodal invasion, and EBV DNA load:
                - High-risk intermediate stage NPC - concurrent chemoirradiation
                - Low-risk intermediate stage NPC - RT alone
            - <u>Locoregionally advanced NPC</u> - induction chemotherapy followed by concurrent chemoirradiation (concurrent chemoirradiation w/ adjuvant chemothrapy as an alternative)
            - <u>Metastatic NPC</u> - concurrent chemoradiation (+/- adjuvant chemotherapy) or systemic therapy
        - **Mx of recurrent NPC** - dependent on the nature of recurrence:
            - <u>Locoal recurrence</u> - reirradiation or salvage surgery
            - <u>Isolated neck recurrence</u> - neck dissection or reirradiation
            - <u>Distant recurrence</u> - treat as metastatic NPC
