# Frailty

- **Definition** - a biological syndrome of <u>decreased reserve and resistance to stressors</u>, resulting from cumulative decline across multiple physiological systems, and causing <u>vulnerability to adverse outcomes</u>
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - varies depending on definitions (7.9% in HK)
    - <u>Demographic</u> - more common in older women
- **Conceptual Models for frailty:**
    - <u>Phenotypic frailty</u> (Fried et al 2001) - 1) unintentional weight loss, 2) self-reported exhaustion, 3) objective demonstration of weakness (grip strength), 4) slow walking speed, 5) low physical activity
    - <u>Deficits Accumulation</u> (Rockwood 2007) - accumulation of physical deficits of multiple etiologies, resulting in increased vulnerability
- **Given same age, frail patients are a/w increased risk of adverse outcomes, resulting in increased healthcare cost:** 
![](~/Documents/notes/img/survival_curve_for-frail_vs_non_frail_patient.png)
    - Falls
    - Disability
    - Hospitalisation
    - Institutionalization
    - Death
    - And many more
- **Pathophysiology of fraility** - biological underpinnings are poorly understood:
    - **Endocrine dysfunction:**
        - Insulin resistance
        - Excess exposure to ctabolic functions
        - Vitamin D deficiency
        - Relative deficiencies in androgens
        - Decreased growth hormone and IGF-1
    - **Immune dysfunction and inflammation:**
        - Chronic CMV infection
        - Comprimised altered immune function
        - Proinflammatory state
        - Micronutrient deficiency and oxidative stress
    - **Other factors:**
        - Anaemia
        - Autonomic neuropathy
        - Sarcopenia
- **Indications for assessment of frailty:**
    - All persons \> 70u
    - Adults w/ multiple chronic illnesses or weight loss \> 5kg for over a year
- **Common screening tools for frailty** - complicated by \> 70 screening tools, with no consensus on how to screen:
    - Fraility phenotype (Fried et al 2001)
    - Frality index
    - Clinical Frality Scale (SFS)
    - "FRAIL" scale
- **Frality index** - based on deficits accumulation model of fraility:
    - <u>Basic principles</u> - vulnerability determined by number of deficits present (21-70 deficits recommended in assessment): 
    ![](~/Documents/notes/img/frailty_index.png)
    - <u>Extremely variable</u> - different FIs may incorporate different variables

- **"FRAIL" Score** - scoring system resembling model of phenotypic frailty:

- **Clinical application of frailty** - enhanced awareness increased susceptibility to adverse effects:
    - <u>A&E and triage setting</u>:
        - **Disposal of patients** - hospitalisation in acute or geriatric hospitals
        - **Consider community based care** - e.g. CGAT, CVMO, ECEOL etc.
    - <u>Mx of frail patient in acute and convalescence wards</u>:
        - **General measures** - fall prevention, pressure sore prevention, nutrition support, prevention of delirium
        - **Prescribing** - avoiding polypharmacy
        - **Discahrge** - early DC planning, appropriate placement, and rehabilitation
    - <u>Considers in surgical and invasive intervention</u> - consider poorer surgical outcomes:
        - **Enhanced pre-operative risk Mx** - careful medication review, nutritional augmentation, prehabilitation by PT/OT
        - **Enhanced post-operative Mx** - early mobilisation, attention to hydration, nutrition etc.
    - <u>Considerations of EOL care</u> - consider frailty and worsening frailty status is an independent risk factor for mortality
