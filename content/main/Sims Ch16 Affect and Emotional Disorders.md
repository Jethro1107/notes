# Ch16 Affect and Emotional Disorders

- **Terminology:**
    - <u>Affect</u> - broad term encompassing mood, feeling, attitude, preference and evaluation, referring to the expression of emotion as judged by the external manifestations a/w a certain feeling (e.g. laughter, crying)
    - <u>Mood</u> - a prolonged pervasive state or disposition
    - <u>Emotion</u> - spontaneous, transitory experience similar to, but not identical to feeling (in a sense that it does not require physical accompainments of experience)
- **Theories of emotion:**
    - **James-Lange theory** - emotions are self-awareness of physiological changes in the presence of a stimuli:
        - <u>Limitations</u>:
            - Visceral responses to stimuli are too slow to account for the rapidity of emotions
            - Similar physiological responses result in different emotion, or even lack there of (e.g. adrenaline injection)
            - Spinal lesions do not prevent experience of emotions despite reduced stimuli
    - **Cannon-Bard theory** - physiological and behavioural changes follow emotion, leaving no room for any cognitive aspect to the origin of emotions
    - **Two factor theory of emotion** (Schachter and Singer \[1962\]) - physiological arousal and cognition
- **Basic emotions** - Ekman six basic emotions expressed in the face:
    - Anger
    - Disgust
    - Fear
    - Happiness
    - Sadness
    - Surprise
- **Communication of mood** - affect is not directed towards others, but expression is conveyed both deliberately and unintentionally to others:
    - <u>Concept of mirror neurons</u> - neurons found in primates and birds, and existence inferred in humans, where the same neurons fire when an animal performs an action and also when an animal observes the same action performed by another neuron (i.e. mirror neurons mirro behavior of another animal):
    - <u>Implications of mirror neurons</u>:
        1.  Underlies imitative learning and important for development of human culture and acquisition of language
        2.  Objectified others becomes for us another experiencing self, empathy has a biological basis - mirror neurons system are an embodied simulation system wherein we not only see action, emotion, or sensation, but form internal representation of such based on evocation of the same neural systems when we perform the same action or experience the same emotion
        3.  In psychiatric assessment of anothers affective response, it is said to be assessed empathetically due to this mimetic nature and need not to be verbal to be acted on (e.g. upon meeting this person, I feel unhappy, therefore it is logical to suspect that the other person is also unhappy)
- **Classification of pathology of emotions:**
    - **Abnormalities of basic emotion** - abnormal state in terms of intensity, duration, timing, quality of experience, expression and appropriateness to the object or social setting
    - **Abnormalities of physiological arousal** - e.g. elexithymia
    - **Abnormalities of evaluation of social context** - abnormalities in cognitive evaluation of the social worlds and perception of the emotions of others

- **Pathological changes in basic emotion:**
    - **Changes in intensity of emotion** - subjective change of mood can be quantified conceptually on a mood chart: 
    ![](img/mood_chart.png)
        - **Diminution of intensity** - feeling a loss of feeling, a deficiency that is all-pervasive, affecting all basic emotions, which is often a subjective experience of loss of feeling that was formerly present than objectively observed absence (e.g. no relgion connection with God, no feeling towards one's own children)
        - **Anhedonia** (fundamental Sx of depressive illness) - loss of the capacity to experience joy and pleasure (a subset of diminution of intensity)
        - **Exacerbation of emotions** (melancholia, mania, ecstasy)
        - **Intensification of fear, anger and surprise**
    - **Changes in timing, duration and appropriateness to situation** - whether emotional expression is appropriate to its context
    - **Abnormalities in expression and appropriateness to object:**
        - <u>Blunting or flattening of affect</u> - e.g. poverty of expressive gesturs, poor eye contact, affective unresponsivity and lack of vocal inflection where blunting refers to a lack of emotional sensitivity, while flattening is a limitation of the usual range of emotional expressivity
        - <u>Bodily feelings associated w/ emotion</u> - somatic Sx in relation to emotional response
