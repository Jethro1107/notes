# Approach to Gram Positive Rods

- **Overview:**
    - Gram+ rods/ bacillin are less commonly encountered compared with G+ or G- rods
    - Main way to classify G+ rods is by their aerobic status (e.g. aerobes vs anaerobes)
    - Most organisms are a/w specific infective syndromes
- **Classification of G+ rods:**
    - <u>Aerobic GPR</u> - bacillus, listeria monocytogenes, Corynbacterium, Nocardia and Rhodococcus, Mycobacterium
    - <u>Anaerobic GPR</u> - Clostridium spp, Actinomyces spp

  
  ![](img/medically_important_gpr.png)
- **Aerobic GPR:**
    - **Bacillius** - rods primarily found in environment (e.g. soil), and almost never causes bacteraemia:
        - <u>Food poisoining</u> - caused by spores formed by **B. cereus**
        - <u>Anthrax</u> - caused by inhalation of spores produced by **B. anthracis**
    - **Listeria monocytones** - food-bourne (mild and chicken) CNS infections:
        - Mainly causes <u>meningitis</u> in the extremes of age or immunocomprised
        - <u>Resistant to cephalosporin</u>, however Sn to ampicillin (add ampicillin to cephtriaxone if those with suspected Listeria meningitis)
    - **Corynbacterium** - G+ rods, where the most clinically significant is Diptheria:
        - <u>Diptheria</u> - respiratory or cutaneous manifestation by **C. diptheriae** a/w systemic manifestations such as cervical lymphadenopathy, or toxin-mediated cardiac and neurological complications
        - <u>Other Coryneforms</u> - usually oppourtunistic pathogens and contaminants in clinical specimens
    - **Nocardia and Rhodococcus** - weakly acid-fast rods forming hypae or filaments, presenting as opportunistic infections:
        - <u>Nocardia</u> - results in pulmonary, CNS and cutaneous nocardiosis
        - <u>Rhodococcus</u> - zoonotic organisms presenting w/ pneumonia, lymphadeenopathy or wound infections
    - **Mycobacterium** - acid-fast bacilli, classified into MTB and NTM
- **Anaerobic GPR** - spore-forming (Clostridum spp.) and non-spore-forming (Actinomyces):
    - **Clostridium species** - a/w host of infectious syndromes:
        - <u>C. tetani</u> - causes tetanus
        - <u>C. botulinum</u> - causes botulism
        - <u>C. perfringens</u> - classically a/w gas gangrene, but more commonly attributed to billiary sepsis
        - <u>C. difficile</u> - pseudomembranous colitis
    - **Actinomyces** - GI and genital tract colonisers resulting in pulmonary, pelvic, abdominal or cervicofascial actinomycosis
