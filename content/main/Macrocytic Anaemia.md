# Macrocytic Anaemia

- **Definition and terminology:**
    - <u>Macrocytosis</u> - a morphological term describing increase in RBC size, as documented by PBS review or by MCV from an automated haematology analysers
    - <u>MCV</u> - mean corpuscular volume, a red cell indices (reference range age-dependent) detected by the automated haematology analyser, suggesting an increase in RBC size but w/ exceptions (e.g. clumping seen in cold agglutinin disease)
    - <u>RDW</u> - red cell indices describing variations in RBC size
- **Etiology of macrocytosis:**
    - <u>Reticulocytosis</u> - any cause of haemolysis in the presence of sufficient hemantic material, appropriate erythropoietin stimulation, and functional bone marrow
    - <u>Megaloblastic anaemia</u> - abnormal RBC nucleic acid metabolism, commonly caused by deficiencies of vitamin B12, folate, copper, or other drugs that interfere w/ DNA synthesis
    - <u>Myelodysplastic syndrome</u> - dysplastic changes to erythroid lineages, and is a/w characteristically low reticulocyte reponse to anaemia
    - <u>Alcohol</u> - macrocytosis is a feature in chronic alcoholics, even in a B12- and folate-repleted indivudal
    - <u>Hyperlipidaemia</u> - excessive cholesterol deposition onto RBC membrane
    - <u>Hypothyroidism</u> - mildly increased, causes is unknown
    - <u>Drug-induced macrocytosis</u> - caused by drugs that result in:
        - Haemolytic anaemia
        - Inhibition of folate metabolism
        - Direct inhibition of DNA synthesis resulting in megaloblastic changes
- **Ix:**
    - **CBC:**
        - <u>MCV</u> - severe macrocytosis (\> 120 fL) is almost exclusively due to megaloblastic anaemia (but possibly due to RBC agglutination)
        - <u>Pancytopaenia</u> - cytopenias points to a bone marrow pathology, e.g. megaloblastic anaemia or myelodysplastic syndrome
    - **Peripheral blood smear:**
        - <u>Findings suggestive of megaloblastic processes</u> - macro-ovalocytes, hyper-segmented neutrophils 
        ![](~/Documents/notes/img/macro_ovalocytes_and_hypersegmented_neutrophils.png)
        - <u>Findings suggestive of myelodysplastic syndrome</u> - hypolobulated/ hypogranular neutrophils
        - <u>Other findings</u>:
            - Polychromasia - suggestive of reticulocytosis
            - RBC agglutination - falsely elevated MCV
            - Spherocytes - as in hereditary spherocytosis or warm AIHA
    - **Specific blood tests** - Retic count, serum B12 and folate levels, TFT, LFT:
        - <u>Retic count</u> - elevated (\> 4-5%) suggests reticulocytosis and a haemolytic process
        - <u>Serum B12, folate levels</u> - initial screening for B12, folate deficiency respoectively
        - <u>TFT</u> - screen for hypothyroidism
        - <u>LFT</u> - detection of liver disease
    - **Additional Ix:**
        - <u>Methymalonic acid and homocysteine levels</u> (+/- holotranscobalamin) - more Sn evaluation of B12 deficiency
        - <u>Cu levels</u> - causing megaloblastic anaemia especially after gastric bypass
        - <u>Bone marrow examination</u> - indicated if myelodysplasia is suspected, e.g. pancytopenia unexplained by a megaloblastic process (e.g. normal B12, folate, Cu, or drug-cause)
        - <u>SPE</u> - very ocassionally MM presents w/ macrocytosis and suspicion raised if presence of myeloma-defining events
