# Haematuria

- **Definition** - presence of red blood cells in urine, which reflect bleeding from somewhere in the urinary tract:
    - <u>Macroscopic haematuria</u> (VH) - visible to naked eye (cancer until proven otherwise, demanding full workup)
    - <u>Microscopic haematuria</u> (NVH) - detected on dipstick testing (\> 15000-20000 cells/mL), but strightly defined as having \>= 3 RBC per high-power field (HPF) on microscopic examination
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Prevalence - accounts for 20% of adult urological consultations
- **Causes of dipstick-negative dark urine:**
    - Foods - Beeturia
    - Drugs - e.g. Rifampicin, Levodopa, phenazopyridine
    - Obstructive jaundice - +ve for bilirubin
- **Dipstick haematuria:**
    - **Principles of dipstick haematuria** - indicates presence of Hb in urine:
        - <u>Biochemical tests</u> - dependent on <u>peroxidase activity of Hb</u> on chromogen of dipstick:
        - <u>Test properties</u>:
            - False +ve - menstrual blood contamination, haemoglobinuria, myoglobinuria, dehydration
            - False -ve - urine with high SG, ascorbic acid, nitrites, H+, and protein
        - <u>Implications</u> - all dipstick haematuria requires **microscopic confirmation** (20.3%-74% will have subsequent normal urine microscopy)
    - **Etiology of dipstick haematuria:**
        - **Haematuria** - caused by 1) glomerular bleeding, 2) infections, 3) stones, and 4) malignancy of the urinary tract
            - <u>Glomerular bleeding</u> - GN, vasculitis, polyarteritis nodosa, other glomerular diseases
            - <u>Infections</u> - [[Lower Urinary Tract Infections and Cystitis]], [[Renal Infections]], [[Tuberculosis of the Kidney and Genitourinary Tract]]
            - <u>Stones</u> - [[Renal Stone Disease]] , ureteric stones, bladder stones
            - <u>Malignancies</u> - Prostate cancer, Bladder cancer, [[Renal Cell Carcinoma]]
            - <u>Miscellaneous causes</u> - bleeding BPH, trauma, renal cysts (ADPKD), radiation cystitis, coagulation disorders

        - **Haemoglobinuria** - e.g. Paroxysmal nocturnal haemoglobinuria
        - **Myoglobinuria** - rhabdomyolysis
        - **Miscellaneous causes** - dehydration (increased RBC concentration), menstrual blood contamination
- **Salient points of Hx:**
    - **HPI** - onset (number, frequency, previous episodes), character of haematuria (colour, pain, presence of clots), associated symptoms, exacerbating and relieving factors:
        - <u>Onset</u> - duration, frequency, any previous episodes of <u>gross haematuria</u>
        - <u>Character of haematuria</u> - colour, clots, pain, and timing:
            - **Colour** - smoky, brown urine in glomerular bleeding vs fresh blood
            - **Presence of clots** - presence of clots excludes glomerular bleeding and signifies severe bleeding
            - **Pain** - painless haematuria (cancer until proven otherwise) vs painful haematuria (stone-related)
            - **Timing** - classical teaching signifies origin of bleeding, but is clinically inaccurate:
                - Whole stream
                - Start stream
                - Terminal stream
                - Independent of micturation - urethral bleeding
        - <u>Exacerbating and relieving factors</u> - urine colour changes with exercise (exercise-induced haematuria)
        - <u>Associated Sx</u>:
            - **Unilateral flank pain** (+/- radiation to groin) - identify ureteric obstruction (by stone or clot), but ocassionally as a result of primary renal maligancy
            - **Dysuria +/- fever** - indicative of a urinary tract infection (presence of fever suggestive of upper tract infection)
            - **Previous LUTS** - possibility of bleeding BPH
            - **Weight loss** - non-specific for malignancy
            - **Recent URTI** - possibility of PSGN
            - **Oedema in dependent areas** - nephritic syndrome
            - Other rheumatic features - e.g. purpura, rash, joint pain suggestive of CTD with glomerular involvement
    - **PMH** - identify comorbid conditions a/w haematuria:
        - Bleeding tendancies - prior bleeding disorders or anti-thrombotic therapy
        - Past Hx of kidney stones
        - Hx of kidney diseases - e.g. IgA Nephropathy, TBM disease, Alport syndrome, ADPKD
        - HTN and DM
    - **SHx** - risk factors of malignancy:
        - Smoking and alcohol
        - Occupational exposure - to dyes or industrial chemicals (e.g. printers, painters, chemical plant workers)
    - **FHx** - FHx of kidney stones, kidney disease (ADPKD), and urothelial malignancies
- **P/E** - often unremarkable:
    - BP measurement - new or worsening HTN in patients suggestive of GN
    - Oedema/ fluid retention - suggestive of glomerular disease
    - Abdominal examination:
        - Inspection - skin changes (rashes or purpura)
        - Palpation - flank or suprapubic tenderness masses, and ballotable kidney
        - Examination of external genitalia - urethral lesions, varicocele
        - PR examination - bleeding BPH
- **Ix to gross haematuria:**
    - **Urine** - dipstick, microscopy and microbiology:
        - <u>Repeat dipstick</u> - for protein (r/o GN), WBC (r/o infections)
        - <u>Microscopy</u> - confirm presence of RBC and differentiate between different types of bleeding:
            - **RBC morphology** - RBC casts, dysmorphic RBCs suggestive of <u>glomerular bleeding</u>
            - **WBC and organisms** - suggestive of urinary tract infections
            - **Urine cytology** - no role as 1st-line Ix:
                - <u>Principles</u> - detection of abnormal epithelial cells in freshly voided urine, only able to detect high-grade urothelial cancers
                - <u>Test properties</u> - low Sn (50%), high Sp (96%)
                - <u>Results</u>:
                    - 1\. Unsatisfactory for examination
                    - 2\. Negative for malignant cells
                    - 3\. Atypical cells
                    - 4\. Suspicious for malignancy
                    - 5\. Carcinoma cells present
        - <u>Microbiology</u> - C/ST, EMU for AFB
    - **Baseline bloods** - CBC, RFT, clotting profile:
        - <u>CBC</u>:
            - Hb - rarely causes anaemia alone, but ACD in malignancies, or may result in secondary polycythaemia (RCC)
            - WBC - leukocytosis in urinary tract infections
        - <u>RFT</u> - assess baseline renal functions (viability for contrast studies)
        - <u>Clotting profile</u> - r/o clotting abnormalities
    - **Upper and lower tract workup** - anatomical diagnosis on endoscopy or imaging indicated for <u>all gross haematuria</u>:
        - **Cystoscopy** (lower-tract workup) - assessment of lower-tract for bladder pathologies
        - **Upper-tract workup:**
            - <u>Renal USG</u> - initial non-invasive Ix for renal mass, cysts or hydronephrosis
            - <u>Contrast CT urogram</u> - gold standard for upper tract assessment but requires adequate renal functions
            - <u>Alternative Ix</u> - IVU (superceded by CT urogram), NCCT urogram, retrograde pyelography (reserved for those w/ inadequate renal functions)
    - **Renal biopsy** - workup for GN if other nephritic features (proteinuria, HTN, renal insufficiency, fluid retention) present
