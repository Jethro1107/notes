# Idiopathic Non-Specific Interstitial Pneumonia

- **Definition** - a distinct clinical entity, although idiopathic, defined by characteristic clinical, radiological, and histopathological features which are often polar opposite to findings of UIP
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - not well established
    - <u>Demographic</u> - usually non-smoking women in 5th decade (often w/ ?ANA seropositivity):
        - Age - 5th decade of life
        - Sex - female preponderance
        - Exposure - NS
- **Difference between idiopathic NSIP and NSIP:**
    - <u>Idiopathic NSIP</u> - a disease entity characterised by radiological and histopathological findings of NSIP, w/ no features suggesting an alternative diagnosis
    - <u>NSIP</u> - a **radiographical and histopathological pattern**, may be idiopathic, but also observed commonly in CTD-associated ILD, less frequently w/ familial interstitial pneumonia, drug toxicity and infection
- **Clinical manifestation** - typical manifestation of chronic ILD (dry cough and progressive exertional dyspnoea)
- **HRCT findings in idiopathic NSIP** - NSIP pattern:
    - <u>Characteristic NSIP pattern</u>:
        - Symmetrical and subpleural involvement w/ groud glass poacities (GGO) and reticular opacities
        - Volume loss and tractional bronchiectasis in lower zones can be found
        - Subpleural sparing, peribronchiolar thickening and honeycombing is rare
- **Histopathology in idopathic NSIP** - varying amounts of interstitial inflammation and fibrosis w/ <u>uniform pattern</u>:
    - Active inflammation characterised by cellular infiltrates (predominant cellular pattern potentially more likely to resolve w/ medical infiltrates)
    - Fibrosis and subpleural reticulation is common, while honeycombing and fibroblast foci is rare
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>Non-pharmacological therapy</u> - pulmonary rehabilitation, O2 therapy, lung transplantation w/ progressive disease
        - <u>Pharmacological therapy</u> - oral steroids (prednisone), cytotoxic agents (MMF, AZA, CTX), biologics (rituximab, tocilizumab)
- **Prognosis** - relatively good prognosis (80% 5y survival):
    - Better prognosis in those w/ predominent cellular NSIP pattern
    - Worse prognosis in those w/ fibrosing NSIP pattern
