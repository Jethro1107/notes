# Giant Cell Arteritis and Polymyalgia Rheumatica

- **Terminology** - GCA and PMR are considered the same disease because <u>most patients presenting with GCA have PMR Sx</u>, and <u>most untreated PMR will develop GCA</u>:
    - **GCA** - a granulomatous arteritis that predominantly affects medium-sized arteries of the head and neck (e.g. temporal artery)
    - **PMR** - Symmetrical muscle pain and stiffness affecting shoulder and pelvic girdles
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Prevalence - 10-20/100000 in those \> 50y
    - Incidence - rare \< 60y, female preponderance (F:M = 3:1)
- **Clinical features of GCA and PMR:**
    - **Constitutional Sx** - fever, weight loss, malaise, night sweats
    - **Clinical features of GCA:**
        - <u>Headaches</u> - cardinal Sx of GCA and is often localised to the temporal or occipital region, often a/w scalp tenderness
        - <u>Ischaemic jaw pain</u> - jaw pain on chewing or talking caused by ischaemia of the masseter muscles
        - <u>Visual manifestations</u> - arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (AAION) caused by occlusion of the posterior ciliary artery
            - Clinical presentation variable - Sudden mono-nuclear vision loss (classical), loss of visual acuity, reduced clour perception, papillary defects
            - Fundoscopic findings - pale, swollen optic disc with haemorrhage
    - **Clinical features of GCA PMR** - symmetrical muscle pain and stiffness affecting shoulder and pelvic girdles, but absent of weakness and muscle wasting:
        - <u>Onset</u> - typically sudden over a few days, or insiduous
        - <u>ROM</u> - stiffness and painful restriction of active ROM, but passive ROM preserved
- **DDx of PMR** - consider the age of onset:
    - Rheumatic diseases:
        - Inflammatory myopathy - Pm, Dm, Inclusion-Body myositis
        - Systemic vasculitis
        - Rheumatoid vasculitis
    - Fibromyalgia
    - Hypothyroidism
    - Spine pathologies - cervical spondylosis
- **Ix:**
    - ESR - extremely elevated (\> 100)
    - Temporal artery Bx - findings of fragmentation of internal lamina, with necrosis of the media with mixed inflammatory cell infitrates
- **Mx:**
    - **Induction therapy** - urgent high dose corticosteroids for 3-4d to prevent visual loss:
        - <u>Regimen</u> - 60-80 mg/d for GCA, 15-30 mg/d for PMR alone (evidence for different dosage is weak)
    - **Graded dose reduction of steroids:**
        - <u>Initial target</u> - \< 10-15 mg/d within 8 weeks (titrated against symptoms and ESR)
        - <u>Subsequent target</u> - slower dose reduction at 1 mg/mo until acceptable maintenance dose is required (5-7.5 mg/d)
    - **Maintenance therapy** - low-dose steroid +/- steroid sparing agents (e.g. azathioprine, methotrexate)
    - **Mx of symptom recurrence** - increase steroid dosage to documented dosage that could control Sx
