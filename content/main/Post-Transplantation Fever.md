# Post-Transplantation Fever

- **Definition** - pyrexia in a transplant patient, further classified into haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) and solid organ tansplant
- **DDx of post-transplantation fever:**
    - <u>Infection</u> - most likely organism dependent on time-of-onset: 
    ![](img/infections_in_post_transplant_patient.png)
    - <u>Graft rejection</u> - a/w failure of the graft
    - <u>Graft-versus-host disease</u> (GVHD) - if HSCT
- **Infections in post-solid organ transplant patients** - etiology dependent on time of onset:
    - <u>0-1 mo</u> - bacterial/ fungal infections related to surgical complications or underlying conditions (e.g. pneumonia)
    - <u>1-6 mo</u> - opportunistic infections related to **impaired cellular immunity**:
        - **Bacterial** - Legionella, Salmonella, **Listeria**
        - **Viral** - classically **CMV infection**
        - **Fungal** - pneumocystitis jirovecci pneumonia
    - <u>\> 6 mo</u> - community-acquired infections (including CAP), shingles, cryptococcal infections
- **Infections in HSCT patients** - etiology dependent on pre-engraftment vs post-engraftment period:
    - **Pre-engraftment** - typically a/w **neutropenia**:
        - <u>Bacterial</u> - aerobic GNR (e.g. E. coli, P. aeruginosa, K. pneumoniae), or GPC (Staphylococcus, viridans streptococci, enterococi)
        - <u>Fungal</u> - Candida spp, Aspergillus
    - **Post-engraftment:**
        - <u>Early</u> (\< 100d) - opportunistic infections
        - <u>Late</u> (\> 100d) - community-acquired infections, shingles, CMV, PTLD
