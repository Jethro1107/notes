# Meniere's Disease

- **Definition** - idiopathic endolymph hydrops, i.e. excsesive build up of endolymph
- **Epidemiology:**
    - <u>Prevalence</u> - 0.1% (20% if +ve FHx)
    - <u>Demographic</u>:
        - Age - onset \< 65y (commonest in 30-50y)
        - Sex - female preponderance (F:M = 3:2)
- **Clinical features of Meniere's syndrome** - recurrent, episodic acute vertigo w/ marked autonomic and aural complaints:
	- **Vertigo:**
		- _Onset_ - abrupt onset
		- _Duration_ - each attack lasts 30 min to several hours (bedridden for 1-2h), but variable interval between ttacks
	- **Autonomic complaints** - N/V, sweating, pallor
	- **Aural Sx** - ipsilateral tinnitus and SNHL occurs w/ each attack, and deafness may be permanent and progressive
- **Natural Hx of Meniere Syndrome:**
	- Recurrent paroxysmal attacks of vertigo, vomiting, tinnitus, and sensorineural hearing loss
	- Deafness is progressive w/ each attack
- **Signs:**
	- _Nystagmus_ - slow-phase jerks towards the normal ear
	- _Hearing test_ - demonstration of sensorineural deafness (predilection to low tones)
- **Ix:**
	- Audiometry
	- Electrocochleography

- **Mx:**
	- **Principles of Mx:**
		- _Symptomatic relief of acute episodes_ – vestibular sedatives and antiemetics to reduce symptoms.
		- _Chronic Mx_ – lifestyle and ENT interventions, with surgical options for refractory cases.
	- **Mx of acute episodes:**
		- **Prodromal phase (anticipation of attack)** – when fullness or tinnitus warns of an impending attack:
			- Prochlorperazine 25 mg suppository
			- *or* Urea 30 g crystals dissolved in orange juice (ideally taken 30 minutes before the expected attack)
		- **Established acute attack:**
			- Diazepam 5 mg IV ± prochlorperazine 12.5 mg IM
			- Consider betahistine 8 mg orally three times a day if vertigo is persistent or episodic
		- **Supportive medication:**
			- _Vestibular sedatives_ – diazepam, cinnarizine
			- _Antiemetics_ – prochlorperazine, maxolon (metoclopramide), ondansetron
	- **Long‑term Mx** - reduce endolymphatic pressure by decreasing Na and water content of endolymph:
		- **Reassurance and explanation** – carefully explain the condition, as patients often fear malignancy.
		- **Lifestyle modifications:**
			- Low‑salt diet is the mainstay (<3 g per day)
			- Avoid excess intake of salt, tobacco and coffee
			- **Stress management:** stress can promote fluid build‑up; use meditation, stress reduction techniques, or consider long‑term sedation if needed
		- **Diuretics:** e.g. hydrochlorothiazide/amiloride daily – monitor serum electrolytes regularly
		- **Betahistine** (8 mg orally three times a day) may be continued for persistent or episodic symptoms.
	- **Meniette device:**
		- _Principles:_
			- Tympanotomy and grommet tube insertion
			- Air pulses generated by the device are transmitted to the inner ear to help regulate endolymph pressure
	- **Surgical intervention** – indicated for progressive symptoms failing medical treatment:
		  - Endolymphatic sac decompression
		  - Labyrinthectomy
		  - Vestibular neurectomy
