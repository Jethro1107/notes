# Physical Examination in a Nephrology Patient

- **Position** - Supine, relax the abdomen by placing the head on a pillow:
    - Patients w/ fluid overload and HF may not be able to lie comfortably on the bed.
- **Exposure** - Classically nipple to mid-thigh, but typically full exposure up to the inguinal ligament (additional exposure when required) to preserve patient's dignity
- **General flow of the abdominal examination in a GU patient:**
    - **General examination:**
        - **General inspection** - Mental state, hyperventilation, hiccups, uraemic complexion, hydration, subcutaneous nodules
        - **Examination of the face:**
            - Hearing aid (Alport's syndrome)
            - Rash - lupus, vasculitis etc.
            - Eyes - pallor, jaundice, band keratopathy
            - Mouth - dryness, ulcers, fetor, gingival hypertrophy
        - **Examination of the neck** - JVP, carotid bruits, scars (previous vascath insertion, parathyroidectomy scars)
        - **Examination of the arms and hands:**
            - Stigmata of chronic liver disease
            - AV fistula
            - Leukonychia
            - Flapping tremor
            - Neuropathy and myopathy
            - Bruising, pigmentation, scratch marks and
        - **Examination of the legs:**
            - Pitting oedema
            - Bruises, pigmentation, scratch marks, neuropathy
    - **Examination of the abdomen:**
        - Inspection - mass, scars, distension
        - Palpation - superficial palpation, deep palpation, organ specific palpation and percussion (ballottement of kidneys, palpation of transplanted kidneys)
        - Percussion - shifting dullness
        - Auscultation - bowel sounds, renal bruits
    - **Examination of other systems:**
        - Examination of the external genitalia, hernial oriface and per-rectal examination
        - BP measurement - supine/erect BP for HTN or orthostatic hypotension
        - Cardiovascular examination - signs of pressure overload signs of HF,
        - Respiratory examination - consolidation, pleural effusion, pulmonary oedema
        - Fundus examination
        - Urinalysis

## General Examination

- **General inspection:**
    - **Mental state** - assessment of uraemic encephalopathy in terminal renal failure
    - **Respiration** - assessment of hyperventilation and hiccups:
        - Hyperventilation (Kussmaul breathing) - related to metabolic acidosis
        - Hiccups - ominous sign of uraemia
    - **Skin:**
        - **Cafe au lait complexion** - dirty brown appearance with pallor
        - **Subcutaneous skin nodules** - deposition of CaPO4
        - **Bruises** - uraemia-driven platelet dysfunction
        - **Scratch marks** - uraemic pruritis (especially with hyperphosphataemia)
        - **Uraemic frost** (extremely rare) - white powder on skin due to precipitation of concentrated urine in sweat
    - **Fluid balance** - supine/erect BP, JVP, I/O charts, oedema, hydration status, skin turgor
        - Volume depletion - precipitate pre-renal AKI
        - Volume overload - may be an attempt to correct AKI and conversely cause pulmonary oedema
- **Examination of the eyes and face:**
    - Look and ask for **hearing aid**, which can be suggestive of **Alport's syndrome**
    - **Skin lesions:**
        - Rash - suggestive of underlying SLE
        - Skin tethering - suggestive of underlying SSc
    - **Eyes:**
        - **Pallor** - anaemia of renal disease
        - **Jaundice** (rare in renal pts) - can be due to haemolytic anaemia due to retention of nitrogeous wastes
        - **Band keratopathy** - calcium deposition beneath corneal epithelium in line with interpalpebral fissure
        - \

    - **Mouth:**
        - Hydration status
        - **Uraemic fetor** - ammonical musty odour from breakdown of urea into ammonia in saliva
        - **Mucosal ulcers** - sign of decreased salivary flow
        - **Oral thrush** - oral candidiasis in CKD patients who are prone to infections
        - **Gingival hypertrophy** - transplant patients on calcineurin inhibitors (e.g. cyclosporin or tacrolimus)
- **Examination of the neck:**
    - **JVP** - assessment of intravascular volume status (remember to put patient in 45 degrees for this)
    - **Carotid bruits** - generalised atherosclerotic disease which can complicate from CKD, or imply renal artery stenosis
    - **Scars:**
        - Previous vascular access (vascath) insertion for haemolysis
        - Parathyroidectomy scar - indicates surgical management of tertiary hyperPTH
        - \
        ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2Fdqs7Si4_y8.png?alt=media&token=d74f564b-09c9-456f-9919-325be21eaafc>)
- **Examination of the arms and hands:**
    - For completeness sake, also look for stigmata of chronic liver disease even in a renal ward
    - **Hands** - renal-specific signs include:
        - Anaemia of palmar creases
        - Flapping tremor - uraemic encephalopathy in terminal CKD
        - Finger prick scars indicative of diabetes
    - **Nails** - look for **leuconychia**:
        - **Muehrche's nail** - two transverse white lines indicative of long-standing hypoalbuminaemia (e.g. nephrotic)
        - **Terry's nail** - proximal half white
        - **Mee's nail** - single transverse white line suggestive of CKD, but also arsenic poisoning
    - **Arms:**
        - **AV fistula** - longitudinal swelling from arterialization of a vein for easy vascular access
            - **Palpation** - continuous thrills with a buzzing quality
            - **Auscultation** - for bruits
            - \
            ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2FGR0x_qRvxk.png?alt=media&token=6d83a655-e2d4-4907-ad6a-809d15ac41b3>)
        - **Scars over wrists:**
            - **Previous carpal tunnel surgery** (look for signs of CTS)
            - **Autotransplantation scar** in parathyroidectomy
        - **Scratch marks** - uraemic pruritis (very common among CKD patients and is extremely debilitating)
        - **Bruising**
        - **Uraemic frost** (extremely rare)
- **Examination of the lower limbs:**
    - **Pitting oedema**
    - **Skin lesions:**
        - **Purpura** - palpable purpura as a vasculitic phenomenon, non-palpable purpura due to bleeding tendancy
        - **Livedo reticularis** - red-blue reticular pattern as a sign of vasculitis, or atheroembolic disease
        - **Scratch marks** - uraemic pruritis
    - Peripheral neuropathy and myopathy (from DM or late uraemic neuropathy) - performed as indicated

## Abdominal Examination

- **Inspection** - Mass, shape, scars, striae, pulsations, dilated veins
    - **Scars and devices:**
        - Tenckhoff catheter (PD catheter) - noted for patient on peritoneal dialysis
        - Nephrectomy scar - oblique scar over flanks (ask patient to roll over)
        - Hockey-stick scar - right or left iliac fossa (note for any visible bulge over scar reflecting a transplanted kidney, and palpate over that area carefully)
        - PD scars - multiple small scars over the midline
        - \
        ![\](<https://firebasestorage.googleapis.com/v0/b/firescript-577a2.appspot.com/o/imgs%2Fapp%2FJethro%2FREMY_AxYUv.png?alt=media&token=c386702d-15d3-41bd-b767-69b48274434a>)
    - **Mass** - polycystic kidney, renal transplant
    - **Distension** - Ascites due to nephrotic syndrome, or PD fluid
- **Palpation** - superficial palpation, deep palpation, organ-specific palpation and percussion
    - **Palpation over transplanted kidney:**
        - **Tenderness** - sign of rejection
    - **Ballotment of kidney** - detection of enlarged kidney:
        - Technique - flicking the kidney forward with one hand under the renal angle and the other hand anteriorly in the upper quadrant
    - **Hepatomegaly** - may be a result of polycystic kidney and liver disease in context of renal patients
- **Percussion:**
    - **Shifting dullness** for ascites
    - Percussion of the bladder
- **Auscultation** - bowel sounds, renal bruits
    - **Renal bruits** - suggestive of renal artery stenosis (diastolic bruit more haemodynamically significant)
        - Technique - above umbilicus, 2cm from midline with diaphragm
        - HTN + audible systolic/ diastolic abdominal bruit - LR for RAS \> 40
        - Other features increasing likelihood of RAS - flash pulmonary oedema, HTN

## Additional Examination

- Examination of the **hernial orifiaces**, **external genitalia** and **per-rectal examination**
- **BP measurement** - supine and erect BP for HTN or postural hypotension
- **Cardiovascular examination** - Signs of HTN (heaving apex), Signs of HF (pulmonary oedema, S3), signs of pericarditis (pericardial rub/ signs of cardiac tamponade)
- **Respiratory examination** - Chest infections (consolidation), Pleural effusion, Pleural rub
- **Fundus examination** - look for hypertensive and diabetic changes
- **Urinalysis**
