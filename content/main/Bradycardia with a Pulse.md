# Bradycardia with a Pulse

- **Definition and terminology:**
    - <u>Bradycardia</u> - defined as HR \< 60bpm
    - <u>Symptomatic bradycardia</u> - defined as bradycardia that causes patient to exhibit evidence of inadequate perfusion, usually entails HR \< 50bpm:
        - Hypotension
        - Altered mental status - including lightheadedness, pre-syncopal sensation and syncope
        - Signs of shock
        - Ongoing ischaemic chest pains
        - Acute heart failure - evidence of acute pulmonary oedema
    - <u>Etiology of bradycardia</u> - H's and T's:
        - Hypoxia
        - Hyperkalaemia
        - Toxicologic - overdose of beta-blockers, calcium-channel blckers, digoxin
        - Inferior myocardial ischaemia
- **Clinical approach to bradycardia:**
    - <u>General measures</u> - Establish monitoring (Oximetry, cardiac monitoring), O2 if hypoxaemia, vascular access (IV/IO)
    - <u>Focused assessment of haemodynamic instability</u> - check pulse:
        - **Sx** - ongoing ischaemic chest discomfort, lightheadedness, presyncope, syncope, dyspnoea (APO)
        - **Signs** - hypotension, signs of shock (e.g. deminished capillary refill), APO (basal crepitations)
    - <u>Mx options</u> - immediate synchronised cardioversion or 12-lead ECG (see below)
- **Approach to Mx of bradycardia:**
    - <u>Stable bradycardia</u> - monitor and observe
    - <u>Symptomatic bradycardia</u> - atropine, transcutaneous pacing +/- chronotropic agents
- **Atropine:**
    - <u>Dosing</u> - IV 1mg, repeated every 3-5 min to a total dose of 3mg
    - <u>Absolute indications</u> - symptomatic bradycardia due to:
        - High vagal tone - e.g. inferior myocardial ischaemia due to ACS
        - Medication-induced bradycardia - e.g. overdose of beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digitalis
        - High-degree AV block with narrow QRS complex - thought to emanate at or above the AV node as suggested by the QRS width
    - <u>Limitations</u>:
        - Mobitz-II or 3rd-degree AV block - pathology resides below AV node and is not subjected to vagal control
- **Transcutaneous pacing:**
    - <u>Indications</u> - initiated if persistent symptomatic bradycardia after atropine
    - <u>Precaution</u> - appropriate sedation and analgesia for conscious patients
- **Chronotropic agents** - IV dopamine vs epinephrine indicated if atropine administration and pacing not successful in alleviating Sx:
    - **Dopamine:**
        - <u>MOA</u>:
            - beta-1 agonism (selective at lower doses) - positive chronotrophic effect
            - alpha-1 agonism (occurs at higher doses) - vasoconstrictive effect (note initial vasodilatory effects at lower doses)
        - <u>Dosing</u> - 5-20 mcg/kg/min titrated against patient's clinical response
        - <u>Precaution</u> - low dose may precipate hypotension
    - **Epinephrine** - preferred over dopamine in concomittant hypotension:
        - <u>MOA</u> - beta-1 and alpha-1 agonist
        - <u>Dosing</u> - 2-10 mcg/min IV infusion
