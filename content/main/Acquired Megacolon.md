# Acquired Megacolon

- **Definition** - megacolon that occurs in the absence of Hirschprung's disease, radiologically defined as distended colon full of faeces w/ no narrow segments
- **Differentiation between congenital and acquired megacolon of childhood:**
    - _Urge to defecate_ - typically present in acquired megacolon, but absent in congenital megacolon
    - _Rectum_ - presence of stool in acquired megacolon, but empty in congenital megacolon
- **Causes of acquired megacolon:**
    - _Witholding defecation_ - ignoring cues to defecate (common in children during potty training)
    - _Poor self-care_ - seen often in depressed or demented patients as part of condition +/- S/E of drugs
    - _Neurological disorders_ - e.g. multiple sclerosis, parkinson's disease, stroke, autonomic neuropathy
    - _Drugs_:
    - Opioids - megacolon in "narcotic bowel syndrome"
    - Stimulant laxatives - esp. anthraquinones (e.g. senna) and bisacodyl due to degeneration of the myenteric plexus
    - _Others_ - hypothyroidism, systemic sclerosis, Chagas disease
- **Clinical features:**
    - Constipation
    - Marked abdominal distension
- **PR:**
    - Dilated loaded rectum (cf empty rectum in Hirschprung's disease)
    - Laxed anal sphincter
- **Ix** - AXR shows dilated colon w/ loaded rectum
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
    - _General measures_ - re-education of bowel habits, high-residue diets, Tx of reversible causes
    - _Pharmacological therapy_ - laxatives, enemas, prokinetics
    - _Surgery_ - subtotal colectomy as last resort for most severely affected patients
