# Clostridium Difficile Infection

- **Definition** - an occassional constituent of the normal intestinal flora, and is the most commonly diagnosed cause of antibiotic-associated diarrhoea
- **Microbiology of C. difficile:**
    - **G stain and morphology** - anaerobic G+ rod
    - **Virulence factors:**
        - <u>Toxin mediated disease</u> - toxin A and toxin B
    - **Strains of C. difficle** - ribotype 027, emerged as a hypervirulent strain that produces more toxins and thus more severe disease
    - **Transmission** - faecal-oral route (via production of environmentally stable spores)
- **Pathophysiology of C. difficile infection** (CDI):
    - <u>Effects of antimicrobial therapy</u> - alters the composition of the normal GI flora
    - <u>Colonisation of GI tract by C. difficile</u> - colonisation may occur if an individual is exposed to C. difficile spores
- **Clinical manifestations of CDI** - 80% are associated in frail elderly w/ comorbidities, typically after a <u>course of ABx</u>:
    - <u>Acute diarrhoeal disease</u> - lower abdominal pain, w/ profuse watery diarrhoea
    - <u>Pseudomembranous colitis</u> - similar manifestation as ulcerative colitis w/:
        - Bloody diarrhoea w/ mucus
        - Fever (likely due to bacterial toxin translocation in the dilated bowel)
        - Toxic megacolon w/ predisposition to perforation
        - Functional ileus
- **Ix:**
    - **Stool culture** - +ve culture <u>does not imply clinical disease</u>, as C. difficle can be isolated in:
        - 30% of those presenting w/ ABx associated diarrhoea; 90% of patients w/ pseudomembranous colitis
        - Up to 20% of asymptomatic elderly in residential care (colonisation only)
    - **Stool for C. difficile assay:**
        - <u>Biochemical tests</u> - glutamate dehydrogenase (GDH)
        - <u>Detection of toxins</u> - C. difficile toxin ELISA
        - <u>Detection of nucleic aci</u> - C. difficle PCR
    - **Colonoscopy:**
        - <u>Findings</u> - resemble that of ulcerative colitis:
            - Eryethema
            - Numerous adherent pseudomembranes or white plaques
            - Ocassionally rectal sparing
    - **Serial AXR** - monitoring for toxic megacolon or perforation
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - <u>General measures</u> - stop precipitating ABx, patient isolation, and supportive therapy (e.g. fluid resuscitation)
        - <u>ABx therapy</u> - first line either metronidazole or oral vancomycin
        - <u>Alternative therapy</u> - corticosteroids, IVIG or evenf faecal transplnatation or surgical intervention may be needed in refractory severe cases
    - **ABx therapy:**
        - <u>Selection</u> - prefer metronidazole due to ABx stewardship:
            - Metronidazole 500 mg PO tid for 10d (good anaerobic coverage)
            - Oral vancomycin 125 mg PO qid for 7-10d (effective and minimal systemic effects as not absorbed)
