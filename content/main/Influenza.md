# Influenza

- **Virology of influenza**
    - **Types** - Influenza A and Influenza B
    - **Structure of Influenza A:**

  f - **Immunogenic Surface Proteins:**
    - Haemagglutinin (H) - antigen for viral entry with 18 major subtypes (H1-18)
    - Neuramidase (N) - antigen for viral release with 11 major subtypes (N1-11)

  <!-- -->

    - **Genetic changes:**
        - **Antigenic shift** - major change in antigen leading to novel infleunza virus (pandemic potential - resulting in a new widely pathogenic virus)
        - **Antigenic drift** - minor changes in amino acids of H and N resulting in change in protein immunogenicity (underlies seasonal epidemics, and vaccination)

  <!-- -->

    - **Structure of Influenza B**
        - Two lineages - B/Yamagata and B/Victoria
        - Less genetic changes compared to influenza A.
- **Epidemiology:**
    - Dual peaks in HK (summer and winter)
- **Clinical features:**
    - **Incubation period** - 1-4d with viral shedding around 24-48h prior to onset of S/S
    - **Viral symptoms** - Fever, myalgia and arthralgia
    - **URTI** - runny nose, soar throat, cough and sputum
    - **LRTI**
    - **Systemic involvement** (Complications):
        - **Pneumonia** - secondary bacterial pneumonia (0.5% in young individuals, 2.5% in elderly w/ comorbidities), and primary viral pneumonia
            - Clinical presentation:
                - Primary influenza pneumonia - persistent severe respiratory S/S (esp fever and dyspnoea) after 3-5d of acute influenza
                - Secondary influenza pneumonia - initial improvement, followed by symptomatic progression
            - Radiological findings:
                - Bilateral reticulonodular opacities +/- superimposed consolidations
        - **Cardiac complications:**
            - Acute MI
            - Myocarditis and pericarditis
            - Transient ECG changes
        - **CNS manifestations:**
            - Broad clinical presentation - seizures, encephalopathy, encephalitis, stroke, GBS, Reye's Syndrome etc.
        - **Myositis and rhabdomyositis**
- **Diagnosis**
    - Clinical diagnosis of influenza-like illness, virological diagnosis required if influence Mx (done retrospecively)
    - **Virological diagnosis:**
        - RAT on NPA
        - RT-PCR
        - Viral cultures and serology (rarely used)
- **Management:**
    - **General measures**
        - Personal hygiene
        - Symptomatic treatment
        - Droplet precaution
    - **Antiviral therapy**
        - Amantadines and rimantadines (no longer in use)
            - **Indications** - Effective against flu A
            - **MOA** - interferes with viral uncoating
            - **Adverse effects** - toxic effect, mainly neurological
        - **Neuraminidase inhibitors**
            - **Indications** - Effective against fluA and fluB, Active when given as soon as possible **(within 48 hours)**, but still given if severe and hospitalised
            - **Selection** - Tamiflu (Oseltamivir 75 mg bid for 5d PO), Relenza (Zanamivir 10mg bid for 5d inhalation)
            - **MOA** - Inhibition of neuraminidase, prevention of viral release
