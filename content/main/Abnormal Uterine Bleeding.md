# Abnormal Uterine Bleeding

- **Definition and terminology:**
    - <u>Per vaginal bleeding</u> (PV bleeding) - a presenting problem detected by the patient, but which may be of gynaecological origin (further classified into lower and upper genital tract causes), or non-gynaecological origin (PR bleeding, haematuria) that is mistakened by patients as PV bleeding
    - <u>Abnormal uterine bleeding</u> (AUB) - bleeding that is **assumed to arise from the uterus**
- **Normal menstrual parameters vs abnormal uterine bleeding** - by 1) frequency, 2) regularity, 3) duration, 4) flow, and 5) any unscheduled bleeding: 
	![](img/normal_menstrual_parameters.png)
    - <u>Frequent menstrual bleeding</u> - refers to periods that start at intervals of \< 24d
    - <u>Irregular bleeding</u> - increased cycle length variance, which is dependent on patient age:
        - 18-25y - cycle length variance \> 9d
        - 26-41y - cycle length variance \> 7d
        - 42-45y - cycle length variance \> 9d
    - <u>Prolonged menstrual bleeding</u> - refers to menstrual bleeding consistently lasting for \> 8d (but not always a/w HMB)
    - <u>Heavy menstrual bleeding</u> - excessive total volume of menstrual blood flow irrespective of duration, frequency or regularity, defined objectively as \> 80 ml/L, but clinically defined as any volume that interferes w/ patient's physical, social, and psychological QoL
    - <u>Intermenstrual bleeding</u> - AUB that occurs in between well-defined cyclical menses, which may be cyclical or acyclical:
        - Cyclical midcycle IMB - refers to small amounts of bleeding mid cycle attributed to midcycle drop of circulating E2 levels just after ovulation
        - Acyclical intermenstrual bleeding - bleeding in between cyclincal menses that are less predictable (often a/w non-malignant lesions)
- **Causes of abnormal uterine bleeding** (AUB) - FIGO PALM-COIN system stratifies causes into structural and functional: 
	![](img/figo_palm_coein_system.png)
    - **Structural causes** (PALM):
        - <u>Polyps</u> (AUB-P) - benign polyps of the endometrium w/ increasing prevalence w/ age, typically presenting as **intermenstrual bleeding**, but rarely HMB, post-coital bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding etc.
        - <u>Adenomyosis</u> (AUB-A) - presence of endometrial glands within uterine myometrium, typically presenting w/ 1) **HMB**, 2) **secondary dysmenorrhoea**, and 3) **enlarged uterus** on P/E
        - <u>Leiomyomas</u> (AUB-L) - fibroids usually present w/ **HMB**, as presence of large fibroid results in increased surface area for endometrial growth and heavier flow
        - <u>Malignancy and hyperplasia</u> (AUB-M) - endometrial hyperplasia or endometrial carcinoma (or other rare uterine cancers \[e.g. uterine sarcomas, GTN\]), typically presents as 1) **post-menopausal bleeding**, or 2) **IMB** in pre-menopausal women
    - **Functional causes** (COEIN):
        - <u>Coagulopathy</u> (AUB-C) - disorders of primary or secondary haemostasis may present with **HMB**, and suspicion should be raised when <u>bleeding diathesis</u> is elicited from Hx taking (N.B. note correlation of bleeding severity w/ estrogen levels \[e.g. bleeding tendancies masked by COC pills\])
        - <u>Ovarian dysfunction</u> (AUB-O) - non-cyclical production of estrogen and progesterone due to dysfunction of HPO axis resulting in excessive endometrial growth and shedding, often presenting with irregular, prolonged or heavy menstrual bleeding
        - <u>Endometrial dysfunction</u> (AUB-E) - disorders of local endometrial haemostasis or prior inflammatory or infective endometrial disorders resulting in presentation as 1) **HMB**, and 2) **IMB**
        - <u>Iatrogenic causes</u> (AUB-I) - drug Tx such as contraception, anti-coagulation, replacement hormonal therapy, or drugs that cause hyperprolactinaemia
        - <u>Not otherwise-specified</u> (AUB-N) - unclassified entities inclduing Cesarean scar defect, AV malformation, endometrial myometrial vascularity or others
- **Ovarian dysfunction** - endocrine causes of anovulation and oligo-anovulation in a non-pregnant reproductive-age patient:
    - **Pathophysiology of AUB-O** - estrogen and progesterones are not produced cyclically:
        - <u>Unopposed estrogen production</u> - estrogen continues to be produced but are un-opposed by progesterone allowing for continued proliferation of the endometrium
        - <u>Absence of progesterone-related changes</u> - shedding is thus not uniform, and is often irregular, prolonged and heavy
    - **Clinical features of AUB-O:**
        - Irregular menses prior to amenorrhoea
        - Prolonged and heavy bleeding
    - **Etiology of AUB-O:**
        - **Hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism:**
            - <u>Structural hypothalamic/ pituitary disease</u> - e.g. neoplastic (adenoma, meningioma), infiltrative (e.g. haemosiderosis, Langher's cell histiocytosis histiocytosis, hypophysitis), traumatic (TBI/ parasellar surgery)
            - <u>Functional gonadotrophin deficiency</u>:
                - Anorexia nervosa
                - Excessive physical exercise
                - Psychological stress
                - Other endocrinopathies - e.g. **primary hypothyroidism**, Cushing's syndrome, Hyperprolactinaemia, hyper-androgenism
                - Chronic systemic illness (e.g. malabsorption, renal failure)
            - <u>Isolated gonadotrophic deficiency</u> - e.g. Kallmann's syndrome
        - **Ovarian dysfunction:**
            - <u>Hypergonadotrophic hypogonadism</u> - acquired or congenital gonadal disorders
            - <u>Polycystic ovary syndrome</u> - characterised by sonographic features of the polycystic ovaries and biochemical/clinical hyperandrogenism
- **Approach to AUB in non-pregnant reproductive-age patients** - dependent on haemodynamic stability:
    - <u>Haemodynamically unstable patients</u> - requires resuscitation and urgent admisison prior to workup
    - <u>Haemodynamically stable patients</u> - evaluation by detailed Hx, P/E and Ix
- **Salients points of Hx:**
    - **Recent or current pregnancy** - bleeding is common in all trimesters of pregnancy and after pregnancy loss or termination
    - **HPI** - characterisation of current menstrual pattern:
        - <u>Ensure PV bleeding</u> - is the patient certain bleeding is from the vagina:
            - Blood in toilet after urination (suggestive of haematuria)
            - Blood in toilet after defecation (suggestive of PR bleeding)
            - Blood when wiping (suggestive of anything around the perineum)
            - Blood on tampon/ pads (note the site)
        - <u>Onset and duration</u> - how long the patient has noticed the problem
        - <u>Quality</u> - assess the **pattern of bleeding**:
            - **Frequent menstrual bleeding** - patient suspects an increase in frequency of menstruation
            - **Heavy menstrual bleeding** - suspects an increase in frequency in flow
            - **Irregular menstrual bleeding** - suggestive on an-ovulatory cycle
            - **Intermenstrual bleeding** - bleeding in between menstrual periods
        - <u>Timing of bleeding</u>:
            - **Post-coital bleeding** - consider vaginal or cervical sources of bleeding
            - **instrumentation** - new onset bbleeding in relation w/ instrumentation (e.g. TOP, IUD) localises to the uterus
    - **Menstrual Hx** - characterisation of past menstrual pattern:
        - <u>LMP</u> - note first day of last menstrual periods and several previous menstrual periods (for assessment of frequency and regularity)
        - <u>Menarche and menopause</u> - estimate estrogen exposure
        - <u>Cycle length and regularity</u> - characterize previous and current cycle length and regularity
        - <u>Menstrual flow</u> - characterize previous and current flow objectively by VAS or by frequency of changing pads/ tampons (consider whether full-soaked when changing)
        - <u>Anaemic Sx</u> - physical impact of AUB
    - **Sexual Hx** - consider pregnancy, or STI/ PID
    - **Past gynaecological Hx:**
        - <u>Cervical smear Hx</u> - past excisional cervical procedures or Mx of CIN suggests possibility of recurrent or persistent cervical neoplasia
        - <u>Myomectomy</u> - past Hx of myomectomy suggestive of recurrent or persistent uterine fibroids
    - **Past obstetrics Hx** - consider cesarian scar defect, or uterine surgery
    - **Associated Sx:**
        - <u>Abdominal pain</u> - ongoing abdominal/ pelvic pain should raise suspicion of pregnancy complications, or pelvic inflammatory disease
        - <u>Vaginal discharge/ Fever</u> - presence of vaginal discharge localises to an infective or non-infective gynaecological cause
        - <u>Dysmenorrhoea</u> - pain that precedes periods are classical of endometriosis, while new-onset pain (secondary dysmenorrhoea) may be associated w/ adenomyosis
        - <u>Urinary or bowel Sx</u> - underlying mass effect (e.g. fibroids)
        - <u>Other bleeding manifestations</u> - e.g. recent bruising, epistaxis, PR bleeding
        - <u>Other Sx of endocrinopathies</u> (if appropriate) - Sx of hyper/ hypothyroidism, Sx of PCOS
    - **PMH:**
        - <u>Enodocrine disorders</u> - e.g. thyroid disorders, Cushing's syndrome, hyperprolactinaemia
        - <u>Bleeding disorders</u> - e.g. known bleeding disorders, FHx of bleeding disorders
    - **Drug Hx:**
        - <u>Drugs affecting haemostasis</u> - e.g. anti-platelets and anticoagulants
        - <u>Estrogen</u> - e.g. COC pills, tamoxifen for breast cancer
        - <u>Drugs causing hyperprolactinaemia</u> - e.g. typical anti-psychotics, antidepressants
    - **SHx** - smoking and alcohol
    - **FHx** - FHx of breast, ovarian, colorectal cancer
- **P/E:**
    - **General examination** - for bleeding tendancies, goitre, or evidence of hyperandrogenism (e.g. hirsutism, acne, clitoromegaly)
    - **Pelvic examination:**
        - <u>Inspection</u> - potential sites of bleeding in lower genital tract and perineum (+/- active bleeding)
        - <u>Speculum examination</u> - for any vision lesions in the vaginal wall and cervix
        - <u>Bimanual examination</u>:
            - **Size** - enlargement of uterus a/w pregnancy, adenomyosis (diffuse), fibroids (irregular), or ocassionally malignancy
            - **Mobility** - fixed uterus suggestive of pelvic adhesions (e.g. PID, endometriosis)
            - **Adnexal mass** - e.g. tubo-ovarian abscess
- **Method of evaluation of endometrium** - by invasiveness:
    - Transvaginal USG
    - Saline-infused sonography
    - Blind endometrial sampling
    - Endometrial biopsy
    - Dilatation and curettage
    - Hysteroscopy
- **Approach to selection of initial Ix for endometrial evaluation** - dependent on 1) menopausal state, and 2) risk factors:
    - <u>Post-menopausal women</u> - TVUSS as initial screening Ix, with blind endometrial sampling if TVUSS abnormal (consider if excessive risk factors)
    - <u>Pre-menopausal women with HMB</u> - TVUSS often inconclusive as endometrial thickness fluctuates, endometrial sampling should be performed liberally (esp if risk factors present)
- **Initial Ix:**
    - <u>Urine pregnancy test</u> - r/o pregnancy; if +ve work up along the lines of pregnancy-related per-vaginal bleeding
    - <u>Pelvic USG</u> - typically as initial for identification of structural causes of AUB
    - <u>Endometrial sampling</u> - if presenting w/ intermenstrual bleeding, post-menopausal bleeding, and patients w/ HMB w/ many risk factors (to detect endometrial carcinoma)
- **Transvaginal USG** - non-invasive and allws for additional evaluation of structural lesion of the endometrium (polyps, fibroids), and the adnexa:
    - <u>Role</u>:
        - Assessment of endometrial thickness (role more defined in post-menopausal women)
        - Assessment of other focal structural lesions (e.g. endometrial polyps or fibroids)
        - Assessment of the adnexa
    - <u>Sonographic measurement of endometrial thickness</u> - performed in sagittal view:
        - Endometrial thickness is expected to be thin (\< 4mm) in post-menopausal women, and pre-menopausal women at beginning of cycle
        - Endometrial thickness varies w/ menstrual cycle (4-16 mm)
        - Test properties of TVUSS for detection of endometrial neoplasia is dependent on the cutoff (90% for 5mm, 95% for 4mm, 98% for 3mm but Sp reducing)
    - <u>General cutoff for futher workup</u> - office Bx:
        - \< 4mm - expectant Mx acceptable as unlikely to be malignant
        - \> 4mm - consider at least endometrial sampling
    - <u>Follow-up studies</u>:
        - Saline-infused sonography
        - Diagnostic hysteroscopy
- **Endometrial sampling:**
    - <u>Indications</u> - for all thought to be at risk of endometrial hyperplasia/ neoplasia:
        - Presentation w/ intermenstrual bleeding
        - Higher age (e.g. \> 45y)
        - Persistent HMB w/ risk factors of endometrial cancer (e.g. obesity, chronic ovulatory dysfunction, tamoxifen)
        - Abnormal findings on pelvic USG
    - <u>Procedure</u> - blind sampling of the endometrium using the Pipelle for **cytological specimen**
    - <u>Test properties</u> - test will perform better if pathology is diffuse instead of focal:
        - High Sn (99.6% in post-M vs 91% in pre-M)
        - High Sp (\> 98%)
    - <u>Advantages</u>:
        - Minimal cervical dilatation required
        - Performed w/o anaesthesia or w/ local anaesthesia
        - Relatively quick procedure
        - Lower risk of uterine perforation compared w/ other sampling techniques
    - <u>Disadvantages</u>:
        - Operator-dependent (operator may fail to enter the uterine cavity or only sample the lower segments)
        - Scant specimen (inconclusive studies)
        - Risk of false negative Bx (dependent on the area of endometrium affected)
        - Absence of histological information
- **Additional Ix** - dependent on <u>pattern of bleeding</u>:
    - <u>Heavy menstrual bleeding, intermenstrual bleeding or post-menopausal bleeding</u> - CBC, clotting profile, SIS, endometrial sampling
    - <u>Irregular bleeding, oligomenorrhoea or amenorrhoea</u> - hormone profile (FSH, LH, E2, testosterone, PRL, TFT)
- **Dilatation and currettage** - have been superceded by office Bx as 1st-line Ix:
    - <u>Procedure</u> - dilatation of cervix and curette to suction tissue from uterine lining
    - <u>Indications</u>:
        - Endometrial Bx cannot be performed
        - Non-diagnostic office Bx
        - Persistent bleeding despite demonstrated benign pathology on office Bx
    - <u>Test properties</u> - meta-analysis shows non-inferiority of office Bx compared w/ D&C (hence superceded)
- **Hysteroscopy:**
    - <u>Procedure</u> - insertion of hysteroscopy for visualisation and guided Bx of lesion
    - <u>Test properties</u> - theoretically highest Sn but still not 100%
    - <u>Disadvantage</u> - risk of tumour dissemination due to intraperitoneal spillage of endometrial cells through fallopian tube
- **Mx:**
    - **Principles of Mx:**
        - **Goals of Mx:**
            - <u>Dx and Mx of underlying cause</u> - e.g. identification and Mx of endometrial hyperplasia/ carcinoma; identification of underlying bleeding diathesis; underlying endocrine pathology
            - <u>General medical Mx</u> - establish regular bleeding pattern or amenorrhoea by medical or hormonal therapy; Tx underlying iron-deficiency anaemia
        - **Factors influencing Mx:**
            - <u>Severity of bleeding</u> - acute uterine bleeding may require urgent evaluation and haemostasis
            - <u>Indications for Tx</u> - Tx of AUB should be individualised and is not necessary unless AUB interfering w/ daily activities
            - <u>Medical comorbidities</u> - hormonal therapy may be C/I in estrogen-containing Tx
            - <u>Plans for pregnancy</u> - as hormonal therapies may provide contraception as well
        - **Medical therapies for no known causes of AUB:**
            - <u>Hormonal therapies</u> - e.g. COC pills, Merina, oral progestin, non-contraceptive estrogen-progestin formulations
            - <u>Non-hormonal therapy</u> - tranexamic acid, NSAIDs      
			![](img/selection_of_therapies_for_aub.png)
        - **Surgical Mx** - endometrial ablation, hysterectomy
    - **COC pills:**
        - <u>Indications</u> - indicated for most patients w/ absence of infective, endocrine or structural etiologies w/ **no contraindications to estrogen:**
            - HMB - reduces menstrual flow
            - Anovulation (AUB-O) - cycle regulation and prevention of endometrial hyperplasia and carcinoma
        - <u>Schedule</u> - COC pills can be prescribed in:
            - Cyclic dosing (withdrawal bleeding every 1 mo)
            - Extended dosing (withdrawal bleeding every 3 mo)
            - Continuous dosing (no withdrawal bleeding)
        - <u>C/I for estrogen</u>:
            - **Increased risk of venous thromboembolism:**
                - Past Hx or FHx of VTE
                - Known inherited thrombophilia
                - Other thrombophilic states (e.g. active malignancy, nephrotic syndrome, anti-phospholipid syndrome)
            - **Increased risk of arterial thromboembolism:**
                - <u>Known cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease</u> - known IHD, Hx of stroke or TIA, complicated valvular heart disease (e.g. resulting in AF)
                - <u>Known risk factors of atherosclerosis</u> - smoking (heavy smoker \> 35y), DM, HTN (poorly controlled), obesity
                - <u>Migraine w/ aura</u> - C/I at any age as increased risk of stroke
        - <u>S/E</u>:
            - **Mild S/E** - fluid retention, headache, weight gain
            - **Catastrophic S/E:**
                - Cardiovascular disease and stroke
                - VTE
                - Breast cancer
    - **Levonorgestrel IUD** (Merina):
        - <u>Indications</u> - typically first-line Tx for those where COC pills are contraindicated and do not desire to conceive
        - <u>C/I for IUD</u>:
            - Plans for pregnancy
            - Active pelvic infections
            - Anatomically distorted uterine cavity (since high risk of expulsion)
    - **Tranexamic acid:**
        - <u>MOA</u> - anti-fibrinolytic:
            - Prevents conversion of plasminogen into plasmin
            - Reduces plasmin effect on fibrinolysis
        - <u>Dosing and duration</u> - 1300 mg tid for as long as 5d during menstruation (dose-adjustment for those w/ impaired kidney function)
        - <u>C/I</u>:
            - History of thrombosis
            - Known inherited or acquired thrombophilia (e.g. active malignancy, APLS)
        - <u>S/E</u> - generally well tolerated:
            - N/V
            - Headaches
            - Dysmeonorrhoea (as TXA has no effect on reducing dysmenorrhoea)
    - **NSAIDs:**
        - <u>MOA</u> - reduced PGE2 synthesis in endometrium, resulting in vasoconstriction
        - <u>Dosing and regimen</u> - taken on first day of bleeding and continued for 2-3 d:
            - Ibupprofen 600 mg bid-qid
            - Naproxen 500 mg at onset and 3-5h later, then 250-500 mg bid
            - Mefenamic acid 500 mg bid
        - <u>C/I</u> - concern for bleeding disorders
