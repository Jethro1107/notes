# Eczema


- **Definition** - synonymous with dermatitis, which refers with inflammation of the epidermis and the upper dermis
- **Pathophysiology of eczema** - may be associated w/ different causes:
    - <u>Acute changes</u>:
        - Spongiosis (i.e. epidermal oedema)
        - Intra-epidermal vesiculation (multilocular blisters)
    - <u>Chronic changes</u>:
        - Acanthosis (epidermal thickening)
        - Vasodilation and T-cell infiltration of the upper epidermis

## Atopic Eczema

- **Definition** - generalised prolonged hypersensitivity to common environmental antigens such as pollens and house dust mites
- **Pathophysiology of atopic eczema:**
	- **Genetic factors:**
		- _Atopy_ - genetic predisposition to produce exess IgE, likely to manifest as one or more of a group of diseases such as food allergies, allergic rhinitis, and subsequently asthma
		- _Filaggrin gene mutations_ - results in epidermal barrier impairment results in penetration of allergens through the epidermis and thus causing immune sensitisation and subsequent inflammation
	- **Environmental exposure:**
		- _Early childhood exposures_ - in-utero or early childhood exposures to allergens have an etiological role
- **Clinical features of atopic eczema:**
	- **Eczematous rash** - poorly demarcated erythematous/ oedematous rash where extreme pruritis and scratching alters morphology:
		- _Character_ - dependent on disease stages and demographic:
			- Acute stage - often characterised by erythematous (ocassionally violaceous), oedematous papules or papulovesicles
			- Chronic stage - often a/w secondary changes (see below)
		- _Distribution_ - varies w/ age:
			![[distribution_and_character_of_rash_in_atopic_eczema.png]]
		- _Associated changes_:
			- Pigmentation - hyper and hypo-pigmentations may occur after healing
			- Lichenification - thickening and hardening of skin w/ rough texture due to chronic friction
			- Erythoderma - severe atopic eczema where areas of eczema coalesce into larger regions of generalised erythema
	- **Pruritis** - cardinal feature of eczema; skin involved is extremely itchy and accounts for major secondary changes of morphology (e.g. lichenification)
	- **Xerosis** - skin dryness occurs even when eczema is in remission

## Seborrhoeic eczema

- **Definition** - erythematous scaly rash over areas of skin characterised by seborrhoeic glands, including the scalp (dandruff), central face, nasolabial folds, eyebrows, central chest, and upper back
- **Clinical associations:**
	- Overgrowth of Pityrosporum yeast (may be etiological)
	- Severe immunodeficiency (esp. severe or recalcitrant seborrhoeic eczema)
## Discoid eczema

- **Definition** - discrete coin-shaped eczematous, often impetiginised lesions typically over the limbs of men, secondary to a chronic pruritic condition
- **Etiology** - often secondary to another chronic pruritic condition (see section on pruritis):
	- _Primary skin disorders_ - e.g. atopic eczema
	- _Systemic disorders_ - e.g. renal failure
## Irritant eczema

- **Definition** - eczematous lesions caused by exposure to irritants
- **Common irritants** - chronic exposure may be required for weaker irritants:
	- Detergents
	- Acids and alkalis
	- Solvents
	- Abrasives
- **Predisposition to irritant eczema:**
	- _Endogenous factors_ - elderly, fair-skinned individuals, atopic individuals
	- _Exogenous factors_ - e.g. occupational exposures (dishwashers)
## Allergic contact eczema

- **Definition** - delayed hypersensitivity reaction following contact w/ antigens or haptens
- **Common allergens in contact eczema:**
	- ![[Pasted image 20260523121353.png]]
- **Pathophysiology of allergic contact eczema** - type IV hypersensitivity reaction:
	- _Prior sensitization_ - previous allergen exposure is required for esensitisation such that subsequent reaction is specific to the allergen or closely related chemicals
	- _Delayed hypersensitivity reaction_ - reaction delayed, and oedema may be a prominent features
- **Common patterns of eczema suggestive of allergic contact eczema:**
	- _Eczema of the ear lobe_ - correlates to a Hx of exposure to nickel in ear lobes
	- _Eczema of the wrists_ - correlates to a Hx of wearing watches (metallic or leather)
	- _Eczema near the umbilicus_ - correlates to a Hx of exposure to nickel in jean studs
	- _Eczema of the hands and wrists_ - correlates w/ Hx of wearing rubber gloves
## Asteatotic eczema

- **Definition** - specific form of eczema that occurs in dry skin and common in the elderly
- **Predisposing factors** - low humidity:
	- Central heating
	- Over-washing
	- Diuretics
	- Cholesterol-lowering drugs
- **Clinical features of asteatotic eczema:**
	- Commonly observed in the _lower limbs_
	- Characterised by a "_crazy-paving pattern_", w/ fine fissuring on an erythematous background
	- ![[Pasted image 20260523122217.png]]
## Gravitational (stasis) eczema

- **Definition** - form of eczema of the lower legs often a/w venous insufficiency
- **Associated features** - often a/w signs of venous insufficiency:
	- Telangiectasia and varicosities
	- Oedema
	- Lipodermatosclerosis
	- Venous ulcers

## Lichen simplex

- **Definition** - localised form of lichenification secondary to chronic rubbing and scratchig
- **Common sites of lichen simplex:**
	- Neck
	- Lower legs
	- Anogenital regions
## Pompholyx

- **Definition** - itensely pruritic vesicles and bullae on palms, palmar surface of digits, and soles
- **Etiology of pompholyx:**
	- Atopic eczema
	- Irritant eczema
	- Allergic contact eczema
	- Fungal infections

## Investigation of eczema

- **Ix** - individualised based on Hx:
	- _Patch test_ - usuall performed if contact allergic dermatitis is suspected
	- _Total and specific IgE_ - usually not routinely taken
	- _Microbiology_ - skin swabs for bacteria or viruses (HSV); skin scrapings for suspected fungal infections
	- _Skin Bx_ - not usually required unless there is diagnostic doubt (e.g. drug eruptions, cutaneous lymphoma)
## General management of eczema

- **Principles of Mx:**
	- _Hydration_ - LSM and regular use of emoilments to reduce water loss as prophylactic Mx for most forms of eczema
	- _Topical corticosteroids_ - aimed at limiting use due to cutameous S/E
	- _Other symptomatic relief_ - relieve of pruritis causing sleep disturbances w/ sedative antihistamines
	- _Specific Tx_ - depending on form of eczema
- **Antihistamine and itch in atopic eczema** - no evidence for Mx of pruritis as itch not necessarily due to histaminergic stimulation only:
	- ![[Pasted image 20260523125309.png]]
- **Topical corticosteroids:**
	- **Approach to selection of topical corticosteroids** - selection of 1) vehicle, and 2) potency:
		- _Selection of vehicle_ - based on disease stage (acute vs chronic)
		- _Selection of potency_ - based on severity of disease, and site of manifestation
	- **Selection of vehicle** - consider disease stage, but also ease of application:
		- _Acute eczema_ - creams and lotion-based Tx may be more appropriate
		- _Chronic eczema_ - ointments are preferred due to occlusive effects
	- **General approach to regimen** - may start w/ more intensive regimen and taper down (200g of mild potent, 50g of moderately potent, 30g of potent corticosteroids per week):
		- Low-potenncy topical steroids (e..g hydrocortisone 1% or clobetasone butyrate) generally sufficient especially w/ face involvement
		- Potent or very potent corticosteroids can be used in trunk or limbs
	- **S/E of topical steroids:**
		- _Skin changes_ - telangiectasia, striae, skin atrophy, hyperpigmentation
		- _Infections_ - acne or rosacea, folliculitis
- **Topical steroid sparing agents** - topical calcineurin inhibitors (e.g. tacrolimus or pimecrolimus) may be used for the face
- **Specific Tx for atopic eczema:**
	- _General measures_ - LSM, prophylactic emoilments, topic steroids +/- Tx of secondary infections
	- _Phototherapy_ - narrowband UVB +/- PUVA or UVA1
	- _Systemic Tx_ = e.g. oral corticosteroids, intermittent cyclosporin, azathioprine or methotrexate
- **Specific Tx of seborrhoeic eczema:**
	- Topical azoles (e.g. ketoconazole shampoo, ketoconazole cream)
	- Mild steroids may be present
- **Specific Tx for irritant eczema and allergic contact eczema:**
	- _Irritant avoidance_ - e.g. protective clothing
	- _General measures_ - emoilments, topical corticosteroids
- **Specific Tx of gravitational eczema:**
	- _LSM_ - e.g. leg elevation, compression bandages for oedema and ulcerations
	- _Topical corticosteroids_ - only applied to eczematous areas but not ulcers (high risk of sensitisation to topical preservatives, ABx, and bandages)
