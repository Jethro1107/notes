## Nausea and Vomiting

## Indigestion

- **Pathophysiology of indigestion** - several mechanisms contribute to indigestion, including:
    - **Gastroesophageal reflux** - arises from:
    - _Reduced LES tone_ - occurs in certain patients w/o systemic illness, but may also be due to scleroderma, pregnancy or certain systemic illness
    - _Frequent transient LES relaxations_ (TLESRs) - increased frequency of LES relaxation during meals results in acid reflux
    - _Decreased esophageal motility or salivation_ - reduced downward peristalsis prolons esophageal acid clearance
    - _Increased intra-abdominal pressures_ - as a result of pregnancy, or central obesity
    - _Large hiatal hernia_ - increased symptomatic acid reflux
    - **Gastric motility dysfunction:**
    - _Delayed gastric emptying_ - observed in > 30% of patients w/ functional dyspepsia
    - _Increased gastric emptying_ - observed in ~5% of patients w/ functional dyspepsia
    - _Impaired accomodation_ - impairment of reflex relaxation of gastric fundus may underly selected dyspeptic symptoms such as bloating, nausea, and early satiety, and may predispose to TLESRs and thus acid reflux
    - **Visceral afferent hypersensitivity** - disturbed gastric sensation as another pathogenic factor in functional dyspepsia:
    - _Increased sensitivity to pressure and distension_ - ~ 30% of dyspeptic patients note discomfort w/ gastric or duodenal distention to lower pressures than in healthy controls
    - _Increased sensitivity to chemical stimulation_ - hypersensitivity of the stomach and duodenum w/ capsaicin, duodenal acid, or lipid perfusion
    - **Immune activation** - mucosal eosinophilia and mast cell degranulation adjacent to enteric nervous system:
    - Results in delayed gastric emptying and heightened sensitivity
    - Typically triggered after a viral illness
    - FODMAPs (fermentable oligosaccharides, dissacharides, monossacharides and polyols) may increase duodenal inflammation
    - **Other factors:**
    - _H. pylori infection_ - proven etiologic role in peptic ulcer disease but is a minor factor in functional dyspepsia pathogenesis
    - _Psychological factors_ - anxiety, depression, and stress play contributing roles in some cases
    - _Neuroendocrine abnormalities_ - various imaging studies demonstrate hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis dysregulation as well as autonomic nervous system abnormalities
- **DDx of indigestion:**
    - _Gastroesophageal reflux disease_ - increasing prevalence of GERD becoming one of the most common GI disorders (heartburn/ regurgitation Sx is reported weekly by 18-28% of population)
    - _Functional dyspepsia_ - cause of symptoms in 70-80% of patients w/ dyspepsia w/ prevalence of 7.2%:
    - Defined by ROME IV criteria, as bothersome postprandial fullness, early satiety, or epigastric pain or burning w/ symptom onset > 6mo before Dx in the absence of organic causes
    - Further classified into postprandial distress syndrome (PDS; 6.1%) characterised by meal-induced fullness and early satiety, and epigastric pain syndrome (EPS; 2.4%) w/ epigastric pain/ burning that may or may not be meal-related
    - Associated w/ other DGBIs such as IBS, and nongastrointestinal disorders, such as chronic fatigue, fibromyalgia, and anxiety
    - _Ulcer disease_:
    - Esophageal ulcers - most patients w/ GERD do not develop ulcers (NERD), but 5% develop erosive diseases
    - Gastric or duodenal ulcers - most common causes include H. pylori infection and NSAID use, rarer causes include Crohn's disease, hypercalcaemia, and Zollinger Ellison syndrome
    - _Malignancies_ - upper GI malignancies may present only w/ dyspeptic symptom
    - _Other causes_:
    - Infective esophagitis - opportunistic fungal (candidiasis) or viral (CMV, HSV) esophageal infections do not usually present w/ heartburn but more often causes odynophagia
    - Pill esophagitis - e.g. bisphosphonates, NSAIDs, KCl tablets, iron supplements
    - Eosinophilic esophagitis - eosinophilic inflammation of the esophagus
    - _Gastroparesis_ - 20% of gastroparesis patients note predominance of pain rather than N/V
    - _Food intolerance_ - e.g. lactose deficiency may result in bloating and discomfort
    - _Disease of the intestines_ - disorders causing malabsorption
    - _Extraperitoneal etiologies_ - congestive heart failure, TB
